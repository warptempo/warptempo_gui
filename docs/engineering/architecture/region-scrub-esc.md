# Region, Scrub, the Esc Bindings, x / Shift+X

Moved verbatim from CLAUDE.md in the 2026-07-25 slim-down (project feature-complete). This file is now the authoritative home for this detail; CLAUDE.md carries only a digest and a pointer here. Keep it current with landed rulings.
Continues from marker-ui.md.

THE REGION SIMPLIFICATION (architect-chartered 2026-07-30, the kdenlive redesign's opening arc; landed the same day): the region highlight is TRIM-ONLY FREE SCRATCH — "a temporary selection of what could potentially become a trim region" — and nothing else. The selection no longer paints a span, the cursor playhead is never suppressed, and the whole two-forms model (the region as the playhead's SPAN form, the split half-triangles, exactly-one-form-visible, the cursor/stem suppressions, the never-span-less guarantee and its producer analysis) is RETIRED. RegionProvenance is deleted whole — `RegionState` is `{active, a_frame, b_frame}` and nothing branches on where a span came from. The trim-window highlight coupling is retired with it: no route publishes the trim window as a region any more (`sync_region_to_trim_window` and both wrappers are deleted), the trim window's permanent display being its always-painted bar and endcaps, which are ALWAYS painted now that trim is always-set (normalization-and-boundaries.md owns that model).

TWO GESTURES HAVE BEEN CALLED "THE SCRUB"; ONLY ONE KEEPS THE NAME (architect 2026-07-28, stated here because this file is the docs map's designated scrub owner). (1) THE AUDITION SCRUB — the one-shot waveform CLICK ACT, `scrub_press_at` / `scrub_act_at` / `scrub_launch_at`: stop a live session, else launch one at the clicked column. It owns the word, it is the only thing `src/` names "scrub", and since the Space region launch's retirement (2026-07-30) the scrub is `scrub_launch_at`'s ONLY caller class. ONE ENTRY REACHES IT since 2026-08-13 (THE TWO HALVES BECOME ONE SURFACE): the LOWER-HALF PLAIN PRESS'S MOTIONLESS RELEASE, run as an arm of the deferred click act (`run_nav_click_act`) rather than at the mouse-down. THE PRESS-TIME DISPATCH IS DELETED and the architect's own one-day carve-out from the deferred model ("as soon as I click, it immediately starts to scrub", 2026-08-12) is SUPERSEDED by his own ruling: "the playhead scrub is an outlier. We do everything on lift the finger or on mouse up, but the playhead scrub, we do right on mouse down. We should remove that." THE HALF IS NO LONGER A SURFACE, only an ACT: its boundary is still the press test's own `y >= area.y + area.h/2` (the one expression, `waveform_lower_half` in input_pointer.cpp — now with a SINGLE reader, the press that stashes the half on the pending; the pointer cursor's Scrub zone was the second reader and is deleted with the kind, and the 1px channel-split line that drew the boundary from 2026-08-01 was RETIRED 2026-08-03), so the split is unmarked in paint AND unnamed by the cursor — the lower half is the navigation surface like everything else, and only what a MOTIONLESS CLICK does there differs. A drag past the threshold cancels the act by becoming the pan; a held press does nothing further, as it never did. THE BARE RIGHT full-height entry of 2026-08-01 IS DELETED with the same ruling — "that existed only to serve a very tall waveform, and we're shrinking the waveform" (the waveform-height clamp, the seventh ruling) — and THE RIGHT BUTTON IS FULLY UNBOUND AGAIN: a right press, plain or modified, is a consumed nothing everywhere in the product (the flag editor's right-press outside-close died with the arm it served; a right press closes nothing because it does nothing). The scrub is the REGION'S PREVIEW GESTURE by ruling ("user can click scrub region to preview"): it touches no selection, no region, no cursor and no follow state, so a scratch span rests through it and the user auditions the span by clicking inside it. (2) THE PLAYHEAD STEP — the horizontal ARROW keys, one painted column per press, TWO-LANE by the selection (see selection-model.md's lane model): with an EMPTY selection it is the waveform lane — stop playback, clear the focus, dissolve a resting region, move the playhead one column; with a NON-EMPTY selection it is the MARKER lane — the same press carries the FOCUSED marker (a position nudge in either column's home view; W+target is a consumed refusal), and the playhead rides along VISIBLY because every such route re-lands it on the committed focus. LANE EXIT IS ANY DESELECTING ROUTE (Home/End, a waveform click, a trim setter's deselect, an undo restore that empties the selection); a marker-lane press that refuses is a consumed no-op, never a waveform-lane step.

EVERY CURSOR THE PRODUCT SHOWS IS A NAMED CURSOR FROM THE USER'S OWN XCURSOR THEME (architect 2026-08-03, revising his own ruling of earlier the same day): THE SPEAKER IS RETIRED — the custom-drawn Breeze `player-volume` glyph, its ARGB32 wl_shm buffer, that buffer's dedicated pool and the whole `build_speaker_cursor` path are DELETED WHOLE, along with the `icons::Icon::PlayerVolume` entry and its committed SVG (kIconCount 14 back to 13; git history is the record, and the product now ships no cursor art and draws no cursor pixels). The architect weighed authoring our own set — outline, shadow, a de-plussed magnifier — against reading the theme's, and chose the theme's outright: a cursor is desktop furniture, and the one the user already recognises is better than the one we would draw. SEVEN KINDS, EIGHT NAMES, all present in Breeze_Light and in Adwaita: Arrow `left_ptr`, Pan `grab`, Zoom `zoom-in`, TrimResize `ew-resize`, TrimBoundBegin `left_side`, TrimBoundEnd `right_side`, and TEXT `text` (architect 2026-08-13 — the I-beam, the ONE kind with a second spelling to try before it degrades: `text`, then the older `xterm`, which Breeze ships as a symlink to the first and a legacy theme may ship alone; the table's `alt_name` column is that option and only this row uses it). Text is worn over EDITABLE TEXT UNDER THE POINTER, which is two published rects and nothing else — the open top-strip FLAG EDITOR's unrolled box, which showed the navigation surface's PAN before the kind existed (the marker lane being nav surface under it, and a hand over a text field being simply wrong), and the MODAL DIALOG's inset FIELD. Both are the click-to-caret / text-drag CLAIM regions the press path reads (`app.flag_editor_box.box` and `app.modal_dialog.field`, the painters' own stashes), so the cue is that claim's own shape rather than a new geometry. (SIX for one day, 2026-08-13, when SCRUB `crosshair` was DELETED with the zone it promised — "we need to just get rid of the crosshairs but retain the scrub action": the lower half's audition became a CLICK act and a click carries no cue anywhere in the product, so the whole waveform wears the Pan, which is the drag both halves now take. A kind is deleted when its zone stops being a zone; the last pair joined 2026-08-03 with the endcap split below — `left_side` / `right_side` are the arrow-with-a-bar shapes a window manager shows on a window's left or right edge, which is exactly the act they mark, extending ONE boundary.) Each is a `wl_cursor_theme_get_cursor` lookup at init plus its own never-re-attached `wl_surface`, carrying THE HOTSPOT THE FILE DECLARES — never a centre we compute. What the code reads is `wl_cursor_image`'s INTEGER `xhot`/`yhot` out of the XCursor binary, at the size the theme load resolved (XCURSOR_SIZE, else the freedesktop 24), and the installed Breeze_Light serves that default request from its 32x32 images: `left_ptr` 4,4, `grab` 16,16, `zoom-in` 15,15, `ew-resize` 16,15, `left_side` 4,15, `right_side` 27,15, `text` 16,15. The pointer-ish shapes are centred (grab exactly, zoom-in within a pixel, the I-beam on its own waist), `left_side` and `right_side` sit hard against their OWN edge — which is the whole point of an edge cue — and `left_ptr` sits at its tip; no rule we could compute produces all three, which is why the declaration is taken verbatim (and the numbers scale with the resolved size, so they are the default load's rather than constants). The lookup is ONE per-kind loader driven by a small `{kind, xcursor name, optional second name}` table (`load_theme_cursor` / `load_cursor_theme`, platform_wayland.cpp), not seven copies of the arrow's old one-off. A MISSING NAME DEGRADES TO THE ARROW, PER KIND, with one stderr line naming every spelling that was tried (both, on the one row that carries two) — the theme load's own failure shape, which is the precedent: a theme without these names is a poor environment, not a broken one, and nothing about the product stops working. THE SIZE RIDES THE THEME'S AXIS (XCURSOR_SIZE, else the freedesktop 24), NOT gui_scale: gui_scale is the product's one scale axis for everything the WINDOW paints and a cursor is not painted by the window, so every kind loads at one size and the pointer does not resize as it crosses a zone boundary. THE PLATFORM STRUCTURE THAT SURVIVED THE SPEAKER is the part that turned out to be right: the `GuiCursorKind` enum, the REMEMBERED kind, `apply_cursor_kind` as the one applier with the capture guard inside it (the capture's hide is still the only `set_cursor` outside it, and the release restores the remembered kind rather than the arrow), and `set_cursor_kind`'s apply-only-on-change.

THE CURSOR PROMISES THE GESTURE, which is the whole rule and the whole design constraint, and it is why there is ONE zone map DERIVED FROM THE PRESS PATH rather than a list of situations invented beside it (`pointer_cursor_kind`, input_handler.h — one owner, one caller). The zones, each taken from the branch that actually claims the press (re-derived 2026-08-13 under THE TWO-HALVES RULING): the NAVIGATION SURFACE — the WHOLE waveform + the ruler + the marker lane's empty stretches, in EVERY view, read from its ONE geometry owner `point_on_nav_surface` rather than re-spelled here — answers PAN plain (the cue promises the grab-pan; the motionless deferred click needs no cue, no click anywhere carrying one, and BOTH halves' acts are clicks — the placement and the scrub alike) and ZOOM with CTRL held (since 2026-08-14 the ONE NAV DRAG'S ZOOM MODIFIER — the same drag, ctrl live mid-gesture — so plain and ctrl name the same rect and the hover pair promises exactly the two phases one press can reach; a ctrl press on a FLAG is the membership toggle, no cue); the TRIM BAR splits three ways (below); the gap band and everything unnamed are the Arrow. EVERY MARKER FLAG BOX ANSWERS TrimResize since 2026-08-13 (architect): markers move SIDE TO SIDE, which is the trim bridge's own promise, and the flag box is the marker's ONE pointer surface in every view since the stems went pointer-inert — so the cue names the SURFACE rather than one press branch (the plain drag IS the flag drag in a live view at home; an off-home flag, a locked tab and the `h` view's click-only diff flags wear it too), read through the press path's own `hit_test_flag`, which is the same term the navigation surface carves itself out with and the same stash the diff lane publishes into. The flag boxes were the Arrow's most-quoted member until that day. THE LOWER HALF'S SCRUB ZONE IS GONE FROM THE MAP with the kind itself (2026-08-13) — and with it the mode scope it carried, the `h` view having been its one per-zone consumer. THE TRIM ENDCAPS ARE NOT THE BRIDGE (architect 2026-08-03, splitting what had been one band-wide TrimResize and closing both mismatches the cursor round recorded): an ENDCAP extends ONE boundary and takes its own bound's shape — the BEGIN cap `left_side` (TrimBoundBegin), the END cap `right_side` (TrimBoundEnd) — while the inter-cap BRIDGE keeps `ew-resize`, because the bridge drag moves BOTH bounds together and the move is what the caps are being distinguished from; CTRL and CTRL+SHIFT over the bar take the SAME PAIR matched to the bound each one writes (ctrl is BEGIN, ctrl+shift is END), because those clicks set a bound and arm a single-bound drag on it — boundary extension by another route, and the reason they no longer show the Arrow. AND THE CUE NOW READS THE ROUTER'S OWN OWNERS rather than the band: `hit_test_trim_endcap` and `point_in_trim_bridge_span` for the plain hover (the second hoisted out of `route_trim_bar_press`'s inline body when the cursor needed the same verdict — one owner, two consumers, the endcap test's twin) and `trim_bound_click_frame` for the two ctrl clicks (hoisted out of `set_trim_bound_at_click`, which keeps only the write), so a point on the band that would ARM NOTHING — the bar's outside on a trimmed-in window, or a ctrl click the STRICTLY-INSIDE guard would consume as a no-op — shows the Arrow, and cue and gesture agree BY CONSTRUCTION rather than by proximity. THE MODIFIER ARMS OUTRANK THE PLAIN ZONES ON THE WAVEFORM exactly as the press path ranks them. SHIFT IS NOT IN THE MAP over any surface: it is the REGION FORMER (the one mouse region gesture since 2026-08-12, its surface grown over the lower half 2026-08-13), which carries no cue, so it takes the Arrow like everything unnamed; the one place a shift combination IS named is ctrl+shift on the trim bar, a real bound-set claim rather than an unbound stray. ALT IS NOT IN THE MAP EITHER: its pointer vocabulary is empty (2026-08-12), so an alt hover answers Arrow everywhere. EVERY REFUSAL APPLIES TO EVERY KIND, not just to Scrub — the prompt, the four dialog modal editors (whose veil consumes every press outside their box, 2026-08-12), an open dropdown, a loading or empty audio state, and any live pointer gesture — which is what makes the cues HOVER-ONLY. THE VEIL'S BLANKET HAS ONE EXCEPTION SINCE 2026-08-13, and it is an exception INSIDE the blanket rather than a hole in it: the dialog's INSET FIELD answers Text, because the field is the one rect under the veil that TAKES a pointer act (the caret click and the text drag claim exactly that published rect), so naming it is the map's own rule and not an escape from the veil. The dialog BUTTONS are the veil's other reachable surface and stay Arrow, a button carrying no cue anywhere; a PROMPT publishes a zero field and so cannot reach the arm at all. HOVER-ONLY HAS ITS OWN NAMED EXCEPTIONS (architect 2026-08-03): A LIVE TRIM GESTURE OWNS THE CURSOR. A begin-endcap drag keeps `left_side`, an end-endcap drag `right_side`, the bridge (pair) drag `ew-resize`, from the press to the release and wherever the pointer wanders — the PENDING sub-threshold state rides the same arm, so the cue holds from the moment the press lands — and the kind is read from the drag's OWN RECORD of what it grabbed (`trim_drag`/`pending_trim_drag`'s `both`/`is_begin`), never re-derived from the pointer's position, because dragging a bound is exactly the act of taking the pointer off the band and the cue must not flicker through the band map's answers on the way. Trim can be an exception because on that gesture the thing being dragged is the thing the cursor names, so the cue stays true throughout — AND THE OVERVIEW BOX'S THREE DRAGS ARE THE SAME EXCEPTION ON THE SAME REASONING: the two EDGE drags joined at the lane rework (2026-08-12) and THE BOX PAN 2026-08-13 (architect), so all three keep the kind their own record names (`overview_drag.kind` — TrimBoundBegin / TrimBoundEnd / TrimResize) for the gesture's life, and the lane no longer has one drag that drops to the Arrow mid-slide while its neighbours hold — AND THE MARKER REPOSITION DRAG IS THE THIRD MEMBER, 2026-08-14 (architect: "it should remain left/right arrows during the drag, like trim and overview drag currently do"), in the same member shape and on the same reasoning: the flag box wears `ew-resize` at rest because a marker slides side to side, the drag IS that slide, so the cue is true for the gesture's whole life and falling to the Arrow the moment the marker started moving was the odd one out. It has exactly ONE shape, so the record is simply that it is live (`drag` / `pending_marker_drag`, the trim pair's own pending arrangement) with no kind to read and no position to re-derive from; the gesture is capture-free, so there is a visible cursor to keep. Every other gesture keeps the uniform refusal — no cursor changes during the region, strip or grab-pan drags (the captured two hide the cursor anyway). READ-ONLY IS NOT IN THE MAP AT ALL SINCE 2026-08-07, and the change is a DELETION rather than a re-scoping: read-only protects the AUTHORED MUSICAL CONTENT (the marker stores and the engine settings) and trim is BAND, so every zone the map answers — the strip drag, the pan, the endcap and bridge drags and the two ctrl bound-set clicks — runs unrefused in a locked tab, and every cue promises it. THE RETIRED RECORD, kept because it was quoted widely: read-only used to be PER-ZONE, each zone following its own gesture — the strip drag and the pan navigation and unrefused, the then-separate scrub zone unrefused because an audition is not a mutation, but the TRIM drags refusing through the band-level read-only return at the plain trim-bar press, the endcap and bridge cues refusing with them, and the two ctrl cues taking the same answer through `trim_bound_click_frame`'s own first gate. Both of those gates are deleted; the zone map still tests nothing itself, and now there is nothing for it to test. The `h` HISTORY VIEW is the sole per-zone consumer left (its own paragraph below). The ruling's home is `read_only_key_blocked` (input_key_dispatch.cpp), restated in `validation_topology.md`. THE FLAG editor refuses nothing — it is pointer-transparent by ruling, so a waveform press still reaches the audio under an open one and the cursor may not lie about that; its own BOX is not a refusal but a ZONE, answering Text (above) while everything around it answers whatever surface lies under the editor, and that arm sits ABOVE the modifier arms because the caret claim in the press path does — a ctrl or shift press on the open box still places a caret. THERE IS NO PRESSED-STATE CURSOR AND NO "grabbing", deliberately (architect, explicitly): the grab-pan takes a pointer capture (at its threshold crossing, so a pending click never hides the cursor) and the capture HIDES the cursor, which is what buys unlimited travel, and the strip drag does the same while painting a stem line that shows where the gesture is — a pressed cursor would flash for one frame and then be invisible for the whole gesture. THE CURSOR IS RESOLVED ONCE PER RUN-LOOP ITERATION, BY ONE OWNER (architect 2026-08-03, superseding in one stroke the three per-site rulings retold just after it). The platform fires a hook at the TAIL of every iteration of `GuiPlatform::run`'s body — below the display dispatch, the tick and both worker completions, and deliberately NOT on the EINTR `continue`, on either connection-loss `break`, or once `should_exit_` is set — and main.cpp wires it to `refresh_pointer_cursor`, which re-runs the ONE zone map at the remembered pointer position with the platform's live modifiers and does nothing else at all: no hover recompute, no damage, no gesture logic (and nothing at all while the pointer is outside the window, where those coordinates mean nothing). THE BOUNDARY IS NOT THE CURSOR'S ALONE — later the same day it took a SECOND consumer, the open dropdown's item hover and armed item (`recompute_dropdown_hover`, called beside the cursor from the same hook body), because those faces are the same shape of thing: a pointer-derived answer whose INPUTS — there the painter-published item rects — can move with no pointer event under them, which is what this boundary is for. The cursor's own function did not widen (it still does only the cursor); the sibling call is the hook body's, and main.cpp's hook body is the authoritative list of what rides here. EVERY OTHER APPLIER IS DELETED: the push at the top of `on_motion`, the eighteen gesture-end calls in `input_pointer.cpp` (eight release arms, the button-lost arms, the refused begins), the two force-end tails in main.cpp's resize and WM-close callbacks, the `wl_keyboard.modifiers` hook that existed for this and nothing else, and `on_key`'s `CursorRefreshAtReturn` scope guard with the flag that armed it — twenty-three push sites down to one. THE PER-SITE MODEL IS THE HISTORY AND IT IS WORTH KEEPING, because each of its sites was CORRECT and the set of them was still insufficient. It grew in three rounds. MODIFIERS TAKE EFFECT AT ONCE was the first: with modifiers SELECTING between kinds rather than merely suppressing one, a Ctrl pressed under a resting pointer had to move the cue with no motion under it, so the platform fired a hook at the modifier edge itself. A GESTURE THAT ENDS RE-RESOLVES THE CURSOR was the second and closed the largest hole: the map refuses every cue while a gesture is live (the trim exception keeping its own), so the moment a teardown ran, the kind on screen was a promise about a gesture that no longer existed, and an end is neither a motion nor a modifier edge — so every end re-ran the owner strictly AFTER its own teardown, the refused BEGINS included (a begin that refuses is an end by shape, and the trim one was holding the endcap cue). GESTURES ALSO END ON A KEY was the third, missed by a derivation that walked the pointer only: the editor text drag's bare-Esc / Ctrl+Q hatch and the pointer gestures' Ctrl+Q hatch both fall onward through editor and prompt handling, so neither could re-resolve where the gesture ends and `on_key` carried the fact to its return in one scope guard. THE MAP READS ABOUT TEN INDEPENDENT FACT FAMILIES, so a push was owed by EVERY WRITER OF ANY OF THEM — a set nobody can enumerate and keep enumerated, which two consecutive review rounds each demonstrated by finding a class the previous derivation had missed, and for which whole classes had no event to hang a call on at all: a keyboard zoom moving the trim endcaps out from under a resting pointer, the zoom and navigation keys, `x`/`Shift+X`, an undo restoring trim, `o` toggling read-only, a `gui_scale` relayout, every keyboard editor open and close, and the dropdown item click that closes a menu with no motion following. A LOOP BOUNDARY IS AFTER EVERY SETTLE BY DEFINITION, so all of them are answered by the one call and the ordering rule — "run this after the state it reads has settled" — dies with the sites that owed it. THE COST is one map evaluation per poll wakeup and zero protocol traffic while the answer holds, `set_cursor_kind` applying only on a CHANGE. AND THE REFUSAL TO HANG IT ON A TIMER STANDS: the tick is the playback cadence and a second cadence owning this fact is exactly what was being avoided, while a loop boundary is not a cadence at all but the definition of settled; the pre-paint was refused for a different reason — it runs only when a frame is scheduled, so a state change that damages nothing would never reach it.

THE TWO CAPTURED GESTURES END LIKE EVERY OTHER, and the reason the one owner can serve them is that THE GUI IS NOT THE PLACE THAT KNOWS. A capture makes the GUI's pointer position the unbounded VIRTUAL travel — the compositor keeps delivering motion through the lock, so the GUI keeps naming kinds (the live-gesture Arrow, and then whatever the travel ended over) for a place the pointer is not — and the platform DROPS those kinds rather than remembering them (`GuiPlatform::set_cursor_kind`, the fact owned once as `pointer_position_unknown_`). SO THE KIND THE RELEASE HANDS BACK IS STAMPED FROM THE GESTURE'S OWN IDENTITY, not inferred from what the remembered kind happened to be when the press landed: the capture-begin call takes the cue its gesture wears — Zoom for the overview lane's strip drag (its ctrl press arms exactly on the map's overview Zoom surface), and for the ONE NAV DRAG the MODE AT ITS THRESHOLD CROSSING, Pan or Zoom, where its capture begins; a mid-gesture ctrl edge then RE-STAMPS it through `set_capture_restore_kind` (2026-08-14, the live-ctrl model — the gesture's meaning can change mid-life, so the release restores the phase it ENDED in, the stem column and Zoom after a zoom-phase end, the pointer's NOTIONAL position and Pan after a pan-phase one — never the raw traveled x, which carries the off-window debt) — and the platform writes it straight into the remembered kind on the path that CREATED THE LOCK PROXY (`GuiPlatform::begin_pointer_capture`'s `restore_kind`, the contract there), after which the drop above is what keeps it standing for the whole drag. THE INFERENCE WAS TRIED AND IT DOES NOT HOLD, which is the reason for the stamp: the cursor is re-derived once per RUN-LOOP ITERATION, and one dispatch batch can carry the motion (or the modifier edge) that selects the gesture's zone AND the press that begins the capture, with no re-derivation between them — so "the remembered kind is the last one derived from a real position" named a cue from BEFORE the gesture existed (press Ctrl and click in one batch and the release restored the pre-Ctrl cue). It is the same shape as the live TRIM cue — read the drag's own record, never the pointer's position — and the earlier reading, that the platform's restore already owned this edge so the two arms could abstain, was half true and half false: the Arrow written mid-drag WAS being remembered and it was the Arrow that came back. THE STAMP RIDES THE LOCK REQUEST ONLY (not the degraded returns, not the lock-creation failure), because a cue nobody derived must never reach a cursor that is still visible — and REQUEST is the honest word: the protocol activates a lock asynchronously (the `locked` event announces it, and our listener for it deliberately does nothing), so a created proxy is TAKEN FOR a live lock by ruling (architect 2026-08-03), labwc activating a focus-held lock promptly and a compositor that defers or declines it being an unsupported environment in the adversarial-usage category, with the scope, the rejected pending/active restructure and the accepted degradation stated once at `begin_pointer_capture`'s contract. THE DROP SPAN OUTLASTS THE LOCK deliberately, ending only at the compositor's next absolute event: the restore is a hint, and where the pointer actually came back is not ours to know until we are told — which also disposes of the converse, a resize force-end mid-capture re-deriving a confident cue from coordinates the pointer never occupied. THE COORDINATES DO NOT OUTLAST IT, and the two facts must not be conflated (2026-08-05): the restore writes `pointer_x_`/`pointer_y_` back to its own hint, so past the unlock they name the pixels the cursor is DRAWN on — the drop span is about the KIND, not the position. The split is the fix for a real defect: button events carry no coordinates in the protocol and are delivered at those fields, so while the travel survived the unlock a click made before the next physical motion was routed at the travel's end (a ruler zoom drag then a click flipped the marker view from row 4's W radio; the account is in `zoom-viewport-strip.md`). Kinds can afford to wait for the compositor; button deliveries cannot. THE DEGRADED PATH IS THE SAME CODE: with pointer-constraints or relative-pointer absent nothing locks, nothing is virtual, nothing is dropped, and the loop's next tail is the ordinary end-of-gesture re-resolve that puts the true cue back after a drag spent under the visible Arrow. Nothing in the GUI tests which case it is in.

THE ACCEPTED STALENESS IS ONE POLL WAKEUP WIDE, and that is the whole of it. Every fact the map reads is written from inside a dispatched event — a wayland event, the tick, or a worker completion — and the owner runs at that same iteration's tail, so the classes this paragraph used to enumerate (a load completing under a resting pointer, a menu closing by Esc, a zoom moving the trim endcaps out from under a still pointer, a gesture ending on a key) are simply CORRECT now, and not one of them cost a call site. THE CAPTURED GESTURES ARE THE ONE REMAINING MEMBER, a deliberate deferral rather than a hole: while the platform has no real pointer position it DROPS the kinds the map names, so a modifier released BEFORE the button, or a modal raised by a force-end mid-capture, shows at the compositor's next absolute position — one mouse movement away, and strictly better than a confidently wrong cue derived from coordinates the pointer never occupied.

THE NAVIGATION SURFACE AND ITS THREE DRAGS (architect 2026-08-12, the EIGHTH GLASS RULING, PAN-PRIMARY — reconciling laptop and glass into one gesture economy: "I'm always going to be zoomed in... I want to be able to pan", so the pan takes the PRIMARY drag on both devices and the region becomes the deliberate secondary act; superseding the 2026-07-23 press-time placement model whole). The surface is the WHOLE waveform + the RULER lane + the MARKER lane's empty stretches — the lanes are "essentially an extension of the upper half", within easy reach since the waveform-height clamp, and the LOWER half joined 2026-08-13 when the press-time scrub moved to the lift (THE TWO HALVES BECOME ONE SURFACE, its own paragraph below; `pointer-hit-testing.md` carries the ruling in full) — and it takes ONE unified vocabulary, with one geometry owner, `point_on_nav_surface`:
(1) A PLAIN PRESS IS A PENDING CLICK (`arm_nav_press` / `ScrollDragState`): NOTHING POPS AT PRESS — no deselect, no playhead, no dissolve, NO SCRUB (2026-08-13, which is what made "nothing pops at press" exceptionless), the one-day ruler former's deferred-dissolve pattern generalized to the whole surface. A MOTIONLESS RELEASE (inside the standing 8px gate) runs THE DEFERRED CLICK ACT (`run_nav_click_act`) at the press column, FORKED ON THE PRESSED HALF: on the waveform's LOWER half it is ONE AUDITION SCRUB ACT and nothing else (the arm returns above the deselect and the dissolve — the halves' one difference), and everywhere else it is the placement — everything the old press-time placement did — deselect-all, region dissolve, playhead to the column (`playhead_frame_at_click_column` through the live-domain clamp; marker-BLIND — the stems are pointer-inert), the live-scanner reseek-keeping-alive, the follow override — with playback state read AT the release (the press touched nothing, so the readings agree, and a session that ended under the hold is answered honestly; the drag-modal gate swallows every chord while the pending stands, so nothing can move the state in between). "A click ANYWHERE on the extension moves the playhead" is the ruling's own sentence — a flag click already does, and suppressing the others would be the real asymmetry.
