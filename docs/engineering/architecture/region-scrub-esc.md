# Region, Scrub, the Esc Bindings, x / Shift+X

Moved verbatim from CLAUDE.md in the 2026-07-25 slim-down (project feature-complete). This file is now the authoritative home for this detail; CLAUDE.md carries only a digest and a pointer here. Keep it current with landed rulings.
Continues from marker-ui.md.

THE REGION SIMPLIFICATION (architect-chartered 2026-07-30, the kdenlive redesign's opening arc; landed the same day): the region highlight is TRIM-ONLY FREE SCRATCH — "a temporary selection of what could potentially become a trim region" — and nothing else. The selection no longer paints a span, the cursor playhead is never suppressed, and the whole two-forms model (the region as the playhead's SPAN form, the split half-triangles, exactly-one-form-visible, the cursor/stem suppressions, the never-span-less guarantee and its producer analysis) is RETIRED. RegionProvenance is deleted whole — `RegionState` is `{active, a_frame, b_frame}` and nothing branches on where a span came from. The trim-window highlight coupling is retired with it: no route publishes the trim window as a region any more (`sync_region_to_trim_window` and both wrappers are deleted), the trim window's permanent display being its always-painted bar and endcaps, which are ALWAYS painted now that trim is always-set (normalization-and-boundaries.md owns that model).

TWO GESTURES HAVE BEEN CALLED "THE SCRUB"; ONLY ONE KEEPS THE NAME (architect 2026-07-28, stated here because this file is the docs map's designated scrub owner). (1) THE AUDITION SCRUB — the one-shot waveform press, `scrub_press_at` / `scrub_act_at` / `scrub_launch_at`: stop a live session, else launch one at the clicked column. It owns the word, it is the only thing `src/` names "scrub", and since the Space region launch's retirement (2026-07-30) the scrub presses are `scrub_launch_at`'s ONLY caller class. TWO PRESSES REACH IT since 2026-08-01: the LOWER-HALF PLAIN LEFT press (the original entry — the lower half is still the left button's whole scrub surface, its boundary the press test's own `y >= area.y + area.h/2` — the one expression, `waveform_lower_half` in input_pointer.cpp, since the pointer cursor's Scrub zone below became its second reader; the 1px channel-split line that drew that boundary from 2026-08-01 is RETIRED, architect 2026-08-03, so the gesture split is unmarked IN PAINT again — the two channel bands meet flush there and no pixel announces the threshold, and it is the CURSOR that names the surface now), and the BARE-EXACT RIGHT press ANYWHERE in the waveform, full height (the right button's first and only binding in the product; a modified right press is the standing strict no-op, and no gesture may be in flight — the gate is `any_pointer_gesture_active`, since a captured drag still delivers button events under the pointer lock; a right press outside the flag editor's box closes it exactly as a left press does — one shared owner since the architect ruled the divergence closed at his live pass, 2026-08-01). Both entries share the one derive-column-and-act body — the scrub is the REGION'S PREVIEW GESTURE by ruling ("user can click scrub region to preview"): it touches no selection, no region, no cursor and no follow state, so a scratch span rests through it and the user auditions the span by clicking inside it. (2) THE PLAYHEAD STEP — the horizontal ARROW keys, one painted column per press, TWO-LANE by the selection (see selection-model.md's lane model): with an EMPTY selection it is the waveform lane — stop playback, clear the focus, dissolve a resting region, move the playhead one column; with a NON-EMPTY selection it is the MARKER lane — the same press carries the FOCUSED marker (a position nudge in either column's home view; W+target is a consumed refusal), and the playhead rides along VISIBLY because every such route re-lands it on the committed focus. LANE EXIT IS ANY DESELECTING ROUTE (Home/End, a waveform click, a trim setter's deselect, an undo restore that empties the selection); a marker-lane press that refuses is a consumed no-op, never a waveform-lane step.

EVERY CURSOR THE PRODUCT SHOWS IS A NAMED CURSOR FROM THE USER'S OWN XCURSOR THEME (architect 2026-08-03, revising his own ruling of earlier the same day): THE SPEAKER IS RETIRED — the custom-drawn Breeze `player-volume` glyph, its ARGB32 wl_shm buffer, that buffer's dedicated pool and the whole `build_speaker_cursor` path are DELETED WHOLE, along with the `icons::Icon::PlayerVolume` entry and its committed SVG (kIconCount 14 back to 13; git history is the record, and the product now ships no cursor art and draws no cursor pixels). The architect weighed authoring our own set — outline, shadow, a de-plussed magnifier — against reading the theme's, and chose the theme's outright: a cursor is desktop furniture, and the one the user already recognises is better than the one we would draw. SEVEN KINDS, SEVEN NAMES, all present in Breeze_Light and in Adwaita: Arrow `left_ptr`, Scrub `crosshair`, Pan `grab`, Zoom `zoom-in`, TrimResize `ew-resize`, TrimBoundBegin `left_side`, TrimBoundEnd `right_side` (the last pair joined 2026-08-03 with the endcap split below — `left_side` / `right_side` are the arrow-with-a-bar shapes a window manager shows on a window's left or right edge, which is exactly the act they mark, extending ONE boundary). Each is a `wl_cursor_theme_get_cursor` lookup at init plus its own never-re-attached `wl_surface`, carrying THE HOTSPOT THE FILE DECLARES — never a centre we compute. What the code reads is `wl_cursor_image`'s INTEGER `xhot`/`yhot` out of the XCursor binary, at the size the theme load resolved (XCURSOR_SIZE, else the freedesktop 24), and the installed Breeze_Light serves that default request from its 32x32 images: `left_ptr` 4,4, `crosshair` 17,17, `grab` 16,16, `zoom-in` 15,15, `ew-resize` 16,15, `left_side` 4,15, `right_side` 27,15. The pointer-ish shapes are centred (grab exactly, crosshair and zoom-in within a pixel), `left_side` and `right_side` sit hard against their OWN edge — which is the whole point of an edge cue — and `left_ptr` sits at its tip; no rule we could compute produces all three, which is why the declaration is taken verbatim (and the numbers scale with the resolved size, so they are the default load's rather than constants). The lookup is ONE per-kind loader driven by a small `{kind, xcursor name}` table (`load_theme_cursor` / `load_cursor_theme`, platform_wayland.cpp), not seven copies of the arrow's old one-off. A MISSING NAME DEGRADES TO THE ARROW, PER KIND, with one stderr line naming the cursor that was missing — the theme load's own failure shape, which is the precedent: a theme without these names is a poor environment, not a broken one, and nothing about the product stops working. THE SIZE RIDES THE THEME'S AXIS (XCURSOR_SIZE, else the freedesktop 24), NOT gui_scale: gui_scale is the product's one scale axis for everything the WINDOW paints and a cursor is not painted by the window, so every kind loads at one size and the pointer does not resize as it crosses a zone boundary. THE PLATFORM STRUCTURE THAT SURVIVED THE SPEAKER is the part that turned out to be right: the `GuiCursorKind` enum, the REMEMBERED kind, `apply_cursor_kind` as the one applier with the capture guard inside it (the capture's hide is still the only `set_cursor` outside it, and the release restores the remembered kind rather than the arrow), and `set_cursor_kind`'s apply-only-on-change.

THE CURSOR PROMISES THE GESTURE, which is the whole rule and the whole design constraint, and it is why there is ONE zone map DERIVED FROM THE PRESS PATH rather than a list of situations invented beside it (`pointer_cursor_kind`, input_handler.h — one owner, one caller). The zones, each taken from the branch that actually claims the press: the waveform's LOWER half plain is Scrub (sharing the press's own `waveform_lower_half`), and it is MODE-SCOPED like the trim bar's three — the `h` history view consumes both scrub entries since playback left it (2026-08-05), so its lower half answers Arrow; the waveform EITHER HALF with ALT held is Pan, because the captured grab-pan arms anywhere inside the waveform; the waveform EITHER HALF with CTRL held is Zoom, and so is the RULER band on a plain hover — the two surfaces share a cursor because they share a gesture, `arm_strip_drag_at`'s two entries, the cue covering that gesture whole including its 2026-08-05 CLICK (a press-release that never drags jumps the playhead to the anchor column; zoom-viewport-strip.md owns the record); the TRIM BAR splits three ways (below); everything else is the Arrow. THE TRIM ENDCAPS ARE NOT THE BRIDGE (architect 2026-08-03, splitting what had been one band-wide TrimResize and closing both mismatches the cursor round recorded): an ENDCAP extends ONE boundary and takes its own bound's shape — the BEGIN cap `left_side` (TrimBoundBegin), the END cap `right_side` (TrimBoundEnd) — while the inter-cap BRIDGE keeps `ew-resize`, because the bridge drag moves BOTH bounds together and the move is what the caps are being distinguished from; CTRL and CTRL+SHIFT over the bar take the SAME PAIR matched to the bound each one writes (ctrl is BEGIN, ctrl+shift is END), because those clicks set a bound and arm a single-bound drag on it — boundary extension by another route, and the reason they no longer show the Arrow. AND THE CUE NOW READS THE ROUTER'S OWN OWNERS rather than the band: `hit_test_trim_endcap` and `point_in_trim_bridge_span` for the plain hover (the second hoisted out of `route_trim_bar_press`'s inline body when the cursor needed the same verdict — one owner, two consumers, the endcap test's twin) and `trim_bound_click_frame` for the two ctrl clicks (hoisted out of `set_trim_bound_at_click`, which keeps only the write), so a point on the band that would ARM NOTHING — the bar's outside on a trimmed-in window, or a ctrl click the STRICTLY-INSIDE guard would consume as a no-op — shows the Arrow, and cue and gesture agree BY CONSTRUCTION rather than by proximity. THE MODIFIER ARMS OUTRANK THE PLAIN ZONES ON THE WAVEFORM exactly as the press path ranks them. SHIFT IS NOT IN THE MAP over the waveform: it is the PLACEMENT PRESS since 2026-08-05, which carries no cue on either half, so it takes the Arrow like everything unnamed — and, being a modifier the waveform binds, it still refuses the Scrub cue exactly as it always did; the one place a shift combination IS named is ctrl+shift on the trim bar, a real bound-set claim rather than an unbound stray. EVERY REFUSAL APPLIES TO EVERY KIND, not just to Scrub — the prompt, the three bottom-strip modal editors, an open dropdown, a loading or empty audio state, and any live pointer gesture — which is what makes the cues HOVER-ONLY, WITH ONE NAMED EXCEPTION (architect 2026-08-03): A LIVE TRIM GESTURE OWNS THE CURSOR. A begin-endcap drag keeps `left_side`, an end-endcap drag `right_side`, the bridge (pair) drag `ew-resize`, from the press to the release and wherever the pointer wanders — the PENDING sub-threshold state rides the same arm, so the cue holds from the moment the press lands — and the kind is read from the drag's OWN RECORD of what it grabbed (`trim_drag`/`pending_trim_drag`'s `both`/`is_begin`), never re-derived from the pointer's position, because dragging a bound is exactly the act of taking the pointer off the band and the cue must not flicker through the band map's answers on the way. Trim can be the one exception because on that gesture alone the thing being dragged is the thing the cursor names, so the cue stays true throughout; every other gesture keeps the uniform refusal — no cursor changes during the marker, region, strip or pan drags (the captured two hide the cursor anyway). READ-ONLY IS PER-ZONE, each following its own gesture: the strip drag and the pan are navigation and do not refuse in a locked tab, an audition is not a mutation so the scrub does not either, but the TRIM drags DO refuse there (the band-level read-only return at the plain trim-bar press) and the endcap and bridge cues refuse with them — as do the two ctrl cues, through their own route, `trim_bound_click_frame`'s first gate being that tab's read-only bit, so the zone map tests nothing itself. THE FLAG editor refuses nothing — it is pointer-transparent by ruling, so a scrub still acts under an open one and the cursor may not lie about that. THE SCRUB ZONE IS THE LEFT PRESS'S, NOT `scrub_act_at`'s: the bare RIGHT press scrubs the waveform's FULL HEIGHT and is deliberately unmarked, because a scrub cue over the whole waveform would promise the left button something the upper half does not do. THERE IS NO PRESSED-STATE CURSOR AND NO "grabbing", deliberately (architect, explicitly): the alt-pan takes a pointer capture and the capture HIDES the cursor, which is what buys unlimited travel, and the strip drag does the same while painting a stem line that shows where the gesture is — a pressed cursor would flash for one frame and then be invisible for the whole gesture. THE CURSOR IS RESOLVED ONCE PER RUN-LOOP ITERATION, BY ONE OWNER (architect 2026-08-03, superseding in one stroke the three per-site rulings retold just after it). The platform fires a hook at the TAIL of every iteration of `GuiPlatform::run`'s body — below the display dispatch, the tick and both worker completions, and deliberately NOT on the EINTR `continue`, on either connection-loss `break`, or once `should_exit_` is set — and main.cpp wires it to `refresh_pointer_cursor`, which re-runs the ONE zone map at the remembered pointer position with the platform's live modifiers and does nothing else at all: no hover recompute, no damage, no gesture logic (and nothing at all while the pointer is outside the window, where those coordinates mean nothing). THE BOUNDARY IS NOT THE CURSOR'S ALONE — later the same day it took a SECOND consumer, the open dropdown's item hover and armed item (`recompute_dropdown_hover`, called beside the cursor from the same hook body), because those faces are the same shape of thing: a pointer-derived answer whose INPUTS — there the painter-published item rects — can move with no pointer event under them, which is what this boundary is for. The cursor's own function did not widen (it still does only the cursor); the sibling call is the hook body's, and main.cpp's hook body is the authoritative list of what rides here. EVERY OTHER APPLIER IS DELETED: the push at the top of `on_motion`, the eighteen gesture-end calls in `input_pointer.cpp` (eight release arms, the button-lost arms, the refused begins), the two force-end tails in main.cpp's resize and WM-close callbacks, the `wl_keyboard.modifiers` hook that existed for this and nothing else, and `on_key`'s `CursorRefreshAtReturn` scope guard with the flag that armed it — twenty-three push sites down to one. THE PER-SITE MODEL IS THE HISTORY AND IT IS WORTH KEEPING, because each of its sites was CORRECT and the set of them was still insufficient. It grew in three rounds. MODIFIERS TAKE EFFECT AT ONCE was the first: with modifiers SELECTING between kinds rather than merely suppressing one, a Ctrl pressed under a resting pointer had to move the cue with no motion under it, so the platform fired a hook at the modifier edge itself. A GESTURE THAT ENDS RE-RESOLVES THE CURSOR was the second and closed the largest hole: the map refuses every cue while a gesture is live (the trim exception keeping its own), so the moment a teardown ran, the kind on screen was a promise about a gesture that no longer existed, and an end is neither a motion nor a modifier edge — so every end re-ran the owner strictly AFTER its own teardown, the refused BEGINS included (a begin that refuses is an end by shape, and the trim one was holding the endcap cue). GESTURES ALSO END ON A KEY was the third, missed by a derivation that walked the pointer only: the editor text drag's bare-Esc / Ctrl+Q hatch and the pointer gestures' Ctrl+Q hatch both fall onward through editor and prompt handling, so neither could re-resolve where the gesture ends and `on_key` carried the fact to its return in one scope guard. THE MAP READS ABOUT TEN INDEPENDENT FACT FAMILIES, so a push was owed by EVERY WRITER OF ANY OF THEM — a set nobody can enumerate and keep enumerated, which two consecutive review rounds each demonstrated by finding a class the previous derivation had missed, and for which whole classes had no event to hang a call on at all: a wheel zoom moving the trim endcaps out from under a resting pointer, the zoom and navigation keys, `x`/`Shift+X`, an undo restoring trim, `o` toggling read-only, a `gui_scale` relayout, every keyboard editor open and close, and the dropdown item click that closes a menu with no motion following. A LOOP BOUNDARY IS AFTER EVERY SETTLE BY DEFINITION, so all of them are answered by the one call and the ordering rule — "run this after the state it reads has settled" — dies with the sites that owed it. THE COST is one map evaluation per poll wakeup and zero protocol traffic while the answer holds, `set_cursor_kind` applying only on a CHANGE. AND THE REFUSAL TO HANG IT ON A TIMER STANDS: the tick is the playback cadence and a second cadence owning this fact is exactly what was being avoided, while a loop boundary is not a cadence at all but the definition of settled; the pre-paint was refused for a different reason — it runs only when a frame is scheduled, so a state change that damages nothing would never reach it.

THE TWO CAPTURED GESTURES END LIKE EVERY OTHER, and the reason the one owner can serve them is that THE GUI IS NOT THE PLACE THAT KNOWS. A capture makes the GUI's pointer position the unbounded VIRTUAL travel — the compositor keeps delivering motion through the lock, so the GUI keeps naming kinds (the live-gesture Arrow, and then whatever the travel ended over) for a place the pointer is not — and the platform DROPS those kinds rather than remembering them (`GuiPlatform::set_cursor_kind`, the fact owned once as `pointer_position_unknown_`). SO THE KIND THE RELEASE HANDS BACK IS STAMPED FROM THE GESTURE'S OWN IDENTITY, not inferred from what the remembered kind happened to be when the press landed: the capture-begin call takes the cue its gesture wears — Zoom for the strip drag, whose two entries ARE the map's Zoom surfaces, Pan for the alt-pan — and the platform writes it straight into the remembered kind on the path that CREATED THE LOCK PROXY (`GuiPlatform::begin_pointer_capture`'s `restore_kind`, the contract there), after which the drop above is what keeps it standing for the whole drag. THE INFERENCE WAS TRIED AND IT DOES NOT HOLD, which is the reason for the stamp: the cursor is re-derived once per RUN-LOOP ITERATION, and one dispatch batch can carry the motion (or the modifier edge) that selects the gesture's zone AND the press that begins the capture, with no re-derivation between them — so "the remembered kind is the last one derived from a real position" named a cue from BEFORE the gesture existed (settle over the waveform's lower half, slide onto the ruler and press fast enough to share a batch, and the release put Scrub back over the ruler; press Ctrl and click in one batch and the release restored the pre-Ctrl cue). It is the same shape as the live TRIM cue — read the drag's own record, never the pointer's position — and the earlier reading, that the platform's restore already owned this edge so the two arms could abstain, was half true and half false: the Arrow written mid-drag WAS being remembered and it was the Arrow that came back. THE STAMP RIDES THE LOCK REQUEST ONLY (not the degraded returns, not the lock-creation failure), because a cue nobody derived must never reach a cursor that is still visible — and REQUEST is the honest word: the protocol activates a lock asynchronously (the `locked` event announces it, and our listener for it deliberately does nothing), so a created proxy is TAKEN FOR a live lock by ruling (architect 2026-08-03), labwc activating a focus-held lock promptly and a compositor that defers or declines it being an unsupported environment in the adversarial-usage category, with the scope, the rejected pending/active restructure and the accepted degradation stated once at `begin_pointer_capture`'s contract. THE DROP SPAN OUTLASTS THE LOCK deliberately, ending only at the compositor's next absolute event: the restore is a hint, and where the pointer actually came back is not ours to know until we are told — which also disposes of the converse, a resize force-end mid-capture re-deriving a confident cue from coordinates the pointer never occupied. THE COORDINATES DO NOT OUTLAST IT, and the two facts must not be conflated (2026-08-05): the restore writes `pointer_x_`/`pointer_y_` back to its own hint, so past the unlock they name the pixels the cursor is DRAWN on — the drop span is about the KIND, not the position. The split is the fix for a real defect: button events carry no coordinates in the protocol and are delivered at those fields, so while the travel survived the unlock a click made before the next physical motion was routed at the travel's end (a ruler zoom drag then a click flipped the marker view from row 4's W radio; the account is in `zoom-viewport-strip.md`). Kinds can afford to wait for the compositor; button deliveries cannot. THE DEGRADED PATH IS THE SAME CODE: with pointer-constraints or relative-pointer absent nothing locks, nothing is virtual, nothing is dropped, and the loop's next tail is the ordinary end-of-gesture re-resolve that puts the true cue back after a drag spent under the visible Arrow. Nothing in the GUI tests which case it is in.

THE ACCEPTED STALENESS IS ONE POLL WAKEUP WIDE, and that is the whole of it. Every fact the map reads is written from inside a dispatched event — a wayland event, the tick, or a worker completion — and the owner runs at that same iteration's tail, so the classes this paragraph used to enumerate (a load completing under a resting pointer, a menu closing by Esc, a zoom moving the trim endcaps out from under a still pointer, a gesture ending on a key) are simply CORRECT now, and not one of them cost a call site. THE CAPTURED GESTURES ARE THE ONE REMAINING MEMBER, a deliberate deferral rather than a hole: while the platform has no real pointer position it DROPS the kinds the map names, so a modifier released BEFORE the button, or a modal raised by a force-end mid-capture, shows at the compositor's next absolute position — one mouse movement away, and strictly better than a confidently wrong cue derived from coordinates the pointer never occupied.

THE PLAIN WAVEFORM PRESS splits by HALF (the scrub area, architect-designed 2026-07-23). The UPPER half is the placement press: it CLEARS THE SELECTION, drops the playhead at the clicked column (`playhead_frame_at_click_column` through the live-domain clamp; marker-BLIND — no snap magnet), reseeks a live scanner, suppresses follow for the session, and arms the region drag (`arm_region_drag_at`), which dissolves any resting highlight AT MOUSE-DOWN — so the old span vanishes on press whether the gesture stays a click or becomes a fresh drag. Past the shared 8px press-becomes-drag gate the drag extends a REGION: a span of ACTIVE-DOMAIN playable frames (`RegionState`, session-only, never serialized, outside selection AND undo — a SPAN, never markers), painted as an OPAQUE GROUND RECOLOR (`kWaveformRegionCanvas` since row 6, before the plate blit, so the waveform ink composites over it untouched — the Ableton read) with the CURSOR PLAYHEAD PAINTING STRAIGHT ACROSS IT like any other ink (2026-07-30 — the region is not a playhead form and suppresses nothing) — AND THE DRAG CARRIES THE PLAYHEAD (architect 2026-07-30, the live-test refinement: "the playhead move along with the drag ... more intuitive"): each motion event writes the cursor to the clamped moving endpoint, so the playhead visibly rides the pointer through the drag (playback untouched — the press's reseek stands, no per-motion reseek; a sliver release leaves the playhead where the drag put it). Endpoints are unordered (drag order); the painter and `x` normalize. Sub-threshold rested spans dissolve at release (the sliver rule). A SHIFT-exact waveform press IS THAT SAME PLACEMENT PRESS, AT EITHER HEIGHT (architect 2026-08-05, THE FORMER'S RESHAPE — superseding the one-act former whole): the region ANCHORS AT THE CLICKED COLUMN now, not at the playhead and not at the selected marker FURTHEST from the click, so shift does exactly what the plain upper-half press does — deselect, seat the playhead at the column, arm the drag from there — and THE PLAYHEAD LANDS WHERE THE MOUSE RELEASES, carried on the drag's moving endpoint. A MOTIONLESS shift click-release lands the playhead at that column and rests NO region (the arm dissolved at mouse-down and nothing rebuilt one — where the old former rested its one-act span), and a GUTTER press deselects and lands nothing, having no column. ONE RULE, BOTH HALVES, THE WHOLE WAVEFORM: shift's only remaining job is to reach the placement press on the LOWER half, where the plain press is the scrub. DELETED WITH THE OLD ANCHORING: the playhead-anchor read, the furthest-selected-marker argmax, the one-act span tail `form_region_span_to_click` and the non-dissolving arm `arm_region_drag_preserving` that existed only to preserve it (with which the region-drag motion path's threshold-crossing bypass went too — every arm clears at press now, so the crossing finds no active region to be short-circuited against). EVERY REGION FORMER DROPS THE SELECTION ITS SURFACE OWNS (architect-RATIFIED 2026-08-05, promoting the mode arm's day-old coder reading into the ruling; the family rule is stated in full at `RegionState`, app_state.h): the two live entries deselect the STORE selection at press, and the `h` view's entry clears THE MODE'S focus and diff-flag selection in its place, the view owning no store selection at all. THE FORMER THEREFORE DESELECTS AT PRESS in both live entries, so A REGION DRAWN IN AN ORDINARY VIEW RESTS ONLY BESIDE AN EMPTY SELECTION — structurally: every selecting route clears a resting region before a selection can rest. The `h` HISTORY VIEW'S own press (its full-height placement press, that view having no scrub) deselects no STORE selection, and costs that absolute nothing since the same day: THE VIEW'S REGIONS ARE VIEW-LOCAL — cleared at its exit and at every `,`/`.` step and compare switch — so nothing formed in there can rest in the editor at all. The exception that survives is scoped INSIDE the view, where the mode's own flag selection and a span may coexist; the inventory is at `RegionState` (app_state.h) and github-recheck.md owns the gesture. The absolute is what deleted the phase-reset overlay's mirrored region gates as dead code (the band and `Selection::phase_overlay_subject`), and they stay deleted. Esc mid-drag does nothing at all (pointer gestures have no cancel, 2026-07-29: release/button-lost/Ctrl+Q/resize/WM-close end a gesture through the release bodies and what stands stands; undo is the mitigation). Gutter presses arm nothing; region endpoints hold PLAYABLE live-domain frames only (every former clamps through clamp_playhead_to_live_domain). The coupling is ONE-WAY, REGION → TRIM through `x` alone (the 2026-07-23 downward-only rule's surviving edge — selection no longer produces a region at all, and nothing flows upward: the plain-drag region SELECTS NOTHING, and trim never selects, only ever emptying the selection).

THE REGION'S WHOLE LIFECYCLE: formed by the ONE gesture above in its three entries (the plain upper-half press, the shift-exact press at either height, and the `h` view's full-height press); PREVIEWED by either scrub press (the lower-half left or the full-height right — both leave it resting, and neither exists inside the `h` view, which is silent); CONSUMED by `x` (below — which clears it after the write, its job done, and LANDS THE PLAYHEAD ON THE COMMITTED TRIM START, architect 2026-07-30: "on trim X move playhead to trim start"); CLEARED BY BARE ESC (2026-07-30 — the fifth Esc binding, below; the one clear that moves no playhead and changes no selection) AND BY EVERYTHING ELSE that moves the playhead or changes the selection — the route inventory lives at `clear_region_highlight`'s declaration (input_handler.h) : the navigation jumps (Tab family + `c` through the one jump tail, the waveform-lane playhead step, Home/End, the settings playhead key), every marker click (unconditional — the result-size split died with the extent), `enter_text_edit`, both position nudges' commit tails, both marker drops, the three membership-wholesale routes (undo/redo's visual tail, the `p` swap, the propagate paste), the settings engine-commit, EVERY TRIM WRITE as one class since 2026-08-05 (they all park the playhead at the new trim start now, `x` included — the membership is at input_trim.cpp's header), the history mode's own cursor-moving routes AND ITS THREE EDGES (architect 2026-08-05, THE VIEW-LOCAL REGION RULE: the view's exit and every `,`/`.` step and compare switch clear a resting span, a span drawn in there being a reading mark on the checkpoint it was drawn against — the ENTRY deliberately clears nothing), and the in-place structural clears (file load / load-in-place, Ctrl+Tab, the S/T switch — active-domain endpoints die with the domain — and the kick validator's live-domain reclamp). THE DELIBERATE KEEPERS: the scrub presses (the lower-half left press and, since 2026-08-01, the full-height right press — one body, both region-inert), Space (which no longer reads the region at all and touches nothing), and pure viewport moves (paging, zoom steps, pans, and bare `0`'s ZOOM-OUT arm — its full-out arm IS the `c` command since 2026-08-05 and clears through it). SPACE ALWAYS PLAYS FROM THE PLAYHEAD (architect 2026-07-30: "play issues from playhead OR scrub" — the region left-bound launch is deleted); auditions do not move the cursor, and there is NO LOOPING ANYWHERE (same ruling, normalization-and-boundaries.md).

WHAT PAINTS (2026-07-30, the one-form model): the cursor playhead (the white kPlayheadStem head + full-height stem form, since row 5 — the head sits on the MARKER lane's bottom rows with its tip on the waveform boundary, having moved down out of the ruler at the row-5 live test, and the ruler pass paints it there for the tick columns it needs) paints UNCONDITIONALLY at the resting cursor column — whatever the selection, whatever the region; a cursor on a marker simply passes under that marker's flag (the z-order flip). The scanner is unchanged.
