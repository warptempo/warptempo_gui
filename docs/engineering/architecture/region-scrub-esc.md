# The Trim Region Overlay, Scrub, the Esc Bindings, `[` / `Shift+[`

Moved verbatim from CLAUDE.md in the 2026-07-25 slim-down (project feature-complete). This file is now the authoritative home for this detail; CLAUDE.md carries only a digest and a pointer here. Keep it current with landed rulings.
Continues from marker-ui.md.

### The Region IS the Trim (architect 2026-08-18)

THE TRIM HAS TWO PAINTED SURFACES AND ONE STATE. The 9 px trim bar is right for a mouse and unusable with a fingertip, so trim gained a large WAVEFORM OVERLAY — and the overlay is not a second span but THE SAME TWO NUMBERS PAINTED TWICE. `trim_overlay_span(app, audio)` (app_state.h) is the one owner of "where the overlay is": it derives the span from the RESTING TRIM BOUNDS every frame, crossing each through `source_frame_to_active_domain` (identity in source view, the display map's forward hop in target) and then the live-domain clamp, and hands out an ordered lo/hi pair because a resting trim pair is ordered by construction and the display map is monotone. NOTHING IS STORED, so the two surfaces cannot drift, and a tempo change in target view, an undo that restores a map, a pan or a zoom all re-derive the overlay with nothing to invalidate. `RegionState` is therefore ONE FIELD, `bool shown` — the overlay's VISIBILITY and the whole of the state; the free span's `a_frame` / `b_frame` endpoints are deleted with the model that needed them.

THE THREE MOTIONS ON THE OVERLAY ARE TRIM'S OWN DRAGS, NOT A PARALLEL VOCABULARY. A plain press on the overlay asks `region_manipulation_hit` and maps the verdict straight onto a bound through `arm_pending_trim_drag`: `BoundLo` (the trim BEGIN) and `BoundHi` (the END) arm the SINGLE-bound endcap drag on that bound, and `Move` arms the BRIDGE (pair) drag. That is the SAME pending an endcap or bridge press on the 9 px bar arms, so the 8 px crossing, `begin_trim_drag`'s anchor capture, the partner clamp, the first-accepted-change deselect and playback stop, the release column-snap and the shared commit tail are unforked. `PendingTrimDrag` carries one new field, `waveform_click_act`, which marks the SURFACE and nothing else: on the bar a motionless lift is still the consumed nothing of 2026-07-30, while on the overlay it falls to the waveform's ORDINARY CLICK ACT — which is what keeps a full-window overlay dismissable by clicking. A BOUND DRAG KEEPS ITS COLLAPSE-TO-COINCIDENT ESCAPE into `auto_clear_crossed_trim`, the drag's own route back to the whole song and the only one a finger has.

THE SWEEP IS A DIRECT TRIM WRITE. Its two entries are the navigation surface's SHIFT+DRAG former and the TOUCH REGION HOLD, and both write per motion event through `write_trim_from_sweep` (input_trim.cpp), ordered lo/hi, so even mid-gesture the overlay derives from the trim like everything else. IT SHOWS THE OVERLAY AT ITS FIRST ACCEPTED TRIM WRITE (architect 2026-08-19 — a shift+drag or a longpress+drag should leave the overlay shown and light the trim region button; moved off the press 2026-08-21, the paragraph on RAISING below), whether or not the overlay was up: the surface you are drawing on should be visible while you draw it, and because the overlay derives from a trim the sweep is writing per motion event, a shown overlay TRACKS THE STROKE LIVE. `apply_region_drag_motion` (input_pointer.cpp) is the ONE motion path all the entries share, so the raise is written once, and it takes no framing — the stroke is under the pointer, which is on screen by construction. The lamp needs nothing of its own: the button reads `app.region.shown`. AND THE SHOW IS BRACKETED BY THE STROKE since 2026-08-20 (architect — a shift+drag / longpress+drag on the waveform should collapse the region as soon as it is set, bare `[` bringing it back, "this method usually indicates 'this exact region'"), so the raise lasts exactly as long as there is a region the stroke has drawn: `commit_region_sweep` hides it again UNCONDITIONALLY at every end path, and a motionless press-release, having raised nothing, finds the bit already down. The former holds its ANCHOR alone (`RegionDragState::anchor_frame`) — there is no span to keep. The write takes the SETTER's regime whole at its FIRST ACCEPTED bound change (the trim-mutation playback stop and the setter's deselect, once, past the refusals), while the PLAYHEAD PARK and the shared commit tail belong to the RELEASE, a per-frame cursor chase being a cursor fighting the gesture that is moving the bounds. `commit_region_sweep` is the ONE end owner and every end path calls it (clean release, button-lost motion, the touch hook's end, the force-end finalizer); a sweep that wrote nothing — motionless, refused by geometry, or run inside the `h` view — runs no commit tail, and every one of them hides the overlay, that hide being unconditional and outside the `wrote` gate. THE `h` VIEW'S CARVED-OUT PLAYHEAD SWEEP THEREFORE COLLAPSES AN OVERLAY CARRIED INTO THE VIEW, deliberately: that former moves the playhead's position in the music, which is what the hide rule hides for, and it only ever escaped the rule by writing the cursor direct. THE `h` VIEW IS CARVED OUT OF THE TRIM WRITE at the one site the three arms share (`apply_region_drag_motion`) AND OUT OF THE SHOW WITH IT, the raise sitting inside that same accepted-write branch since 2026-08-21 (the owner keeps its own carve-out besides): that view promises the trim window is untouched throughout, so its shift former carries the playhead and, with no span store left and no trim write to derive one from, forms no span and raises no overlay — `region_manipulation_hit` answers None in there besides.

THE SWEEP ENFORCES NO MINIMUM SPAN (architect 2026-08-19, retiring the floor one day after it landed). `kMinTrimSpanFrames` — 65536 whole source frames, about 1.49 s at 44100 Hz, DERIVED as `(kTempoMaxCents/100.0) * kScaleMax * kScaleMax` = 16, the resolved tempo-scale product's ceiling, times `kN` = 4096, so a swept window's TARGET span could never fall below one analysis window — is DELETED, constant and application together. THE RULING: the enforced minimum was distracting in the hand, and the span it enforced was too short to be worth the machinery holding it up, so the degenerate cases get the no-backstop treatment. A sweep now writes exactly the span its two ends describe, walls apart.

ITS TWO DEGENERATE SHAPES ARE ANSWERED WHERE THEY ALREADY WERE, not by anything new. A COINCIDENT PAIR falls into THE ENDCAP DRAG'S ESCAPE, NOW GLOBAL: the release runs the shared commit tail and `auto_clear_crossed_trim`'s `end <= begin` compare resets to the WHOLE SONG — the same compare that has always caught a handle dragged onto its twin, so the sweep POINTS AT that owner rather than copying it, and there is now ONE statement of "a collapsed trim means the whole song" for every former. A TINY BUT NON-EMPTY PAIR simply rests and renders: the RENDER boundary owns honorability, `validate_trim_frames` refusing only a pair whose TARGET span rounds below one output sample, and a refusal there means RENDER UNTRIMMED (one stderr line), never a failed render. A trim smaller than `kN` is therefore authorable and renders trimmed and short. THE ONE ARGUMENT WORTH KEEPING from the floor, for whoever proposes the next span rule: it was a FLAT COUNT of source frames rather than a duration because a duration paints a variable width in target view — two rules wearing one number. Any future span rule must be one number in both views; the ruling above is that there should be no such rule at a gesture at all.

BARE `[` IS THE SHOW / HIDE TOGGLE (`handle_toggle_trim_region`), the icon row's **Show trim region** button and its keyboard twin, with the lamp reading `app.region.shown`. THE BUTTON'S NAME IS THE ARCHITECT'S OWN (2026-08-19) and it is the name the enumerator always carried (`RedesignButton::IconShowRegion`); the tooltip's first line is `"Show trim region ([)"` and the second is unchanged. THE TOOLTIP NAMES A CONSTANT ACT WHILE THE LAMP CARRIES THE STATE — the read-only toggle's settled precedent — so a show/hide toggle needs no second name any more than the padlock does. THE KEY WAS THE SCISSORS' OWN: it had SET THE TRIM FROM A REGION, setting the region IS setting the trim now, so the act it named stopped existing and the emptied key took the act that needed a home — the architect's own pairing, the show region on the freed key with the maximizer kept on its shift form and on the icon's long press. `Ctrl+Shift+X`, which carried the show act from 2026-08-16, IS UNBOUND AGAIN. The toggle writes NO trim, NO selection and NO playhead; its SHOW half also BRINGS THE SPAN INTO VIEW through `bring_span_into_view` (the group undo/redo restore's own scroll-else-frame, one shared owner), and its HIDE half discards nothing. `Shift+[` is unchanged — the MAXIMIZER, the recovery route trim's absence from undo requires — and it has THREE routes: the key, a SHIFT-CLICK on the trim region button and a LONG PRESS on it at `kChromeShiftHoldMs` (the button inherited the deleted scissors' shift admission; `redesign_button_shift_admits` has four members — the two bottom-row SKIPS admit CTRL instead, on their own predicate, since 2026-08-24, kdenlive-redesign.md). Its tooltip is `{"Show trim region ([)", "Press Shift for the whole song."}`.

THE FAMILY MOVED ONTO THE BRACKET ON 2026-08-24 (architect): the pair had lived on `x` / `Shift+X`, and he moved it because `x` "is too easy to hit accidentally instead of `c`, and it can mess up the viewport" — this act's show half FRAMES the trim span through `bring_span_into_view`, so a mis-hit for the neighbouring working-zoom command moved the camera; `[` looks like the begin-trim endcap, which is the mnemonic he chose. ONLY THE SPELLING MOVED — the two acts, the button, its lamp and its shift admission are untouched, and the `Shift+[` form arrives as `GuiKeys::BracketLeft` plus the shift bit because `key_from_keycode` reads level 0 of the keymap (platform_wayland.cpp), never the shifted level. BARE `x` AND `Shift+X` ARE UNBOUND, answering nothing anywhere under strict modifier validation exactly as `Ctrl+Shift+X` has since 2026-08-18.

IT IS A TOGGLE AGAIN AND THE 2026-08-16 HOLE CANNOT COME BACK — worth stating, because that hole is easy to re-invent. That day's design was a toggle whose lamp read A SPAN'S EXISTENCE, and the architect found the flaw before it was built: "what if user draws a region, then moves the viewport away via drag? The region toggle is on, but the region view can't be accessed because the toggle is already on." A lamp derived from EXISTENCE says nothing about VISIBILITY, so a span scrolled offscreen left the button lit with only a clearing press available. His fix then was to remove the state — "it's just a region-shower, and that's it" — and the button was MOMENTARY and stateless from 2026-08-16 to 2026-08-18. Under the derived model the state is back and the hole is not: the lamp reads the overlay's VISIBILITY, and the SHOW HALF ALWAYS FRAMES, so a lit button means the overlay is on screen or one press away from being re-shown there. THE FULL-WINDOW CASE STILL NEEDS NO REFUSAL and that is deliberate (the architect walked it and ruled it self-resolving; do not add a guard): showing there gives an overlay covering everything, whose Move is clamped to a no-op and whose two bounds sit at the screen edges where they are still grabbable, and one upper-half click dismisses it.

### The Hide Rule (architect 2026-08-19, amended 2026-08-20 and 2026-08-21)

THE OVERLAY HIDES WHEN THE PLAYHEAD'S POSITION IN THE MUSIC CHANGES, WHEN A MARKER IS TOUCHED, AND WHEN THE SWEEP ENDS. Nothing else hides it. The architect gave the MENTAL MODEL rather than a list — "moving the playhead or touching an event (midi clip, warp marker etc) does collapse the region. That is how Ableton treats it … the mental model is anything in the timeline (the part of the GUI that can be panned via drag left/right) hides region (except touching trim/region)" — and the rule is stated once, at `clear_region_highlight`'s declaration (input_handler.h). THERE IS NO CALL-SITE INVENTORY ANY MORE: the count went 29 sites (2026-08-17) to 28 (2026-08-18, the whole trim-write class leaving with `park_playhead_at_trim_start`) to FIVE calls in three classes (2026-08-19) to six in four (re-grepped 2026-08-20, the sweep's own end joining) and back to **FIVE calls in three classes** (2026-08-21, bare Esc's hide retired — the Esc section below carries that ruling and its succession), the first class being a pair of OWNERS that every future caller inherits from. THE THIRD CLAUSE IS THE SAME INSTINCT READ FORWARD (architect 2026-08-20): a shift+drag / longpress+drag on the waveform should collapse the region as soon as it is set, bare `[` bringing it back, because "this method usually indicates 'this exact region'". A stroke that has just declared a window has nothing left to look at, so the sweep's raise is bracketed by the stroke rather than left standing.

SPATIALLY, WHICH IS HOW HE ARRIVED AT IT: the TIMELINE is the ruler, the marker lane and the waveform — the part a drag pans left and right. Anything in the timeline hides, except touching the trim itself. The trim bar and the overview strip are lanes 5 and 4, ABOVE the timeline, so the carve-out is a different BAND rather than an exception and the layout needs no special case for it.

THREE THINGS THE RULE SAYS THAT A LIST COULD NOT. **A TRANSLATION IS NOT A MOVEMENT** — "`t` translates the playhead, it doesn't move it technically (it does the closest thing it can do to not moving it by mapping it)", and the Up/Down cent step's target-view re-land is the same act for a marker whose IMAGE moved under a resting cursor. **A RESTORE IS NOT A MOVEMENT** — "A/B are treated as two virtual playheads (when in fact they are not) synced to the same source/target", so a tab switch brings the other cursor forward rather than moving one. **THE CAMERA IS NEVER A MOVEMENT** — the grab-pan, the wheel pan, every zoom, the overview strip's whole vocabulary and the `h` view's edges move the WINDOW, not the cursor. And **THE TRIM'S OWN SURFACES ARE EXEMPT BY CONSTRUCTION**: touching the thing the overlay depicts cannot be a reason to stop depicting it. The sweep's END is the one hide inside that family and it is no leak but an explicit call — the exempt thing is TOUCHING the trim, and a stroke that has ended is no longer touching it.

THE THREE CLASSES, five calls. (1) **The MOVEMENT OWNERS, two owners in three entry points**: `Viewport::move_playhead_to` (the cursor's live chokepoint — the Left/Right step, Home/End, the drops, the nudges, the typed `playhead_cursor=`, the navigation click's placement, the `h` mode's Home/End) and `land_playhead_on_marker` / `land_playhead_on_source_frame` (the whole Tab family, `c`, the marker click, the editor opens, the undo/redo restore, the propagate paste, the Ctrl+N collapse, the `h` mode's Tab cycle and diff-flag clicks). BOTH HIDE UNCONDITIONALLY, never gated on whether the write moved anything — a Home pressed on the frame the cursor already holds still hides, which is what the bottom row's ungreyed skip buttons promise (architect 2026-08-15). (2) **The MARKER TOUCH**, `run_marker_click_act`, which hides on all three arms even where the arm lands nothing (a ctrl-toggle that empties the selection): touching a flag is an act on the timeline whether or not the cursor was already on it, and that is why the rule is not simply "the playhead moved". (3) **THE SWEEP'S COMMIT**, `commit_region_sweep`, which hides UNCONDITIONALLY at every end path it owns and whatever the stroke wrote — the written stroke, the collapsed-onto-anchor one whose trim resets to the whole song, the motionless press-release that raised nothing and finds the bit already down (the raise moved off the press to the first accepted write on 2026-08-21 — the RAISING paragraph below), the touch region hold, the force-end finalizer, and the `h` view's carved-out playhead sweep, which is carved out of the trim WRITE and not out of this. A FOURTH CLASS, **BARE ESC** — the one route that hid and did nothing else — stood beside these from 2026-07-30 to 2026-08-21 and is RETIRED, so the three clauses above are exactly the surviving hides, with no fourth clause and no exception (the Esc section below carries the ruling).

THE TWO NON-CALLERS write the visibility bit themselves: **bare `[`**, the toggle, and **the FILE LOAD**, whose in-place reset pairs the hide with a whole new piece.

THE EXEMPTIONS NEED NO SUPPRESSION, and that is the shape that makes this a rule rather than an inventory in disguise. The trim family writes `app.playhead_cursor_sample` DIRECT (`park_playhead_at_trim_start`, the sweep's per-motion carry), so it passes through neither owner. The A/B restore writes the entering tab's stored cursor direct. The camera writes no playhead at all. Only TWO NAMED non-hiding entry points carry the callers who wanted an owner's write without its hide: `Viewport::reseat_playhead_to` (six callers since 2026-08-25, the map-change re-land family — the caller inventory is at its definition, viewport.cpp) and `reseat_playhead_on_marker` for the S/T flip's re-express of a surviving focus and for the coincidence auto-select's provable no-op. A parameter meaning "skip the rule this time" would be the hand-listed inventory in disguise and would rot the same way.

WHAT THE RULE DELIBERATELY LEAVES STANDING, since a reader will look for each: the grab-pan and the wheel pan; every zoom, bare `0` included; the overview strip's teleport, box pan and bound drags; the A/B tab switch, the S/T flip and the `p` W/P swap; entering, walking and leaving the `h` history view; the scrub, which auditions without moving the cursor; the playback scanner; every trim and region gesture INCLUDING the ones that park the playhead at the new trim start, and the trim bar's three presses, which since 2026-08-20 neither hide nor show — the sweep's END being the one exception, by its own class above; the settings engine commit, whose rebuilt map the overlay simply re-derives against; a settings-only undo/redo entry, for the same reason; the propagate paste's no-created arm; and any chrome that dispatches no playhead command. None of these is an exception written anywhere — each simply never reaches an owner.

THREE MEMBERSHIP CHANGES THE RULE PRODUCED, recorded because each was a call-site judgement the rule overrode. The **Ctrl+N inherit collapse** now HIDES (it lands the playhead on the focus and it touches markers; its old "a value edit is not a turn to other work" argument was exactly the kind of per-site weighing the rule replaced). The **`p` W/P swap** now does NOT (a column switch empties the selection, so it lands nothing and moves no playhead, and the trim belongs to the TAB rather than the column). The **settings engine commit** and the **settings-only 'S' undo entry** now do NOT (their argument was a rebuilt map under a stored span, and the span stopped being stored on 2026-08-18).

THE `h` HISTORY VIEW HIDES AND NEVER RAISES, AND THE ASYMMETRY IS THE RULING RATHER THAN A GAP (architect 2026-08-19, declining a proposal to admit bare `[` in the mode): "`h` has no playback so trim is meaningless — its purpose is to speed up target view segment renders." The window exists to make a TARGET-VIEW SEGMENT RENDER FAST (render-pipeline.md owns that statement), and this view auditions nothing — playback is removed from it whole — while neither render chord acts in it; a window whose job is to shorten both has nothing to do in there, and a surface for working one has nothing to work on. WHAT STANDS IS EXACTLY THAT: the mode consumes bare `[` and greys the Show trim region button, `show_trim_region_overlay` carves the view out once for every caller, and the HIDE's own routes go on working — the view's Home/End, its `c`, its Tab cycle and its diff-flag clicks all write through the movement owners, and since 2026-08-20 the view's OWN SHIFT FORMER puts it away at its end as well (`commit_region_sweep` hides unconditionally — the playhead sweep is carved out of the trim WRITE, not out of the collapse). So an overlay carried in can be put away and stays away for the rest of the visit, AND THAT COSTS NOTHING: hiding discards nothing, the trim is frozen for the duration, and one `[` on the way out draws the identical overlay again. The gesture is not missing — there is nothing in the view for it to do.

`clear_region_highlight` IS A HIDE AND NEVER TOUCHES THE TRIM, which is what lets the rule be a rule rather than a negotiation: hiding DISCARDS NOTHING, because the trim persists and one `[` re-shows an identical overlay, so no site has to weigh what a hide costs.

### Touching the Trim Shows the Trim (architect 2026-08-19)

THE OVERLAY'S VISIBILITY IS NOT A PLAYHEAD, SELECTION OR MUTATION CONCERN. It is a VIEW PREFERENCE — whether the user is looking at the trim — and the trim itself is per-tab document state none of those gestures touches. Hiding discards nothing, because the span is derived; that is what made the broad hide inventory safe, and it is equally why a hide that buys nothing should not happen. The ruling has two halves — and the SAME DAY'S hide rule above is its third, which is what finally collapsed the inventory to a rule.

RAISING: THE SURFACE YOU ARE DRAWING ON SHOULD BE VISIBLE WHILE YOU DRAW IT, and since 2026-08-20 that is the WHOLE of the raise. `show_trim_region_overlay` (input_handler.h) is the ONE raise owner — the hide's counterpart, a no-op when the overlay already stands, and deliberately WITHOUT the framing that bare `[`'s show half runs, its caller being a live pointer gesture already under the pointer or the finger. IT HAS ONE CALL SITE, THE SWEEP'S FIRST ACCEPTED TRIM WRITE (`apply_region_drag_motion`, the one motion path all three arms share, so the shift former and the touch region hold raise it alike — the sweep leaves the overlay shown and the trim region button lit), AND THE RAISE IS STROKE-SCOPED: `commit_region_sweep` collapses it again at every end path, so the big surface is up for exactly as long as a stroke is drawing a region of its own, and bare `[` is the recall.

THE RAISE MOVED OFF THE PRESS ON 2026-08-21, the third step of a three-day succession and the architect's own live finding: "on shift+click on empty waveform, current region is highlighted. on drag, the new region is shown then dissolved. the first part (showing cur region) is incorrect." It was BORN AT THE PRESS on 2026-08-19 (`arm_region_drag_at`, under "touching the trim shows the trim"), NARROWED TO THE SWEEP on 2026-08-20 when the 9 px band's claims left the inventory, and MOVED TO THE FIRST WRITE on 2026-08-21. The defect is structural rather than incidental: the overlay is DERIVED from the RESTING trim, so a raise that happens before the stroke has written anything can only put up the OLD window — the very region the user is about to replace, flashed at him as if it were the one he was drawing. Raising inside the accepted-write branch fixes it at the root, since the write runs anchor→pointer and the first accepted one already draws the NEW span; the `h` former, carved out of the write, therefore raises nothing, and a refused write raises nothing either. A MOTIONLESS SHIFT CLICK NOW SHOWS NOTHING AT ALL — it is a pure playhead placement, with the press's own hide (through the movement owner) standing unanswered, and `commit_region_sweep`'s unconditional hide reaching a bit that is already down.

THE 9 PX BAND'S THREE PRESS CLAIMS LEFT THE INVENTORY ON 2026-08-20, and the succession is worth keeping. They joined it on 2026-08-19 under TOUCHING THE TRIM SHOWS THE TRIM ("that is normal DAW behavior") — the plain endcap/bridge press wherever on the band it landed and the `Ctrl` / `Ctrl+Shift` bound-set presses, the raise being the BAND'S rather than any one arm's. The architect reversed that half the next day, live: "trim lane bar drag/resize should not show region — touching the tiny lane means I'm on the laptop, and the region exists mostly for the touchscreen." A 9 px lane under a POINTER is already its own display of the trim window; the big waveform surface exists for GLASS, where that lane is unusable under a fingertip. So the band's presses now raise nothing at all, their arms, refusals and deferred-click shape untouched, and a laptop press that wants the big surface asks for it with bare `[`. The lamp needs nothing of its own — the button reads `app.region.shown`, so raising the bit lights the button. The waveform overlay's own three presses need no call: they are reachable only through `region_manipulation_hit`, which answers None while it is hidden. The `h` view is carved out once, inside the owner.

PERSISTING: A VIEW CHANGE HAS NO REASON TO PUT IT AWAY. The A/B TAB SWITCH and the S/T AUDIO-VIEW SWITCH each held an IN-PLACE `app.region = RegionState{}` — never calls of `clear_region_highlight`'s — and hold none now: the entering tab has a trim of its own for the overlay to derive from, and the other audio view has the same pair in another domain. The `p` W/P SWAP, which DID call the hide, lost it the same day and for the same reason. THE `h` HISTORY VIEW keeps it across the whole visit, its three own edges (the exit, every `,`/`.` step, every walk-or-reading switch) having stopped hiding that morning. That view raises the overlay by no gesture at all — bare `[` is consumed in the mode and its button greys — so persisting IN is the one way it is ever visible in there, which is the settled answer and not a gap (the ruling and its reason are in the hide-rule section above). THE FILE LOAD KEEPS ITS RESET, the last in-place one: a new piece starts hidden rather than greeting the user with a stranger's window already lit.

WHAT STILL HIDES IN THE `h` VIEW: its PLAYHEAD-MOVING routes, which is the rule and not an exception — its Home/End, its `c` and its Tab cycle write through the movement owners exactly as their live twins do, and its diff-flag clicks land the playhead too. So an overlay carried into the view can be put away by a placement click or a flag click and stays away until the visit ends — the ruled behaviour, a view with nothing to audition and nothing to render having no use for the window the overlay draws. Bare Esc was the one deliberate hide there until 2026-08-21; with that binding retired the view's hides are the rule's alone, and bare `[` — consumed by the mode — is waiting outside it.

DELETED PRODUCER-LESS WITH THE MODEL, not moved: `RegionEditKind`, `RegionEditDragState`, `app.region_edit_drag`, `apply_region_edit_drag_at`, `region_edit_frame_at_column`, `end_region_drag_min_size_check`, the viewport kick-validator's region reclamp, and `run_span_framing_command`'s REGION ARM (the overlay and the trim window are one span, so that ladder collapsed to trim sub-window / whole song). `RegionHit` survives, as the overlay's hit verdict.

THE TWO RETIRED PROTECTIONS, KEPT HERE BECAUSE EACH IS EASY TO RE-INVENT AND EACH WAS RETIRED DELIBERATELY. (1) THE SET-FROM-REGION ACT'S DEGENERATE-RESULT REFUSAL — with the region a free span, an inverse-mapped, wall-clamped pair coming out `end <= begin` (reachable for a narrow span over ~16x bracket-legal compression) made it write NOTHING at all. It is DELETED with the act it guarded. The sweep's own MINIMUM WIDTH FLOOR answered the same problem from the other side for one day — it widened where the refusal refused — and is retired in turn (2026-08-19, above), so neither a refusal nor a floor stands at a gesture: a collapsed sweep clears to the whole song at its release and a merely tiny one renders. (2) THE RELEASE-TIME SLIVER DISSOLVE — a sub-threshold rested span dissolved at the release rather than resting as a two-pixel highlight. It is DELETED too, and stayed deleted through the floor's retirement: a jitter drag past the 8 px gate COMMITS the two-pixel trim it drew, nothing dissolving it and nothing widening it. `Shift+[` is the way back from either.

READ-ONLY-LEGAL AND OUTSIDE UNDO, exactly as the bar's own gestures are: trim is BAND, not authored content (the ruling at `read_only_key_blocked`), so the overlay's drags, the sweep and the `[` toggle all run in a locked tab. TRIM STAYS OUT OF THE UNDO STACKS, which is why `Shift+[` is the recovery route and why the sweep and the drags each commit once, at their release.

THE REGION SIMPLIFICATION (architect-chartered 2026-07-30, the kdenlive redesign's opening arc; landed the same day): the region highlight is TRIM-ONLY FREE SCRATCH — "a temporary selection of what could potentially become a trim region" — and nothing else. The selection no longer paints a span, the cursor playhead is never suppressed, and the whole two-forms model (the region as the playhead's SPAN form, the split half-triangles, exactly-one-form-visible, the cursor/stem suppressions, the never-span-less guarantee and its producer analysis) is RETIRED. RegionProvenance is deleted whole and nothing branches on where a span came from. (THAT MODEL IS ITSELF SUPERSEDED: `RegionState` was `{active, a_frame, b_frame}` from this ruling until 2026-08-18, when the region BECAME the trim and the struct fell to one visibility bit — the section at the head of this file is the current model.) The trim-window highlight coupling is retired with it: no route publishes the trim window as a region any more (`sync_region_to_trim_window` and both wrappers are deleted), the trim window's permanent display being its always-painted bar and endcaps, which are ALWAYS painted now that trim is always-set (normalization-and-boundaries.md owns that model).

TWO GESTURES HAVE BEEN CALLED "THE SCRUB"; ONLY ONE KEEPS THE NAME (architect 2026-07-28, stated here because this file is the docs map's designated scrub owner). (1) THE AUDITION SCRUB — the one-shot waveform CLICK ACT, `scrub_press_at` / `scrub_act_at` / `scrub_launch_at`: stop a live session, else launch one at the clicked column. It owns the word, it is the only thing `src/` names "scrub", and since the Space region launch's retirement (2026-07-30) the scrub is `scrub_launch_at`'s ONLY caller class. THE CURSOR IS RESOLVED ONCE PER RUN-LOOP ITERATION, BY ONE OWNER (architect 2026-08-03, superseding in one stroke the three per-site rulings retold just after it). The platform fires a hook at the TAIL of every iteration of `GuiPlatform::run`'s body — below the display dispatch, the tick and both worker completions, and deliberately NOT on the EINTR `continue`, on either connection-loss `break`, or once `should_exit_` is set — and main.cpp wires it to `refresh_pointer_cursor`, which re-runs the ONE zone map at the remembered pointer position with the platform's live modifiers and does nothing else at all: no hover recompute, no damage, no gesture logic (and nothing at all while the pointer is outside the window, where those coordinates mean nothing). THE BOUNDARY IS NOT THE CURSOR'S ALONE — later the same day it took a SECOND consumer, the open dropdown's item hover and armed item (`recompute_dropdown_hover`, called beside the cursor from the same hook body), because those faces are the same shape of thing: a pointer-derived answer whose INPUTS — there the painter-published item rects — can move with no pointer event under them, which is what this boundary is for. The cursor's own function did not widen (it still does only the cursor); the sibling call is the hook body's, and main.cpp's hook body is the authoritative list of what rides here. EVERY OTHER APPLIER IS DELETED: the push at the top of `on_motion`, the eighteen gesture-end calls in `input_pointer.cpp` (eight release arms, the button-lost arms, the refused begins), the two force-end tails in main.cpp's resize and WM-close callbacks, the `wl_keyboard.modifiers` hook that existed for this and nothing else, and `on_key`'s `CursorRefreshAtReturn` scope guard with the flag that armed it — twenty-three push sites down to one. THE PER-SITE MODEL IS THE HISTORY AND IT IS WORTH KEEPING, because each of its sites was CORRECT and the set of them was still insufficient. It grew in three rounds. MODIFIERS TAKE EFFECT AT ONCE was the first: with modifiers SELECTING between kinds rather than merely suppressing one, a Ctrl pressed under a resting pointer had to move the cue with no motion under it, so the platform fired a hook at the modifier edge itself. A GESTURE THAT ENDS RE-RESOLVES THE CURSOR was the second and closed the largest hole: the map refuses every cue while a gesture is live (the trim exception keeping its own), so the moment a teardown ran, the kind on screen was a promise about a gesture that no longer existed, and an end is neither a motion nor a modifier edge — so every end re-ran the owner strictly AFTER its own teardown, the refused BEGINS included (a begin that refuses is an end by shape, and the trim one was holding the endcap cue). GESTURES ALSO END ON A KEY was the third, missed by a derivation that walked the pointer only: the editor text drag's bare-Esc / Ctrl+Q hatch and the pointer gestures' Ctrl+Q hatch both fall onward through editor and prompt handling, so neither could re-resolve where the gesture ends and `on_key` carried the fact to its return in one scope guard. THE MAP READS ABOUT TEN INDEPENDENT FACT FAMILIES, so a push was owed by EVERY WRITER OF ANY OF THEM — a set nobody can enumerate and keep enumerated, which two consecutive review rounds each demonstrated by finding a class the previous derivation had missed, and for which whole classes had no event to hang a call on at all: a keyboard zoom moving the trim endcaps out from under a resting pointer, the zoom and navigation keys, `[`/`Shift+[`, an undo restoring trim, `o` toggling read-only, a `gui_scale` relayout, every keyboard editor open and close, and the dropdown item click that closes a menu with no motion following. A LOOP BOUNDARY IS AFTER EVERY SETTLE BY DEFINITION, so all of them are answered by the one call and the ordering rule — "run this after the state it reads has settled" — dies with the sites that owed it. THE COST is one map evaluation per poll wakeup and zero protocol traffic while the answer holds, `set_cursor_kind` applying only on a CHANGE. AND THE REFUSAL TO HANG IT ON A TIMER STANDS: the tick is the playback cadence and a second cadence owning this fact is exactly what was being avoided, while a loop boundary is not a cadence at all but the definition of settled; the pre-paint was refused for a different reason — it runs only when a frame is scheduled, so a state change that damages nothing would never reach it.

THE TWO CAPTURED GESTURES END LIKE EVERY OTHER, and the reason the one owner can serve them is that THE GUI IS NOT THE PLACE THAT KNOWS. A capture makes the GUI's pointer position the unbounded VIRTUAL travel — the compositor keeps delivering motion through the lock, so the GUI keeps naming kinds (the live-gesture Arrow, and then whatever the travel ended over) for a place the pointer is not — and the platform DROPS those kinds rather than remembering them (`GuiPlatform::set_cursor_kind`, the fact owned once as `pointer_position_unknown_`). SO THE KIND THE RELEASE HANDS BACK IS STAMPED FROM THE GESTURE'S OWN IDENTITY, not inferred from what the remembered kind happened to be when the press landed: the capture-begin call takes the cue its gesture wears — for the ONE NAV DRAG, which is the only capturing gesture left since the overview lane's strip drag was deleted (2026-08-15), the MODE AT ITS THRESHOLD CROSSING, Pan or Zoom, where its capture begins; a mid-gesture ctrl edge then RE-STAMPS it through `set_capture_restore_kind` (2026-08-14, the live-ctrl model — the gesture's meaning can change mid-life, so the release restores the phase it ENDED in, the stem column and Zoom after a zoom-phase end, the pointer's NOTIONAL position and Pan after a pan-phase one — never the raw traveled x, which carries the off-window debt; and a pan-phase end whose TRAVEL RAN OUT restores at the column the capture opened at instead, the small-drag / runaway-drag split whose statement and reason live in `zoom-viewport-strip.md`) — and the platform writes it straight into the remembered kind on the path that CREATED THE LOCK PROXY (`GuiPlatform::begin_pointer_capture`'s `restore_kind`, the contract there), after which the drop above is what keeps it standing for the whole drag. THE INFERENCE WAS TRIED AND IT DOES NOT HOLD, which is the reason for the stamp: the cursor is re-derived once per RUN-LOOP ITERATION, and one dispatch batch can carry the motion (or the modifier edge) that selects the gesture's zone AND the press that begins the capture, with no re-derivation between them — so "the remembered kind is the last one derived from a real position" named a cue from BEFORE the gesture existed (press Ctrl and click in one batch and the release restored the pre-Ctrl cue). It is the same shape as the live TRIM cue — read the drag's own record, never the pointer's position — and the earlier reading, that the platform's restore already owned this edge so the two arms could abstain, was half true and half false: the Arrow written mid-drag WAS being remembered and it was the Arrow that came back. THE STAMP RIDES THE LOCK REQUEST ONLY (not the degraded returns, not the lock-creation failure), because a cue nobody derived must never reach a cursor that is still visible — and REQUEST is the honest word: the protocol activates a lock asynchronously (the `locked` event announces it, and our listener for it deliberately does nothing), so a created proxy is TAKEN FOR a live lock by ruling (architect 2026-08-03), labwc activating a focus-held lock promptly and a compositor that defers or declines it being an unsupported environment in the adversarial-usage category, with the scope, the rejected pending/active restructure and the accepted degradation stated once at `begin_pointer_capture`'s contract. THE DROP SPAN OUTLASTS THE LOCK deliberately, ending only at the compositor's next absolute event: the restore is a hint, and where the pointer actually came back is not ours to know until we are told — which also disposes of the converse, a resize force-end mid-capture re-deriving a confident cue from coordinates the pointer never occupied. THE COORDINATES DO NOT OUTLAST IT, and the two facts must not be conflated (2026-08-05): the restore writes `pointer_x_`/`pointer_y_` back to its own hint, so past the unlock they name the pixels the cursor is DRAWN on — the drop span is about the KIND, not the position. The split is the fix for a real defect: button events carry no coordinates in the protocol and are delivered at those fields, so while the travel survived the unlock a click made before the next physical motion was routed at the travel's end (a ruler zoom drag then a click flipped the marker view from row 4's W radio; the account is in `zoom-viewport-strip.md`). Kinds can afford to wait for the compositor; button deliveries cannot. THE DEGRADED PATH IS THE SAME CODE: with pointer-constraints or relative-pointer absent nothing locks, nothing is virtual, nothing is dropped, and the loop's next tail is the ordinary end-of-gesture re-resolve that puts the true cue back after a drag spent under the visible Arrow. Nothing in the GUI tests which case it is in.

THE ACCEPTED STALENESS IS ONE POLL WAKEUP WIDE, and that is the whole of it. Every fact the map reads is written from inside a dispatched event — a wayland event, the tick, or a worker completion — and the owner runs at that same iteration's tail, so the classes this paragraph used to enumerate (a load completing under a resting pointer, a menu closing by Esc, a zoom moving the trim endcaps out from under a still pointer, a gesture ending on a key) are simply CORRECT now, and not one of them cost a call site. THE CAPTURED GESTURES ARE THE ONE REMAINING MEMBER, a deliberate deferral rather than a hole: while the platform has no real pointer position it DROPS the kinds the map names, so a modifier released BEFORE the button, or a modal raised by a force-end mid-capture, shows at the compositor's next absolute position — one mouse movement away, and strictly better than a confidently wrong cue derived from coordinates the pointer never occupied.

### The Overlay's Mechanics and Its Succession

THE SUCCESSION FROM SCRATCH SPAN TO TRIM SURFACE, in three steps, kept because each was a ruling and the last one is why the middle two are gone. (1) 2026-08-15, THE REGION BECOMES THE TRIM EDITOR: a standing region stopped being sweep-once-and-consume scratch and became a MANIPULABLE SPAN on the waveform, carrying the overview box's own three motions — drag inside to move it, drag within a bound's grab band to move that bound, drag anywhere else to pan — with the set-from-region act still the commit. The argument was TRIM HAS NO UNDO, so a mis-dragged endcap is unrecoverable except by re-dragging it, and the region gave the one feature that cannot be taken back a PREVIEW STAGE, on a surface hundreds of pixels tall instead of nine. (2) 2026-08-15..16, SHOW REGION closed the loop from the other end: with no region standing, a seed at the current trim window let those same three motions edit the trim itself — on the scissors' own bare key for one day, then on `Ctrl+Shift+X` and its own button, because one key had come to mean two unrelated acts, show and commit. (3) 2026-08-18, THE REGION BECOMES THE TRIM OUTRIGHT: with the overlay DERIVED from the trim there is no second span to preview INTO, no seed to copy and no commit step at all — the three motions write the trim directly through trim's own drags, and the toggle is left holding the one act that survived, show and hide. THE PREVIEW-STAGE ARGUMENT DID NOT SURVIVE AND SHOULD NOT BE RE-INVENTED FROM THIS PARAGRAPH: the recovery route is `Shift+[`, and the sweep enforces no width at all (the floor retired 2026-08-19, the record below), so a mis-drag lands the exact span it drew — or, if it collapsed onto its anchor, the whole song through `auto_clear_crossed_trim`.

NO SECOND STATE MEANS NO CONTRACT TO KEEP, and the distinction the architect drew in 2026-08-15 is worth keeping because it names the design that must NOT come back. The retired TRIM-WINDOW HIGHLIGHT SYNC (2026-07-30) was a CONTINUOUS INVARIANT binding two states together, republishing the trim window as a highlight whenever it moved; the 2026-08-15 seed answered it with a ONE-TIME COPY that then let both move freely. THE DERIVED MODEL IS NEITHER: there is no second state at all, so nothing syncs and nothing is copied — the overlay is the trim's own numbers read fresh on every frame. If a stored span is ever proposed again, this is the paragraph that says what it costs.

THE HIT TEST is `GuiInputHandler::region_manipulation_hit` (input_pointer.cpp), ONE owner with THREE consumers so it cannot drift: the plain waveform press claim (which asks it BEFORE arming the nav drag), the pointer cursor map's overlay arm, and THE TOUCH PAN ZONE. It is meaningful only while `app.region.shown`, and: (a) it is Y-GATED TO THE WAVEFORM RECT ALONE — the RULER and the MARKER LANE answer None even where the span covers their columns, the architect's ruling and the reason a pan and a zoom stay reachable while a full-width overlay stands; (b) THE SPAN IT MEASURES AGAINST IS DERIVED (`trim_overlay_span`) and projected to columns on the DISPLAYED (PLATE) basis through the painter's OWN owner, `GuiPaintHandler::region_columns` on `plate_viewport_basis` — the very call `paint_region_ground` makes — so a grabbed bound is exactly a painted one by construction rather than by two derivations agreeing; (c) BoundLo IS THE TRIM BEGIN and BoundHi the END, a resting pair being ordered by construction, which is what lets the press claim map a verdict straight onto that bound's own endcap drag; (d) the GRAB BAND is `trim_endcap_grab_px()` per side, THE SAME 10 px THE TRIM ENDCAPS AND THE OVERVIEW BOX EDGES TAKE, on purpose — the same gesture on the same shape, the constant's third reader; (e) OVERLAP resolves NEARER-BOUND-WINS with ties to the LO bound, `hit_test_overview_endcap`'s own rule, which keeps both bounds reachable down to a 1 px span; (f) inside the span but outside both bands is Move; (g) an OFFSCREEN bound is simply not grabbable (clamping it to the edge would manufacture a handle where nothing is painted, which is the one thing the shared-owner rule exists to prevent); (h) it answers None while the `h` HISTORY VIEW stands — trim is frozen in there, the toggle's key is off the mode's allowlist and its button greys, and the view's press router forks far above the arm, so a verdict would only let the cue and the touch zone promise a gesture that does not run.

THE DRAGS ARE TRIM'S OWN, NOT BORROWED AND NOT REINVENTED (2026-08-18). `BoundLo` / `BoundHi` arm the SINGLE-bound endcap drag on that bound and `Move` arms the BRIDGE, all three through `arm_pending_trim_drag` — so the threshold crossing, the anchor capture, the partner clamp (INCLUSIVE, with a coincident release resetting to the full window through `auto_clear_crossed_trim`), the bridge's rigid invariant-gap delta, the walls, the release column-snap and the shared commit tail are trim's rules unchanged and unforked. NO CAPTURE, no stem, and no playhead movement until the release, which parks it at the new trim start like every other trim write. THE SEPARATE EDITOR THAT STOOD HERE FROM 2026-08-15 TO 2026-08-18 IS DELETED WHOLE rather than moved — `RegionEditKind`, `RegionEditDragState`, `app.region_edit_drag`, `apply_region_edit_drag_at`, `region_edit_frame_at_column` and `end_region_drag_min_size_check` — and with it its own free-scratch rules: the partner-less bound swap (a bound could be pushed past its partner because nothing committed) and the min-size check are gone, the partner clamp and the crossed reset being the trim's answer to both. SHIFT AND CTRL BYPASS THE OVERLAY ENTIRELY and keep their meanings: ctrl always zooms, and SHIFT ALWAYS SWEEPS A NEW TRIM WINDOW even inside the overlay — the architect's reason being that a shift press is still a press, so "shift always draws" costs nothing and gives a way to re-sweep without hiding first.
