# The Kdenlive Redesign (row-by-row)

Chartered 2026-07-30, implementation began 2026-07-31. The architect works TOP-DOWN, row by row, by dropping screenshot crops into `tmp/screenshots/kdenlive/redesign/` and pointing the planner at them — every state a surface has (rest, hover, clicked, ...) gets its own crop. Do not run ahead of the screenshots. Design parity is "loosely held, with room for tailoring to our needs"; workflow parity stays Ableton. Design and testing happen at 100% `gui_scale`; 1920x1080 remains the only supported resolution.

## The standing rulings (apply to every row)

- THE CSS FLOAT MODEL (architect 2026-07-31, ruled at the row-1 live test): the architect describes rows in CSS box vocabulary — content dimensions, margins, borders, padding — and a stated dimension NEVER includes a border. A flat button with nothing stated fills its whole row; the first row starts at the very top; NO margin/inset/gap exists unless he states one ("I will tell you when there's spacing — don't assume that"). Adjacent buttons get a 2px gap that is an INVISIBLE SEPARATOR, not margin.
- CORRECT CAPITALIZATION (architect 2026-07-31 row-by-row; GENERALIZED 2026-08-01; COMPLETED 2026-08-02): with the rows complete, ALL GUI-facing text uses proper capitalization — and the terminal stderr/stdout pass landed 2026-08-02 (text-only, otherwise byte-identical, the wide frozen touch recorded per file), so the all-lowercase rule is fully retired. The completed rules live in conventions.md.
- THE REDESIGNED ROWS ARE WHEEL-INERT: one band test at wheel_context (returns the inert class above the area tests); future rows join by extending the band.
- BUTTONS DISPATCH THEIR CHORDS THROUGH on_key (a synthesized GuiInputState — the button IS its chord): every gate the keyboard route passes (loading/blank, keyboard-modal, read-only, the arm's own refusals) applies identically and nothing can drift. The exception is a button whose action is not a chord dispatch, and re-derived from `kToolbarChords` against the roster that is the THREE MENU ANCHORS — FILE, EDIT AND SETTINGS, each toggling its own dropdown, which no keyboard chord does (Settings alone from 2026-07-31, joined by Navigation when the second menu landed 2026-08-02 and by File when the Quit BUTTON retired into it 2026-08-13, which made them THREE; Quit was a chord button of this table until that day, having joined it once Ctrl+Q's route was verified byte-equivalent. THE NAVIGATION ANCHOR LEFT WITH ITS MENU 2026-08-15 and the anchors were two again — the roster's first LOSS of a non-chord entry, the split going from 45 + 3 back to 45 + 2 — and EDIT ARRIVED 2026-08-20 with the propagate relocation, the roster's first GAIN of one, taking the split to 47 + 3 while the same act deleted two chord rows). Everything else on rows 1 through 4 and row 8 dispatches — with ONE ruled mid-render divergence on Render, recorded in its row-8-era revision below: while a render is live the button is the CANCEL ACT, not its chord.
- ICONS are in-tree Breeze paths rendered by src/gui/icons.{h,cpp} (an SVG d-string subset interpreter over cairo paths, GUI-only; provenance SVGs committed verbatim under assets/icons/breeze/, read by no code). Icon colors are the SVGs' own, hard-coded per the color ruling.

- THE SCREENSHOTS OVERRIDE ALL PRIOR COLOR WORK (architect 2026-07-30/31; the shrink COMPLETED with rows 5-6): every color a redesign row touched is HARD-CODED from its sampled crop (constexpr, not a key) — and the rows ultimately touched everything, waveform and markers included. The shrink's endpoint arrived 2026-08-02: colors.conf AND ITS MACHINERY RETIRED WHOLE (the loader, the grammar, the ~/.config read all deleted; the last two live keys — the chrome erase and the scanner line — hardcoded at their conf-identical values, zero pixel change; the record is the render.h palette header, the deleted keys' values git history).
- THE SANS FACE IS THE PRODUCT'S ONLY FAMILY (the redesign's default, and since row 7 the only one): cairo toy selector "sans" (fontconfig → Liberation Sans on the target) at 12pt through the points×4/3 convention (16px at 100%), scaled on gui_scale alone (redesign_font_size_px, render.h — the product's ONE text size). The monospace face is DELETED WHOLE (row 7, 2026-08-01) and the font_size key with it.
- ALL REDESIGNED TEXT GOES THROUGH THE SHAPING CHOKEPOINT (`src/gui/text_shape.h`, HarfBuzz over the cairo font's own FT face): the shaped run is the single width truth — measurement, paint, truncation and hit geometry all read the same run. No Pango, no cross-font fallback, no bidi — one wide-coverage face is the model.
- ALL DIMENSIONS RIDE THE gui_scale AXIS (`gui_scale_factor()`, render.h — the product's ONE scale axis since row 7 deleted the font axis; push plumbing at the three application points, recorded at the accessor's declaration). Crop-measured 100% values are the authored constants; every conversion rounds with std::nearbyint. Fills land on integer pixel bounds; axis-aligned 1px strokes, when a row ever has them, take the cairo +0.5 half-pixel alignment so nothing axis-aligned antialiases.
- HIT GEOMETRY IS PUBLISHED BY THE PAINTER where a rect depends on shaped text (the displayed-basis doctrine applied to proportional surfaces): the painter stashes the rect it painted; pointer code reads the stash and never re-shapes.
- THE HOVER RECOMPUTE STAYS LIVE UNDER KEYBOARD MODALS, AND THE DIALOG VEIL DECIDES WHAT IT DERIVES (2026-07-31 as revised 2026-08-12): the modal motion branches still run the recompute — a walk that skipped them would freeze a lit pill, the original staleness bug — but under the MODAL DIALOG its veil term re-derives the roster DARK — EVERYTHING, under a prompt and under an editor dialog alike, because a hover face is a promise of pressability and the veil consumes those presses. The editor half admitted the modal-trap reach-through's own buttons (`modal_veil_admits_button`, derived from the editors' command admission: the Quit/Save pair, then Save alone with the File menu) until that reach-through was retired 2026-08-13, and the two rules collapsed into this one. The 2026-08-12 revision RETIRES the old accepted residual ("a lit pill under a prompt advertises a press the prompt will swallow") — that lit pill is exactly what the veil term now extinguishes. Only the top-strip FlagPayload editor raises no veil (pointer- and wheel-transparent; `modal_dialog_editor_active` names the four veiled editors and is also the wheel swallow). Active pointer GESTURES still freeze hover. No close-edge hook exists or is needed. THE TOOLTIP dwell refusal under modals (2026-08-03) stands unchanged — the tooltip record below carries the rule.
- THE UNFOCUSED-WINDOW GROUND (architect 2026-07-31): the crops' #292c30 is the FOCUSED Breeze header shade; on window deactivation rows 1-2 swap their ground to kRedesignRowGroundUnfocused #202326 (matching the labwc titlebar's own darkening; row 3 already lives on that value and never swaps; nothing else changes color, hard swap, no fade). Plumbing: the xdg_toplevel configure states array yields XDG_TOPLEVEL_STATE_ACTIVATED → GuiPlatform::window_activated(), an EDGE-ONLY activation hook (resizes re-deliver the same set) mirrors into app.window_activated and damages the top strip. THE FACE MIXES FOLLOW THE CURRENT GROUND: the disabled 0.322 and the click 0.30 are RATIOS the crops pin (the sampled #2f5368 is exactly mix(accent, ground, 30%) — a relationship, not a fifth sample; the kRedesignClick literal is replaced by kRedesignClickMix), so focused paint reproduces both crop samples bit-for-bit and unfocused paint derives consistently.

A rule stated as prose in a doc with no line of code that enforces it is not a rule. Cold, an anchor answers a CLICK and nothing else — today's behaviour exactly, and deliberately, because a row that sprang a menu open at a pointer merely crossing it, with no click ever given, is the misfeature this feature stands one step away from. THE RULES, each edge deliberate: ARMED by a click that OPENS a dropdown, and by nothing else — the arming line sits in `toggle_dropdown`'s open path, the ONE route that opens either menu (the click, the hover switch and the armed re-open all arrive through it, and no keyboard chord opens a dropdown at all), so "a menu is open" implies "the row is armed" by construction with no second producer to keep in step. WHILE ARMED WITH A MENU OPEN nothing above changes: the other anchor switches, a non-anchor row-1 button closes and STAYS ARMED, the open menu's own anchor does nothing. WHILE ARMED WITH NO MENU OPEN — the state a Quit hover leaves behind — the pointer ENTERING either anchor's rect opens that menu through `toggle_dropdown`, the same owner the click uses, so the anchor expression, the open edge's damage and the roster clear are one route with nothing restated. DISARMED BY every ordinary dismissal (bare Esc, an item activating, the anchor click that closes its own menu, a press anywhere else that closes the popup, the wheel, Ctrl+Q, the WM close, a resize) through the one close owner, because a dismissal the user MEANT must end the mode, or Esc would put away a menu the next pointer twitch reopens; DISARMED BY ANY POINTER PRESS and ANY KEY PRESS, at the tops of `on_button_press` and `on_key`, which is what covers the dismissals that never reach the close owner at all — a view-bar press or a `;` arriving with no popup open — and which needs NO exception list, because the one press that must keep the mode is the anchor press and it re-arms through `toggle_dropdown`'s open a few lines later, while no keyboard chord opens a dropdown at all; DISARMED BY the pointer leaving row 1's band with no menu up, which is what keeps the mode from outliving the visit (wander down to the waveform and Settings needs a click again); and DISARMED BY the pointer leaving the window OUTSIDE ROW 1'S BAND — and by EVERY pointer-capability loss whatever the position said, the scoping paragraph below — on the platform's existing pointer-leave hook beside the row's other face clears — the band exit's own reason at a coarser edge (a pointer that has left the window has left the visit), and deliberately NOT an argument that no motion can follow: capability loss ends that stream outright, but an ordinary leave may re-enter with a synthesized motion, and what that motion must find is a COLD row that takes a click again. THE LEAVE THROUGH ROW 1 IS THE ONE EXCEPTION (architect 2026-08-08, the Quit-stickiness fix): row 1 ABUTS THE TITLEBAR, so the commonest way to leave the window from that row is to slide one pixel UP off it, which is a step onto the titlebar rather than out of the visit — no more a dismissal than leaving the window with a menu STANDING is, a case that already keeps the popup up, keeps the mode and behaves stickily on return, and the two must not disagree over which pixel the pointer crossed. So the hook asks the mode's own band question OF THE REMEMBERED POSITION (`point_in_menu_row_band`, app_state.h — the exact predicate the motion exit uses, wrapping `top_menu_row_area`, the press claim's own rect, so "on the row" means one thing to the claim, the exit and the leave alike) and, on a yes with the mode armed, SKIPS BOTH the disarm AND the roster hover clear: the mode survives and THE HOVERED ROW-1 BUTTON KEEPS ITS FACE — Quit stays lit under a pointer resting on the titlebar, the visible half of the rule — with the first motion back in re-deriving hover normally and, over an anchor, opening its menu. THE FACE IS KEPT WHOLESALE rather than per button, because with the pointer inside row 1's band no OTHER roster button can be hovered (hover is one position against disjoint rects), and NOTHING MAY CLEAR IT WHILE THE POINTER IS OUT: `recompute_redesign_button_hover` — the tick's per-frame repair and the only writer of those bits that runs with no pointer event — now returns early while `app.pointer_in_window` is false, its own first line, the same guard-at-the-derivation shape `recompute_dropdown_hover` carries, so the tick can neither re-light what the hook cleared nor clear what it keeps. A RESIZE STILL ENDS THE MODE, deliberately (it runs `close_dropdown`, and a relayout is a real dismissal), and any OTHER leave — below the row, or with the mode not armed — behaves exactly as it always did. THE EXCEPTION IS SCOPED TO THE ORDINARY LEAVE (codex 2026-08-08, a correctness term rather than tidiness): the hook body is shared with POINTER-CAPABILITY LOSS, the hard end of the stream, where no leave, no motion and no release will ever arrive again — so the platform now HANDS THE REASON IN (`GuiPointerLeaveReason`, input_core.h: `OrdinaryLeave` vs `CapabilityLoss`, each fire site passing its own and neither inferring it) and the reason is the FIRST TERM of the test. Keeping anything across the hard edge would strand it: a lit row-1 button with no event left to unlight it (the in-window refusal that PROTECTS the kept face would then also be what prevents its repair), an armed mode with no pointer, and a later capability RETURN whose first motion over an anchor would spring a menu open with no click ever given. So the ruled titlebar trip keeps its face and its mode; the hard end keeps nothing and runs the unconditional clear and disarm. THAT SENTENCE IS BIGGER THAN THE HOOK, and its other half is the fire site's own (2026-08-08): the same edge now ends the LOGICAL LEFT HOLD IN BOTH ITS SOURCES, the synthesized bare-`e` one included — `on_seat_capabilities` calls `end_left_hold_source` for the physical AND the synthesized source, in either order, the pair delivering exactly ONE release because each fires only on the logical OR's 1→0 edge. A bare-`e` hold is a POINTER gesture in every respect that matters (it reads pointer coordinates, it is fed by pointer motion, and `primary_button_held` ORs the two sources), so left standing it would survive the destruction of the object its whole existence is expressed against: a capability return would synthesize an enter motion, the new stream's first event would read the OLD hold as still down, and the gesture would resume at the new stream's coordinates — crossing a threshold or committing a mutation with no click in between, and holding the keyboard's drag-modal until the key came up. Ending it is a COMMIT, not a cancel (pointer gestures have no cancel; the ordinary release is delivered at the last known coordinates), and it zeroes the owning keycode, so the `e` keyup that eventually arrives matches nothing and is a plain no-op rather than a second release. The hold's other hard end, keyboard leave / keyboard-capability loss, is unchanged and neither edge relies on the other having fired. THE BRANCH IS ORDERED HOOK, HOLDS, TEARDOWN, one clause each: the HOOK LEADS because its `clear_dropdown_pointer_state` drops the popup's press claim and armed item, so a held menu item does not fire on a hardware edge — a menu item is a CLICK CONVENTION, not a drag commit (architect 2026-08-08), while the no-cancel ruling governs the gestures below; the HOLDS then precede `end_pointer_capture` because unlocking rewrites the tracked pointer position to the cursor RESTORE HINT and the release — with a moved strip drag's final apply riding it — must read the drag's true last position, the GUI's own release body tearing the capture down from inside and leaving the later call a no-op backstop for a lock no hold owned; the wl_pointer goes last, that restore running through it. AND THE BRANCH IS HANDLED TO NOT-BROKEN, NOT TO POLISHED (architect 2026-08-09, SLIMMING the branch and RETIRING the proof walls it had grown): the hook fires ONCE and the second fire — the post-release invariant restore — is DELETED with the reasoning that justified it. THE ACCEPTED GLITCH, recorded rather than re-guarded: a staged-motion flush during the owed release re-enters `on_motion`, whose prologue sets `pointer_in_window` true unconditionally, so after a capability loss that bit may stand and a hover face may be stale until the pointer next ENTERS — self-healing on that entry, and it joins the product's recorded-residuals family. What the edge PROMISES is therefore bounded and no wider than the fire site delivers: both hold sources end, the popup's claim drops, every face clears ONCE. It does not promise a cold stream at a later capability return, and the enum's contract says so. ACCEPTED, and it is the hook's cost rather than the guard's: a kept face is FROZEN while the pointer stays out, so a row-1 button whose enabled or selected bit changed meanwhile keeps the hovered bit it left with until the re-entry motion re-derives the roster.
