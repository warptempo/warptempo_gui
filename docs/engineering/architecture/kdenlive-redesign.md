# The Kdenlive Redesign (row-by-row)

Chartered 2026-07-30, implementation began 2026-07-31. The architect works TOP-DOWN, row by row, by dropping screenshot crops into `tmp/screenshots/kdenlive/redesign/` and pointing the planner at them — every state a surface has (rest, hover, clicked, ...) gets its own crop. Do not run ahead of the screenshots. Design parity is "loosely held, with room for tailoring to our needs"; workflow parity stays Ableton. Design and testing happen at 100% `gui_scale`; 1920x1080 remains the only supported resolution.

## The standing rulings (apply to every row)

- THE CSS FLOAT MODEL (architect 2026-07-31, ruled at the row-1 live test): the architect describes rows in CSS box vocabulary — content dimensions, margins, borders, padding — and a stated dimension NEVER includes a border. A flat button with nothing stated fills its whole row; the first row starts at the very top; NO margin/inset/gap exists unless he states one ("I will tell you when there's spacing — don't assume that"). Adjacent buttons get a 2px gap that is an INVISIBLE SEPARATOR, not margin.
- CORRECT CAPITALIZATION (architect 2026-07-31 row-by-row; GENERALIZED 2026-08-01; COMPLETED 2026-08-02): with the rows complete, ALL GUI-facing text uses proper capitalization — and the terminal stderr/stdout pass landed 2026-08-02 (text-only, otherwise byte-identical, the wide frozen touch recorded per file), so the all-lowercase rule is fully retired. The completed rules live in conventions.md.
- THE REDESIGNED ROWS ARE WHEEL-INERT: one band test at wheel_context (returns the inert class above the area tests); future rows join by extending the band.
- BUTTONS DISPATCH THEIR CHORDS THROUGH on_key (a synthesized GuiInputState — the button IS its chord): every gate the keyboard route passes (loading/blank, keyboard-modal, read-only, the arm's own refusals) applies identically and nothing can drift. The exception is a button whose action is not a chord dispatch, and re-derived from `kToolbarChords` against the roster that is the PAIR SETTINGS AND NAVIGATION, the two menu openers each toggling its own dropdown, which no keyboard chord does (Settings alone from 2026-07-31, joined by Navigation when the second menu landed 2026-08-02; Quit is not one of them — it joined the table once Ctrl+Q's route was verified byte-equivalent). Everything else on rows 1 through 4 and row 8 dispatches — with ONE ruled mid-render divergence on Render, recorded in its row-8-era revision below: while a render is live the button is the CANCEL ACT, not its chord.
- ICONS are in-tree Breeze paths rendered by src/gui/icons.{h,cpp} (an SVG d-string subset interpreter over cairo paths, GUI-only; provenance SVGs committed verbatim under assets/icons/breeze/, read by no code). Icon colors are the SVGs' own, hard-coded per the color ruling.

- THE SCREENSHOTS OVERRIDE ALL PRIOR COLOR WORK (architect 2026-07-30/31; the shrink COMPLETED with rows 5-6): every color a redesign row touched is HARD-CODED from its sampled crop (constexpr, not a key) — and the rows ultimately touched everything, waveform and markers included. The shrink's endpoint arrived 2026-08-02: colors.conf AND ITS MACHINERY RETIRED WHOLE (the loader, the grammar, the ~/.config read all deleted; the last two live keys — the chrome erase and the scanner line — hardcoded at their conf-identical values, zero pixel change; the record is the render.h palette header, the deleted keys' values git history).
- THE SANS FACE IS THE PRODUCT'S ONLY FAMILY (the redesign's default, and since row 7 the only one): cairo toy selector "sans" (fontconfig → Liberation Sans on the target) at 12pt through the points×4/3 convention (16px at 100%), scaled on gui_scale alone (redesign_font_size_px, render.h — the product's ONE text size). The monospace face is DELETED WHOLE (row 7, 2026-08-01) and the font_size key with it.
- ALL REDESIGNED TEXT GOES THROUGH THE SHAPING CHOKEPOINT (`src/gui/text_shape.h`, HarfBuzz over the cairo font's own FT face): the shaped run is the single width truth — measurement, paint, truncation and hit geometry all read the same run. No Pango, no cross-font fallback, no bidi — one wide-coverage face is the model.
- ALL DIMENSIONS RIDE THE gui_scale AXIS (`gui_scale_factor()`, render.h — the product's ONE scale axis since row 7 deleted the font axis; push plumbing at the three application points, recorded at the accessor's declaration). Crop-measured 100% values are the authored constants; every conversion rounds with std::nearbyint. Fills land on integer pixel bounds; axis-aligned 1px strokes, when a row ever has them, take the cairo +0.5 half-pixel alignment so nothing axis-aligned antialiases.
- HIT GEOMETRY IS PUBLISHED BY THE PAINTER where a rect depends on shaped text (the displayed-basis doctrine applied to proportional surfaces): the painter stashes the rect it painted; pointer code reads the stash and never re-shapes.
- THE HOVER RECOMPUTE STAYS LIVE UNDER KEYBOARD MODALS, AND THE DIALOG VEIL DECIDES WHAT IT DERIVES (2026-07-31 as revised 2026-08-12): the modal motion branches still run the recompute — a walk that skipped them would freeze a lit pill, the original staleness bug — but under the MODAL DIALOG its veil term re-derives the roster DARK: everything under a prompt, everything but the veil-admitted Quit/Save under an editor dialog (`modal_veil_admits_button`, derived from the editors' command admission — the modal-trap pair whose presses reach through), because a hover face is a promise of pressability and the veil consumes those presses. The 2026-08-12 revision RETIRES the old accepted residual ("a lit pill under a prompt advertises a press the prompt will swallow") — that lit pill is exactly what the veil term now extinguishes. Only the top-strip FlagPayload editor raises no veil (pointer- and wheel-transparent; `modal_dialog_editor_active` names the four veiled editors and is also the wheel swallow). Active pointer GESTURES still freeze hover. No close-edge hook exists or is needed. THE TOOLTIP dwell refusal under modals (2026-08-03) stands unchanged — the tooltip record below carries the rule.
- THE UNFOCUSED-WINDOW GROUND (architect 2026-07-31): the crops' #292c30 is the FOCUSED Breeze header shade; on window deactivation rows 1-2 swap their ground to kRedesignRowGroundUnfocused #202326 (matching the labwc titlebar's own darkening; row 3 already lives on that value and never swaps; nothing else changes color, hard swap, no fade). Plumbing: the xdg_toplevel configure states array yields XDG_TOPLEVEL_STATE_ACTIVATED → GuiPlatform::window_activated(), an EDGE-ONLY activation hook (resizes re-deliver the same set) mirrors into app.window_activated and damages the top strip. THE FACE MIXES FOLLOW THE CURRENT GROUND: the disabled 0.322 and the click 0.30 are RATIOS the crops pin (the sampled #2f5368 is exactly mix(accent, ground, 30%) — a relationship, not a fifth sample; the kRedesignClick literal is replaced by kRedesignClickMix), so focused paint reproduces both crop samples bit-for-bit and unfocused paint derives consistently.

THE RULES, each edge deliberate: ARMED by a click that OPENS a dropdown, and by nothing else — the arming line sits in `toggle_dropdown`'s open path, the ONE route that opens either menu (the click, the hover switch and the armed re-open all arrive through it, and no keyboard chord opens a dropdown at all), so "a menu is open" implies "the row is armed" by construction with no second producer to keep in step. WHILE ARMED WITH A MENU OPEN nothing above changes: the other anchor switches, a non-anchor row-1 button closes and STAYS ARMED, the open menu's own anchor does nothing. WHILE ARMED WITH NO MENU OPEN — the state a Quit hover leaves behind — the pointer ENTERING either anchor's rect opens that menu through `toggle_dropdown`, the same owner the click uses, so the anchor expression, the open edge's damage and the roster clear are one route with nothing restated. DISARMED BY every ordinary dismissal (bare Esc, an item activating, the anchor click that closes its own menu, a press anywhere else that closes the popup, the wheel, Ctrl+Q, the WM close, a resize) through the one close owner, because a dismissal the user MEANT must end the mode, or Esc would put away a menu the next pointer twitch reopens; DISARMED BY ANY POINTER PRESS and ANY KEY PRESS, at the tops of `on_button_press` and `on_key`, which is what covers the dismissals that never reach the close owner at all — a view-bar press or a `;` arriving with no popup open — and which needs NO exception list, because the one press that must keep the mode is the anchor press and it re-arms through `toggle_dropdown`'s open a few lines later, while no keyboard chord opens a dropdown at all; DISARMED BY the pointer leaving row 1's band with no menu up, which is what keeps the mode from outliving the visit (wander down to the waveform and Settings needs a click again); and DISARMED BY the pointer leaving the window OUTSIDE ROW 1'S BAND — and by EVERY pointer-capability loss whatever the position said, the scoping paragraph below — on the platform's existing pointer-leave hook beside the row's other face clears — the band exit's own reason at a coarser edge (a pointer that has left the window has left the visit), and deliberately NOT an argument that no motion can follow: capability loss ends that stream outright, but an ordinary leave may re-enter with a synthesized motion, and what that motion must find is a COLD row that takes a click again. THE LEAVE THROUGH ROW 1 IS THE ONE EXCEPTION (architect 2026-08-08, the Quit-stickiness fix): row 1 ABUTS THE TITLEBAR, so the commonest way to leave the window from that row is to slide one pixel UP off it, which is a step onto the titlebar rather than out of the visit — no more a dismissal than leaving the window with a menu STANDING is, a case that already keeps the popup up, keeps the mode and behaves stickily on return, and the two must not disagree over which pixel the pointer crossed. So the hook asks the mode's own band question OF THE REMEMBERED POSITION (`point_in_menu_row_band`, app_state.h — the exact predicate the motion exit uses, wrapping `top_menu_row_area`, the press claim's own rect, so "on the row" means one thing to the claim, the exit and the leave alike) and, on a yes with the mode armed, SKIPS BOTH the disarm AND the roster hover clear: the mode survives and THE HOVERED ROW-1 BUTTON KEEPS ITS FACE — Quit stays lit under a pointer resting on the titlebar, the visible half of the rule — with the first motion back in re-deriving hover normally and, over an anchor, opening its menu. THE FACE IS KEPT WHOLESALE rather than per button, because with the pointer inside row 1's band no OTHER roster button can be hovered (hover is one position against disjoint rects), and NOTHING MAY CLEAR IT WHILE THE POINTER IS OUT: `recompute_redesign_button_hover` — the tick's per-frame repair and the only writer of those bits that runs with no pointer event — now returns early while `app.pointer_in_window` is false, its own first line, the same guard-at-the-derivation shape `recompute_dropdown_hover` carries, so the tick can neither re-light what the hook cleared nor clear what it keeps. A RESIZE STILL ENDS THE MODE, deliberately (it runs `close_dropdown`, and a relayout is a real dismissal), and any OTHER leave — below the row, or with the mode not armed — behaves exactly as it always did. THE EXCEPTION IS SCOPED TO THE ORDINARY LEAVE (codex 2026-08-08, a correctness term rather than tidiness): the hook body is shared with POINTER-CAPABILITY LOSS, the hard end of the stream, where no leave, no motion and no release will ever arrive again — so the platform now HANDS THE REASON IN (`GuiPointerLeaveReason`, platform_wayland.h: `OrdinaryLeave` vs `CapabilityLoss`, each fire site passing its own and neither inferring it) and the reason is the FIRST TERM of the test. Keeping anything across the hard edge would strand it: a lit row-1 button with no event left to unlight it (the in-window refusal that PROTECTS the kept face would then also be what prevents its repair), an armed mode with no pointer, and a later capability RETURN whose first motion over an anchor would spring a menu open with no click ever given. So the ruled titlebar trip keeps its face and its mode; the hard end keeps nothing and runs the unconditional clear and disarm. THAT SENTENCE IS BIGGER THAN THE HOOK, and its other half is the fire site's own (2026-08-08): the same edge now ends the LOGICAL LEFT HOLD IN BOTH ITS SOURCES, the synthesized bare-`e` one included — `on_seat_capabilities` calls `end_left_hold_source` for the physical AND the synthesized source, in either order, the pair delivering exactly ONE release because each fires only on the logical OR's 1→0 edge. A bare-`e` hold is a POINTER gesture in every respect that matters (it reads pointer coordinates, it is fed by pointer motion, and `primary_button_held` ORs the two sources), so left standing it would survive the destruction of the object its whole existence is expressed against: a capability return would synthesize an enter motion, the new stream's first event would read the OLD hold as still down, and the gesture would resume at the new stream's coordinates — crossing a threshold or committing a mutation with no click in between, and holding the keyboard's drag-modal until the key came up. Ending it is a COMMIT, not a cancel (pointer gestures have no cancel; the ordinary release is delivered at the last known coordinates), and it zeroes the owning keycode, so the `e` keyup that eventually arrives matches nothing and is a plain no-op rather than a second release. The hold's other hard end, keyboard leave / keyboard-capability loss, is unchanged and neither edge relies on the other having fired. THE BRANCH IS ORDERED HOOK, HOLDS, TEARDOWN, one clause each: the HOOK LEADS because its `clear_dropdown_pointer_state` drops the popup's press claim and armed item, so a held menu item does not fire on a hardware edge — a menu item is a CLICK CONVENTION, not a drag commit (architect 2026-08-08), while the no-cancel ruling governs the gestures below; the HOLDS then precede `end_pointer_capture` because unlocking rewrites the tracked pointer position to the cursor RESTORE HINT and the release — with a moved strip drag's final apply riding it — must read the drag's true last position, the GUI's own release body tearing the capture down from inside and leaving the later call a no-op backstop for a lock no hold owned; the wl_pointer goes last, that restore running through it. AND THE BRANCH IS HANDLED TO NOT-BROKEN, NOT TO POLISHED (architect 2026-08-09, SLIMMING the branch and RETIRING the proof walls it had grown): the hook fires ONCE and the second fire — the post-release invariant restore — is DELETED with the reasoning that justified it. THE ACCEPTED GLITCH, recorded rather than re-guarded: a staged-motion flush during the owed release re-enters `on_motion`, whose prologue sets `pointer_in_window` true unconditionally, so after a capability loss that bit may stand and a hover face may be stale until the pointer next ENTERS — self-healing on that entry, and it joins the product's recorded-residuals family. ACCEPTED, and it is the hook's cost rather than the guard's: a kept face is FROZEN while the pointer stays out, so a row-1 button whose enabled or selected bit changed meanwhile keeps the hovered bit it left with until the re-entry motion re-derives the roster. THE TWO MOTION HALVES ARE SPLIT BECAUSE THEIR GUARD LISTS ARE OPPOSITE, and that is the second correction of the same day's first form, which had both inside one function in the no-gesture tail: THE OPEN HALF (`open_menu_row_anchor_on_hover`) keeps that tail and every guard it confers — its placement IS its guard list, so every condition it must not fire under is a branch that already returned above it (the open dropdown, the prompt, the editor text drag, the two bottom-strip keyboard-modal editors, every live gesture and pending), nothing is restated, no guard is added, and that tail is EXACTLY the reachability the anchors' own press claim has, so the hover opens a menu in precisely the states in which a click opens one — including the one asymmetry the press claim already carries, the pointer-transparent FLAG editor, which gates neither route, and which both routes END, since the discard sits in `toggle_dropdown`'s open path they share rather than at the press claim. THE EXIT HALF (`update_menu_row_exit`) runs at the TOP of `on_motion`, above every branch, because a modal owning the pointer is a reason not to OPEN a menu and no reason whatsoever to forget that the pointer left the row: in the tail it could not run while a prompt, an editor or an editor text drag owned motion, so the pointer could walk off row 1 under one of them and carry an invisible armed mode out of the visit. Running it during a live gesture costs nothing and changes no rule — the gesture's own press already disarmed — and it stays correct with a menu open through the same `disarm_menu_row` gate. The mode paints nothing and damages nothing; what it changes is what the next motion does.

THE ARMED ITEM FOLLOWS THE POINTER (architect 2026-08-03, from his live pass on the same round): press and HOLD a Settings item, drag onto the next one, and the ARM MOVES WITH THE POINTER — the item under the pointer wears the pressed face and every other item is plain. What it replaces is the only state in the product where two items in one menu were distinguished at once: the arm stayed where the button went down (`pressed_item` was written by the press and by nothing else) while `hovered_item` followed the pointer, so the first item kept the full accent fill and the second took the hover tint beside it, against kdenlive's one-item-at-a-time rule. THE HOVER AND THE ARM ARE NOW ONE ANSWER FROM ONE WALK (`recompute_dropdown_hover`, which the motion path already called): one hit test decides which item is distinguished, and the press decides only which FACE it wears — pressed while the button is down, hovered when it is not — so the painter's two faces need no rule of their own and nothing distinguishes an item the pointer has left. Sliding onto the separator, the popup's chrome or off the box sets the arm to -1 WITH THE PRESS STILL LIVE and leaves nothing lit; sliding back on re-arms. SLIDING OUT OF THE WINDOW ENTIRELY IS THE ONE EDGE THAT WALK CANNOT ANSWER — codex 2026-08-03 sharpened WHY: capability loss ends that pointer stream outright, but an ordinary leave only has no event WHILE the pointer stays outside, and may re-enter with a synthesized motion and a later, normally-delivered release — so both faces are dropped at the platform's pointer-leave hook instead (`clear_dropdown_pointer_state`), which clears `press_began_on_item` and leaves any such re-entry and release unowned rather than relying on those events being unable to arrive, the menu itself staying up; without it a flick out fast enough that the last on-surface motion was still over an item left that item lit while the pointer was outside, until a return motion recomputed, since the painter reads `hovered_item` with no in-window term of its own. THE LIVENESS TEST IS THE ONE TRAP HERE and it is spelled at the site: `pressed_item >= 0` was "a press is live" only while the arm could not move, and reading it that way now would strand the drag at the first separator crossing — so the two facts come from their own owners, WHETHER the button is still down from the platform's own tracking (`GuiInputState::primary_button_held`, threaded into the motion call rather than copied into a second bool that could desync) and WHERE it went down from the popup's own `press_began_on_item`, set by the item press, cleared by the release, by the pointer-leave drop and by every close. That second term is the SCOPE LINE and it is deliberately one condition: a press on the ANCHOR button dragged into the popup is the other half of the standard menu gesture, it did not work on this round (the anchor press opened the menu and armed nothing), whether to support it was the architect's, and with the button state alone the walk would have armed items under that drag and fired them on release. HE RULED IT IN THE SAME DAY and the one-condition design paid out exactly as scoped — see THE ANCHOR-PRESS GESTURE below. Damage is the hover move's existing pair — the strip and the popup's WHOLE published box — so a frame in which the arm leaves one item and lands on another repaints both with no per-item arithmetic and no second invalidation.

THE RELEASE FOLLOWS FROM THE ARM, and it is a real behavior change: pressing `GUI scale` and releasing on `Playback speed` ran NOTHING before (the body compared the item under the release point against the armed one and refused when they differed) and now runs `Playback speed`, which is what the press claim's own "press, slide, release on what you meant" already promised. THE COMPARE IS DELETED AS STRUCTURALLY DEAD rather than kept as defence, and the proof is the platform's delivery discipline: the arm is recomputed at every delivered motion while the press is live, and a release is delivered at the coordinates of the last motion delivered — absolute motion goes out synchronously from `on_pointer_motion`, and the one deferred kind (a captured drag's coalesced relative motion) is flushed immediately before any button event with the held bit crossing on the pre-release side, so the flushed motion still reads the button as down. With an item armed the compare could not be true; the release's consumed no-op is the ARMED-NOTHING return above it, which predates the compare and answers a different case (a release with nothing lit — nothing runs, the popup stays open, which is now the escape hatch a press on the wrong row uses). `finish_dropdown_release` therefore stopped REFUSING on a position: it acts on the item under the pointer, which is what the arm names — and it reads that item from the release's own coordinates rather than from the recorded arm (the paragraph below, which completes this one where the arm can lag its own definition).

THE ANCHOR-PRESS GESTURE (architect 2026-08-03, retiring the exclusion the paragraph above scoped): press `Settings` or `Navigation`, HOLD, drag down into the menu that came up, release on an item, and that item fires — the desktop menu bar's one continuous gesture, which kdenlive supports and which this product deliberately left out when the armed item was made to follow the pointer. Nothing in the walk, the release, the faces, the damage or the one-lit-item rule moved. THE CLAIM IS THE PRESS SITE'S, NOT `toggle_dropdown`'s — that owner is shared with two routes carrying no held button at all (the menu-row hover open, the hover switch), and a claim written inside it would be a lie on both — and its VALUE IS THE TOGGLE'S OUTCOME READ BACK (`app.dropdown.open()` after the call) rather than predicted, which is what makes the toggle's OTHER half correct for free: an anchor press that CLOSES its own menu claims nothing, there being no popup left for a gesture to belong to. A SWITCH — this anchor pressed while the other menu stood — is an open like any other and claims like one. THE FIELD'S NAME PREDATES ITS SECOND PRODUCER and is kept: both producers state the same fact, "the button now down was pressed on this popup — on one of its items, or on the anchor that brought it up". WHAT THE RELEASE DOES IN EACH CASE, all of it from the existing body: on an item it fires (close first, then act); on the anchor itself, on a separator, on the popup's chrome or off the box it is a CONSUMED NOTHING WITH THE MENU STILL UP, since none of those is an item and the arm is therefore -1. That last is what makes the classic click — press and release on the anchor without moving — open-and-stay-up BY CONSTRUCTION rather than by exception, and it rests on the product's standing default, now stated at `finish_dropdown_release` where the two gestures meet: DISMISSAL IS A PRESS ACT and a release never dismisses. EVERY CLAIM-KILLER STILL KILLS THIS PRODUCER TOO, each verified at its site: the pointer-leave / capability-loss drop (`clear_dropdown_pointer_state`, which clears the claim above its transition gate, so a re-entry reporting the button still down resurrects no arm); every close, through the struct reset — which is what keeps the recorded MID-HOLD SWITCH rule intact, since dragging from one anchor onto the other switches menus through `toggle_dropdown`, whose close resets claim and arm together, so the new menu arms nothing under that same hold and its release runs nothing; and the release itself, which drops the claim whatever it armed.

THE ITEM FACES JOIN THE SETTLED BOUNDARY (2026-08-03, from codex, closing a miss the anchor-press round left open and the switch behaviour had carried all along): the popup's item rects are PAINTER-PUBLISHED and are zero from the open until `paint_dropdown` publishes them, so a motion delivered before that paint resolves NO item — and the standing reading, "self-correcting on the next motion", was true only while the pointer kept moving. THE CLASS IS THE POINT, not the site: a pointer-derived FACE whose INPUTS can move with no pointer event under them cannot be maintained by pushes at whatever moved them, which is exactly the argument that retired the cursor's twenty-three push sites. `ON_MOTION`'s CALL STAYS, and per DELIVERED MOTION rather than per iteration: the settled hook does NOT run between the events one `wl_display_dispatch_pending` delivers, and a PAINT is one of the things that batch can carry, so a batch delivering a motion onto the next item and then a frame callback would light the previous one if that call moved to the tail. What the walk owns is the FACES; the release owns its own read (the paragraph below). THE LEAVE EDGE IS UNCHANGED because the walk now refuses while `app.pointer_in_window` is false (its own first lines, the same guard and the same reason as `refresh_pointer_cursor`'s): a leave is not a dismissal, the menu stays up, and without that guard the per-iteration call would find the last on-surface position still inside an item rect and re-light, every wakeup, the two faces `clear_dropdown_pointer_state` had just dropped. The ROSTER's button hover deliberately did NOT join: its stale classes are cosmetic and its recompute carries heavier context (the tooltip dwell), and the boundary is here should it ever need the same cure.

THE RELEASE READS THE ARM'S DEFINITION, NOT THE RECORDED ARM (2026-08-03, from codex, the residual the settled boundary left and the completion of the deleted compare's premise): the settled hook fires once per RUN-LOOP ITERATION, and a paint is not a loop boundary — `paint_one_frame` runs from the frame callback, which `wl_display_dispatch_pending` delivers out of the SAME BATCH as the pointer events — so one batch can carry [frame done, item rects published] and then [the release] with nothing resolving the arm in between. Press the anchor, flick onto an item and release inside the frame the menu came up in, and the release still read the pre-paint `-1`: the activation LOST, consumed, the menu standing, and the tail that followed could only light a hover for a claim already false. Narrow — a sub-frame flick-click — but permanent for that click, and it falsified the comments the settled round landed, which claimed the publishing iteration necessarily resolves the arm before a later release. THE FIX IS THE MODEL'S OWN SENTENCE READ TO ITS SECOND CLAUSE: "the release acts on the arm, and the arm IS the item under the pointer" — so `finish_dropdown_release` takes the release's `(x, y)` back (its caller always had them; they were dropped when the compare went) and, while the press CLAIM is live, DERIVES the acted-on item as `dropdown_item_at(x, y)`, the arm's own defining expression evaluated at delivery. THIS IS A DERIVE, NOT THE COMPARE RETURNING, and the difference is the act: the compare REFUSED when the release position disagreed with the arm; the derive ACTS on the freshest truth and CANNOT DISAGREE WITH A CORRECT ARM — whenever the arm is current, `recompute_dropdown_hover` set it from the same expression, at the same coordinates (the release is delivered at the platform's remembered pointer position, the delivery discipline the compare's deletion already rested on), against the same published rects, so the two answers are equal by construction and where they differ the ARM is the stale one. Nothing that fires today stops firing. THE GATE IS THE CLAIM, NOT THE ARM: with `press_began_on_item` false nothing is derived and the recorded arm answers — `-1` in every reachable unclaimed state. The producer invariant that makes this hold is stated once, at `finish_dropdown_release`'s own site (input_pointer.cpp): the item press raises the arm while setting the claim, `recompute_dropdown_hover` may raise it only while the claim and the platform's held bit are both live, and every clearer of the claim drops the arm with it — so a held button this popup never saw still fires nothing, and a claimed press releasing on the separator, the chrome, the anchor or off the box derives `-1` and stays the consumed nothing with the menu up, exactly as before. THE TWO RECOMPUTE CALLERS BOTH STAY and neither has to be LAST any more; what they own is the FACES. ACCEPTED COSMETIC RESIDUE, recorded at the site: in that same sub-frame window the item ACTS without its pressed face ever having been painted, which is strictly better than an activation nobody gets.
