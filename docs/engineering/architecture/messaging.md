# Messaging — the state cell and the notification cards (architect design 2026-08-29)

This file is the ruling for BOTH halves of the messaging redesign: the STATE CELL, row 8's own text in the clock's own run, which carries what is true right now; and the NOTIFICATION CARDS, the top-right stack, which carry what just happened. The design record with his words is `tmp/notifications_design.md`; the mockups he picked from are `tmp/previous/messaging_mockups/` (`cards_AB.png`'s look 1 is the card, `bar_ground_AB.png`'s look A the ground he picked for the bar). The cards landed first; a STATUS BAR on the window's foot landed the next day and the tab row's STATUS CHAIN — the four-tier ladder the state strings and the events had shared since 2026-08-13 — was deleted with it (the tab row paints tabs and nothing else); and that same evening the architect ruled the bar off and its state text folded into row 8, where it is.

## The split: state and events

Every message the product emits is one of two things, and the split is the file's opening rule because it decides the surface. STATE is what is true right now, replaced as it changes, never timed out and never cleared by a key press — the progress line and the `h` walk line (the RESOLVED READOUT was the third and retired whole with the bar). STATE LIVES IN ROW 8'S STATE CELL. An EVENT is something that happened: an act was answered with a sentence, or something the user was not watching finished badly. EVENTS ARE NOTIFICATION CARDS. The architect is "not a big fan of notifications": the cards are minimal, and the inventory below is closed.

The split is also why the state surface has NO TIMEOUTS. The retired transient class was an event on a state surface, and it inherited every fault of that: it was hidden under a progress line, REVEALED STALE when the line above it cleared, wiped by the next key press, and invisible altogether under the `h` view's top-tier line. Nothing in the state cell can go stale, because nothing in it is ever a claim about a past moment.

## The state cell — row 8, in the clock's own run

"Status bars are generally the last row" (architect 2026-08-29) — every DAW and KWave agree, and kdenlive's own is the model: `tmp/previous/screenshots/kdenlive/kden3.png` shows the bar at its foot, `kden2.png` two text cells butted with padding and no separator. Messages were stuffed into the tab row in the first place because the returned Pi rig's 1024x600 had no room; the tablet has room. **A LANE ON THE WINDOW'S FOOT IS WHAT THAT REASONING BUILT, AND IT LASTED ONE DAY.** The architect saw it that evening and ruled it off: he prefers the state text on the toolbar he already reads, and the bar duplicated that panel's own foot. THE STATE TEXT IS ROW 8'S OWN CELL NOW, and the whole tenth lane — `status_bar_area`, `status_bar_content_area`, `kStatusBarContentPx`, the three height accessors, `paint_status_bar` and the window-foot seam `kRedesignBottomLine` — is deleted. The vertical stack is EIGHT lanes again, seven top plus the one bottom row on the foot, and gap 2 got the bar's 33 authored px back the same way it had given them.

THE CELL IS NOT A CELL ANY MORE — **the lower left is ONE MONOSPACE RUN** (architect 2026-08-31): `| sep | kTransportSepToClockPx | 00:00.100 | Updating... |`, the clock, a LITERAL PIPE and the state text in one string, in the clock's own monospace face at the row's size, on the clock's own baseline, from the clock's own origin. The pipe is a character in the string and not a painted separator, so the SEPARATION IS TYPOGRAPHY and there is no gap left to measure. With no state string standing, the clock paints alone exactly as it always has.

WHAT THAT SUPERSEDES, in the two days the state text was a neighbouring cell: it was a SANS run in `kRedesignLabel` on the row's own centred baseline (not the clock's, whose authored 1 px nudge is a monospace-cell correction), laid a SECOND READ of `kTransportSepToClockPx` off the clock — `| sep | gap | 02:42.608 | gap | <state text> |`, one constant and no new number, with no separator between them (kden2's way: two runs and padding alone) — and then, from 2026-08-30, laid INK TO INK, because one authored number read twice still read wrong: the architect saw `Rendering...` standing about two pixels further from the digits than the separator stands from them, the SIDE BEARINGS falling on opposite sides of the same gap (the clock's cell is an ADVANCE sum whose last column is air, and the sans run opens with its own left bearing), measured at 100 % as 9.0 px of ink on the left against 11.625 on the right. The clock's reserved WIDTH and its damage box stay the advance's, untouched.

UNCHANGED BY THE MOVE: the run is CLIPPED, never ellipsised (a cairo rectangle clip, the folder overlay rows' precedent), where the row's right-hand button cluster begins less one `icon_row_pad_x` — the clip is the STATE's and not the clock's, so the digits alone still paint unclipped and the row's crop-at-its-floor rule is untouched. The painter is `paint_bottom_row_buttons_and_clock`'s, laid out with the clock it is now part of; the `h` walk line keeps its own small composer (`history_walk_line`) beside it.

THE CONTENT is exactly the retired bar's LEFT cell: the PROCESS LINE — `queue_progress_text` (`Loading...` / `Rendering...` / `Rendering N of M <label>...` / `Updating...`), or the derived `Synchronizing...` under it (the mirror's paragraph below) — or the `h` view's walk line (`n/N <label> Scale: [-]… [+]…`). THE TWO CAN COEXIST AND THE WALK LINE WINS — the recorded coexistence, kept: nothing STARTS a render from inside the `h` view (both render chords are off its allowlist since 2026-08-08), but a render or a target preview dispatched BEFORE the visit runs on through it — bare `h` refuses only for a checkpoint in flight, cancels no render and clears no progress text, and `GuiTargetRender` gates on the `T` audio view, which the mode does not move. So the walk line stands while the view does, and the progress line is back the moment it closes.

**SYNCHRONIZE IS THE THIRD PROCESS STRING** (architect 2026-09-04): `Synchronizing...`, spelled with the three ASCII periods its two neighbours wear because the cell is the monospace surface and the ASCII-in-grammars rule governs. It is the `Synchronizing to …` dispatch NOTICE come back onto the surface that owns it — a running mirror is state by this file's own division, and the notice was dropped in 2026-08-29 for being that fact on the event surface rather than for being unwanted. THE SYNC YIELDS, and that is the whole precedence ruling: a render, the batch queue or the startup load can run beside the mirror, and the render's progress line is both the more frequent and the more informative state, so it wins for as long as it stands.

**AND THE MIRROR'S LINE IS DERIVED, NOT WRITTEN** (architect 2026-09-04, the same day, "make it correct"): `queue_progress_text` is the render side's and the startup load's alone, and the ONE READER composes the cell's process line out of that string first and `GuiExternalSyncWorker::is_busy` second — `process_line_text` (paint_handler.cpp), which returns the written string when there is one, else `Synchronizing...` while the worker is in flight, else nothing. NO STACK AND NO PRIORITY ENUM still, and now no second writer either: a render's line wins exactly while it stands and the mirror's returns the instant it goes. IT WAS WRITTEN AT THE DISPATCH INTO AN EMPTY CELL AND CLEARED AT THE COMPLETION while the cell still held its own string — the "is the text ours" test `GuiTargetRender`'s hold takes among its own strings, which remains that side's mechanism and is untouched here — and that shape was right for the two orderings the ruling was reasoned on and WRONG FOR THE OTHER TWO. A render started after the mirror overwrote the cell and then cleared it at its own end, leaving the cell BLANK with the copying still running; and a mirror started while a render's line stood never wrote at all, so nothing appeared when that line went. Everything involved is on the GUI thread: it was a precedence gap, not a race. THE TWO SITES IN THE MIRROR'S ACT NOW OWN THE DAMAGE AND NOTHING ELSE — the dispatch invalidates after raising the bit, the completion invalidates once the worker has gone Idle — which is what makes the fallback appear and disappear on time. (The state cell is also what the QUIT'S OWN REFUSAL under a running mirror names: a close is refused outright while the worker is in flight, `A synchronization is already running` on a card, and the line above it is already saying so — the refusal and the line are the SAME BIT asked twice and cannot disagree; `external_sync.h`'s no-cancel paragraph owns that ruling.)

THE RESOLVED READOUT RETIRED WHOLE with the bar (architect 2026-08-29). It was the bar's RIGHT cell for that one day, the bottom strip's before that and the tab row's chain's in between — `~= 1.28*1.0112 (from a.23 @ 00:19.208)`, the selected pass or reference marker's resolved tempo. THE ROW YIELDS WHOLE TO A MODAL, so the state text is HIDDEN while a prompt, a dialog editor, the render player or the picker stands, and the live string is back the instant it closes — architect-accepted at the fold, and the one thing the separate lane bought that the cell does not. ## The two classes

- NORMAL — a small card that leaves on its own `kNotificationMs` (5000 ms, `gui_input.h`, beside `kHoldBeatMs` and deliberately NOT derived from it: a card's life is neither a hold nor a second tap, and Plasma's own default is what it takes; a duration, so it rides no scale) after ITS PUSH — every card is on screen from that instant since the queue retired (2026-08-30), so there is no later surfacing for a clock to wait for — or at its X, or at a BUMP. The pointer resting on it PAUSES the clock (the remaining life is banked at hover-enter and re-armed at hover-leave; the pointer-left hook ends a hover too; glass has no hover and no finger ever holds a clock). A card whose deadline has already passed when the pointer arrives is NOT banked — the deadlines are polled, so a pointer can land in the gap between the deadline and the tick, and hover pauses a clock rather than resurrecting one: that card leaves on the next poll as an unhovered one would.
- CRITICAL — never TIMED out and never BUMPED. No clock, and nothing that comes after takes it down: a later success raises nothing and clears nothing, AND NO BUMP EVER REMOVES ONE (architect 2026-08-30: "critical cards keep standing"). What does take one down is a DELIBERATE dismissal, and two roads reach it: its X on the pointer, and BARE ESC with the WHOLE STACK — the key reads no class (2026-08-31) and takes every card (2026-09-01), which makes it the one act that reaches a critical card without a pointer; the Esc rulings are below. The three checkpoint failures (`Checkpoint failed: nothing was committed` / `Checkpoint failed: files written but not committed` / `Checkpoint committed but not pushed`) are its only producers — a fourth, `Checkpoint could not be confirmed`, stood from 2026-08-29 until 2026-09-06, when the act started deciding on git's exit status and had no unanswerable question left to report (github-recheck.md).

No remaining-time bar, no actions, no title/body split, no sound. The glyph is what tells the classes apart at a glance: `dialog-information` on a normal card, `dialog-error` on a critical one.

## The look and the placement

The stack sits top-right, `kPanelPadPx`-class air (the folder overlay's `pad_px`) below ROW 1 (the menu row, whose right-floating view bar carries the S+W / T+P / T+W radios) AND THE SAME NUMBER in from the window's right edge — the two margins are ONE constant read twice (architect 2026-08-29; the right margin was `icon_row_pad_x()` for the cards' first day and read wider than the air above them; a card's INTERNAL pad is a different concept and a different number, the one derived pad below) — newest on top, growing DOWN over whatever lies there (the tab row's right stretch, the icon row's empty right, the thin lanes, the waveform). THE STACK IS UNCAPPED AND EVERY CARD IN IT IS VISIBLE (architect 2026-08-30, retiring the three-card cap and the queue with it: "cards bump each other off the screen — newest on top, the oldest leaving; critical cards keep standing"). What limits it is THE ROOM, counted in one-line cards — `notification_capacity(a)` = `floor((room + gap) / (card + gap))`, at least 1, a pure function of the window and the scale like the room itself: 20 at a 1080 px window and 100 %, 11 on the tablet's 1440 px panel at 225 %, far more than the architect will ever stack. A push past that count BUMPS the oldest NORMAL card and nothing else — never a critical one, and never the card just pushed, which is the answer to the act the user has this instant performed (reachable, not theoretical: the capacity is 4 at 1080 px and 350 %, where a run of checkpoint failures fills it). The capacity counts ONE-LINE cards because no pure function of the window can know a line count, so two overflows escape it — a burst of WRAPPED cards, and CRITICALS the bump will not touch — and both take the same answer, THE PAINTER'S CLIP AT THE ROOM'S FOOT: the overflow paints on down and is cut there rather than over the bottom row, and the painter publishes its rects clipped too, so nothing under that foot is claimed by a card either. One rule, not a second cap.

Every constant a card reads, none of its own but TWO (`notifications.h` — `kNotificationMinWidthPx` and `kNotificationMaxLines`; `kNotificationVisibleMax` was a third until the stack went uncapped 2026-08-30 and the room's own capacity replaced it):
- height = `icon_row_content_h_px()` — the icon row's 46: the 32 px button box plus its 7 px margins — THAT IS THE ONE-LINE HEIGHT, and a card of `n` lines is it plus `(n − 1)` face line-heights (`cairo_scaled_font_extents(font).height`, `nearbyint`ed, read once per paint);
- the gap between cards = `kIconBtnGapPx` (2), which the room's capacity count reads too;
- THE PAD = `notification_pad_px()` (7 at 100%), ONE NUMBER FOR ALL SIX DISTANCES (architect 2026-08-30, his first symmetry pass — he saw the X's side margin exceed its top/bottom margin): left edge → glyph box, glyph box → text, text → X box, X box → right edge, top → boxes, boxes → bottom. Nothing is authored for it: it IS `(notification_card_h_px() − kIconBtnPx) / 2`, the icon row's own centering that the card's height already comes from, so a retune of either moves all six together. Where the difference is odd the integer floor puts the extra pixel BELOW the boxes, the icon row's own arithmetic and not a second rule. The glyph and the X each sit in a `kIconBtnPx` (32) box at that pad, the `kIconGlyphPx` (22) glyph at the box's own `(32-22)/2` inset. `icon_row_pad_x()` and the folder overlay's icon-to-name gap `kRowIconGapPx` — the card's two foreign constants until that day — LEFT the painter with it; the overlay's own rows keep their gap, that being their surface;
- width = the content's (four pads, two boxes, the shaped text) clamped to [`kNotificationMinWidthPx` 272 authored px, `kNotificationMaxWidthPx` 640 authored px]. THE FLOOR IS THE UNDO CARD'S OWN WIDTH (architect 2026-09-01): he steps undo and redo deliberately as a sanity check, and the two walls' cards — `There is nothing to undo` and `There is nothing to redo`, one letter apart — came out a few pixels apart and the difference read as a flicker between two presses of the same shape. THE MEASUREMENT, at 100 % in the card's own face (the one sans at `redesign_font_size_px`, 16 px): the undo sentence shapes to 172.73 px and the redo one to 169.16, and the card adds its chrome — four pads and two button boxes, 4 × 7 + 2 × 32 = 92 — for 265 px and 262 px. 272 IS THE NEXT MULTIPLE OF 8 ABOVE THE WIDER, a clean authored round-up with 7 px of air over the sentence that set it, so a face retune of a pixel or two cannot silently put the pair back at two widths; and because the floor is AUTHORED px scaled like the sentence, the relation holds at every `gui_scale`. Every shorter sentence joins them at the one width, which is the point. IT DOES NOT MEET THE CEILING and changes nothing about the clamp: `notification_card_max_w_px` already floors its window safety here, so `floor ≤ ceiling` holds as before and a window narrower than the floor still keeps the floor and lets the card overhang. THE CEILING IS THE LAPTOP'S OWN, AUTHORED AND SCALED (architect 2026-08-31): 640 is what the retired `window / 3` gave on the 1920 px laptop, so the width the laptop already had is the canonical one and `scaled_px` carries it to every device — the fraction was DEVICE pixels and starved the tablet, whose 1440 px panel at 225 % gave a 480 px card for text shaped half again as large. A CARD MAY NOW COVER BUTTONS on the tablet, accepted in his own words ("it's okay if it covers up some buttons"): a card is a sentence to read and it leaves on its own. The WINDOW survives only as a SAFETY under the ceiling — the window less the stack's two `kPanelPadPx` margins — and never below the floor, that number being the stack's ROOM as well, which the painter clips to and the damage owner erases; a window narrower than the floor keeps the floor and lets the card overhang, the contrived window this design declines to cater for;
- ground `kModalFieldGround` #141618 (the player's and the picker's band ground — "that colour stands out"; the dropdown's ground was rejected as "what's already in use throughout") under a 1 px `kRedesignTabLine` #4c4e51 outline (the popup's own border), through the one popup box painter `paint_popup_chrome` at the popup's radius;
- ink `kRedesignLabel`, the one sans at the redesign's size, the FIRST line's baseline the one-line card's through `redesign_baseline` over that first band;
- THE WRAP (architect 2026-08-30): a sentence that does not fit the text's room GROWS DOWNWARD UNDER THE TWO ICONS to at most `kNotificationMaxLines` (3, an authored count — his "a few"). The glyph box and the X box keep the one-line card's placement and NOTHING REFLOWS beside them; each further line sits one line-height lower; the air under the last line is the first line's own by construction, so the one pad still rules every edge. The WIDTH rule is unchanged — the whole sentence's shaped width inside the same clamp — so a card that fitted one line still does. Breaking is GREEDY AT ASCII SPACES ON MEASURED ADVANCES, each candidate line re-shaped as one run through the shaping chokepoint so the run that is measured is the run that is painted (`notification_text_lines`, the one wrap owner, beside the painter); a break's space and any leading spaces are consumed and every other byte is carried verbatim — the lines are substrings, never a rejoin. A SINGLE WORD WIDER THAN THE ROOM stands alone and clips at the run's right edge, and THE LAST PERMITTED LINE TAKES THE WHOLE REMAINDER and clips the same way — no word is ever silently dropped, only cut where the eye can see the cut. No ellipsis (the folder overlay rows' precedent) and no memo: shaping runs at paint, as the overlay's rows already do;
- the X wears the icon button's hover face — the 1 px `kRedesignAccent` outline through `redesign_face_box`, the icon row's own — while the pointer is in its box; the card's body wears no hover face.

The three glyphs are fresh verbatim transcriptions into the roster (`icons.h`, which `kIconCount` counts — 54 at the cards' landing, 55 since the Copy resolved value button's edit-copy later the same day; that constant is the one authority): `status/22/dialog-information` and `status/22/dialog-error` (symlinks in Breeze Dark onto `data-information` / `data-error`, copied resolved into `assets/icons/breeze/`) and `actions/22/window-close`. ## The hit

THE X, AND ONLY THE X, DISMISSES A CARD ON THE POINTER (architect 2026-08-29, superseding the design's tap-anywhere) — AND SINCE 2026-08-31 THE X HAS A KEYBOARD TWIN, BARE ESC (architect). The succession is exact and the 2026-08-29 ruling is untouched: it says where a PRESS may land on a card, and the key lands on no card at all. Esc CLEARS THE WHOLE STACK, CRITICALS INCLUDED (architect 2026-09-01: "Esc should clear all notifications"). It reads no class: A CRITICAL CARD IS DISMISSED LIKE ANY OTHER, Esc being the X's twin and the X taking any class (the bump is the act that skips criticals, and it is not this one), so the key is the one act that reaches a critical card without a pointer and therefore clears exactly what the X's CLOCK never touches. That asymmetry is deliberate and RECORDED SO A REVERSE IS EASY: a deliberate press is a decision where a timeout would be an accident, and if the criticals should ever survive it, `GuiNotifications::dismiss_all` is the one line to change. THE TWO DIFFER IN RANK, deliberately: the X's press claim sits ABOVE EVERY VEIL because a card must be dismissable under any modal, while Esc sits UNDER all of them — it is the LAST of bare Esc's NINE places (region-scrub-esc.md is the Esc owner; the src enumeration is at `on_key`, the arm at `handle_plain_bare_keys`), so the key reaches the stack only when no prompt, editor, player, picker or dropdown is standing and no render is in flight. With an empty stack the arm is the ruled silence it has always been (the list below).

THE ARM'S SUCCESSION IS TWO RULINGS A DAY APART. It was born 2026-08-31 taking THE STACK'S OLDEST CARD alone — the bottom, not the top, in his own reasoning: "clearing the newest would let the oldest stick around preferentially", so the key emptied the stack from the back the way the clock does, one press per card. A BULK FORM, CTRL+ESC, was bound beside it on the morning of 2026-09-01 at the HEAD of `on_key`, above every gate, editor, player, picker, dropdown, drag and loading state, so that a whole stack could be cleared from under any modal — and IT RETIRED THE SAME EVENING, its act moving onto the bare key on his ruling above: one act wants one chord, and two chords for one act is a second road. CTRL+ESC IS UNBOUND-SILENT NOW like every other modified Escape (`chord_is_bound`'s Escape arm is bare-exact, pinned by a static_assert that reads the other way round from the one the chord had), the place contract and its rank are untouched, and what the retirement cost is the reach: bare Esc yields to every modal, so a card raised under a standing prompt, editor, player or picker waits for that surface to close (a cost the retirement carries, surfaced to him at the landing and not yet ruled on; the X still dismisses under any modal on the pointer). The pointer's own rule, unchanged: a press anywhere on a visible card is consumed whole — arms nothing, moves nothing, lands no playhead, opens no drag, reaches nothing underneath — and the LEFT press on the card's X box dismisses it; any other button over a card is consumed in the veil's own manner. ONE RULE ON BOTH BACKENDS: the router cannot fork on tap versus click (no origin bit rides a press — `GuiInputState` carries modifiers alone), so the rule must be one, and the X's box is the icon row's 32 px button box, already the product's glass target, which is why no finger-fattened body target exists.

The claim sits at `on_button_press`'s head, after the tooltip hide and the menu row's disarm (a press anywhere still does both) and ABOVE EVERY VEIL — the on-screen keyboard's claim, the prompt's, the folder overlay's, the player's, the picker's, the AV sync panel's, the dialog editors' — because a card must be dismissable under any modal. It is not an exception to the veil: a card is not a reach into the veiled surface, it is the message about the act the veil stands over (the record at the retired reach-through's site, `input_pointer.cpp`). The press is the act (content acts the moment its identity is certain; a card press can mean one thing), so the release owes nothing and no arm is left standing.

The geometry a card is HIT by is the geometry it was PAINTED with: the one hit owner reads the publication and then asks the live stack whether that id is still there — which since 2026-08-30 is the whole test, "in the stack", the queue clause having had the queue as its only producer (published geometry may only SELECT; live state decides — `ModalDialogGeometry`'s doctrine) — so a card that expired or was BUMPED between the paint and the press is hit by nothing: it is never dismissed twice, and one already gone is never dismissed at all (`dismiss` asks the same test of its argument), and the press is claimed by nothing and falls through to the surface underneath, whose pixels are what the next paint shows there anyway. A card under the open dropdown's box yields to it (the one pointer-owning surface that paints above the cards), in the one owner `notification_card_at`.

- CURSOR: the zone map's arm 0 — the Arrow over a card, whatever lies under it (the card is opaque to the pointer).
- MOTION: the hover is re-derived at the head of the motion handler, above every branch, like the menu row's exit; the X's face is the only thing it paints. THE OPACITY IS CARRIED BY THE TWO HOVER WALKS A CARD CAN STAND OVER, not by an early return there: the roster's (`recompute_redesign_button_hover`) and the folder overlay band's (`update_folder_overlay_hover`) each ask the one hit owner and answer "nothing under the pointer", so no button beneath a card wears a hover face or starts a tooltip dwell. It has to live at the roster's walk rather than at the motion, because that walk's SECOND caller is the tick and a pointer RESTING on a card is exactly the case; an early return at the motion would also freeze a live gesture whose pointer merely crossed a card.

## The clock and the damage

The clock rides the run loop's existing deadline tick, polled: `GuiNotifications::fire_if_due` at the HEAD of `main.cpp`'s `on_tick`, above every early return that body has — the startup load's, the render player's fork, the loading/blank guard and the playing-only guard. A card is a message about something that has ALREADY happened, so nothing the tick does below it can be a precondition for retiring one, and every one of those returns is a mode a card can stand over: an idle window must still retire an expired card, and the render player's own `No audio device to play the wav` card must not stand for the whole player session. The tick's software deadlines are FOUR — key repeat and the touch disambiguation window (`GuiInputCore::tick`), the audition's rests, the cards — and none is scheduled; all read `monotonic_ms()`. The same call re-answers the hover from the remembered pointer, so a card that slid up under a resting pointer — which is what a bump or an expiry above it does — starts pausing without a motion.

Damage has one owner, `Viewport::invalidate_notification_stack`: every stack change (a push, a dismissal — the X's one card, or bare Esc emptying the stack whole — an expiry) damages the stack's ROOM — `notification_stack_bound`, the maximum card width from `kPanelPadPx` under row 1 down to that same `kPanelPadPx` above the bottom row's lane — which erases what stood and admits what comes without shaping a glyph off the paint clock; the hover face takes `invalidate_rect` on the X box alone. IT IS THE ROOM AND NO LONGER A TIGHT BOUND since the wrap landed (2026-08-30): it was "three cards of one line each", which a wrapped card outgrows, and no pure function of the window can know a card's line count — that needs a shaped run, and shaping needs the paint's own font. So the damage owner takes the whole space the stack may grow into and THE PAINTER CLIPS TO IT, which is what keeps a contrived stack off the bottom row; a window with no room between row 1 and that lane answers a zero height and paints no card at all.

## The chokepoint, the duplicates, the bump

`GuiNotifications::notify` is the ONE writer of a card; every producer below calls it and nothing else pushes.

DELIBERATE PRESSES STACK THEIR DUPLICATES (architect 2026-09-01, retiring the unconditional dedup of 2026-08-30): each PHYSICAL press pushes its OWN card, so a wall hit three times shows three cards. That count IS the confirmation — "I am very deliberate with my presses" — and it is the one thing the roster's grey cannot give him, a key's refusal being what he is reading at a wall. THE ONE CARVE-OUT IS A HELD INPUT'S SYNTHESIZED REPEATS, which coalesce exactly as everything used to: a text identical to a card already in the stack in the same class removes that card and re-pushes it AT THE TOP with a fresh clock, in both classes alike. The rule is *multiples are for distinct presses, not for a 30 Hz flood* — a burst at the compositor's cadence is one gesture, not thirty presses, and letting it stack would empty the room of every other card in half a second.

THE SEAM IS THE KEY EVENT'S OWN BIT, not a parameter and not a global: `GuiInputState::synthesized_repeat` — whose TWO producers, the held KEY (`GuiInputCore::maybe_fire_repeat`) and the held BUTTON (`tick_chrome_press_repeat`), both dispatch THROUGH `on_key` — is read once at that body's head and parked in `AppState::Notifications::held_repeat_dispatch` by an RAII guard that restores it at every one of that body's returns, the early gate returns included. `notify` is its one reader. It is dispatch-scoped for a reason: the raise sites are everywhere on that road (the gates, the ops' returned `GuiOpRefusal` sentences, the editors' red flashes), so threading a parameter down every chain would be a dozen signatures for one fact that arrives once, at the top, with the event — while a bit written where the event arrives cannot drift from it, and cannot be read stale by a worker's verdict, a pointer gesture or a paint, all of which see false and stack like the deliberate presses they answer.

WHY A REPEAT MOVES ITS CARD rather than re-arming it where it stands (the 2026-08-30 reasoning, unchanged): "in the stack" is not "on screen". An overflowing stack paints past the room's foot and the painter clips it there, so a card can be live and wholly invisible — and a re-arm in place would answer the act the user is this moment performing with nothing visible at all. The top is the one place the answer is certain to be seen, and it is also what a repeat means to the reader: the top says the last time it happened.

THE BUMP IS UNCHANGED AND NOW LOAD-BEARING: repeated presses of one refusal fill the room with copies of one sentence instead of refreshing a single card, so the walk runs on ordinary use and not only in a burst of different events. It needs nothing new for that — the victims are still the oldest NORMAL cards, never a critical one and never the card just pushed, and the count is still the room's — and what the user sees at a wall is the stack topping out at the capacity with the newest press on top, which is the picture the count is for.

THE BUMP replaced the queue on 2026-08-30. A push inserts at the top and starts that card's clock; then, while the stack is past `notification_capacity`, the OLDEST NORMAL card is removed — the walk runs from the back, SKIPS every critical card, and STOPS ABOVE THE CARD JUST PUSHED, so the two things that never leave at a push are a critical card (by the ruling) and the sentence answering the act the user has this moment performed (by that sentence's whole reason for existing). When neither leaves a victim the loop simply stops and the overflow paints past the room's foot, where the painter's clip cuts it. A bumped card that was hovered drops the hover. NOTHING WAITS UNSEEN any more: `expiry_ms` 0 means "no clock" and belongs to critical cards alone, `notification_visible` is "in the stack", and `start_visible_clocks` / `is_visible_index` are deleted with the queue that produced them.

The stack is a member of the per-project `AppState`, so a reopen starts with an empty stack by construction — the chip's own per-project scope, and no clear site exists because none is needed.

## The basename rule

A message that carries a filesystem path names the FILE — its basename, or the batch cell's `N_tag/NN` id — never a full path: the subject is the highlighted row on screen, and the clip is the backstop. THE MECHANISM IS STRUCTURED FAILURE DATA (architect 2026-09-02, the four-tier review's R-11 — "option 1, applied universally"): every failure that reaches a card composes TWO CLAUSES at its ONE failure point, a `GuiFailure` (`failure.h`) whose `diagnostic` is the stderr line's clause — full paths, every word, the instruction a terminal reader can act on — and whose `display` is the card's, one clause with the path named the way its family names it and the system's words after it. ## The words on a card

The product's text rules are stated ONCE, at `paint_handler.cpp`'s menu-row block (the one statement every other site points at), and since the evening of 2026-09-03 they are TWO CLASSES rather than one rule with an exception: A CONTROL'S NAME IS TITLE CASE — a dropdown item, a modal row's word button, a panel title, and a tooltip that NAMES a glyph button's act — while A DESCRIPTION IS SENTENCE CASE — a label, a field prefix, a tooltip that answers with a STATE or a REASON, the second modifier line, a card, a prompt and the row-8 state cell. (The rule is kdenlive's, as the whole convention is: the Title Case hover on an icon button is the QAction's name echoed by Qt, and every tooltip kdenlive writes by hand is a sentence. The four dropdown tables — five until the Iterations menu's 2026-09-04 deletion — Title Case since that morning as the product's ONE declared exception, are an INSTANCE of the name class now and no exception at all.) SO ONE ACT SPLITS BY SURFACE: a card naming an act stays the card's sentence case (`BPM iterations work in source view`) while the tooltip naming it is that control's name (`Render Grid Iterations (Ctrl+Alt+R)`). Acronyms keep their caps; data — marker labels, titles, filenames, settings keys — is verbatim; key names are Qt's, with a bare letter uppercase and the cap for shifted punctuation (`spell_chord`'s head, `gui_input.h`, owns the spelling). What the card surface adds is the composition rules:

- ONE CLAUSE PER CARD, and the card is a statement, not a paragraph: no sentence-final period, no second sentence, no instruction clause after the reason, and no number said twice. A prompt, by contrast, is always a QUESTION.
- NAMES AND PATHS ARE SINGLE-QUOTED (`'…'`), never backticked — one quoting form across cards, prompts and stderr alike.
- AN APPENDED REASON IS LOWERCASE. `<Act> refused: <reason>` is ONE sentence, so its tail starts no second one, and the composers stop deciding case per site. A producer whose string is ever used WHOLE is itself a sentence and capitalizes at THAT producer — which is why the past-EOF wall defects stay capitalized at their frozen owner (`first_past_eof_wall_defect`, used whole by the picker's dry-run card and by the CLI) and lowercase at each of their four GUI APPENDING seams through the one helper `lowercase_initial` (`notifications.h`).
- THE SYSTEM'S OWN WORDS ARE THE ACCEPTED CLASS: `ec.message()` and `strerror` arrive capitalized ("No such file or directory", "Permission denied") and are appended as they come. The architect ruled the Synchronize card carries "the system's words"; nothing lowercases libc.
