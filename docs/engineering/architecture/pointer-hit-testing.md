### Mouse Reach: No Global Halo, One Grab Constant Per Surface

THE ONE GLOBAL HIT HALF-WIDTH IS GONE — `kMarkerHitHalfPx`, the 4 px tolerance stems, flags and trim bounds all shared, DELETED 2026-08-02 with its last reader long behind it (no code had read it since 2026-07-20; the deletion record, including the rule that outlived it, is at the site in `app_state.h`). The redesign gave each surface its own authored, `gui_scale`-aware constant instead, of which ONE survives:

- `kTrimEndcapGrabPx` = 10 (`render.h`, via `trim_endcap_grab_px()`) — added to EACH SIDE of the drawn 2px endcap, making the target 22px. THREE consumers, re-grepped 2026-08-19: the TRIM BAR's endcaps (`hit_test_trim_endcap`), the OVERVIEW BOX's edge handles (`hit_test_overview_endcap`, the trim model reused verbatim) and — since the region became the trim — the WAVEFORM OVERLAY's two bounds (`region_manipulation_hit`), so a retune moves all three surfaces together — BY CONSTRUCTION, not coincidence: all three are the same gesture on the same shape (a 1-2px vertical edge dragged absolutely along x, one bound moving while its partner holds), so whatever tolerance a fingertip needs on one it needs on the others, and a second constant here would only be a way for them to drift apart. It was 4 from row 5's landing (the retired square chip's width) until 2026-08-14, when the architect widened it — "endcaps are very useful and currently too small", leaning 6 to 10 and ruling 10, THE TOUCH PANEL being the reason — 15 from 2026-08-15, his own second widening from the rig once both lanes had been driven on glass, 5 from 2026-08-18, his narrowing of that after driving the unified region/trim, and 10 SINCE 2026-08-19, SETTLED there. THE THIRD CONSUMER IS WHY IT CAME BACK UP: the waveform overlay's bound bands exist precisely because the 9px trim bar is unusable with a fingertip, so 5 per side reproduced ON THE FINGER'S OWN SURFACE the problem that surface was built to solve, while 15 was more than the two THIN lanes want; 10 serves all three. THE COST IS RECORDED AT THE CONSTANT and is the trim BRIDGE's: the endcap claim outranks it, so the bridge survives only on a window drawn wider than `3 + 2*grab` columns — 24 at 10, against 14 at 5, 34 at 15 and 12 at 4. Zoom-recoverable rather than lost (drawn width is a zoom state, both bounds stay independently draggable, and the band's framing double-click is tested ABOVE the router). The caps against EACH OTHER and the overview box's two edges against each other are unaffected in kind — both arbitrations (leftmost-wins/Begin-first, and nearest-edge/Begin-on-tie) are grab-independent — while the overview box's INTERIOR moves the same way the bridge does: a box drawn narrower than `2 + 2*grab + 1` px (23 at 10, against 13 at 5 and 33 at 15) is all edge handle. A NARROWER BAND GIVES BACK EXACTLY WHAT A WIDER ONE TOOK, and a wider one spends it: less of the overview lane resolves as an edge, so more of it resolves as an OUTSIDE press — which teleports the box and then arms its pan — and more of the waveform overlay's span resolves as its MOVE zone. The one place in the trim lane where paint and hit differ, and it is stated at both owners.
- FLAGS HAVE NO TOLERANCE AT ALL: the box IS the reach, its 1px left border included (2026-08-02 — the stash carries the painted extent, so a marker's reach grew one column to the LEFT of its frame column and a press on the border resolves the marker).
- (`kMarkerStemGrabPx` — the marker stem's per-side grab half-width — is DELETED with the stem surface, 2026-08-12; the stem section below.)

THE PRESS ROAD'S OWN LENGTHS JOINED THAT CLASS ON 2026-08-27 (architect), AND THEY ARE THE AUTHORITATIVE STATEMENT FOR EVERY "8 px" IN THESE FILES THAT NAMES A PRESS GATE: the numbers below are AUTHORED 100 % lengths resolved through `scaled_px` like every other dimension in the tree, not raw device counts, so a doc sentence saying "the 8px crossing" means eight authored pixels — 8 device px at `gui_scale` 100 and 18 at the tablet's 225. THE REASON IS PHYSICAL: 8 raw px is 1.7 mm on the retired road rig's 1024x600 panel and 0.8 mm on the tablet's 249 PPI one, and a fingertip's roll between two relaxed taps exceeds 0.8 mm — so on glass the second tap fell outside the double-click slack (two single clicks, no flag editor) or the first crossed the drag gate (a flag drag and a 1 px nudge instead of a double-click seed). A LENGTH SCALES; A DURATION DOES NOT — the 500 ms double-click window, the 60 ms disambiguation window and `kHoldBeatMs` are all untouched by this ruling, which is the standing rule read in the other direction. FOUR MEMBERS, one number twice over and one rate:

- `kDragMovedThresholdPx` = 8 authored px (`app_state.h`, via `drag_moved_threshold_px()`, floor 1) — THE ONE GENERIC PRESS-BECOMES-DRAG GATE every drag surface shares (nav grab-pan capture, the marker flag drag, the overview lane, the region/trim endcap and bridge drags, the trim bar). Six `<` compares in `input_pointer.cpp`, all Chebyshev from the press, all reading the accessor and never the constant.
- `kDoubleClickSlackPx` = 8 authored px (`app_state.h`, via `double_click_slack_px()`, floor 1) — the double-click seed's spatial tolerance, five `<=` compares in `input_pointer.cpp`. It EQUALS the drag gate at every scale (equal authored constants through the one `scaled_px` conversion), which is what keeps "never became a drag" and "never left the slack" one answer.
- `kNavZoomPxPerLevel` = 200 authored px per zoom level (`app_state.h`, via `nav_zoom_px_per_level()`, floor 1.0), JOINING THE CLASS ON 2026-08-27 (architect, on glass: "it is zooming a little fast") — the nav drag's ctrl-phase zoom RATE, one reader (`apply_nav_zoom_at`, `input_pointer.cpp`, the divisor of `dx`). IT IS A LENGTH, NOT A GATE: nothing crosses it, it is the hand travel one zoom level costs, and the band the 200 was landed inside is physical (the millimetres a pinch spends on a level — the derivation at the constant). 200 device px is ~43 mm on the retired road rig and ~20 mm on the tablet's panel, so unscaled it zoomed twice as fast per millimetre there; at 225 % a level costs ~450 device px and the feel is the rig's again. Being a rate it is a DOUBLE and does NOT come through `scaled_px` — rounding a divisor to an int would quantize the feel for nothing — while the multiply stays exact at `gui_scale` 100. THE PINCH IS UNTOUCHED AND MUST STAY SO: its level is log2 of the finger-gap RATIO, dimensionless, so the same spread of the hand is the same number of levels at any PPI; scaling the desk's rate is what keeps the two surfaces on the shared band, not a divergence from it.
- THE TOUCH SLOP is that same drag-gate number PUSHED DOWN into `GuiInputCore` (`touch.md` owns its three uses). The core sits below the GUI model and must never learn `gui_scale`, so it cannot resolve the value itself: it holds a settable `touch_slop_px_` whose default is the authored 8, and the GUI hands it `drag_moved_threshold_px()` through `GuiPlatform::set_touch_slop_px` at BOTH `gui_scale` application points — the source load's tail (`file_loader.cpp`, the init road on both backends) and the settings editor's `gui_scale=` commit (`GuiInputHandler::apply_gui_scale`, the live road). The inventory of those two sites lives at the setter and nowhere else. ONE NUMBER NOW REACHES BOTH GATES at every scale, where before they were two literals kept equal by comment — which is what still makes a slop-crossing touch resolution cross the GUI's drag gate in the same burst as its press.

REACH IS VISIBLE-PAINTED-ONLY, and since row 5 that is structural rather than a rule to obey: the flag/stem stashes contain only what the painter emitted (the cull in `iterate_visible_flags_impl` is ASYMMETRIC because the box is — a left margin of `marker_flag_max_width_px` admits a marker whose box reaches in from offscreen, and the right bound is EXCLUSIVE at `viewport_end_sample` so a marker sitting exactly on the displayed end paints and publishes nothing into the inert non-multiple-of-16 gutter), and `hit_test_trim_endcap` culls a bound whose column is outside the viewport exactly as the painter does. The offscreen-halo reach and its blind-grab pull-in machinery were removed 2026-07-18 as a non-use-case; the drags' viewport clamp survives as a live tracking clamp. A GRAB TOLERANCE IS NOT A SPACING GAP — the rule the deleted constant used to carry, now belonging to nothing in particular: markers may sit arbitrarily close, overlap exactly, and cross during gestures; ordering degeneracy collapses at the render boundary, not at authoring time.

### The Painter's Stash IS the Hit Geometry (row 5, 2026-08-01)

`AppState::flag_hit_rects` and `AppState::marker_stems` are published BY THE PAINTER as it paints — one producer each, the flag-cache rebuild's `render_flags` / `render_phase_reset_flags` calls — against the displayed basis those pixels were laid out on. (Since the stems-inert ruling of 2026-08-12 only `flag_hit_rects` is HIT geometry — `marker_stems` survives as the stem PAINTER's input alone; the retirement record below.) A row-5 flag's WIDTH is its SHAPED label's width, so there is no formula to re-derive it from: recomputing at the hit site would mean a second HarfBuzz pass that could disagree with the pixels. This is the STRONGER form of event-synchronized hit geometry (below): the rects ARE the painted rects, for free and at every moment, rather than by two derivations agreeing. The old live rebuild — `item_viewport_basis` + the displayed map + the drag overlay threaded into `compute_flag_hit_rects` — is deleted with the functions it called, as are `marker_hit_at`, the `MarkerHit` pair and the marker-text lane's run resolver (they died with that lane).

- `hit_test_flag` walks `flag_hit_rects` BACKWARDS and returns the first containing rect: the painter walks the store forward and later boxes cover earlier ones, so last-painted = topmost = what a click grabs. Boxes OVERLAP freely; selection does not lift one (it is a colour swap now), so this is the whole arbitration — one pass, no class split. The shape is a PLAIN RECT (the fused tip-down triangle and its taper test died with the triangle lane). The stash holds the ACTIVE column's boxes only, because that is the column the painter drew. Cold (nothing painted yet) nothing is clickable — the honest answer for a flag with no pixels.
- THE STEM SURFACE IS RETIRED — MARKER STEMS ARE POINTER-INERT IN ALL CONTEXTS (architect 2026-08-12, the seventh glass ruling: "I definitely don't want to be concerned about accidentally touching a marker"). `hit_test_marker_stem` — the marker's SECOND surface from 2026-08-01: a plain press within `kMarkerStemGrabPx` of an ENABLED marker's stem column, in the waveform's upper half, resolving through the flag's own click bodies (plain-exact from the start; shift/ctrl were flag-only because both modifiers own a waveform gesture at the stem's pixels, a gate the 2026-08-06 symmetry ruling made universal) — is DELETED with both of its callers (the live views' plain stem click, which armed the marker drag too, and the `h` view's diff-flag stem click of 2026-08-05..12), and `kMarkerStemGrabPx` with it. A plain upper-half press over a stem column is the ordinary placement press now, stem or no stem; the FLAG BOX is the marker's one pointer surface; the stems still paint exactly as before. The succession is marker-ui.md's (open through the touch arc, "stems stay" while the scrollbar plan lived, closed the other way once the waveform-height clamp put the flag lane in easy reach everywhere); the code-side record is at the retired declaration site, app_state.h.
- THE SUPPRESSED EDITING FLAG PUBLISHES NO HIT RECT (codex 2026-08-02, a6ecf72, correcting the same-day suppression): the open flag editor owns every pixel of its marker's box, so `render_flags` skips that box — and the rect must match the pixels, which is this stash's whole doctrine. The earlier unreachable-by-ordering argument held only while the editor was at least as WIDE as the committed flag, an assumption that pass removed: a narrowed editor left a visually BLANK tail, and a press there closed the editor as an outside press and then fell through to `hit_test_flag` IN THE SAME PRESS. The close edge is safe by construction — closing changes `fp_editing_flag_target`, which misses the flag cache's fingerprint, and ONE `render_flags` pass repaints the box and republishes its rect together; no frame has one without the other in either direction. THE STEM STAYS PAINTED for the whole editing session (its published entry with it) — while the stem was a hit surface (2026-08-01..12) that made it the deliberate paint-equals-claim exception here; since the stems-inert ruling the entry feeds the painter alone and there is no stem claim for the exception to carry. RECORDED DELTA: a double-click in the narrowed editor's blank tail now drops a marker as an empty-lane press — blank pixels behave as blank.

### The Two Halves Become One Surface (2026-08-13)

THE PRESS-TIME SCRUB IS DELETED AND THE LOWER HALF JOINS THE NAVIGATION SURFACE WHOLE (architect 2026-08-13): "the playhead scrub is an outlier. We do everything on lift the finger or on mouse up, but the playhead scrub, we do right on mouse down. We should remove that. And that should allow the dragging on the lower half of the waveform as well, the pan — because zoom is already allowed on the lower half... likewise on the lower half it waits for the mouse up, and if the mouse doesn't go up it becomes a drag, and then you pan the waveform on both the top and the bottom. And shift plus drag to map out a region should also be allowed in the lower half, for consistency." So the surface is the WHOLE waveform + the RULER + the MARKER lane's empty stretches, in EVERY view, and the vocabulary is one: a PLAIN press arms the pending click (`arm_nav_press` — nothing pops at press ANYWHERE now), a MOTIONLESS RELEASE runs THE PRESSED HALF'S ACT, MOVEMENT is the grab-pan, SHIFT is the trim SWEEP (its y-gate grown back over the lower half, superseding the eighth ruling's "no region sweep at all in the lower half"), and CTRL is the zoom, which already covered both halves and did not move — the ruling's own premise ("zoom is already allowed on the lower half"); since 2026-08-14 it is the one drag's live ctrl phase rather than a separate gesture.

THE ONE DIFFERENCE, STATED HONESTLY — it is TWO, and both predate this ruling. The UPPER half's act is the playhead placement, which ALSO deselects, HIDES the trim region overlay and overrides follow for the session; the LOWER half's act is `scrub_act_at` at the pressed column, which touches NO selection, NO overlay, NO cursor and NO follow state, and that omission is exactly what makes the scrub the overlay's PREVIEW gesture — click inside the span to audition it and the overlay stays up. "That becomes the only difference between the upper and lower half" is the ruling's own sentence and it is true of the ACT; the act's side effects are where the halves still part, and the code says so at the fork.

MECHANICS, one owner each. The half is read ONCE, at the press, by `waveform_lower_half` — its ONLY reader now, the cursor having stopped asking — and stashed on the pending as `ScrollDragState::scrub_release`, beside `history` and `seed_empty_lane` (three surface facts, mutually exclusive by geometry); the release forks `run_nav_click_act` on it, the scrub arm returning ABOVE the deselect and the hide. `scrub_press_at`'s body did not change at all and its ONE caller moved from the press to that arm. THE SURFACE'S GEOMETRY MOVED TO ONE OWNER TOO, `point_on_nav_surface` (input_pointer.cpp), read by the shift former's claim, the ctrl strip-drag claim, the cursor map, the `h` view's own press router and the TOUCH PAN ZONE — five hand-spelled derivations collapsed onto one call, which is also what made the pan zone follow (it had been a hand COPY of the press router's spelling, not a derivation, so it would not have followed on its own).
