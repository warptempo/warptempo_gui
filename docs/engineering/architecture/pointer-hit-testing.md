### Strictly As Painted (architect 2026-09-24)

THE RULE, his words: *"the live painted face should correspond to reality, and reality to the face, with cards being preferred over no-ops on a face that falsely advertises an action."* Stated as the rule this project strives for: **INPUT ACTS ON THE PICTURE THE USER IS LOOKING AT.** A press resolves against the geometry and the face the painter last put on screen — never against a live derivation that may be a tick ahead of the pixels — and the painter is kept honest by the tick, so that what the press does and what the screen promised are the same thing, and where they cannot be (a state moved inside the one tick between paint and press) the act answers with a card rather than a silent nothing. It has two halves, each older than its name:

- ON THE WAVEFORM AND THE TOP STRIP it is "the painter's stash IS the hit geometry" (row 5, below) and "gesture mechanics run on the displayed paint basis" (below), completed 2026-09-24: the flag hit stash advances with the pixels that show it (staged by the rebuild, promoted by the frame that blits the flag surface — not on the tick, where it led the pixels by a frame), the trim bar's endcaps and bridge are a painter-published stash rather than a per-press re-derivation on the live trim, every pending press that aims a painted subject freezes the displayed basis (the trim bar's deferred bound-set click included), and a gesture's mechanics read the painted VIEWPORT as well as the painted map — the marker drag's anchor and motion, the trim drags' conversions and commit snap, the sweep's trim half, the bound-set click's decider, both nudges' pixel anchoring and the drag's viewport clamp all take `item_viewport_basis`, superseding the 2026-07-25 live-viewport ruling; the live forms `painted_column_of_source_frame` and `authored_frame_at_column` are deleted with their last caller and the `_on_basis` pair is the whole pixel-anchoring API (warp_frame_map_view.h). RECORD OF THE ONE BREACH OF THE FREEZE'S PREMISE, found 2026-09-24 by that pass and closed the same day on his ruling: a grab of a flag whose STEM was left of the viewport towed the playhead through `reseat_playhead_to`, which scrolled and kicked the synchronous plate render mid-drag, drawing the flag off the pointer. The tow now writes through `translate_playhead_to` (active-domain, no edge-align), the playhead may ride offscreen for the drag's life and catches up at the release, and `force_synchronous_waveform_rebuild` has no in-freeze caller. THE CLICK-PLACEMENT FAMILY MOVED ON HIS RULING (architect 2026-09-24, "reality to the face" reads the same for a click as for a drag): the nav click, the scrub click, the sweep's playhead half and the empty-lane create convert their column through `playhead_frame_at_click_column` on `item_viewport_basis`, and the waveform-lane playhead step's hold takes the cursor's prior column as the plate painted it; the drops at the playhead author at the playhead's frame and convert no column. Damage follows the basis of the pixels it erases.
- ON CHROME it is the truthful-buttons ruling (2026-08-30) read in the other direction: every chrome claim — roster press, lift and hold-repeat, menu anchors, dropdown rows, the render player's row — reads the FACE the painter published, the per-tick comparators carry reality onto that face, and a face painted live dispatches, its act's own body answering for itself; `kdenlive-redesign.md`'s closing section owns the inventory, `messaging.md`'s greyed-lift bullet the card division.

THE PRESS ROAD'S OWN LENGTHS JOINED THAT CLASS ON 2026-08-27 (architect), AND THEY ARE THE AUTHORITATIVE STATEMENT FOR EVERY "8 px" IN THESE FILES THAT NAMES A PRESS GATE: the numbers below are AUTHORED 100 % lengths resolved through `scaled_px` like every other dimension in the tree, not raw device counts, so a doc sentence saying "the 8px crossing" means eight authored pixels — 8 device px at `gui_scale` 100 and 18 at the tablet's 225. THE REASON IS PHYSICAL: 8 raw px is 1.7 mm on the retired road rig's 1024x600 panel and 0.8 mm on the tablet's 249 PPI one, and a fingertip's roll between two relaxed taps exceeds 0.8 mm — so on glass the second tap fell outside the double-click slack (two single clicks, no flag editor) or the first crossed the drag gate (a flag drag and a 1 px nudge instead of a double-click seed). A LENGTH SCALES; A DURATION DOES NOT — the double-click window (`kDoubleClickMs`, which since 2026-08-27 IS `kHoldBeatMs`), the touch window (the 60 ms off-zone deadline since deleted, 2026-09-25) and `kHoldBeatMs` itself are all untouched by this ruling, which is the standing rule read in the other direction. FOUR MEMBERS, one number twice over and one rate:

- `kDragMovedThresholdPx` = 8 authored px (`app_state.h`, via `drag_moved_threshold_px()`, floor 1) — THE ONE GENERIC PRESS-BECOMES-DRAG GATE every drag surface shares (nav grab-pan capture, the marker flag drag, the region/trim endcap and bridge drags, the trim bar, and since 2026-08-28 the FOLDER OVERLAY'S BAND SCROLL under all three of the overlay's contents alike — the stats panel's band scrolls though its text rows are inert; the EDITOR FIELD'S TEXT DRAG was a member for the one day of 2026-09-05 and is not — the desk's sweep moves on every motion with no gate, and the glass's caret drag is the touch translation's own stream, gated by the slop alone). SIX compares in `input_pointer.cpp` (re-greped 2026-09-24), all Chebyshev from the press, all reading the accessor and never the constant: five spelled `<` as the still-a-click return, and the band scroll's `>=` as the arm's own upgrade — one number, two spellings of the same crossing.
- `kDoubleClickSlackPx` = 8 authored px (`app_state.h`, via `double_click_slack_px()`, floor 1) — the double-click seed's spatial tolerance, five `<=` compares in `input_pointer.cpp`. It EQUALS the drag gate at every scale (equal authored constants through the one `scaled_px` conversion), which is what keeps "never became a drag" and "never left the slack" one answer.
- `kNavZoomPxPerLevel` = 200 authored px per zoom level (`app_state.h`, via `nav_zoom_px_per_level()`, floor 1.0), JOINING THE CLASS ON 2026-08-27 (architect, on glass: "it is zooming a little fast") — the nav drag's ctrl-phase zoom RATE, one reader (`apply_nav_zoom_at`, `input_pointer.cpp`, the divisor of `dx`). IT IS A LENGTH, NOT A GATE: nothing crosses it, it is the hand travel one zoom level costs, and the band the 200 was landed inside is physical (the millimetres a pinch spends on a level — the derivation at the constant). 200 device px is ~43 mm on the retired road rig and ~20 mm on the tablet's panel, so unscaled it zoomed twice as fast per millimetre there; at the tablet's own 225 % a level costs 450 device px (~46 mm) and the feel is back on the rig's order. Being a rate it is a DOUBLE and does NOT come through `scaled_px` — rounding a divisor to an int would quantize the feel for nothing — while the multiply stays exact at `gui_scale` 100. THE PINCH IS UNTOUCHED AND MUST STAY SO: its level is log2 of the finger-gap RATIO, dimensionless, so the same spread of the hand is the same number of levels at any PPI; scaling the desk's rate is what keeps the two surfaces on the shared band, not a divergence from it.
- THE TOUCH SLOP is that same drag-gate number PUSHED DOWN into `GuiInputCore` (`touch.md` owns its three uses). THE INVENTORY OF THOSE TWO SITES LIVES AT THE SETTER (input_core.h) AND NOWHERE ELSE, this line naming only the class: the init push moved out of the source load's tail into `gui_main` on 2026-08-27, when the scale stopped being a `.settings` key and became a device fact, and a site named here would have gone stale with it. ONE NUMBER NOW REACHES BOTH GATES at every scale, where before they were two literals kept equal by comment — which is what still makes a slop-crossing touch resolution cross the GUI's drag gate in the same burst as its press.

REACH IS VISIBLE-PAINTED-ONLY, and since row 5 that is structural rather than a rule to obey: the flag/stem stashes contain only what the painter emitted (the cull in `iterate_visible_flags_impl` is ASYMMETRIC because the box is — a left margin of `marker_flag_max_width_px` admits a marker whose box reaches in from offscreen, and the right bound is the ROUNDED COLUMN: a marker whose column rounds to grid point w paints and publishes its flag, stem and hit rect in the permanent right gutter, one whose column rounds past w is culled — architect 2026-09-26, zoom-viewport-strip.md), and `hit_test_trim_endcap` reads the painter's own publication (`TrimBarHit`, since 2026-09-24), in which a bound whose column is outside the viewport paints no cap and so publishes none. A GRAB TOLERANCE IS NOT A SPACING GAP — the rule the deleted constant used to carry, now belonging to nothing in particular: markers may sit arbitrarily close, overlap exactly, and cross during gestures; ordering degeneracy collapses at the render boundary, not at authoring time.

### The Painter's Stash IS the Hit Geometry (row 5, 2026-08-01)

`AppState::flag_hit_rects` and `AppState::marker_stems` are published BY THE PAINTER as it paints — one producer each, the flag-cache rebuild's `render_flags` / `render_phase_reset_flags` / `render_history_diff_flags` calls (re-greped 2026-09-24) — against the displayed basis those pixels were laid out on. SINCE 2026-09-24 THE REBUILD STAGES THEM (`staged_flag_hit_rects`, `staged_marker_stems`, `flag_stash_staged`) AND THE FRAME THAT BLITS THE NEW FLAG SURFACE PROMOTES THEM at the top of its first `on_redraw`, unconditionally — the flag surface never waits behind the freeze (a dragged flag must move on screen), so its stash advances exactly with it, one frame behind the rebuild; before that day the rebuild wrote the live stash on the tick and it LED the pixels by a frame. (Since the stems-inert ruling of 2026-08-12 only `flag_hit_rects` is HIT geometry — `marker_stems` survives as the stem PAINTER's input alone; the retirement record below.) A row-5 flag's WIDTH is its SHAPED label's width, so there is no formula to re-derive it from: recomputing at the hit site would mean a second HarfBuzz pass that could disagree with the pixels. This is the STRONGER form of event-synchronized hit geometry (below): the rects ARE the painted rects, at every frame, rather than by two derivations agreeing. The old live rebuild — `item_viewport_basis` + the displayed map + the drag overlay threaded into `compute_flag_hit_rects` — is deleted with the functions it called, as are `marker_hit_at`, the `MarkerHit` pair and the marker-text lane's run resolver (they died with that lane). SINCE 2026-09-05 THE FLAG HIT SURFACE HAS A SECOND PRODUCER BESIDE THE LANE STASH: the open PAYLOAD editor's painter, `render_flag_editor_box`, publishes its marker's riding bound cells (`FlagEditorBox::riding_cells`, a `FlagHitRect` of their own — render.h) every frame it paints them, and resets the field to cold every frame it does not, whether or not an editor stands.

- `hit_test_flag` and `hit_test_flag_cell` share ONE walk, `topmost_flag_rect` (app_state.cpp), which asks the riding publication BEFORE the lane stash — the editor paints LAST, so its run covers whatever the lane pass drew under it, the same last-painted-wins rule the backward walk below applies inside the stash. In practice there is nothing of this marker to arbitrate against there anyway, since the flag pass suppresses the edited marker whole (below). Failing that rect, the walk falls to `flag_hit_rects`, walked BACKWARDS to return the first containing rect: the painter walks the store forward and later boxes cover earlier ones, so last-painted = topmost = what a click grabs. Boxes OVERLAP freely; selection does not lift one (it is a colour swap; a selection lift ran for one day, 2026-09-25, and was struck as poor design, marker-ui.md's flag-shape section), so this is the rest of the arbitration — one pass, no class split. The shape is a PLAIN RECT (the fused tip-down triangle and its taper test died with the triangle lane). The stash holds the ACTIVE column's boxes only, because that is the column the painter drew. Cold (nothing painted yet, and no riding publication standing) nothing is clickable — the honest answer for a flag with no pixels.
- THE STEM SURFACE IS RETIRED — MARKER STEMS ARE POINTER-INERT IN ALL CONTEXTS (architect 2026-08-12, the seventh glass ruling: "I definitely don't want to be concerned about accidentally touching a marker"). `hit_test_marker_stem` — the marker's SECOND surface from 2026-08-01: a plain press within `kMarkerStemGrabPx` of an ENABLED marker's stem column, in the waveform's upper half, resolving through the flag's own click bodies (plain-exact from the start; shift/ctrl were flag-only because both modifiers own a waveform gesture at the stem's pixels, a gate the 2026-08-06 symmetry ruling made universal) — is DELETED with both of its callers (the live views' plain stem click, which armed the marker drag too, and the `h` view's diff-flag stem click of 2026-08-05..12), and `kMarkerStemGrabPx` with it. A plain upper-half press over a stem column is the ordinary placement press now, stem or no stem; the FLAG BOX is the marker's one pointer surface; the stems still paint exactly as before. The succession is marker-ui.md's (open through the touch arc, "stems stay" while the scrollbar plan lived, closed the other way once the waveform-height clamp put the flag lane in easy reach everywhere); the code-side record is at the retired declaration site, app_state.h.
- THE SUPPRESSED EDITED BOX PUBLISHES NO HIT RECT IN THE STASH (codex 2026-08-02, a6ecf72, correcting the same-day suppression): an open marker-lane field owns every pixel of the box it stands in for, so `render_flags` skips that box — and the rect must match the pixels, which is this stash's whole doctrine. The earlier unreachable-by-ordering argument held only while the editor was at least as WIDE as the committed flag, an assumption that pass removed: a narrowed editor left a visually BLANK tail, and a press there closed the editor as an outside press and then fell through to the walk IN THE SAME PRESS. The close edge is safe by construction — closing changes the flag cache's suppression fields (`fp_suppressed_box_index` / `fp_suppressed_box_cell`), which miss its fingerprint, and ONE `render_flags` pass repaints the box and republishes its rect together; no frame has one without the other in either direction. THE STEM STAYS PAINTED for the whole editing session (its published entry with it) — while the stem was a hit surface (2026-08-01..12) that made it the deliberate paint-equals-claim exception here; since the stems-inert ruling the entry feeds the painter alone and there is no stem claim for the exception to carry. RECORDED DELTA: a double-click in the narrowed editor's blank tail now drops a marker as an empty-lane press — blank pixels behave as blank. THE RIDING BOXES ARE A SECOND PRODUCER SINCE 2026-09-05, UNDER EVERY EDITOR KIND THAT HAS BOXES TO ITS RIGHT: the flag pass drops the edited box AND everything right of it, and the editor's own painter paints those boxes at the field's right edge for the whole session, so THEY publish the rect (`FlagEditorBox::riding_cells`) at the pixels they occupy rather than none. Which boxes those are follows from the field's own: every box right of it in rank order (payload, lower, upper) — the payload field's run is the two bound cells, the lower-bound field's the upper cell, the upper-bound field's empty. A press on one is an ordinary outside press: `close_top_flag_editor_for_outside_press` — which is KIND-BLIND, testing the published field and nothing else — sees it outside the field, closes the editor with no commit and schedules damage but does not repaint synchronously, so the riding publication SURVIVES the close and is what the same press resolves against, the walk answering the last painted frame's truth exactly as the stash does. The next painter invocation, scheduled by that damage, resets it; it cannot cross into the `h` view either, since these editors are keyboard-modal and swallow bare `h`, so the mode's own paint zeroes the field before the mode can be entered with one open. The pass STILL PUBLISHES for a marker under a bound field, covering what it did draw — the flag box, plus the lower cell beneath an upper field — and stopping where the field begins, so the two rects are disjoint and a point falls in exactly one.

### The Placement Lanes (2026-09-25)

THE RULER AND THE MARKER LANE LEAVE THE NAVIGATION SURFACE, ON BOTH DEVICES (architect 2026-09-25: "out of both — we want symmetry as much as possible; the horizontal zoom, a first as far as I know in this kind of interface, was designed to unify the motions for zoom on both devices"). ### The Navigation Surface and the Deferred Click (pan-primary, 2026-08-12)

(The surface this section and the next describe included the RULER and the MARKER lane's empty stretches until 2026-09-25, when they became the placement lanes above; the waveform's vocabulary below is unchanged.)

THE NAVIGATION SURFACE IS ONE SURFACE WITH ONE VOCABULARY (architect 2026-08-12, the EIGHTH glass ruling, ratified whole across three exchanges — reconciling laptop and glass into one gesture economy): the waveform's UPPER half + the RULER lane + the MARKER lane's empty stretches — the lanes are "essentially an extension of the upper half", in easy reach since the waveform-height clamp — and inside the `h` history view the WHOLE waveform + those lanes (no scrub in there). THE DEFERRAL is the press-becomes-drag fork read honestly: a press cannot know it is a click until the release says so, and a pan must move no playhead and clear nothing — so the PLAIN press arms a PENDING CLICK (`ScrollDragState`, nothing at press — the one-day ruler former's deferred-dissolve pattern generalized: NOTHING POPS AT PRESS), a motionless release runs THE CLICK ACT at the press column (`run_nav_click_act`: deselect-all — the mode-focus clear in the `h` view — playhead placement, live-session reseek, playback state read at the act; FOLLOW IS UNTOUCHED since the lamp of 2026-09-23, and the trim overlay's hide left with the resting overlay on 2026-09-22), and the 8px crossing begins the pan AND its pointer capture (deferred off the press so a click never blinks the cursor). THE DEFERRAL IS SAFE BECAUSE THE FOLLOW CHASE IS PAUSED UNDER A LIVE AIM (2026-08-12): the pre-paint autopager — the product's one autonomous viewport mover — refuses while any pointer gesture (this pending included) or any touch contact stands, so the press column still names the frame the user aimed at when the release converts it, and the pan's first leg folds its whole press→crossing delta over a viewport nothing else moved. It is a PAUSE, not a suppression: nothing is written, the follow producer inventory is unchanged, and the chase resumes and catches up on the next tick — a long motionless hold visibly freezes the chase while held, which is the intended reading.
