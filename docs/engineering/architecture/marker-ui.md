### Marker Gestures
A marker is ONE pointer item WITH ONE SURFACE: its painted flag box (the painter's stash; one BACKWARD walk, last painted topmost). THAT SURFACE HAS ITS OWN CURSOR since 2026-08-13 (architect): a flag box hover answers TrimResize (`ew-resize`) — markers move SIDE TO SIDE, which is the trim bridge's promise, and the cue names the SURFACE rather than one press branch, so it stands where the drag refuses (off the column's home view, a locked tab) and in the `h` view's click-only diff lane too; the arm reads `hit_test_flag` and sits under the modifier arms, region-scrub-esc.md's cursor section being authoritative. THE STEMS ARE POINTER-INERT IN ALL CONTEXTS (architect 2026-08-12, the seventh glass ruling — "I definitely don't want to be concerned about accidentally touching a marker"): they still PAINT exactly as row 5 shipped them (class-colored, always on, disabled-no-stem) but no press resolves one, so a plain upper-half waveform press over a stem column is the ordinary placement press, a drag can never accidentally grab a marker, and the waveform is purely trim / playhead / pan / zoom — the trim's own sweep and, inside the shown overlay, its own bound and bridge drags; marker work happens in the flag lane. THE SUCCESSION, recorded honestly: the stem was the marker's SECOND surface from 2026-08-01 (plain-exact from the start — the shift and ctrl clicks bound to the flag alone because both modifiers mean a waveform GESTURE at the stem's pixels, a rule the 2026-08-06 symmetry ruling made universal by deleting the `h` view's one-day stem-based multi-select); the stems question stayed OPEN through the touch arc and was answered "stems stay" while the scrollbar plan lived; it CLOSED THE OTHER WAY at this ruling — the scrollbar plan is dead, and the waveform-height clamp (kdenlive-redesign.md's vertical rule) keeps the flag lane in easy reach on every display, which is what the removal was waiting for. `hit_test_marker_stem` and `kMarkerStemGrabPx` are DELETED (the record at their retired site, app_state.h); `marker_stems` survives as the stem painter's stash alone. A plain click ON THE FLAG selects and LANDS THE PLAYHEAD (selection-model.md owns the re-coupling, shift/ctrl semantics, and the never-parked rule); the flag is the drag handle — and THE WHOLE CLICK ACTS AT THE PRESS (architect 2026-08-17: CONTENT ACTS THE MOMENT ITS IDENTITY IS CERTAIN — a flag press can only mean one thing, so nothing here waits for a lift; the third clause and its certainty audit are `conventions.md`'s). IT HAS ACTED AT THE PRESS FOR THE PRODUCT'S WHOLE LIFE EXCEPT ONE DAY: the act-at-lift sweep of 2026-08-15 ("all actions should be on mouse-up / finger-up") reached this surface — it began at the ICONS, grew to the overview's teleport, and the flag was what was left — and the architect's first real drive of that sweep inverted the flag half the next day, the double-click open having become "a tad slow compared to using enter key". ONE ACT OWNER, `run_marker_click_act`, with TWO call sites, both in `on_button_press`'s marker claims (the ctrl-exact toggle branch and the plain / shift branch): the STOP leads on every shape, then the modifier fork (ctrl toggles membership, shift ranges from the anchor, plain single-selects — and since 2026-08-18 the toggle arm has a SECOND PRODUCER, the ADD TO SELECTION mode, folded into the same term as `ctrl || (add_to_selection && !shift)` so a lit mode makes a plain flag press a ctrl press in every respect; shift beats it, and `selection-model.md` owns the contract), the LAND on the focus each arm leaves, THE TRIM REGION OVERLAY'S HIDE (a marker click is a point command; it discards nothing, the trim persisting under the overlay since 2026-08-18), and — plain only — the double-click CONSUME-OPEN. THE HIT INDEX IS THE PRESS'S OWN LIVE HIT TEST, resolved in the same event that runs the act, so there is no armed subject that could go stale and the three staleness arguments the lift model had to carry an index for are answered structurally instead: touch's synthesized press at the finger's DOWN point IS the point acted on, a flag stash republished under a held press cannot overtake an act that already ran, and the press point is what the user aimed at. THE DOUBLE-CLICK VERDICT reads the press-time candidate SNAPSHOT (`dc_at_press` — `on_button_press` clears the shared field at the top of every press, so any intervening press invalidates a candidate), and ITS GATES ARE READ LIVE AT THIS PRESS, which IS live state — and they FORK ON THE SPAN since 2026-08-19: the FLAG half keeps its two (read-only, the P view, matching Enter's own allowlist and view refusal — the off-home column refuse RETIRED 2026-08-24, the payload editor now a member of the fifth ruled exception and opening in W+target too), the MEASURE half asks READ-ONLY ALONE, measures being the home-view binding's fourth ruled exception. A refused consume stays a plain second select that seeds afresh at its release. THE CONSUME PREEMPTS THE ARM: a consumed open returns ahead of it, so no drag can begin under the editor it just opened, and it seeds nothing either — the family rule. THE PRESS THEN ARMS, ON THE PLAIN ARM ALONE: `PendingMarkerPress` (renamed from `PendingMarkerDrag` on 2026-08-15 and KEPT, the record holding more than a drag) is FIVE FIELDS — active, the marker, the press x/y and, since 2026-08-19, the `MarkerClickSpan` the press landed on — and it holds exactly the two things that belong to a later edge: the reposition DRAG the press may become and the double-click SEED only a motionless release can write (the span rides for the SEED's sake alone; the drag is one gesture on one marker whichever half started it). The press-time SHIFT and CTRL bits and the carried verdict went with the lift model: the two modified clicks have no drag to become and no double-click meaning, so they commit whole and arm NOTHING, which makes an armed marker pending PLAIN BY CONSTRUCTION (the cursor map reads that directly). It stays a first-class armed gesture — `any_pointer_gesture_active`, the key gate and the force-end finalizer `finalize_active_drags` all include it — now naming a drag that may still begin rather than a click that has not run. THE MOTIONLESS LIFT OWES ONLY THE SEED, written with the PRESS's coordinates and the RELEASE's timestamp (the position looking back so the spatial pairing stays press-to-press; the stamp the seed's own, so the window is measured release-to-press as it is for TrimBar, EditorText and EmptyLane). A LOST BUTTON, the force-end finalizer and the touch layer's ABNORMAL end all disarm and seed nothing — none of them is a clean click sequence — and none of them takes the CLICK back: it committed at the press, undo is the mitigation, and that is the arc's one recorded regression (the abnormal end of codex round 19 could un-commit a flag press only while the lift model stood; `conventions.md`). CROSSING the one generic `kDragMovedThresholdPx` (8 AUTHORED px through `drag_moved_threshold_px()`, Chebyshev, latched — the `gui_scale` ruling is in `pointer-hit-testing.md`) RUNS NO ACT AT ALL — the click's acts already stand from the press — and simply begins `marker_drag.begin_drag(marker, press_x)`, anchored at the PRESS x so the first apply folds the whole catch-up, so a DRAG'S OUTCOME IS WHAT IT ALWAYS WAS. THE DRAG DEPENDS ON TWO OF THE PRESS'S OWN ACTS AND GETS THEM FOR FREE: the STOP is load-bearing (the crossing needs no follow override precisely because the press already stopped playback, and nothing can restart it under the held button — the drag-modal gate swallows every chord), and the SELECT is load-bearing FOR THE PAINT and not for the arithmetic (`begin_drag`/`apply_drag_motion`/`commit_drag` never read `selected_markers`, but the dragged flag's brightened face IS the selection, so without it the user drags an unmarked flag). AND THE DRAG MACHINERY NOW HOLDS NO SELECTION DEPENDENCY AT ALL (codex round 23's L1, 2026-08-15): `begin_drag` had kept its own `set_single_selection` at the crossing, which once the click act owned the selection at both of its act sites was a SECOND mutation of one invariant — not an assertion, since that call clears and rebuilds the selection and schedules top-strip and status-chain damage, so every drag start issued it twice — and it is DELETED, taking `MarkerDragOps`' `Selection&` member with it, since the click act is the one owner at BOTH act sites. The wall-saturated argument that call carried is KEPT and restated where it belongs: the assert has to be unconditional and at the CROSSING rather than behind an `any_changed` test, because a clamped delta can pin the proposal (the marker at the EOF wall, or the viewport clamp saturated) while `commit_drag` lands the playhead on the dragged marker regardless of net change — and the PRESS's click act satisfies that identically, unconditionally and ahead of every crossing. Do not re-wire the edge to "assert" a selection this gesture does not own. NO SHIFT OR CTRL ARM EXISTS TO CROSS any more, and the one deliberate cost of the lift model went with it: an 8px wobble during a shift-click used to select nothing, and now selects at the press like every other flag click, the wobble reaching a crossing that has no gesture to begin. The two AUTHORING gates (`!read_only`, `active_column_authoring_allowed`) guard the DRAG and not the click, and they live at the CROSSING: read-only selects, lands and stops as it always did, and only the drag is refused. Mid-drag the overlay proposes positions through the displayed map with the fractional time rounded BEFORE the map lookup (`apply_drag_motion` maps `nearbyint(new_t)` — the row-5 refinement fix); the release commits through the pixel-anchored column snap (zoom-viewport-strip.md). POINTER GESTURES HAVE NO CANCEL (rulings 3+4): Esc mid-gesture is a consumed no-op, any end commits, undo is the mitigation.

THE EMPTY MARKER-LANE STRETCHES ARE THE NAVIGATION SURFACE'S LANE MEMBER (architect 2026-08-12, the eighth glass ruling — the lane is "essentially an extension of the upper half", so it takes the upper half's whole vocabulary; the 2026-07-23 press-time parity placement is superseded): PLAIN press = the PENDING CLICK / GRAB-PAN (nothing at press; the MOTIONLESS release runs the deferred placement — deselect + playhead + the trim region overlay's hide, no stop call, a live session reseeking — AND seeds the EmptyLane double-click candidate; SHIFT+S DROPS A PHASE RESET FROM ANY VIEW (architect 2026-08-28): "Shift+S should set a phase reset marker in T+P from S+W at the current playhead frame, switch to T+P, and highlight the marker that was just created — which it would automatically do because it's the one under the playhead." It is BARE `s`'s ACT WITH A VIEW TRIP IN FRONT OF IT and never a second drop body: the act (`GuiInputHandler::drop_phase_reset_in_target_view`, input_handler.cpp) runs `switch_active_audio_view_to('T')` then `switch_active_markers_view_to('P')` and then calls the same `drop_phase_reset_lead_in_at_playhead` bare `s` calls in T+P, so in T+P the two chords are ONE CALL and the trip has nothing to do. THE ORDER IS DECIDED, NOT CHOSEN, on two counts: the drop body reads the playhead in the ACTIVE domain and offsets it by N/2 OUTPUT samples, so it is only correct with target view already live (run in source view the same subtraction would take N/2 SOURCE frames off a source cursor); and the COLUMN switch clears the selection, so it has to precede the drop's own single-select rather than follow it — which is the whole of "highlight the marker that was just created", the drop single-selecting what it creates and re-seating the playhead onto it, exactly as it does for bare `s`. ONE UNDO ENTRY, the drop's own, tagged T / P / the standing tab: a restore lands where the op was AUTHORED, and the op was authored in T+P (a phase-reset entry's `op_mode` is its KIND as well as its column tag, so the pressing view is not a thing the entry can carry — selection-model.md owns the three-tag rule). REFUSALS are every gate the chord already passes and none of them new: the read-only lock (blocked beside bare `s` — a phase reset is authored content), the `h` view (its allowlist admits neither), a standing modal, a blank session, and the drop body's own two. It is NOT a sixth exception to the home-view binding — it goes home and then drops (tempo-and-home-view.md). ### The Flag Editor and the Editor Lifecycle
THERE ARE THREE ROADS INTO THE FLAG EDITOR AND ONE ACT OWNER BEHIND THEM. ENTER (bare Return/KpEnter, no editor open) opens the flag editor on the FOCUSED marker (W view only — phase resets have no per-flag editor; read-only refuses at the allowlist); a plain DOUBLE-CLICK on a warp flag opens the SAME editor on that marker (the first click single-selected it and armed no drag; P view and read-only refuse silently, matching Enter); and since 2026-08-27 the BOTTOM ROW'S **Edit flag** BUTTON (`IconMarkerEditFlag`, bare Enter in `kToolbarChords`, the verb group's fifth box, seated between Toggle inherit and the Measure — the architect: "before Measure, away from the delete button") dispatches that same chord at its LIFT, so it inherits every gate the key has and adds no body. IT IS ENTER IN EVERY STATE, A STANDING EDITOR INCLUDED (architect 2026-08-27, naming the button by its hotkey: "just say Enter, because that's what triggers the editor"): a button that IS a key does what the key does everywhere, so a press while the flag or the MEASURE editor stands COMMITS that editor on valid text and RED-FLASHES it on invalid, exactly as the keyboard's Enter and the on-screen keyboard's own Enter key do — the bottom row's band claim sits above the outside-press closer, so nothing tears the editor down under it, and no special case exists anywhere. THE DOUBLE-CLICK DIFFERS AND THAT IS THE POINT: it is close-then-reopen (below), because a double-click on a flag names a MARKER, the one under the finger; the button names the KEY, and has no marker under it at all. THE BUTTON IS THE ARCHITECT'S ANSWER TO THE DOUBLE TAP (2026-08-27, on glass): the double-click is unreliable with a fingertip, a TOUCH-ONLY HIT HALO built that evening to rescue it was retired the same night (pointer-hit-testing.md carries that one-sentence record), and "instead we should get rid of double tapping as the ONLY way to enter the editor" — a keyboardless panel now has a road that cannot miss. Its FACE is the Measure's exactly (greyed on a locked tab and in the `h` view, lit otherwise): the nothing-focused and P-view refusals stay consumed no-ops, the 2026-08-15 no-blink ruling holding here as it does for every verb in the group. THE WARP COLUMN IS THE WHOLE VIEW GATE ON ALL THREE ROUTES SINCE 2026-08-24: the editor is a PAYLOAD surface — tempo, `label_def`/`label_ref`, per-marker scale, the disabled `#`, the iter bracket — and edits no position, so it is the home-view binding's FIFTH ruled exception and opens in W+target as well as W+source, its commit carrying the target-view re-warp and playhead re-land (tempo-and-home-view.md is authoritative for the exception and its contract). The editor is the FLAG UNROLLED (kdenlive-redesign.md row 5 checkpoint C): the box grows from the flag's own painted column to the FULL text width, wearing the marker's resolved face — opening changes the flag's SIZE and nothing else; invalid commits red-flash in the lane's red pair; `text_editor::State::view_offset_px` gives this one surface minimal-travel horizontal scroll; click-to-byte is nearest-boundary ties-low through the ONE owner `editor_byte_index_at` on the shaped run's `byte_offsets_px`. THE LIFECYCLE IS GUARD-FREE (2026-07-22): while the editor is open, a click in its rendered text places the caret; ANY other click CLOSES it without committing (exactly Esc's teardown, `exit_top_flag_edit_no_commit`; Enter stays the sole commit route, the close placed ahead of every band claim so it is unconditional) — a double-click on the open editor's own marker is close-then-reopen, which IS the double-click-opens rule, no special case. EVERY editor opens with its SEEDED content fully selected (open-selected, one universal rule at the `enter_text_edit` chokepoint; the blank-seeded settings editor degenerates to caret 0). Modality — which keys reach an open editor, the playback stop, the wheel transparency, the dialog veil — is conventions.md's; the four dialog editors' box, field and button geometry (they left row 9's status lane for the centered modal dialog, 2026-08-12) are kdenlive-redesign.md's dialog section.

### The Lane's Text Ink is Black (architect 2026-08-20)
THE MARKER LANE'S TEXT IS #000000 IN EVERY CLASS AND EVERY STATE, and it is a MEASUREMENT rather than a taste call: the architect went back to kdenlive itself with a bitmap font on its flag text and read black out of it for every colour and every state (reference crops under `tmp/screenshots/kdenlive/redesign`). ONE CONSTANT, `kMarkerFlagLabel` (render.h), covers the whole lane — the warp and phase-reset flag LABELS on all three live classes, the MEASURE BOX's text, the `h` view's DIFF-FLAG labels (which wear this lane's anatomy, so they wear its ink), and the flag editor's unrolled text and caret, which resolve through the same `FlagFace`/`MeasureFace`. THE ONE EXCEPTION IS THE FLAG EDITOR'S SELECTED SUBSTRING, white on the accent since 2026-08-28 (the selection paragraphs below).

IT IS ITS OWN CONSTANT AND NOT A RETUNE OF `kRedesignLabel`, which stays #fcfcfc: that value is the whole redesign's label ink (the menu row, the icon row, the bottom row, the tooltips, the dropdowns, the clock, the ruler's emphasized labels) and this ruling is about the marker lane alone — the only place the product paints text on SATURATED FILLS. Two facts, two constants.

WHAT IT BUYS, black against the fill versus the ink it replaces: the calm purple is a wash (4.50 vs 4.51) and every other class gains, the brighter the fill the more — selected purple 7.21 vs 2.82, red 7.68 vs 2.64, the measure blue 8.43 vs 2.41 (the case that prompted it — light ink on #3daee9 was illegible), selected measure blue 12.1 vs 1.66, the diff lane's green 8.72 vs 2.33 and its red 4.93 vs 4.11. The one class that does not gain does not lose either, which is what lets a single ink be honest across the whole ladder.

THE DISABLED FACE KEEPS ITS MECHANISM AND INVERTS ITS DIRECTION. The label still blends toward the surface it sits on, through the one `mix_color` owner — but from BLACK, so a dimmed label resolves DARKER than its dimmed flag where it used to resolve lighter.

THE LABEL TOOK ITS OWN FRACTION THE SAME DAY (architect, on the drive): the surfaces' 25% was calibrated against the OPPOSITE problem — near-white ink STICKING OUT of a dimmed flag — so applying it to black suppressed something that was no longer sticking out, and the dimmed label sank to ~#2f2438 on ~#3f304a, ~1.21:1. `kMarkerDisabledLabelMix` splits off at 0.75 and `kMarkerDisabledMix` keeps 0.25 for every SHAPE (fill, top edge, border, on both boxes) — one mechanism, two calibrations, neither borrowing the other's number.

0.75 IS A CEILING PICK RATHER THAN A TARGET, and the arithmetic is why: a darker-than-fill ink cannot reach 2:1 on the calm purple's dimmed #3f304a at all — that fill's relative luminance is 0.0367, so even PURE BLACK tops out at (0.0367 + 0.05) / 0.05 = **1.73:1**. The curve is flat near it (0.25 → 1.21, 0.50 → 1.42, 0.75 → **1.60**, 1.00 → 1.73), so 0.75 takes nearly all the reachable contrast while leaving the label visibly inside the disabled face instead of painting it at full live strength. The brighter dimmed pairs land where the ruling asked — selected flag **1.80**, red flag **1.81**, and the MEASURE BOX **1.90** against its own #274557, whose own ceiling is 2.10 because a lighter fill has headroom the calm purple has not. First guess; glass retunes.

THE TWO PARAGRAPHS THAT FOLLOW ARE THE SELECTION BAND'S 2026-08-20 HISTORY, SUPERSEDED WHOLE ON 2026-08-28 by the accent ruling below them; they are kept because what they record — why the band left the ink, and why a saturated re-show against white is a defect — is still load-bearing.

THE EDITOR'S SELECTION BAND INVERTED WITH THE INK AND WAS RULED BACK THE SAME DAY. The band had never been a colour of its own — it filled in `face.label` while the selected glyphs repainted in the fill, which read as the intended near-white field under dark glyphs only for as long as the ink was #fcfcfc, and became a BLACK field under purple glyphs the moment the ink went black. The architect restored the old look on kdenlive's own text-input precedent (WHITE FIELD, BLACK TEXT — the same pairing the flag itself now has), and the band moved off the ink onto `kMarkerEditorSelectionBand`, holding exactly the value `face.label` used to resolve to, so no pixel moved from the look that stood before the flip; only the SOURCE did. A SELECTED SPAN RESOLVES FROM TWO PLACES, not three: the FIELD from that constant and the INK from `face.label`, which is the CARET's source too — a caret being ink rather than field.

IT WAS THREE FOR A FEW HOURS, and the third is DELETED rather than retired quietly. A fourth pass re-showed the SELECTED SUBSTRING in `face.fill` — the two-tone convention the retired monospace box carried, so a selected run read as purple or blue glyphs on the white band — and the architect struck it the same day, certain about it: those glyphs were ANTIALIASED AGAINST WHITE, and blending a saturated fill into white leaves a pale off-white fringe on every glyph edge, visible in his own screenshots. The text looked washed rather than coloured. **THE TEXT DOES NOT CHANGE COLOUR UNDER THE BAND**: black on white, which is what an ordinary word processor's selection is and what the flag itself already does. The look the re-show produced was the bug, so reinstating it to "restore" two-tone would reinstate the fringe.

**SELECTED TEXT IS BREEZE BLUE WITH WHITE LETTERS, ON EVERY TEXT SURFACE IN THE PRODUCT (architect 2026-08-28, R28** — *"what Breeze Light does with dark text... let's just do that everywhere for consistency"***).** The band is `kRedesignAccent` #3daee9 and the selected glyphs are `kRedesignLabel` #fcfcfc, in the marker lane's flag editor and in the four dialog editors' shared field alike — Breeze Light's own selection, which is where that accent value came from to begin with. It RETIRES the white-field / black-text convention above and `kMarkerEditorSelectionBand` with it: a selection ground needs no derivation, so both painters read the accent directly and there is no third constant to keep in step.

WHAT MOVED, PER SURFACE. The FLAG EDITOR keeps its unselected run black (`kMarkerFlagLabel`, the lane's ink) and gains a second show for the selected substring alone, the whole run re-shown under a clip that takes THE BAND'S OWN ROUNDED COLUMNS, so ink and ground start and stop together — never a run shaped from the selected bytes, which could kern its first glyph differently and shift the ink. The DIALOG FIELD loses a pass instead: its run already paints in `kRedesignLabel`, so the selected glyphs are the right white with no second show, and the KNOCKOUT that re-showed them in the resolved field ground is deleted with the band it served (a consequence worth naming: the red-flash ground no longer reaches the selected glyphs, so a selection standing through an invalid flash reads accent-on-red rather than red-on-white). ### The Seam Divider — AN EXPERIMENT (architect 2026-08-20, revertible whole)
THIS SECTION DESCRIBES A TRIAL, NOT A SETTLED RULING, and it landed as its own commit so it can be reverted without touching anything else. Nothing below has been driven for long on the glass.

THE OBSERVATION IS AN OPTICAL ONE. Two saturated fields of different hue meeting edge to edge produce CHROMOSTEREOPSIS — the eye resolves long and short wavelengths at slightly different depths, so the fields read as lying on different planes rather than side by side. The architect saw it in both places this lane puts two hues together: the PURPLE flag against the BLUE measure box (purple appearing to stand in front), and the `h` view's RED removed half against its GREEN added half, which is the strongest pairing in this palette.

THE MITIGATION UNDER TRIAL IS THE LANE'S OWN LINE. `kMarkerFlagBorder` (#131516) is the 1px column that already sits one pixel LEFT of every flag's fill, where it reads as a drop shadow; it is added at both seams, so a dark rule separates the two fields instead of the hue boundary doing the work alone. Nothing new is sampled and no constant is introduced — the experiment reuses the ink and the width the lane already has, which is what makes it a one-line revert at each site.

TWO SEAMS, FOUR PAINT SITES. The MEASURE SEAM has three renderings of the one boundary and all three take the divider, because leaving any out would make the rule appear or vanish on an editor open — the exact flicker the unroll promises never to cause: the RESTING box in the flag cache, the MEASURE PAD painted beside an unrolled payload editor, and the OPEN MEASURE FIELD, whose left border IS the divider (the field's fill therefore anchors one column further right than it did, so it begins on the same column the resting fill begins on). It rides `face.border` at all three, so it damps with the rest of a disabled marker's face exactly as the flag's own border does. The DIFF-PAIR SEAM is one site, and since 2026-08-22 it takes the DISABLED BLEND like every other border in the product — the reason it was undamped ("the disabled blend is a live-marker face and this lane paints none") retired with the ruling that gave the `h` lane its own disabled axis (github-recheck.md). It dims only when BOTH halves are disabled, the divider belonging to neither half alone, while the pair's own left border dims with the LEFTMOST PAINTED HALF, whose face element it is. THE DIVIDER IS DRAWN ONLY WHERE THERE ARE TWO FIELDS: a purely removed or purely added diff flag has no seam, and a measureless marker has no measure box.

GEOMETRY AND BOOKKEEPING. Both boxes GROW by the column rather than absorbing it, which is the flag's own border geometry (outside the fill, on its left) applied twice more. THE PUBLISHED BOUNDARY NEEDED NO SHIFT: `FlagHitRect::measure_boundary_x` has always been the flag fill's right edge, which is precisely the column the divider now occupies, so a press ON the divider already reads as MEASURE — and it should, the divider belonging to the box it introduces. The hit rects widen with the paint at both lanes (paint equals claim), the measure pad's rect includes its divider, and BOTH CULL BOUNDS are charged for it — the live lane's per-marker own-measure term and the diff lane's whole-commit bound, the latter now carrying two border columns because the widest flag in a commit may be a pair. The measure term is charged EXPLICITLY rather than assumed absorbed by the one-em-per-byte slack: that slack bounds the SHAPED RUN, and an ASCII glyph advancing a full em would spend all of it.

WHAT A REVERT TOUCHES, if the glass says no: the four paint sites, the two width expressions they feed, the two cull terms, and the notes at the borderless record above, at `kMarkerMeasureFill`'s palette block and at `render_history_diff_flags`' declaration. No behaviour outside the marker lane's pixels and its published widths is involved — no gesture, no store, no timing.
