# Marker UI (flags, stems, focus model, editors, key repeat)

REWRITTEN 2026-08-01 after row 5 of the kdenlive redesign replaced the marker painter, hit model, and lane anatomy whole — kdenlive-redesign.md's row-5 record (plus its refinement records) is AUTHORITATIVE for the painted form: text-on-flag boxes, always-on class-colored stems, the painter-stash hit doctrine (flag_hit_rects — marker_stems is the stem painter's stash alone since the stems-inert ruling below), the flag-editor unroll, and the class-color ladder. The retired machinery this file used to spell — the marker-text lane, the occlusion resolver, the hover popup, the fused flag+triangle glyph, the selection ink-triangle and kSelectedStem cues — lives in git history (the pre-2026-08-01 version of this file). What remains AUTHORITATIVE HERE: the gesture semantics, the editor lifecycle, the double-click machinery, Space, and key repeat.

### Marker Gestures
A marker is ONE pointer item WITH ONE SURFACE: its painted flag box (the painter's stash; one BACKWARD walk, last painted topmost). THAT SURFACE HAS ITS OWN CURSOR since 2026-08-13 (architect): a flag box hover answers TrimResize (`ew-resize`) — markers move SIDE TO SIDE, which is the trim bridge's promise, and the cue names the SURFACE rather than one press branch, so it stands where the drag refuses (off the column's home view, a locked tab) and in the `h` view's click-only diff lane too; the arm reads `hit_test_flag` and sits under the modifier arms, region-scrub-esc.md's cursor section being authoritative. THE STEMS ARE POINTER-INERT IN ALL CONTEXTS (architect 2026-08-12, the seventh glass ruling — "I definitely don't want to be concerned about accidentally touching a marker"): they still PAINT exactly as row 5 shipped them (class-colored, always on, disabled-no-stem) but no press resolves one, so a plain upper-half waveform press over a stem column is the ordinary placement press, a drag can never accidentally grab a marker, and the waveform is purely trim / playhead / pan / zoom — the trim's own sweep and, inside the shown overlay, its own bound and bridge drags; marker work happens in the flag lane. THE SUCCESSION, recorded honestly: the stem was the marker's SECOND surface from 2026-08-01 (plain-exact from the start — the shift and ctrl clicks bound to the flag alone because both modifiers mean a waveform GESTURE at the stem's pixels, a rule the 2026-08-06 symmetry ruling made universal by deleting the `h` view's one-day stem-based multi-select); the stems question stayed OPEN through the touch arc and was answered "stems stay" while the scrollbar plan lived; it CLOSED THE OTHER WAY at this ruling — the scrollbar plan is dead, and the waveform-height clamp (kdenlive-redesign.md's vertical rule) keeps the flag lane in easy reach on every display, which is what the removal was waiting for. `hit_test_marker_stem` and `kMarkerStemGrabPx` are DELETED (the record at their retired site, app_state.h); `marker_stems` survives as the stem painter's stash alone. A plain click ON THE FLAG selects and LANDS THE PLAYHEAD (selection-model.md owns the re-coupling, shift/ctrl semantics, and the never-parked rule); the flag is the drag handle — and THE WHOLE CLICK ACTS AT THE PRESS (architect 2026-08-17: CONTENT ACTS THE MOMENT ITS IDENTITY IS CERTAIN — a flag press can only mean one thing, so nothing here waits for a lift; the third clause and its certainty audit are `conventions.md`'s). IT HAS ACTED AT THE PRESS FOR THE PRODUCT'S WHOLE LIFE EXCEPT ONE DAY: the act-at-lift sweep of 2026-08-15 ("all actions should be on mouse-up / finger-up") reached this surface — it began at the ICONS, grew to the overview's teleport, and the flag was what was left — and the architect's first real drive of that sweep inverted the flag half the next day, the double-click open having become "a tad slow compared to using enter key". ONE ACT OWNER, `run_marker_click_act`, with TWO call sites, both in `on_button_press`'s marker claims (the ctrl-exact toggle branch and the plain / shift branch): the STOP leads on every shape, then the modifier fork (ctrl toggles membership, shift ranges from the anchor, plain single-selects — and since 2026-08-18 the toggle arm has a SECOND PRODUCER, the ADD TO SELECTION mode, folded into the same term as `ctrl || (add_to_selection && !shift)` so a lit mode makes a plain flag press a ctrl press in every respect; shift beats it, and `selection-model.md` owns the contract), the LAND on the focus each arm leaves, THE TRIM REGION OVERLAY'S HIDE (a marker click is a point command; it discards nothing, the trim persisting under the overlay since 2026-08-18), and — plain only — the double-click CONSUME-OPEN. THE HIT INDEX IS THE PRESS'S OWN LIVE HIT TEST, resolved in the same event that runs the act, so there is no armed subject that could go stale and the three staleness arguments the lift model had to carry an index for are answered structurally instead: touch's synthesized press at the finger's DOWN point IS the point acted on, a flag stash republished under a held press cannot overtake an act that already ran, and the press point is what the user aimed at. THE DOUBLE-CLICK VERDICT reads the press-time candidate SNAPSHOT (`dc_at_press` — `on_button_press` clears the shared field at the top of every press, so any intervening press invalidates a candidate), and ITS THREE GATES ARE READ LIVE AT THIS PRESS, which IS live state: read-only, the P view and the off-home column refuse SILENTLY, matching Enter's own allowlist and view refusal, and a refused consume stays a plain second select that seeds afresh at its release. THE CONSUME PREEMPTS THE ARM: a consumed open returns ahead of it, so no drag can begin under the editor it just opened, and it seeds nothing either — the family rule. THE PRESS THEN ARMS, ON THE PLAIN ARM ALONE: `PendingMarkerPress` (renamed from `PendingMarkerDrag` on 2026-08-15 and KEPT, the record holding more than a drag) is FOUR FIELDS — active, the marker, and the press x/y — and it holds exactly the two things that belong to a later edge: the reposition DRAG the press may become and the double-click SEED only a motionless release can write. The press-time SHIFT and CTRL bits and the carried verdict went with the lift model: the two modified clicks have no drag to become and no double-click meaning, so they commit whole and arm NOTHING, which makes an armed marker pending PLAIN BY CONSTRUCTION (the cursor map reads that directly). It stays a first-class armed gesture — `any_pointer_gesture_active`, the key gate and the force-end finalizer `finalize_active_drags` all include it — now naming a drag that may still begin rather than a click that has not run. THE MOTIONLESS LIFT OWES ONLY THE SEED, written with the PRESS's coordinates and the RELEASE's timestamp (the position looking back so the spatial pairing stays press-to-press; the stamp the seed's own, so the window is measured release-to-press as it is for TrimBar, EditorText and EmptyLane). A LOST BUTTON, the force-end finalizer and the touch layer's ABNORMAL end all disarm and seed nothing — none of them is a clean click sequence — and none of them takes the CLICK back: it committed at the press, undo is the mitigation, and that is the arc's one recorded regression (the abnormal end of codex round 19 could un-commit a flag press only while the lift model stood; `conventions.md`). CROSSING the one generic `kDragMovedThresholdPx` (8px, Chebyshev, latched) RUNS NO ACT AT ALL — the click's acts already stand from the press — and simply begins `marker_drag.begin_drag(marker, press_x)`, anchored at the PRESS x so the first apply folds the whole catch-up, so a DRAG'S OUTCOME IS WHAT IT ALWAYS WAS. THE DRAG DEPENDS ON TWO OF THE PRESS'S OWN ACTS AND GETS THEM FOR FREE: the STOP is load-bearing (the crossing needs no follow override precisely because the press already stopped playback, and nothing can restart it under the held button — the drag-modal gate swallows every chord), and the SELECT is load-bearing FOR THE PAINT and not for the arithmetic (`begin_drag`/`apply_drag_motion`/`commit_drag` never read `selected_markers`, but the dragged flag's brightened face IS the selection, so without it the user drags an unmarked flag). AND THE DRAG MACHINERY NOW HOLDS NO SELECTION DEPENDENCY AT ALL (codex round 23's L1, 2026-08-15): `begin_drag` had kept its own `set_single_selection` at the crossing, which after this arc was a SECOND mutation of one invariant — not an assertion, since that call clears and rebuilds the selection and schedules top-strip and status-chain damage, so every drag start issued it twice — and it is DELETED, taking `MarkerDragOps`' `Selection&` member with it, since the click act is the one owner at BOTH act sites. The wall-saturated argument that call carried is KEPT and restated where it belongs: the assert has to be unconditional and at the CROSSING rather than behind an `any_changed` test, because a clamped delta can pin the proposal (the marker at the EOF wall, or the viewport clamp saturated) while `commit_drag` lands the playhead on the dragged marker regardless of net change — and the PRESS's click act satisfies that identically, unconditionally and ahead of every crossing. Do not re-wire the edge to "assert" a selection this gesture does not own. NO SHIFT OR CTRL ARM EXISTS TO CROSS any more, and the one deliberate cost of the lift model went with it: an 8px wobble during a shift-click used to select nothing, and now selects at the press like every other flag click, the wobble reaching a crossing that has no gesture to begin. The two AUTHORING gates (`!read_only`, `active_column_authoring_allowed`) guard the DRAG and not the click, and they live at the CROSSING: read-only selects, lands and stops as it always did, and only the drag is refused. Mid-drag the overlay proposes positions through the displayed map with the fractional time rounded BEFORE the map lookup (`apply_drag_motion` maps `nearbyint(new_t)` — the row-5 refinement fix); the release commits through the pixel-anchored column snap (zoom-viewport-strip.md). POINTER GESTURES HAVE NO CANCEL (rulings 3+4): Esc mid-gesture is a consumed no-op, any end commits, undo is the mitigation.

THE EMPTY MARKER-LANE STRETCHES ARE THE NAVIGATION SURFACE'S LANE MEMBER (architect 2026-08-12, the eighth glass ruling — the lane is "essentially an extension of the upper half", so it takes the upper half's whole vocabulary; the 2026-07-23 press-time parity placement is superseded): PLAIN press = the PENDING CLICK / GRAB-PAN (nothing at press; the MOTIONLESS release runs the deferred placement — deselect + playhead + the trim region overlay's hide, no stop call, a live session reseeking — AND seeds the EmptyLane double-click candidate; a crossed drag is the captured pan); SHIFT press = the TRIM SWEEP (the lane joined its y-gate; the "region former" until 2026-08-18, when the span it drew became the trim itself and it began writing the trim per motion event under a minimum-width floor); CTRL press = the strip-drag ZOOM (the grown surface); plain DOUBLE-CLICK = CREATE the marker at the clicked position, SELECT it and LAND the playhead on it (the architect's exact words) — the AUGMENTED drop (warp copy-previous / phase reset N/2 lead-in), the only drop bare `s` performs too, through the standing `_at_playhead` chokepoints (`DoubleClickSurface::EmptyLane`, position-keyed; THE CREATE RUNS AT THE SECOND PRESS again since 2026-08-17 — every double-click's second press acts immediately, "if I'm double clicking specifically to do the double click action, I would never double click into a drag" — and the one-day VERDICT-before-arm shape of 2026-08-15, which armed a `PendingClickKind::EmptyLaneCreate` pending so the create could run at the second LIFT, is deleted with that kind: the press consumes against the candidate snapshot, spends itself on the create and arms NOTHING, so it still never becomes a pan and motion after it is simply DEAD — the create is undoable, and undo is its recovery) — the SELECT and the LAND are the drop's own acts (drop_marker / drop_phase_reset_at_position single-select what they create and re-seat the playhead), so ONE body serves key and click with no divergence, and home-view + read-only refuse silently. The FLAG BOXES carve out of all of it — flag clicks are lane vocabulary and act AT THE PRESS (2026-08-17; the press runs the whole click through `run_marker_click_act` and the plain arm alone then arms `PendingMarkerPress` for the drag and the seed — the section above is authoritative).

### The Playhead Beside the Markers
The resting cursor's form is the MARKER-LANE HEAD (19×12 aliased — it sat on the ruler's bottom rows and moved into the marker lane at the row-5 live test, architect 2026-08-01; the occlusion rationale and the record at the head painter, paint_handler.cpp) + the full-height kPlayheadStem line — always painted, ONE form (the region simplification, 2026-07-30; selection-model.md). THE STEM SUPPRESSION (row-5 refinement, reinstating 035e669's model on the new anatomy): the playhead's stem segment does not paint while exactly coincident with any painted marker stem — the compare is the land formula's own EXACT int64 against the marker_stems stash (or the drag-ride index), never pixels — while the head keeps painting under the flags. Z-order, the marker-and-cursor slice of it — THE AUTHORITATIVE FULL SEQUENCE IS THE PAINT-ORDER BLOCK IN `on_redraw` (paint_handler.cpp) and this states only what these forms do inside it: the cursor's WAVEFORM stem → marker stems → THE SCANNER (its own pass, paint_scanner, since 2026-08-01 — the moving playback line paints OVER the stems and over the cursor where they meet, so it crosses a marker instead of blinking out behind it; the resting CURSOR stays under the stems and flags, the hidden-by-marker model being about a cursor COINCIDENT with a marker, not a sweep) → the ruler pass, which carries its own ticks AND the cursor's head, its tip standing on the waveform boundary where the waveform stem begins so the two meet with no lane pixel between them (the head lands after the stems, in a band they never touch, and the ticks stay under it) → the flag blit over all of them. Damage: the flag-cache rebuild damages STRIP + WAVEFORM (it produces marker_stems, a waveform-pixel input — the delay-and-sync doctrine: stash, staged basis, and pixels advance in the same frame).

### The Flag Editor and the Editor Lifecycle
ENTER (bare Return/KpEnter, no editor open) opens the flag editor on the FOCUSED marker (W view only — phase resets have no per-flag editor; read-only refuses at the allowlist); a plain DOUBLE-CLICK on a warp flag opens the SAME editor on that marker (the first click single-selected it and armed no drag; P view and read-only refuse silently, matching Enter). The editor is the FLAG UNROLLED (kdenlive-redesign.md row 5 checkpoint C): the box grows from the flag's own painted column to the FULL text width, wearing the marker's resolved face — opening changes the flag's SIZE and nothing else; invalid commits red-flash in the lane's red pair; `text_editor::State::view_offset_px` gives this one surface minimal-travel horizontal scroll; click-to-byte is nearest-boundary ties-low through the ONE owner `editor_byte_index_at` on the shaped run's `byte_offsets_px`. THE LIFECYCLE IS GUARD-FREE (2026-07-22): while the editor is open, a click in its rendered text places the caret; ANY other click CLOSES it without committing (exactly Esc's teardown, `exit_top_flag_edit_no_commit`; Enter stays the sole commit route, the close placed ahead of every band claim so it is unconditional) — a double-click on the open editor's own marker is close-then-reopen, which IS the double-click-opens rule, no special case. EVERY editor opens with its SEEDED content fully selected (open-selected, one universal rule at the `enter_text_edit` chokepoint; the blank-seeded settings/load editors degenerate to caret 0). Modality — which keys reach an open editor, the playback stop, the wheel transparency, the dialog veil — is conventions.md's; the four dialog editors' box, field and button geometry (they left row 9's status lane for the centered modal dialog, 2026-08-12) are kdenlive-redesign.md's dialog section.

### Iteration Mode (the flags' bracket adornment)
ITERATION MODE IS TARGET-LEGAL (architect 2026-08-07), which is a MARKER-UI fact before it is a render one: the mode's whole visible form is the inline `+[lo, hi]` bracket `flag_text_iter` splices into every iter-eligible owning marker's composed label, so the flags carry it in BOTH audio views now. Bare `i` toggles the mode in the WARP COLUMN in either view (a phase-reset-view press is the silent no-op it always was; read-only refuses it identically from both views, `i` not being on `read_only_key_blocked`'s allowlist), the bit and the brackets PERSIST ACROSS S↔T IN BOTH DIRECTIONS, and `Ctrl+Alt+R` dispatches the sweep from either view. The DISPLAY needed no relaxing to follow: the bracket rides the one composer, `render_flags` takes `iteration_on` straight from the live bit, and the warp arm is selected by `active_markers_view == 'W'` alone — the painter never consulted the audio view.

WHAT STAYS SOURCE-ONLY IS BRACKET AUTHORING. The flag editor is a warp authoring surface, so both of its open routes still gate on `active_column_authoring_allowed` and its iter grammar with them (`enter_top_flag_edit`'s `iter_on` also requires the warp column and an eligible marker); a press that would open it off home keeps its existing silent refusal, and the S→T toggle still closes a live editor without committing. So in target view a bracket is READ and SWEPT but not typed.

THE SUPERSEDED RULING: entering target view used to exit the mode and wipe every bracket (architect 2026-07-23, commit 434ac13 — "brackets are the step-away batch tool, target view the live-by-hand tool"), and the sweep was gated to source view for its success tail's warp-store write (codex P1, bdf4336). Both premises predate the W+target bare cent step (contortion ruling 8, 2026-07-29), and brackets are DELTA-RELATIVE, so nothing technical bound them to source. The wipe is deleted; the sweep tail's clear survives as a granted home-view-binding exception (tempo-and-home-view.md carries the exception inventory, render-pipeline.md the dispatch record).

### Double-Click Machinery
All double-clicks are hand-rolled (Wayland delivers none) and arbitrated by ONE surface-tagged `DoubleClickCandidate` (surface ∈ {TrimBar, Marker, EditorText, EmptyLane} — app_state.h; `kDoubleClickMs` 500 / `kDoubleClickSlackPx` 8 = `kDragMovedThresholdPx` by construction; Marker additionally keyed on the marker index) so the surfaces cannot cross-fire. The TrimBar candidate is the span-framing double-click and the EmptyLane candidate the marker create (both seed-at-motionless-release and CONSUME AT THE SECOND PRESS, ahead of any arm — the EmptyLane create then arms nothing at all, while the TrimBar framing falls through to the band's ordinary cap/bridge arm; zoom-viewport-strip.md and the lane paragraph above; EmptyLane's seed rides the pending click's release since pan-primary, a crossed pan seeding nothing); the text-editor double-click selects the maximal run of the clicked character CLASS via `select_word_at` (word `[A-Za-z0-9_]`+bytes≥0x80 / whitespace / punctuation, the Qt/Kate convention; the ≥0x80 word-classing is what keeps a selection edge on a codepoint boundary now that the editors carry real UTF-8, and the probe itself is exact — `byte_index_from_shaped_x` reads the shaped run's own per-byte pen offsets, so non-ASCII earlier in the line shifts nothing). ANY INTERVENING KEY PRESS — a command or not, the break sitting at the top of `on_key` beside the tooltip hide, the menu-row disarm and the transient-status clear — plus any wheel command, a moved drag, button-lost, the force-end finalizer, or load clears the candidate.

### Key Repeat
Repeat is ELIGIBILITY-GATED at press time (`repeat_eligible`, an application probe the platform consults BEFORE arming): globally only the continuous step gestures repeat — ALL FOUR BARE ARROWS (Left/Right the two-lane playhead step / marker nudge, Up/Down the tempo cent step; the lane split is decided per fire at dispatch, so the arrows repeat as one family and NO modified arrow repeats), PageUp/PageDown, `=`/`-` zoom, the marker-focus cycle (bare Tab / Shift+Tab / IsoLeftTab; the cycle always recenters on the focused marker, AT THE CURRENT ZOOM — the working-zoom landing of 2026-08-05 was reverted the same day), and the THREE repeating Ctrl chords (re-derived 2026-08-07, when the stay-put alt pair's rollback took the set back down from five) — Ctrl+Shift+Tab plus Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Shift+Z, one `ctrl && !alt && key == Z` condition covering both (history stepping is a continuous step gesture; a shift pressed mid-hold flips undo to redo under the live-modifier rule; Ctrl+Tab stays one-shot). THE MARCH REPEATS IN EVERY STATE SINCE 2026-08-18, its mode scope having gone with the reason for it: inside the `h` history view that chord is the MARCH TOO — over the diff-flag cycle instead of the live one, the mode's Tab act composed with the A/B switch — so a hold continues exactly the gesture it continues outside, and `repeat_eligible`'s `!history_mode.active` term is deleted. (It was the row-3 WALK cycle's REVERSE step from 2026-08-07 to 2026-08-18, one-shot like its forward twin because a held slot walk could only flap, and that term excluded the view for exactly that reason.) — and inside an open text editor the typing/cursor/edit keys (never Enter/Esc/Tab); every other command is one-shot, and a press that OPENS an editor is judged under the pre-open context so it never repeats into what it opened. Repeat fires recompute modifiers AND codepoint live; any pointer-button press or completed wheel emission disarms the hold. A held key's synthesized repeats coalesce into ONE undo entry by repeat identity (normalization-and-boundaries.md owns the contortion-7 detail). A HELD KEY'S FIRST ACT IS ITS PHYSICAL PRESS, at t=0 — that press dispatches, pushes the burst's undo entry and IS the burst's opener, with the compositor's repeat delay standing only between it and the second act. (Held BUTTONS differ and the difference is structural rather than a divergence: a chrome button's press acts at the LIFT, so the arrow buttons' burst opens with its FIRST FIRE instead; undo.h states both surfaces at one site.)
