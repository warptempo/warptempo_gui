### Gesture Commits are Pixel-Anchored
Every pixel-stepping POSITION gesture (the bare Left/Right marker-lane nudges in each column's home view — warp in source, phase reset in target; in W+TARGET the bare arrows are a CONSUMED REFUSAL since contortion ruling 8 deleted the tempo-image step that lived there) anchors to the on-screen column grid: read the item's currently PAINTED column (`painted_column_of_source_frame`, the stem painters' own math), step by whole columns, commit that column's time (`authored_frame_at_column`, which funnels through `snap_authored_frame`). The painted move is exactly the commanded number of columns per press, unconditionally — the whole-frame rounding error is just under 0.291 px at the deepest zoom under the value brackets and the 44100 floor (0.5 / 1.72265625 at the exact bracket edge — codex round 1 corrected the older "at most 0.29" wording, which was not a strict bound), and re-anchoring each press means nothing accumulates (per-press independent rounding would paint occasional 0/2 px jumps). THE ONE-COLUMN GUARANTEE IS A GRID-FINENESS PROPERTY, not a gesture-family property (architect 2026-07-24, the lesson of the min-step churn): it holds because the authored FRAME grid is finer than the pixel grid (≥ 1.72 source frames per target pixel at the deepest zoom, so every adjacent-column target is representable). IT APPLIES TO THE POSITION GESTURES AND NOTHING ELSE, and the trap it documents already bit once: the only other gesture that ever claimed it was the W+target TEMPO-IMAGE STEP, which authored CENTS — a grid COARSER than pixels on ordinary spans (one cent moves the image ~2+ px) — so the guarantee INVERTED there and that gesture needed a minimum-directional-cent rule of its own; it is deleted (contortion ruling 8), and the surviving tempo surface, the bare Up/Down cent step, makes no pixel claim at all (it steps ONE CENT per press by definition). Mouse drag commits snap the release to its painted column (frozen-map coordinates; stored equals shown), uniform in both views at all zooms; mid-gesture positions stay free. EXACTLY ONE ITEM MOVES PER GESTURE (contortion ruling 9, architect 2026-07-29 — horizontal movement is a focus act, groups are never moved): a nudge steps the FOCUS (a 2+ selection collapses to it first, in the twins' shared prologue) and a drag moves the marker it grabbed, so a drag commit is one column snap plus the integer wall belt and nothing more. The RIGID GROUP COMMIT this replaced is recorded here only as a do-not-re-propose: it column-snapped the GRABBED member alone and rode every other member on a uniform active-domain delta D through `snap_authored_frame`, with D re-clamped into the motion's wall intersection at release and the grabbed member re-derived from the clamped value by plain rounding (walls winning over the grid for that one saturated case), and D never recomputed from the re-derived frame — the codex round-6 re-quantization trap. All of it went with the group drag; the release-time D clamp in particular existed ONLY to keep other members off a wall the group had stopped at, so deleting it restores the pre-group singleton commit exactly. The motion loop's verbatim short-circuit survives and still tests the CLAMPED delta, not the raw pointer delta (codex round 6: an outward drag of a half-offscreen flag clamps to zero effective motion but a raw-delta test would write two-hop residue and let commit column-snap a forbidden inward jump). Walls win over the grid everywhere (walls are integer frames). Propagate paste rounds plainly WITHOUT pixel anchoring (computed, view-independent positions — recorded at the site). Wall semantics are one regime per column under the home-view binding (see that paragraph): each column's POSITION nudge runs in its HOME view only (the 2026-07-24 "third exception" — a both-views warp position nudge — was RE-RULED AWAY the same day: no warp position authoring in target view; in W+target the bare arrows are a consumed refusal since ruling 8, see the home-view binding): the warp nudge in SOURCE view CLAMPS its step into the nudged marker's own wall headroom (creep-to-the-wall, walls exactly reachable); the phase-reset nudge runs only in target view and since 2026-07-30 CLAMPS the same way — the unified wall policy (authoritative at position_nudge.h's head): SINGLETON STEPS CLAMP ONTO THEIR WALL with post-clamp identity a silent no-op, GROUP PRESSES REFUSE WHOLE as group rigidity; the old "one regime per column at its home" split is retired. Both regimes are singleton-scoped now: the group forms (the warp intersection over members, the phase whole-press refusal over members) went with the group nudge, contortion ruling 9.

The waveform's EFFECTIVE width is quantized to a multiple of 16 px (`waveform_area` floors `a.width` to `w - (w%16)`, leaving a ≤15 px inert right gutter that `render_background` fills automatically over the full-width damage). 16 = `1600/gcd(44100,1600)`, the strictest grid step among standard sample rates (every standard rate's step divides 16), so at a multiple-of-16 width `logical_spp·W` is integral at every INTEGER numeric zoom level and `painter_samples_per_pixel == logical_spp` exactly there — making the GUI's pixel-anchored grid coincide with the migration tool's frame-0 grid regardless of the compositor-configured width. (The zoom level is a CONTINUOUS double as of 2026-07-19 — see the zoom paragraph below; a fractional rest rides the painter quantization `nearbyint(spp·W)/W`, and the bit-exactness claims hold at the integer rungs, notably the working zoom (2.0) every snap gesture lands on.) All grid consumers (the plate/flag caches and the live trim pass, `samples_visible`, the viewport snap, pointer placement, pixel-anchored commits, and the flag/trim-bound column mapping + hit-test — the last threaded a `waveform_width` param through `iterate_visible_flags_impl` so trim geometry never drifts from stems) route through `waveform_area(a).w`; only strip backgrounds and invalidation damage use the full `a.width`. No-op at the deployment widths (1920/2560/3840 are all multiples of 16).

The VIEWPORT itself is snapped to whole-pixel (grid) boundaries at numeric zoom levels — the single chokepoint is `clamp_viewport_start` (main.cpp), which every viewport mutation already funnels through immediately after writing `viewport_start_sample`. It snaps to the nearest grid point using the SAME `painter_samples_per_pixel` (now a declared free function in `warp_frame_map_view.h`, shared with `authored_frame_at_column`), then clamps into `[0, max_start_grid]` where `max_start_grid` is the smallest grid point ≥ `max_start = total − visible` — so the FLUSH-RIGHT viewport also rests on the grid, showing <1 px of inert padding past EOF (subsumed in the last painted column; `get_peak_range` clamps past-EOF reads to silence, so no paint change). This is what lets an exact-grid marker commit stay valid at maximum scroll. The snap runs at EVERY level (the retired fit-file mode's skip is gone; at the effective zoom-out ceiling max_start ≈ 0 and the snap parks the start at 0). Consequence: source-view marker and click-playhead commits land EXACTLY on the frame-0 authoring grid (no ±1 residue), via the shared single-rounding basis `source_grid_position_at_column` (`warp_frame_map_view.h`): recover the grid-snapped viewport's column index `m = nearbyint(viewport_start/q)` and round ONCE, `nearbyint((m+col)·q)`, instead of the two roundings `nearbyint(viewport_start + col·q)`. Both `authored_frame_at_column`'s SOURCE branch (guarded `ctx.domain==Source`, funnels through `snap_authored_frame`) and the two click→playhead sites (a shared `playhead_frame_at_click_column` in input_pointer.cpp) use it, so click-drop, drag, and nudge cannot drift. So GUI zoom-level-2 authoring agrees with the migration tool's frame-0 grid BIT-FOR-BIT (the ±1 became 0 once Phase A/B guaranteed every rest viewport is a true grid point). The m-recovery is exact for product-reachable audio lengths (at the deepest numeric zoom `q ≥ ~27.5` frames/px, far above the double ULP), NOT over the whole int64 range. `painted_column_of_source_frame` is UNCHANGED and the round-trip `painted_column(authored_frame_at_column(col))==col` still holds (`|residue| ≤ 1/q < 0.037` px). Target view keeps its inverse-map form (no source-grid claim). The playhead is NOT snapped except at a click; arrow-key fine-tuning (`move_playhead_pixels`) still authors off-grid, deliberately. This made the continuous-pan fractional-sample accumulator redundant — it was removed, and the Alt+drag scroll-drag was retired outright at the time (2026-07-19; pan moved to the strip rows — see the strip-row paragraph below), whose drags likewise apply an absolute viewport per event and let the snap re-anchor. THAT RETIREMENT WAS ITSELF REVERSED LATER and this sentence had gone stale (caught in the 2026-07-28 docs sweep): the pan-strip ROW is the thing that is retired now, and pan lives back on the ALT+DRAG waveform grab — a captured, continuous 1:1 `ScrollDragState` (`app_state.h`, armed at the alt-exact waveform press), navigation-class, matched against the ctrl-exact waveform press's dual-axis zoom drag and mirrored by the alt+wheel stepped pan. It is one of the two alt gestures that survive the 2026-07-28 alt retirement, precisely because that pan/zoom pairing works.

ZOOM IS CONTINUOUS (Ableton-style free rest, architect 2026-07-19): the zoom level is a real-valued DOUBLE exponent resting anywhere in the ONE continuous domain `[kMinZoom, kMaxZoom]` = [1.0, 17.0] — the fit-file 0.0 SENTINEL is ELIMINATED (legacy of the retired digit-key zoom era; there is no fit MODE, no sentinel branches anywhere). ms-per-pixel = `0.625·2^(level−1)` (computed in `samples_per_pixel_at`; the table is deleted), so integer rungs reproduce the historical 2x ladder bit-for-bit. `kMaxZoom` = 17.0 is DERIVED from audio_io's structural source caps, not invented (derivation at the constant, settings_file.h): the RIFF uint32 data limit bounds any loadable source at ≈16232 s (24-bit stereo at the 44100 floor), and 17 is the provably smallest whole level covering that at the 640 px minimum window. The EFFECTIVE per-file ceiling is `effective_max_zoom_level` = `clamp(1 + log2(total·1000/(0.625·sr·width)), kMinZoom, kMaxZoom)` — the fit-equivalent level, where spp·width == total — so FULL ZOOM-OUT RESTS AT WHOLE-SONG-VISIBLE for every loadable file (a multi-hour file just gets a coarse pyramid plate); the ceiling is a GUI-runtime clamp (tick reclamp + on_resize), deliberately NOT schema-checked. `kWorkingZoomLevel` = 2.0 (RENAMED from kSnapZoomLevel — say "working zoom", not "level 2") is the ruled fine-tuning zoom where the authoring-grid bit-exactness claims hold: `c` lands EXACTLY on it, and it alone among the marker-navigation commands: THE TAB FAMILY DOES NOT ZOOM (architect 2026-08-05 — the working-zoom landing that family carried for one commit, one expression in `cycle_marker_focus` mirrored by the history view's diff-flag cycle, was REVERTED the same day, "no zoom on Tab": the walk lands and recentres at whatever level it was pressed at, in both families, because a walk must not re-frame the view under the reader). `0` IS NO LONGER A TOGGLE (architect 2026-08-05, replacing the working-zoom ↔ full-out two-way): below the effective ceiling it goes to FULL ZOOM OUT, and pressed AT the ceiling it RUNS THE `c` COMMAND (`run_overview_command`, RENAMED from run_zoom_toggle_command — its own route: the trim-bar double-click runs the separate span-framing `run_span_framing_command` described below). There was never a stashed return level to delete, the toggle's other end having been the fixed constant. THE SECOND ARM IS `c` ITSELF, not a bare centre (architect 2026-08-05, superseding the bare `center_viewport_on_playhead` that arm carried for its first day — that call is structurally a no-op at the ceiling, since `samples_visible == total` by the fit-level solve leaves no pan room, so the round trip was lost): `0` twice is therefore OVERVIEW, then the working zoom on the focus, and the focus semantics are `c`'s own rather than a second, weaker centring.
