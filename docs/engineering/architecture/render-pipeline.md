# Render Pipeline (parser compilation, driver geometry, trim stage, dispatch/cancel)

Moved verbatim from CLAUDE.md in the 2026-07-25 slim-down (project feature-complete). This file is now the authoritative home for this detail; CLAUDE.md carries only a digest and a pointer here. Keep it current with landed rulings.

### Parser Owns the Phase Reset Compilation
The parser is the sole authored-to-engine compiler on both columns; the engine consumes only engine-domain inputs — blind to the `N/2` query-origin correction. A phase reset fires exactly at the authored frame: there is NO anticipation offset, no target-domain displacement, and no lead-in dropzone in the parser or engine (the RENDER-TIME offset concept is removed product-wide; the surviving `N/2` uses are all GUI-side and paint/authoring-only, ONE QUANTITY since 2026-09-02 — `kPhaseResetLeadInSamples = kN/2` in `app_state.h`, its `static_assert` pinning it to the seed window's half-width and to two hops — read by the target-view overlay band and by the target-view lead-in gestures, both BARE (architect 2026-07-28): bare `s` — the only phase-reset drop there is — AUTHORS a reset at `max(0, playhead − N/2)` (a target-time seed snapped to the nearest whole source frame), and bare `Space` AUDITIONS playback from `playhead + N/2` whenever the phase-reset overlay has a subject (`Selection::phase_overlay_subject`, the selection-state predicate — never the painted band, whose geometry gates would let a scroll or zoom change what Space does). The two N/2 offsets are deliberately inverse, so drop-then-Space auditions from exactly where the cursor was; the authored frame stays a whole source frame the parser fires exactly at, so engine and parser remain offset-free). THE LEAD-IN IS DERIVED, NOT CHOSEN (architect 2026-09-02, stated as the requirement — "full coherence and full volume at the point where the playhead stands when I drop the reset, such that the offset places the actual reset marker enough in advance that both are reached by the time we get to that point"; the one prose home of the derivation is the drop's comment, `GuiPhaseResetMarkersOps::drop_phase_reset_lead_in_at_playhead`, and this is its record): the engine is WINDOW-CENTRED — it seeds the LAST schedule frame whose window centre C ≤ the authored frame S (`engine.cpp` pass 1 over `stft_container.h`'s schedule, the parser's `S − N/2` being the window START), so C ∈ (S − R_a, S] and its image ∈ (T − R_s, T]; the seed frame re-synthesizes its window `[C − N/2, C + N/2)` verbatim (the SEED GRAIN), and the measured OLA crossover dips from `C − 1.5·R_s` to `C + 0.75·R_s` (deepest at `C − R_s/2`, its depth the old/new phase mismatch, closed from `C + R_s`). The drop point P must be past the dip AND outside the grain (only there is it placed by propagation, on the map, with the new phase established), and since the grain ends at most at `T + N/2` TO THE SCHEDULE'S ROUNDING, that holds for every placement iff the reset is authored ≥ N/2 output samples before P — while ONE HOP fails on every placement (`C + 2·R_s > T + R_s`, the smearing heard by ear in `2a290c98`, 2026-06-29). So the offset is N/2 = 2 hops, the MINIMUM that satisfies the requirement; the number stood already and the window-START picture the GUI carried ("full scale N/2 after the authored frame") is retired from the drop's comment, HELP and this file. Existing resets take NO migration (he adjusts by hand and ear); S+P stays a STUB — no overlay, no lead-in, no decision resting on it. THE BAND IS DERIVED with it (`GuiPaintHandler::phase_reset_overlay_band`, the constant `kPhaseResetOverlayHops` retired): left edge the reset's column, right edge the seed frame's target image + N/2 — the seed frame found by the engine's own placement rule mirrored in `phase_reset_seed_frame_index` (a SOURCE-terms compare in lockstep with `engine.cpp` and the schedule, never a target-domain floor), the lattice `m·R_s` the same under a trim because the GUI's target coordinates are the full target domain and the trimmer's pre-roll re-anchors on its hop multiples — so the band depicts the seed grain's remaining extent past the authored event, stands still while a reset is nudged within one hop cell, snaps a whole hop when it crosses a schedule centre (the byte-difference moment), and is at most N/2 wide TO THE SCHEDULE'S ROUNDING. THE ROUNDING RESIDUE IS ACCEPTED AND RECORDED (architect 2026-09-02, "no effect on audio output; accept and record", closing the deep dive's codex round on item A; RESTATED after codex round 2 of 2026-09-02, which found the first telling short by two terms and wrong about its slope): it has THREE TERMS, and the one prose home of the arithmetic is the drop's comment. (1) THE SCHEDULE'S ROUNDING — the engine places its seed by a compare on the ROUNDED window start `llrint(map_target_to_source(m·R_s) − N/2)`, which admits a seed centre up to HALF A SOURCE FRAME past the authored `S`, so the seed grain can end up to `½·dT/dS` output samples past `T + N/2`, where `dT/dS` is the LOCAL SEGMENT SLOPE `1 / (tempo · marker_scale · settings_scale)` and NOT `1/tempo` (both scales divide the segment too, `warp_frame_map_build.cpp`): under half an output sample at unity tempo, up to ~8 at the NUMERIC slope ceiling of 16 — the reciprocal of the bracket floors' product `0.25 · 0.5 · 0.5` (`value_format.h`) — and UNBOUNDED across a label-reference segment, whose implied stretch has no ceiling by design ("extreme implied multipliers are the author's concern"). (2) THE DROP'S OWN SNAP — the drop inverse-maps `P − N/2` and snaps it to a whole source frame, so the authored marker's image `T` sits up to another half source frame, at the same slope, from the point the lead-in was measured against. (3) THE BAND'S OWN ROUNDING — the painter subtracts `nearbyint(T)`, up to half an OUTPUT sample of painted width, slope-free. Together: a few output samples at ordinary tempos, up to ~8 + ~8 + ½ at the numeric slope ceiling, unbounded only across a label-reference segment — THERE IS NO UNIVERSAL TWO-SAMPLE CEILING (the retired "`½/tempo`, two at the 0.25 floor" was one term of three, on the wrong slope). The drop point can therefore sit inside the grain's last samples, at the grain's own zero-weight edge where the Hann² window has tapered to nothing, and the band can paint that much wider than `N/2` (the worked case, codex round 1: tempo 0.30 with a target playhead at 3071 drops at `S = 307`, `m = 1` qualifies at `llrint(0.30·1024 − 2048) = −1741 = S − 2048` while `T = 1023.33…`, so the grain ends at 3072 — one sample past the playhead — and the band paints 2049 where `N/2` is 2048). Every "at most", "iff" and "by construction" in this paragraph and at its three code sites carries that qualifier. NOTHING IS CHANGED FOR IT: the audio output is untouched, the authored frame is untouched (the marker is never off and the render stays deterministic), the offset is NOT made quantization-aware and the painted band is NOT clamped to `N/2` — a clamp would hide the engine's geometry rather than fix the guarantee — on his ruling, "prefer slight untruthfulness in an overlay that affects no output to a truthfulness that serves no output reason", and it is not worth a third rounding rule. IT IS NOT THE TRIM CROP'S ROUNDING CLASS (the deep dive's item K): that comparison stood for one day and is WITHDRAWN — the trim crop's rounding is TRANSIENT, a tool's own window that the next stroke replaces, while this seam sits in the geometry of every deliverable. It is invisible where it is read: sub-pixel at the working zoom for every numeric slope (`c` is zoom level 2.0 = 1.25 ms/px, ~55 output samples per pixel at 44.1 kHz). THE SEED FRAME IS UNSTRETCHED (recorded 2026-09-02 at `synthesis.cpp`'s seed comment, comment-only under architect approval 2026-09-02): the seed seats theta = phi verbatim while every propagated frame's content is placed on the map through the alpha-scaled frequency spread, so the grain's content at source offset δ from its centre lands `δ·(1 − R_s/R_a)` off the map — up to +4.6 ms late at tempo 1.20, +7.7 ms at 1.33 (measured +130 samples on a real onset at 1.20), early under a slow-down — and "fires exactly at the authored frame" above is true of WHICH frame seeds (0–R_a before the authored frame), not of where its content lands. The design's answer is the N/2 rule itself: the drop point is outside the seed grain by construction TO THE SCHEDULE'S ROUNDING (the residue above — its three slope-scaled terms, at the grain's zero-weight edge, where the seed's own taper has reached nothing), so the grain's lateness never touches the point the user marked; the nearest-centre placement trade and a stretched seed are NOT opened. Phase reset markers are stored at user-clicked/onset positions as whole int64 source frames (`.phaseresetmarkers`, the authored domain). `build_phase_reset_source_frames` (parser) widens each exactly into the double intermediate — and remains the chokepoint applying the past-EOF wall refusal and the exact-equal collapse (equal-frame enabled resets are one event; one stderr line per collapsed timestamp); its output is the authored intermediate used by target-view display (the phase-reset overlay) and the derivation. `derive_phase_reset_frame_map` (parser, `phase_reset_frame_map_build.h`) compiles that intermediate into the engine's origin-centered query domain, always against the FULL map (the parser knows nothing of trim): each position forward-maps to its target image ONLY for the render-end participation verdict (a reset at or past the map's final anchor target drops), then is emitted directly as `S - N/2` — no inverse mapping. The `N/2` is a coordinate-domain correction conforming to canonical PGHI (docs/references/ltfatnote050.pdf: the STFT window is concentrated around the origin, so an analysis position is the window's CENTER), and a reset whose window centers at authored frame `S` has query position `S - N/2`; a reset at frame 0 derives to `-N/2`, the engine's first analysis frame — inert but harmless. Doubles throughout; quantization to the engine's integer query schedule is engine-owned at placement time. The `.phaseresetframemap` artifact is that full derivation against the exact map shipped beside it, so the cache-dir framemap pair is exactly the engine's input, consumed as-is. Trimmed renders take the same full derivation through the prepost trimmer's translate/filter (`plan_trim`), never a separate windowed derivation. Authored-domain surfaces stay authored: marker files, GUI paint positions, and render-entry sidecars carry the user-clicked onset position (a whole int64 source frame; the parser and engine intermediates are the only doubles) unchanged.

### Driver Geometry
The shared geometry contract — `N` and the synthesis hop `R_s = N/4` — lives in `engine_geometry.h` (`kN`, `kRs`); production `N = 4096` yields `R_s = 1024`. The engine consumes BOTH constants directly: `stft_container.h` pins `N = kN`, `M = 2N`, and `R_s = kRs` as compile-time constants (the runtime-N parameter, its per-init derivations, and its validator were removed 2026-07-17 — N and R_s stay symmetric, both from the geometry header). The orchestrator-side parser/prepost stages (`derive_phase_reset_frame_map`, `plan_trim`) still take `N` as an argument, and every driver hands them `kN`.

### Trim is a Prepost Stage
WHY TRIM EXISTS AT ALL (architect 2026-08-19), stated here once and pointed at from everywhere else it explains something. The trim window is there to MAKE A TARGET-VIEW SEGMENT RENDER FAST — "its purpose is to speed up target view segment renders" — so a passage can be iterated on at the cadence of a thought instead of the whole piece being synthesized again for every change. THE PIPELINE BELOW IS THAT MOTIVATION MADE STRUCTURAL rather than a crop bolted onto a finished render: `pre_trim` hands the engine a CUT SOURCE VIEW and rebased maps, so a narrower window is less audio to analyse and synthesize, and `post_trim` only pares the emission back to the exact authored bounds. WHAT IT EXPLAINS ELSEWHERE, each ruling already recorded in its own file and none of them arbitrary once this is in hand: the window is BAND rather than authored content and sits OUTSIDE UNDO, because it says where you are listening rather than what the piece is — which is also why it stays legal on a READ-ONLY tab, a lock protecting the musical content alone; the target-view audition ends at the preview buffer's end, which IS the window, so a shorter window shortens what you hear as well as what you wait for (source-view listening runs to the song's end and is not bounded by trim, playback having stopped reading the window on 2026-08-05 — normalization-and-boundaries.md owns that split); and `Home`/`End` jump to the bounds outside the history view, the window being the passage under work (`Ctrl+Home` / `Ctrl+End` force the piece's own ends there instead, 2026-08-24). THE COROLLARY THE `h` HISTORY VIEW TURNS ON: a view that auditions nothing and dispatches no render has no use for a window whose whole job is to shorten both, which is why the trim is frozen in there, why bare `[` is consumed and its button greys, and why the overlay is raised by no gesture in the view at all (github-recheck.md and region-scrub-esc.md carry that consequence).

Trim is a render window on the wav on EVERY road, the deliverable's included (accepted caveats: head phase is wrong until the first phase reset marker; the tail may fade over `R_s` multiples). This paragraph opened with a second, contradicting sentence until 2026-09-02 — "a transient inspection tool for auditioning a segment, not an archival deliverable" — and the two are reconciled to the one the code implements, the statement below (architect 2026-09-02, the truthfulness deep dive's item F, closed as is): the deliverable arm plans a sub-window like any other request, so a trimmed Ctrl+Alt+R lands the EXCERPT under the full title in `render/` and the Synchronize mirror carries that excerpt to the stick. It is truthful at the press — the trim bar shows the window the render will take — and silent afterwards, in the name, the folder and the mirror; it is the user's own act and it is accepted. The pipeline is: parser builds the FULL maps (knows nothing of trim) -> trimmer `pre_trim` for a PROPER SUB-WINDOW only (`plan_trim` in `src/prepost/trimmer.h` — the trimmer REQUIRES both bounds; under ALWAYS-SET trim (architect 2026-07-30) the store always holds a full ordered pair and the FULL WINDOW [0, total-1] takes NO TRIM PLAN AT ALL, rendering untrimmed through the shared predicate `trim_window_is_full` (settings_file.h) at all three orchestrators — a (0, total-1) pair through the trimmer would NOT be byte-identical to untrimmed, the crop fires and the authored end-wall drops the last frame's target span, so the translation is load-bearing — and it is load-bearing PRECISELY BECAUSE THE CROP IS END-EXCLUSIVE on an inclusive authored domain (recorded 2026-09-02, item K: the kept output is `[llrint(tgt(b)), llrint(tgt(e)))`, so the end frame the user authored is the first frame not rendered; the acceptance and its reasoning are at data-model.md's domain ruling and at `plan_trim`'s crop); lone bounds and the orchestrator completion are deleted with the unset state ((0, X) trims only the tail — zero head cut, the head-phase caveat vanishes — and (X, total) trims only the head, tail coinciding with the full render's): validates first — a refusal is interpreted by both orchestrators as "render untrimmed" with one stderr line, never a failed render — then cut source view + translated maps) -> engine (trim-ignorant, renders its map wholesale, emits its map's extent; for trimmed renders it also ADOPTS the plan's precomputed source-frame schedule — derived by `plan_trim` from the FULL map's own evaluations and translated by the integer cut, so schedule identity with the full render holds by construction rather than by re-interpolating the translated map; `EngineParams::source_frame_schedule`, count/order-validated, null on full renders) -> trimmer `post_trim` (crop to the exact authored window; `apply_post_trim` returns `expected` and REFUSES a crop the emission cannot supply — internal-breach policy, never zero-fill) -> peak limiter (unconditional; lifted out of the engine, lives in `src/prepost/`) -> encode. Full renders bypass both trimmer stages. The engine is buffer-out only; encode and the shared post-engine chain (`finish_render`, returning `FinishRenderStatus` with an optional cancel flag checked at stage boundaries — the GUI passes it, the CLI passes null) are orchestrator-side, one implementation compiled by both the GUI and `warptempo_cli`. The null condition (a trimmed render phase-inverts to null against the full render past the first phase reset, through the last kept sample) is carried by the hop-multiple target re-anchor plus the by-construction schedule; it is a PHASE/SYNTHESIS contract — the peak limiter when it engages, and the spectral limiter's nonlocal per-peak decision layer, sit outside it by architect ruling (recorded at `plan_trim`, whose comment block carries the full geometry derivation). Trim is a render window on the wav (the only product); the cache-dir framemap pair is always the FULL maps, untouched by trim.

AND THE FALLBACK SAYS SO NOW (architect 2026-09-02, deep dive item L): `Trim window too small to render; rendered untrimmed` on a NORMAL card (`kTrimFallbackCard`, messaging.md carries the full inventory entry). Only ONE of `plan_trim`'s refusals is reachable from a resting store — the TARGET span rounding below one output sample, a one-to-three-frame window under a fast tempo × scale — and until this ruling it answered with the worker's stderr line alone while the trim bar and the waveform overlay went on painting the hairline window that produced a full-length render. That is the success rule's "what shows would mislead", not a silence the strictness arc left standing; a MINIMUM TRIM SIZE was weighed and stays rejected on its own 2026-08-19 reasoning (normalization-and-boundaries.md's sweep section, where `kMinTrimSpanFrames`' retirement and the one argument worth keeping are recorded — nothing of it is retold here). The card has ONE VERDICT OWNER, the GUI-thread mirror `GuiTargetRender::compute_buffer_start_frame_for`, which the buffer anchor, the reuse rungs' stderr lines and both card producers all consume: the preview raises it at the top of `dispatch_render_now` on the FALLBACK-SUBJECT EDGE (every rung, one card per newly-too-small window): the whole record `{fell_back, tab, trim begin, trim end}` changing INTO a fallback, so a distinct tab or a distinct trim pair cards again even under a verdict that stays true, and a non-fallback dispatch re-stamps `fell_back = false` so re-entry into the same tab and pair cards again — `TrimFallbackSubject` in `target_render.h` is the contract. The two archival CHORDS raise it at the press. The two SWEEPS keep the stderr line alone — their per-cell markers describe maps the live verdict cannot see — and every stderr line in this section is unchanged.

### No Subprocesses in the Render Pipeline
The render path invokes no subprocesses. All DSP runs in-process via the engine sources. THE RULING IS ABOUT THE PATH AND NOT ABOUT THE PRODUCT: the GUI does spawn children, and every one of them is OUTSIDE this path — TWO since 2026-08-28: the GitHub recheck's synchronous `git` runners and the load-in-place's `gio trash` (the `l` command's `posix_spawnp` of the external `audio_player` was a third until the in-app render player retired that key whole, render-player.md; the score video's mpv was a fourth from 2026-08-20 until the 2026-08-21 sunset removed it). The roster and the signal dispositions they share live at `main.cpp`'s two `std::signal` calls; what matters here is that no render reaches any of them. The former `adapters/` reference folder was deleted from the repo 2026-07-15; git history preserves it.


### Render Dispatch Kills the Running Render
A render dispatch kills the running render, unconditionally (the former session-fingerprint match-wait — where a dispatch identical to the running render waited and adopted instead of killing — was removed as over-engineering; a preview that would have matched now simply re-derives through the reuse rungs below). ARCHIVAL COMMANDS ARE TWO CHORDS SINCE 2026-08-02, not three: Ctrl+Alt+R carries BOTH single render and iteration sweep, selected by the iteration-mode bit (mode on, it performs exactly what the retired Ctrl+Alt+I did — the same body, the same tmp/<N>_iterations batch, the same no-brackets / inverted-bracket / kMaxIterSweepCells refusals), and Ctrl+Alt+Shift+R is a CONSUMED NO-OP in iteration mode because archival is a plain-mode act; Ctrl+Alt+I is retired outright, unbound rather than refused (strict modifier validation makes an unbound chord structurally inert, so no refusal arm exists). THE SWEEP DISPATCHES FROM EITHER AUDIO VIEW since 2026-08-07, when the architect ruled ITERATION MODE TARGET-LEGAL: its source-view gate is DELETED (it was added by codex P1 bdf4336, 2026-07-22, because the success tail's `wipe_iter_state` writes the warp store off warp's home view — that write is now a GRANTED home-view-binding exception recorded at the tail, session-only fields reaching neither disk nor a render; the inventory lives in tempo-and-home-view.md), the S→T bracket wipe that made mode-off-in-target an invariant is deleted with it, and the bit alone selects the command from either view — which is what keeps the Render button's "Render grid iterations" face honest wherever it is lit. The sweep BODY needed no change and carries no view assumption: it enumerates the Cartesian product off the warp store into per-cell marker copies with the iter fields cleared, and a render is view-independent (the engine consumes authored source-domain markers; nothing in the request set reads `active_audio_view`). Archival commands (Ctrl+Alt+R, Ctrl+Alt+Shift+R — the miscellaneous single-cell render into tmp/<N>_miscellaneous, appending to the most recent misc folder or creating the next-index one; batch folders are N_iterations / N_bpm / N_miscellaneous — and the iteration and bpm sweeps) kill via the Esc-cancel semantics (request_cancel + queue_cancel_requested) and park the fully built request set in the one-slot `pending_archival` (newest command wins), pumped at worker idle AHEAD of the pending preview (a command outranks a derived preview; the preview defers behind the new session and re-derives after). Target-view preview triggers kill too (source view never triggers — archival renders run in the background there); the preview then re-derives at worker idle and may reuse an identical completed render through dispatch_render_now's two reuse rungs (render_cache — archival completions publish into it synchronously — then the on-disk artifact against its .fingerprint sidecar), avoiding a second synthesis when the inputs are unchanged; the reuse rungs are LOAD-BEARING for the unconditional-trigger model — undo/redo is a COMMON event and its dominant shape is A->B->A, which the cache absorbs (architect 2026-07-17: a proposed cache removal was rolled back for exactly this). THE FINGERPRINT'S PHASE-RESET COMPONENT IS TRIM-AWARE (2026-08-01, closing the spurious out-of-trim re-render the architect reported as the "Updating..." flash): the key serializes the reset set that ACTUALLY REACHES THE ENGINE — under a surviving trim plan, plan_trim's own translated range-filtered list (the trimmer's column asymmetry: point events outside the window filter away); a full window or a plan_trim refusal keys the full set, mirroring the untrimmed engine input. Both fingerprint sites take it — do_render's arm IS the plan optional itself (never a re-derived predicate), and compute_live_render_fingerprint mirrors by CALLING plan_trim over the memoized target-view map (no second arithmetic; a hand-rolled boundary margin would be the one unsafe direction, a wrong-bytes hit) — cross-site identity argued at the live site. THE VERSION BUMPED 18→19 with it (the codex round REFUTED the same-day no-bump decision: a pre-fix TRIMMED sidecar's raw-domain reset bytes could byte-collide with a post-fix key's translated bytes under identical version/flag/bounds while naming DIFFERENT engine input — old audio served for a different reset schedule through the sidecar comparison and both reuse rungs; the trim_window_is_full precedent covers byte-IDENTICAL reuse, not a re-domained field under the same spelling — accepted cost: a one-time cold cache, pre-bump entries and sidecars all unreachable); the per-keystroke plan_trim under a proper sub-window is the recorded accepted cost. So an out-of-window reset mutation (nudge, drop, delete, disable, drag commit, undo) keeps its fingerprint, hits the cache synchronously, and neither renders nor flashes. Batch-internal continuation is one session; Esc disarms the parked slot along with the running render; killed sessions may leave partial batch folders (accepted — tmp/ is transient, taken wholesale at a `'` load-in-place: MOVED TO THE DESKTOP TRASH when `gio trash` is available — the product's ONE trashed deletion, since these are user-visible artifacts — and DELETED otherwise, with one stderr line; the fallback is the detection, no probe and no setting, and every other deletion in the product stays a native remove). A FAILED RENDER TAKES ITS OWN OUTPUT WITH IT AND SAYS SO (architect 2026-09-02, closing the deep dive's item E — a failed archival render used to be invisible on screen, `finalize_render_run` clearing the state cell to the same blank a rung-served success shows). A `Failed` single archival completion on the DELIVERABLE arm removes the pair that render was to publish — `render/<title>.wav`, its `.fingerprint` and the staging sibling, every path composed from its own owner at DISPATCH (so a title edited in the settings editor mid-render cannot redirect the removal) — *"the missing audio is the clue"*: what survives a failure is stale BY CONSTRUCTION, since a render only reaches engine work past the up-to-date rung, so the standing wav no longer matches the authored state and the Synchronize mirror would ship it as if it did; silence plus a plausible wav is the shape that misleads, an absent wav cannot. A `Cancelled` completion is UNTOUCHED — the cancel is the user's own deliberate act and he knows the deliverable is the old one — and DELETION AT DISPATCH was weighed and not chosen (*"no need to overthink"*: a successful render replaces the pair anyway, and a cancel would then have destroyed it for nothing). Beside the removal, a NORMAL card `Render failed: <reason>` (messaging.md), carrying `do_render`'s own sentence: every `Failed` return in the pipeline composes its reason ONCE through the one `fail` helper, which prints the `Render error:` line and hands the same string back through `do_render`'s `failure_reason` out-parameter (`GuiAsyncRenderer` clears it per dispatch and carries it to the GUI thread beside the outcome), so the terminal and the card cannot drift; BOTH arms of the single dispatcher card, the deliverable's and the miscellaneous cell's. A FAILED BATCH CELL removes its own file set by the same rule — the wav, its staging sibling, the fingerprint and the three load-in-place sidecars, composed from the cell's folder and basename captured before the request was moved onto the worker — because a cell left behind claims a recipe it does not render, which `'` would apply and the player would offer; it raises no card of its own (a sweep runs up to 391 cells and answers once), and the batch's TAIL cards `Rendered N of M` when it came up short, a complete or cancelled sweep saying nothing. The BATCH FOLDER's own removal, when the whole sweep left it empty, is the TAIL's and not the failing cell's: `do_render` does not create a batch folder, so taking it away mid-sweep would fail every cell that followed. `warptempo_cli` gets none of this, beside the prune asymmetry below. A killed session can never publish Success: `do_render` checks the cancel flag before every final-name publication and after every expensive stage, `finish_render` takes the optional flag at stage boundaries (including after the buffer route's in-place PCM24 snap, before Completed), the limiter checks through its pre-queue phases and per-peak loop, and the engine re-checks the raw flag at pass boundaries and before its Success return. The target-buffer route gates twice more: `insert_master_floats` threads the per-dispatch session cancel token into the cache (the token is created fresh per dispatch and never reset, so the writer thread's own loads name exactly the dispatching session; the job drops at `start_writer_job`'s post-join check or at the writer thread's post-encode re-check, both before anything goes externally live) — and a final buffer-route check ahead of `finish_success` keeps a cancelled preview from ever binding its abandoned buffer. Every publication stages then gates: the reuse rungs re-check between the byte copy and the rename. (The cache-dir framemap pair is deliberately ungated — inert future-proofing files, not deliverables; a cancelled render's pair is swept.) Two accepted tails: once the wav has renamed to its final path, the sidecar/cache finalization completes — the publication gate sits before the rename, and finishing an already-published artifact's attestations beats deleting it — and a master-floats cache writer whose session cancel lands only after the writer's post-encode check completes its write (the entry is a complete, fingerprint-consistent render; Success is still refused). Batch completion no longer auto-opens anything (the 2026-07 render-VIEW player was removed, and with it the WAV-only auto-open and its `batch_.wav_browseable` gate): a finished batch just leaves its artifacts on disk. What audits them is THE RENDER PLAYER (architect 2026-08-28, render-player.md): bare `l` — or bare `'` outside the `h` view — opens the in-app player over `tmp/` on both backends (it walked `render/` too until 2026-09-01, when the architect moved the player inside `tmp/`: the deliverable is a naming-for-sharing convenience outside the workflow, carrying no sidecars and so unloadable in place), plays a cell through the product's own playback engine, and its Load in place button loads the highlighted cell in place. (The external player the `l` command used to spawn, and the typed `'` load editor, are both retired whole — `audio_player` with them.)

### The Two Output Folders
THE PROJECT ROOT HOLDS NO OUTPUT (architect 2026-08-27). IT WRITES NO `.fingerprint` EITHER, the CLI's SECOND asymmetry (recorded 2026-09-02, the truthfulness deep dive's item Q, record-only): the GUI publishes the deliverable wav together with its fingerprint sidecar, which is what lets a later dispatch recognize that deliverable as current and serve the up-to-date rung, while `warptempo_cli` publishes the wav alone — so a CLI deliverable can never prove itself current and the GUI re-renders after one.
