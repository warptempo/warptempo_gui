### Why it exists (design §0, his use cases)

THE CAR IS THE REASON. Parked, at night: open the player, see the project's renders, start one — without leaving the app and without Audacious (U1). Queue a variation series parked and audition it one after another while driving, WITHIN ONE FOLDER — *"in realistic use I would contain my work to one or the other folder anyway"* (U3). Play-scrub: tap a later point and the audio jumps there; grab the marker and drag, and the seek commits at the release (U4). Load a recipe deliberately from the player, with a confirmation (U6). DELIBERATELY UNSERVED, and the list is the design's own: a waveform for the played render; loops (*"advancing is not looping"*); auto-advance ACROSS folders; a nested tree with connector lines; folder navigation BETWEEN projects; arbitrary paths; a recent-files list; the stick as the car route (Bluetooth is); the sunset score video; a real second window (tried, dropped — Wayland trouble); a centred floating modal box (tried, dropped — *"flat, no drop shadow, small, blocked the whole screen"*).

### The model (R1, R2, R4, revised by R16 / R17, then by R36 / R38)

THE PLAYER NAVIGATES THE PROJECT'S OUTPUT FOLDERS THE REGULAR WAY — *"if we do it by folders in Open project, we may as well do it the regular way in the player too"*. The root lists `render` iff the deliverable folder holds THE CURRENT TITLE'S wav and `tmp` iff a batch cell exists; `tmp` lists its batch folders in the enumeration's own order; a batch lists its cells in that same order — the order `'` walks, reused as the play order; the deliverable folder lists AT MOST THAT ONE WAV.

`render` IS A ONE-FILE FOLDER, AND THE LISTING IS WHAT MAKES IT ONE (architect 2026-08-29 — *"player should only list a file if it matches the current title, and delete the rest also"*, closing the seam the week's review found: the player listed every wav in `render/` while Synchronize mirrors only the current `title=`'s wav and deletes the rest from the stick). `GuiRenderPlayer::deliverable_wav` is the one question — an `optional<path>`, the title's wav iff it is there as a regular file — and it PRUNES FIRST: `prune_render_folder` (renders_dir.h, the one owner, whose declaration carries the whole contract) deletes every regular `.wav` and `.fingerprint` in the folder whose stem is not the current title's. TWO CALLERS, and they are the two triggers the architect chose: this listing (which is also the opener's "is there anything to play" and the root row's question) and THE DELIVERABLE'S PUBLISH — the single archival render's GUI-thread completion, on Success and on the deliverable arm alone, after `do_render` has written the wav AND its fingerprint. A batch cell publishes into `tmp/`, a different folder, never pruned; `warptempo_cli` writes the same deliverable and gets no prune, the recorded asymmetry at the owner. WHAT THE ONE-FILE FOLDER NEEDS NOWHERE ELSE: the folder's play order, Previous / Next and the two folder ends are the degenerate one-item case of walks they already were, so no arm of theirs changed. The `..` row stands at the top of EVERY NON-ROOT listing and nowhere else (R13). A listing is built WHEN ITS FOLDER IS ENTERED and never kept fresh (R4): no cache, no watcher — a render that completes meanwhile shows on the next entry of that folder. `GuiRenderPlayer::rebuild_rows` is the one builder and `AppState::RenderPlayer::folder` (Root / Deliverable / Batches / Batch, the batch named by `batch_dir`) is where the listing is — never a free path.

**A CLICK ACTIVATES** (architect 2026-08-29, R39, superseding R16's *"I'm expecting to use click on the item and then press a button to play or to load or whatever"* of the day before): a single click or tap on a wav row PLAYS it from its start, on a folder row ENTERS it, on `..` goes UP — Plasma's single-click, which he runs, and GNOME's touch agree, and the same ruling governs the Open project picker's rows one section down. THE ACT RIDES THE MOTIONLESS LIFT: the press still only ARMS, because that same press may become the band's scroll drag and past the drag gate it IS the drag; the HIGHLIGHT moves onto the clicked row first and then the act runs, so what opens is what the band marks. THE HIGHLIGHT IS ONE FIELD (`folder_overlay.highlight_row`): the band AND the list's keyboard focus, one thing, and ENTER is the keyboard's own click on it while Up/Down walk it without opening anything. **THE DOUBLE-CLICK RETIRED** with the ruling — `DoubleClickSurface::FolderRow`, its motionless-release seed, its press-time consume and the listing rebuild's clear of that candidate are all deleted, no second meaning being left for a second press. There is no ctrl-click and no multi-selection here, ruled out with it.

THE TRANSPORT HAS THREE STATES AND PLAY/PAUSE ANSWERS THEM, NEVER THE HIGHLIGHT (architect 2026-08-29, R40 — *play when idle plays the current track*, Audacious's own answer, finishing what R36 began the evening before against R17's *"the play button plays whatever is highlighted"*). `AppState::RenderPlayer::transport` STORES the three — IDLE (nothing to resume: no item, or an item a STOP, a natural end or a fresh open left resting at its start), LIVE, PAUSED (an item the transport parked, AT WHATEVER FRAME) — and `play_button_act` is the fork whole:

| Transport | `Play`/`Pause`, Space, the car's Play or PlayPause |
|---|---|
| LIVE | PAUSE the item |
| PAUSED | RESUME the item |
| IDLE with an item | at the folder's END its folder's FIRST wav (R27), else the item from ITS START, ALWAYS — a seek while idle is a consumed no-op (`seek_to`'s own head, the one owner every road shares), so `resume_frame` is always 0 at an idle rest and every rest the transport's own acts leave it in (a STOP, a natural end, a fresh bind) plays from frame 0 |
| IDLE with no item | nothing |

R17 IS RETIRED WHOLE with this table: the highlighted-folder OPEN act the button carried (the car-stereo OK/Play convention) went with the highlight's other arms, because a tap opens a folder now.

PAUSED AT FRAME ZERO IS PAUSED, NOT IDLE, and that is why the state is a field rather than a reading of the resume point (2026-08-28, the same day's second correction): the tick's no-device arm parks a wav that never sounded a frame — start a wav with the device away, highlight another, let the device come back — and the next Play must resume THAT item rather than fall through to the highlight's row. `resume_frame` is the resume POINT alone; a seek moves it and moves no state. THE WRITERS ARE THREE CLASSES: LIVE at the two plays (`play_wav`, `toggle_pause`'s resume arm), PAUSED at THE ONE STOP BODY'S PLAYER FORK (`stop_playback_if_playing` — every live transport that stops passes there, the pause, the no-device pause, the natural end and the close alike, and it is that value's one writer), and IDLE at the four acts that mean "resting at the start" — `stop()`, the natural end's own rest, `open()` and `close()` — each written after that fork returns, `open()`'s reset being the one that never meets it (it runs with the mode down).

THE BUG THAT RULED IT, in his words: *"if I double-click a track to play it, then hit Next, playback advances to the next song but the highlight stays on the one I double-clicked. If I then hit the stop button (the Play/Pause button's live face), it actually triggers Play on the one that's highlighted, and then I can click it again to actually stop."* The live button is about what is SOUNDING, and the row the band happens to sit on has nothing to say about it.

THE TRANSPORT'S ITEM IS NOT THE HIGHLIGHT. It keeps playing while the listing is navigated elsewhere, its row wears the transport glyph, and Previous / Next / the two Shift+Page ENDS / AUTO-ADVANCE walk `item_folder` — THE WAV ROWS OF ITS FOLDER AS THEY WERE LISTED WHEN IT WAS PLAYED — never another folder and never a wrap (R2).

THE HIGHLIGHT FOLLOWS THE TRANSPORT'S ITEM (architect 2026-08-28, R38, superseding R17's *"Previous and Next never move the highlight"*): at every item change THE TRANSPORT MAKES ON ITS OWN — Previous, Next, the folder's ends, the auto-advance, the folder-end restart — the band moves onto the new item's row and scrolls it into view WHEN THAT ROW IS IN THE LISTING ON SCREEN; with the listing elsewhere (the user has walked into another folder) THE BAND STAYS WHERE IT IS. A user's own highlight moves are untouched — nothing drags the band back onto the item. Its ONE owner is `play_wav`, the one place the item changes, so the rule needs no membership list and the user's own plays get it for free (the row is already the highlight there) — AND THE SEAT THERE FIRES AT A CHANGE, not at every call: `play_wav` is re-entered on the item already resting there by the REPEAT ONE replay at every natural end, so an ungated seat would drag the band off the row a user walked to under a lit lamp, again at each replay and under whatever Load in place he pressed next. The gate is the incoming path against the item as it stood at the call's head; the replay itself keeps the full rebind (2026-08-29).

NOTHING LOOPS, WITH ONE SANCTIONED EXCEPTION — **REPEAT ONE** (architect 2026-08-28, R26; the row's lamp and bare `r`). It is a TWO-STATE toggle, off or repeat the ONE item: *"the user can just press play once the playlist finishes... repeat one is much more useful"*, which is why there is no repeat-all. While it stands the natural end REPLAYS THE ITEM FROM ITS START through the player's own play road — the same road a user's Play takes, so the item stays the transport's and the head unit is published at that edge as at any play. THE ARM IS TERMINAL, SOUNDED OR REFUSED: a replay that cannot decode (the file deleted or republished in another shape while it played) puts the decode vocabulary's own words on the status line and leaves the transport resting on that item at its start — it does not fall through to the next wav, and at the folder's last wav it does not set the folder-end bit under a lit lamp. Repeat ONE means this wav and nothing else, failure included. The state is SESSION-ONLY (`AppState::RenderPlayer::repeat_one`, false at every `open()`, serialized nowhere), no media command maps to it and `publish_media_state` publishes no repeat mode. The exception is recorded at the rule's other statements — `render_player.h`'s model, `playback_lifecycle.h`'s bounded audition, CLAUDE.md's two digests and HELP.

AT THE FOLDER'S LAST WAV, with the lamp off, the transport stops with the item resting at its start — and **THE NEXT PLAY STARTS THE FOLDER'S FIRST WAV** (R27, the car's Play at the end of a playlist) rather than replaying that last one. `AppState::RenderPlayer::ended_at_folder_end` is the one bit that says the transport is resting THERE: the natural end's own last-wav arm is its ONE writer (an advance that REFUSED to decode leaves it clear — the bit names the end, not a failure), every play, resume, seek, row open, open, close AND STOP clears it, and `play_button_act`'s TRANSPORT-ITEM arm is its one reader, so the act has one owner and the car's Play reaches it through the same Space as every other Play. The restart moves the band onto that first file, like every other item change the transport makes for itself (R38).

**STOP IS A BUTTON OF ITS OWN** (architect 2026-08-28, R36): *"one button that's either play or pause, and the other one is stop. So if the user is playing a track and wants to go back to the beginning of that track, they can hit stop and then play again. This is different from the live transport, because there we have the scrub, and a pause wouldn't make sense — the scrub always returns to the playhead when not playing."* THE ACT: the transport goes idle with the item resting AT ITS START — the resume point cleared to 0 and the folder-end bit with it — and THE ITEM STAYS THE TRANSPORT'S, so the next Play replays it from the beginning. A live transport takes the one stop body (the fence, the row's damage, the head unit's "paused" and the transport parked) and then writes IDLE over the PAUSED that fork leaves; a transport that is not sounding only moves its state and its rest. It is a consumed no-op with no item and on an item already resting at its start. `GuiRenderPlayer::stop` is the one body, reached by the button, by bare `s` and by the car's Stop.

### The surface (R3, R5, R9, R24, R31–R33, R35)

THE OVERLAY SITS WHERE THE ON-SCREEN KEYBOARD SITS — *"the overlay sits in the on-screen keyboard's place above the bottom strip, replacing the keyboard there"* (R3), *"the way the keyboard helps the bottom strip"*. THE BAND IS THE SLOT'S AND THE ROW IS THE BUTTON'S (R31/R33, the second pass the same day; the panel's first hours took both from the keyboard). The band takes the slot's x, its width and its BOTTOM EDGE — the bottom row's own lane, lifted, through the one owner `keyboard_slot_band` (app_state.h) that the keyboard's own `surface_rect` reads too — and ITS HEIGHT IS FIXED AT THE CEILING: from THE MIDDLE OF THE WAVEFORM (`keyboard_slot_max_height_px`, the waveform's own midpoint through the vertical stack's one geometry owner, which is the window's midpoint wherever the centering is feasible) down to the bottom row, EVERY TIME IT STANDS, whatever the listing's length — *"we should automatically make the height from the midpoint of the waveform down so that it's not a fluid height — it's always a fixed height"* (architect 2026-08-28, R35, that evening's plastic-and-glass pass). A SHORT LISTING LEAVES THE REST OF THE BAND AS GROUND and a long one scrolls. R35 retired the growing half of R33 (*"from the bottom strip up to the middle of the waveform... it grows with its content up to that cap, then scrolls"* — which had itself replaced a fixed four-row band that read too short against his dummy folders) and kept R33's ceiling untouched; the content's own height is the SCROLL CLAMP's input and nothing else's. THE ROW IS EXACTLY THE ICON ROW'S BUTTON — the same 32 px box, the same 2 px gap between boxes, the same corner radius and the same 22 px glyph seated at the box's own `(32-22)/2` inset, every number read from render.h's icon-button block (*"we've gone for the button analogy"*; *"the buttons are good enough size for my finger"*) — and A NAME TOO LONG FOR THE LINE RUNS OFF THE EDGE, no wrap and no ellipsis (*"project and file names will be short"*), the painter clipping it to the row. The panel's one authored number of its own is a 2 px outer inset, pcmanfm-qt's 1 px item margin grown with the taller rows. THE COLOURS ARE THE FILE MANAGER'S (R32, kdenlive's project bin and pcmanfm-qt's compact view, which agree): the band's ground is `kModalFieldGround` #141618 and NOTHING ALTERNATES; a resting row paints no fill at all; HOVER is `kFolderRowHover` (the accent at the 0.3 click mix over that ground — the derivation ships, and it reproduces the architect's #204357 within one 8-bit step on the green) under a 1 px `kFolderRowHoverOutline` #3694c5 frame; the HIGHLIGHT is `kRedesignAccent` #3daee9; HOVERED + HIGHLIGHTED is `kFolderRowHoverSelected` #44bfff; the ink is `kRedesignLabel` white in every face, and a live press arm wears the highlight's own fill. THE TWO NEVER BOTH STAND, STRUCTURALLY: the overlay's owners — the player and the picker — are modes and not editors, each opener refuses under every editor, each router consumes every editor opener and each veil consumes every pointer press that could raise one, so the keyboard's editor term is false whenever the band stands. (`onscreen_keyboard::stands` carried `!folder_overlay_stands(a)` as a THIRD term for the afternoon the Open prompt kept a text editor under the picker's band; the term lost its producer with that field and was DELETED — a gate term exists iff a producer exists — with the record at the predicate.) R3's *"neither use needs typing"* is literal now on every content. `waveform_paint_area` gates on EITHER TENANT and clips on THE STANDING ONE'S OWN RECT: one gate, one clip, and the waveform is not painted where the slot paints — a real fork since the two tenants' heights parted, the overlay's being the ceiling and the keyboard's its four key rows. THE PANEL HAS ONE RECT, which is what the fixed height bought: the band a listing rebuild must erase IS the band every other damage covers, so `folder_overlay::band_damage_rect` — the band at its ceiling, whose single reader was that rebuild — went with the growing band that gave it its reason (a rect exists iff it answers a question, and the two answers became the same rect). The slot's SHOW/HIDE comparator (main.cpp) still takes `onscreen_keyboard::slot_damage_rect`, the taller of the two tenants' bands, neither of which it can ask for a height at the moment it fires. The as-painted bit is the SLOT'S (`AppState::keyboard_slot_painted_standing`, written only by `GuiPaintHandler::paint_keyboard_slot` on a rect that fully covers the STANDING TENANT'S band — the slot's tallest where neither stands, that being the frame the hide's own damage draws), not either tenant's. AND THE BAND YIELDS THE TOUCH PAN ZONE the way the keyboard's does (`touch_point_in_pan_zone`): the panel paints over the waveform, whose whole area is that zone, and both of the band's gestures live on the POINTER — the row press and the scroll drag that same arm becomes — so without the carve-out a finger crossing the slop on a row would resolve to the phone-model pan and a held one to the region former, and the list would answer a tap and nothing else.

UNLIKE THE KEYBOARD IT SCROLLS: `folder_overlay.scroll_px` is THE PRODUCT'S FIRST SCROLL OFFSET — a pixel offset clamped through the one owner `folder_overlay::clamp_scroll`, reset to 0 at every rebuild, stepped ONE ROW PER WHEEL DETENT (wheel context 4, live over the band alone and swallowed everywhere else), carried by the band's own drag on glass, and moved by `scroll_row_into_view` so a walked highlight stays visible. No header row, no columns, no scrollbar. PAINT AND HIT CANNOT DRIFT: both walk the one `folder_overlay::for_each_row`, and `row_at` answers −1 for any point outside the band whatever a scrolled row's rect would contain.

ROW GLYPHS: `icons::Icon::Folder` (Breeze places/22/folder, what pcmanfm-qt and kdenlive's Open dialog paint) on folder rows AND on the `..` row (it names a folder), `icons::Icon::AudioXWav` (Breeze mimetypes/22/audio-x-wav, R14 — `audio-x-generic` stays the program's logo alone) on wav rows, swapped for `MediaPlaybackStart` on the transport's item's row (the bottom row's one-button-two-faces precedent). Both icons are fresh verbatim transcriptions; the roster is 51.

THE ROWS ARE CHROME (conventions.md's third clause). A row press ARMS — the same press may still become the band's scroll drag, so its identity is not certain — and a MOTIONLESS LIFT HIGHLIGHTS THE ROW AND THEN OPENS IT (R39). Past THE DRAG GATE — `drag_moved_threshold_px`, the product's one 8-authored-px crossing, asked here exactly as at every other pending press: CHEBYSHEV from the press (`max(|dx|,|dy|) >= threshold`), so it crosses on EITHER AXIS and at the same `>=` the input core resolves a touch at (the twin-gate invariant, input_core.h) — the arm IS the scroll drag and its act is gone; once a drag, always a drag. A purely SIDEWAYS crossing therefore scrolls nothing and ends with no act, which is the point: a slide across a row is not a click, on glass or on plastic. The panel has NO double-click surface: `DoubleClickSurface::FolderRow` was the fifth for one day (2026-08-28) and went with R39, taking its seed, its press-time consume and the listing rebuild's clear of the shared candidate — that clear existed because the candidate named a row INDEX and a rebuild renames every index, and no other surface's target names a row of a table the app rewrites, so the mutator-side clear left with the surface that needed it. A shift or ctrl press on a row is a consumed no-op and nothing on a row reads `kHoldBeatMs`: the design's long-press load (R10) was RETIRED BEFORE IT WAS BUILT by R18, and R43 confirms nothing of it ever landed — THE PLAYER'S LOAD IS THE BUTTON OR `'` ON THE HIGHLIGHT and nothing else. (THE MODAL ROW below is where the mode's one hold and its one admitted modifier live since R37 — the two skips' shifted twin — and the LIST is untouched by it.)

NOTHING POPS MID-GESTURE HERE EITHER. The row press's arm and the scrub's marker drag are MEMBERS OF `any_pointer_gesture_active`, the product's one "some pointer gesture is in flight" predicate, so the player's two arms hold the keyboard, the wheel and the cursor exactly as a marker or trim drag does: `on_key`'s player block asks that predicate itself and SWALLOWS every key while an arm stands — Esc included, so the mode cannot close under a held press, and Enter and Space cannot open a row or start a sound under one — with THE ONE HATCH the drag-modal gate's own modifier-exact Ctrl+Q, which falls through to the close road that takes the player down and drops both arms; `wheel_context` answers −1 for the same reason, so the band cannot scroll under a live arm; and the cursor map answers Arrow.

### The modal row: the bottom strip's THIRD and FOURTH owners

`ModalDialogOwner` is `{None, Prompt, Editor, Player, Picker}`. The player's transport row takes the lane whole, with its own session id from the one modal counter, and `modal_dialog_live_session()` ranks prompt > player | picker > editor. A PROMPT STILL OUTRANKS BOTH (the load confirmation and the reopen's unsaved-tab question paint over their own rows exactly as the quit prompt paints over an editor); the player, the picker and an editor are NEVER LIVE TOGETHER — each opener refuses under the others and each router consumes every other opener — so the order among the three lower ranks is free. THE PICKER (2026-08-28, R22/R23) is the FIELD-LESS modal over the overlay's list: state `AppState::Picker` (one bit and a session id; the overlay's owner tag says which content), and ITS ROW IS **Cancel** AND NOTHING ELSE since 2026-08-29 (R44) — no label, no field, no OK, because a click on a row IS the open act and Enter on the list is the same act from the keyboard. Its one-day OK was NAMED BY THE ACT it ran (**Open** on the project picker, **Load in place** on the history picker) and went with the row-click ruling, taking the picker's share of the editor dialogs' OK bit with it: `dispatch_modal_dialog_button`'s picker arm reads no bit and runs the one close body. Cancel stays LAST, which on a one-button row makes it the escape sentinel by construction, and its tooltip is the composer's "Cancel (Escape)", exactly the picker router's key for it.

**THE ALIGNMENT RULE** (architect 2026-08-29, R47; recorded at `paint_modal_dialog`'s layout block and in its picker branch, and in conventions.md): A MODAL THAT CARRIES A FIELD OR A MESSAGE SITS FLUSH LEFT WITH IT — the prompts, the four dialog editors and the player, whose transport is that row's left-hand content — WHILE A LIST'S BUTTONS SIT FLUSH RIGHT: *"if my hand is on the left edge to type, the buttons should be right there; if I'm using the picker my hand stays on the right"* (and *"I am right-handed"*, 2026-08-28, on seeing the picker on the tablet). So the picker's one button starts at `buttons_x_max`, the rightmost seat every other owner uses as its CAP, with nothing to its left.

THE ROW, left to right (R25, the relaid row of his first plastic pass; it opened as Previous · Play/Pause · Next · Load in place · Close, then the clock, then the scrub — and R36 that evening put STOP after Play/Pause, Audacious's own order): **Previous · Play/Pause · Stop · Next** · SEPARATOR · **the PLAY-SCRUB** · **the CLOCK** · SEPARATOR · **Repeat one** ......... **Load in place · Close**, the last two FLUSH RIGHT. The arrangement is Audacious-Qt's under Breeze Dark — *"not the screenshot as a whole, just the layout: transport, separator, scrub, then the time, then another separator, then the repeat button"*. CLOSE IS LAST, the escape sentinel by construction as on every prompt, and the plan's order is the RING's, so Tab walks the row left to right.

FIVE GLYPH BUTTONS AND TWO WORD ONES, and the faces fork on exactly that. A glyph button is the roster's 22 px icon centred in the modal's 32 px box — `MediaSkipBackward` / `MediaPlaybackStart` swapped to `MediaPlaybackPause` while the transport is live (R36 gave the row a Stop button, so the two-faced one wears a real PAUSE glyph instead of the stop square it had borrowed; the ROSTER's transport button is untouched, bare Space over the project's audio being one toggle with no pause state) / `MediaPlaybackStop` on Stop itself / `MediaSkipForward`, and `MediaRepeatSingle` in both of the lamp's states (R30: *"a plain toggle — off is the unpressed face, on is the pressed/lit face"*, so the glyph never changes; the roster is 51, media-playback-pause being one fresh verbatim transcription) — and IT WEARS NO RESTING OUTLINE (R25: *"icon buttons have no border — the regular transport has none; text buttons keep theirs"*), which makes the player's transport read as the transport it replaces. Load in place and Close keep the word box and its resting `kRedesignLine` frame. Everything else about the faces is the modal ladder unforked, in ONE body: hover, the live press, the two-strength focus ring with its halo, the feint, the arm-at-press / act-at-lift rule and the 700 ms tooltip dwell — plus the SELECTED face the row's one lamp brought with it (`kRedesignSelectedFill` under the resting line, the roster's own, ranked under the press and over the focus fill, because the fill says the STATE and the outline says who is claiming the button). The tooltips are `render_player_button_hint` in the roster's `"<word> (<key>)"` form — "Previous (Page Up)", "Play (Space)" / "Pause (Space)" (the two-faced button names the face it wears), "Stop (s)", "Next (Page Down)", "Repeat one (r)", "Load in place (')", "Close (Escape)" — derived from the act the button dispatches, never hand-listed. `PlayerButtonAct` is the third dispatch vocabulary beside a prompt's response key and an editor's OK bit.

THE TWO SKIPS ADMIT A MODIFIED PRESS (architect 2026-08-28, R37: *"make sure shift-enabled gestures have their corresponding button allow long press, and update their tooltips"*) — the roster's own shift-click / long-press pair over this surface, and the row's first: a SHIFT+CLICK on plastic and a press HELD past `kChromeShiftHoldMs` on glass both run the button's SHIFTED TWIN, the item folder's FIRST / LAST wav. ### The engine road

THE ITEM IS A WAV PLAYED AS IT IS. THE DECODE VOCABULARY, in order, every refusal its own words on the status line with the item unchanged: `wav_probe` → the RATE AND CHANNEL EQUALITY against the DEVICE ("Not the source's rate and channel count" — the engine was `init`ed with the source's and nothing in the tree resamples, so this is a refusal and never a conversion; a render of this project matches by construction) → `checked_audio_sample_count` on the probed shape → `wav_read_full` → THE SAME EQUALITY AGAIN, on the DECODED buffer's own `WavInfo`, in the same words → "Empty wav". The frozen `audio_io` reader is CALLED, NEVER CHANGED — no frozen touch in this arc.

THE EQUALITY IS ASKED TWICE BECAUSE THE PROBE'S ANSWER IS STALE BY CONSTRUCTION: `wav_read_full` REOPENS the path, and every writer in this tree publishes by rename (the render's own staging, the Synchronize act, wts), so the file it decoded need not be the one the probe read. The probe stays as the CHEAP EARLY REFUSAL — it is what lets the allocation policy answer off a header before any payload is read — and THE POST-DECODE CHECK IS WHAT THE BIND RESTS ON, the decoded info being the buffer describing itself: a mono or other-rate buffer under the engine's stride would be read past its end or played at the wrong speed. It is a SHAPE check and not an IDENTITY one (unlike the render cache's post-decode re-stat, which holds a fingerprint and must prove the bytes are that artifact's): any wav at the path is a legitimate item, and all the player needs is that what it binds fits the device.

THEN THE FENCE, THEN THE BIND: `stop_playback_if_playing` (the one stop body, whose `GuiPlayback::stop()` is the quiescence proof `rebind_buffer` requires) before the old buffer is released and the new one bound at offset 0.

THE SECOND LAUNCH BODY, recorded here and at its own head. `GuiPlaybackLifecycle::launch_playback_window` is the ONE launch body for THE PROJECT'S AUDIO and the player deliberately does not use it: everything that body seeds — the A/B audition clear, the playable gate against the project's domain, the scanner, the follow-scroll, the waveform damage — belongs to the WAVEFORM, which the player does not display. So `play_wav` / `toggle_pause` / `seek_to` call `playback.play` directly against the item's own `[0, frames)`; the project's resting playhead never moves, its scanner never runs, and `render_player.transport` in its LIVE value is the player's own mirror of `playhead_scanner_active`. THE STOP IS STILL THE ONE STOP BODY: every pause, natural end and close takes `stop_playback_if_playing`, which carries the player's fork inside it (the fence, then the transport moved to PAUSED and the modal row damaged instead of the scanner teardown), so the keyboard stop rule and the fence-before-rebind ordering hold by construction.

A PAUSE reads the resume point from the engine BEFORE the stop; a RESUME at or past the item's end replays from the start (a user act, not a loop). A LIVE SEEK is the engine's keep-alive shape — `play()` over a live session, the window unchanged — stopping one frame short of the exclusive end so the natural end takes over rather than firing a one-frame impulse; a PAUSED seek moves the resume point alone. AN IDLE SEEK IS A CONSUMED NO-OP (architect 2026-08-29 ~01:40, Audacious's own stopped slider): `seek_to`'s head refuses before either branch, the one owner every road — Left/Right, Home, the car's absolute SeekTo, the scrub — shares, so `resume_frame` never leaves 0 while the transport rests. `render_player_position` is the ONE reader class for the clock, the scrub, the press router and the seeks: the engine's cursor while live, the resume point otherwise.

NO RENDER IS DISPATCHED BY THE PLAYER, EVER. A running render continues in the background, and a target preview that completes meanwhile does NOT rebind under the player (`GuiTargetRender::complete_successful_buffer`'s guard) — the close's re-express binds it. The tick is forked at `on_tick`'s head: while the mode stands the player's tick takes the natural end (through the one stop body) and the per-position damage of the two published cells, and nothing below that line applies; the pre-paint scanner sampling returns for the same reason (the engine's cursor is the item's, not the project's).

### What is deliberately not built

DELIBERATELY NOT, and the ruling is recorded at the Java sliver's class comment and in platform-seam.md: no notification, no foreground service, no background playback, no lock-screen transport — the tablet is a kiosk on a stand with the app in the foreground and the head unit reading the session over AVRCP, so the manifest gains no `<service>`, no `FOREGROUND_SERVICE*` / `POST_NOTIFICATIONS`, no `res/`.

THE FRAMEWORK'S DEFAULT `onMediaButtonEvent` IS NOT OURS, and that is why the toggle has a kind. The default holds a `KEYCODE_MEDIA_PLAY_PAUSE` press for `ViewConfiguration.getDoubleTapTimeout()` to see whether a second press follows and turns two quick presses into `onSkipToNext` while `ACTION_SKIP_TO_NEXT` is declared — a delay between the wheel button and the sound with nothing on screen to explain it, and a double-tap-to-Next gesture nobody ruled, Next being its own button on the wheel. So the sliver OVERRIDES it and maps the keycodes at once (the mechanics are platform-seam.md's), the undivided key going down as `PlayPause` and `KEYCODE_HEADSETHOOK` with it, which is what gave the enumerator a producer and put it back in the numbered table (2026-08-28, after one day deleted for having none). `ACTION_PLAY_PAUSE` stays DECLARED — the routing dispatches a key only for a declared action, the override's keys included.

AND THE STICK IS STILL NOT THE CAR ROUTE — Bluetooth is (the deliberately-unserved list above). The SAF road for the OTG stick is Synchronize's contingency, not the player's; it needs an architect ruling (settings.md's Projects section, platform-seam.md).
