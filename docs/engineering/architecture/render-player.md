Landed in `b86f74e1`, `069a2445` and `8308def1`; the car's MediaSession (§3) followed the same day in `3ceb01c2` — the mechanism is platform-seam.md's car section, this file's territory being what the commands MEAN once they arrive — and the OPEN PROJECT PICKER, the overlay's second content, the same day after it. The code's own contracts are at the head of `src/gui/render_player.h` (the model, the decode, the second launch body, enter/leave) and `src/gui/folder_overlay.h` (the panel), and this file agrees with them by construction — where they disagree the headers win.

### Why it exists (design §0, his use cases)

THE CAR IS THE REASON. Parked, at night: open the player, see the project's renders, start one — without leaving the app and without Audacious (U1). Queue a variation series parked and audition it one after another while driving, WITHIN ONE FOLDER — *"in realistic use I would contain my work to one or the other folder anyway"* (U3). Play-scrub: tap a later point and the audio jumps there; grab the marker and drag, and the seek commits at the release (U4). Load a recipe deliberately from the player, with a confirmation (U6). The same auditioning on the LAPTOP, in-app rather than spawned (U5). And the bottom-strip modal STAYS the dialog machinery (U8): the history view's typed load-in-place rides it and *"untangling that will be complicated"*, so the overlay FEEDS the existing modal instead of replacing it.

DELIBERATELY UNSERVED, and the list is the design's own: a waveform for the played render; loops (*"advancing is not looping"*); auto-advance ACROSS folders; a nested tree with connector lines; folder navigation BETWEEN projects; arbitrary paths; a recent-files list; the stick as the car route (Bluetooth is); the sunset score video; a real second window (tried, dropped — Wayland trouble); a centred floating modal box (tried, dropped — *"flat, no drop shadow, small, blocked the whole screen"*).

### The model (R1, R2, R4, revised by R16 / R17)

THE PLAYER NAVIGATES THE PROJECT'S OUTPUT FOLDERS THE REGULAR WAY — *"if we do it by folders in Open project, we may as well do it the regular way in the player too"*. The root lists `render` iff the deliverable folder holds a wav and `tmp` iff a batch cell exists; `tmp` lists its batch folders in the enumeration's own order; a batch lists its cells in that same order — the order `'` walks, reused as the play order; the deliverable folder lists its `*.wav` files in byte order of their names. The `..` row stands at the top of EVERY NON-ROOT listing and nowhere else (R13). A listing is built WHEN ITS FOLDER IS ENTERED and never kept fresh (R4): no cache, no watcher — a render that completes meanwhile shows on the next entry of that folder. `GuiRenderPlayer::rebuild_rows` is the one builder and `AppState::RenderPlayer::folder` (Root / Deliverable / Batches / Batch, the batch named by `batch_dir`) is where the listing is — never a free path.

A SINGLE CLICK ON A ROW HIGHLIGHTS IT AND DOES NOTHING ELSE (R16, the revision after the design's first pass: *"I'm expecting to use click on the item and then press a button to play or to load or whatever"* — the flags are already double-click, and single-click-to-open was pcmanfm-qt's habit, not the product's). THE HIGHLIGHT IS ONE FIELD (`folder_overlay.highlight_row`): the band AND the list's keyboard focus, one thing. OPENING a row is the flags' own DOUBLE-CLICK (double-tap on glass) or ENTER: a folder ENTERS, `..` goes UP, a wav PLAYS FROM ITS START.

THE PLAY BUTTON (and Space) PLAYS WHATEVER IS HIGHLIGHTED (R17): a highlighted wav that is not the transport's item plays from its start; a highlighted folder or `..` takes THE OPEN ACT — the car-stereo OK/Play convention, so glass never needs a double-tap to navigate; on the transport's own item it pauses / resumes. `GuiRenderPlayer::play_button_act` is that fork whole.

THE TRANSPORT'S ITEM IS NOT THE HIGHLIGHT. It keeps playing while the listing is navigated elsewhere, its row wears the transport glyph, and Previous / Next / AUTO-ADVANCE walk `item_folder` — THE WAV ROWS OF ITS FOLDER AS THEY WERE LISTED WHEN IT WAS PLAYED — never another folder and never a wrap (R2). At the folder's last wav the transport stops with the item resting at its start, and a following Play replays it, which is a user act: NOTHING LOOPS holds here as everywhere. Previous and Next never move the highlight.

### The surface (R3, R5, R9, R24, R31–R33)

THE OVERLAY SITS WHERE THE ON-SCREEN KEYBOARD SITS — *"the overlay sits in the on-screen keyboard's place above the bottom strip, replacing the keyboard there"* (R3), *"the way the keyboard helps the bottom strip"*. THE BAND IS THE SLOT'S AND THE ROW IS THE BUTTON'S (R31/R33, the second pass the same day; the panel's first hours took both from the keyboard). The band takes the slot's x, its width and its BOTTOM EDGE — the bottom row's own lane, lifted, through the one owner `keyboard_slot_band` (app_state.h) that the keyboard's own `surface_rect` reads too — and its HEIGHT IS ITS CONTENT'S UP TO A CEILING: it grows a row at a time until it reaches THE MIDDLE OF THE WAVEFORM (`keyboard_slot_max_height_px`, the waveform's own midpoint through the vertical stack's one geometry owner, which is the window's midpoint wherever the centering is feasible), and past that it stops growing and scrolls — *"from the bottom strip up to the middle of the waveform... it grows with its content up to that cap, then scrolls"*, after the fixed four-row band read too short against his dummy folders. THE ROW IS EXACTLY THE ICON ROW'S BUTTON — the same 32 px box, the same 2 px gap between boxes, the same corner radius and the same 22 px glyph seated at the box's own `(32-22)/2` inset, every number read from render.h's icon-button block (*"we've gone for the button analogy"*; *"the buttons are good enough size for my finger"*) — and A NAME TOO LONG FOR THE LINE RUNS OFF THE EDGE, no wrap and no ellipsis (*"project and file names will be short"*), the painter clipping it to the row. The panel's one authored number of its own is a 2 px outer inset, pcmanfm-qt's 1 px item margin grown with the taller rows. THE COLOURS ARE THE FILE MANAGER'S (R32, kdenlive's project bin and pcmanfm-qt's compact view, which agree): the band's ground is `kModalFieldGround` #141618 and NOTHING ALTERNATES; a resting row paints no fill at all; HOVER is `kFolderRowHover` (the accent at the 0.3 click mix over that ground — the derivation ships, and it reproduces the architect's #204357 within one 8-bit step on the green) under a 1 px `kFolderRowHoverOutline` #3694c5 frame; the HIGHLIGHT is `kRedesignAccent` #3daee9; HOVERED + HIGHLIGHTED is `kFolderRowHoverSelected` #44bfff; the ink is `kRedesignLabel` white in every face, and a live press arm wears the highlight's own fill. THE TWO NEVER BOTH STAND: `onscreen_keyboard::stands` carries `!folder_overlay_stands(a)` as its THIRD term, and THE PICKER IS THAT TERM'S PRODUCER — it stands over the Open prompt's editor, so on glass that prompt shows the LIST and not a keyboard, which is R3's *"neither use needs typing"* made structural. Under the player the term never decides, the mode raising no text editor at all (its confirmation is a PROMPT). `waveform_paint_area` gates on EITHER TENANT and clips on THE STANDING ONE'S OWN RECT: one gate, one clip, and the waveform is not painted where the slot paints — a real fork since the two tenants' heights parted, the overlay's being its listing's and the keyboard's its four key rows. THE BAND'S DAMAGE HAS TWO RECTS FOR THE SAME REASON: a damage INSIDE a standing band (the scroll, the highlight, one row's face) takes the band as it stands, while a LISTING REBUILD takes `folder_overlay::band_damage_rect` — the band at its ceiling — because the panel grows upward from a fixed bottom edge and a shorter listing would otherwise leave the departed rows painted above the new band; the slot's SHOW/HIDE comparator (main.cpp) takes `onscreen_keyboard::slot_damage_rect`, the taller of the two tenants' bands, neither of which it can ask for a height at the moment it fires. The as-painted bit is the SLOT'S (`AppState::keyboard_slot_painted_standing`, written only by `GuiPaintHandler::paint_keyboard_slot` on a rect that fully covers the STANDING TENANT'S band — the slot's tallest where neither stands, that being the frame the hide's own damage draws), not either tenant's. AND THE BAND YIELDS THE TOUCH PAN ZONE the way the keyboard's does (`touch_point_in_pan_zone`): the panel paints over the waveform, whose whole area is that zone, and both of the band's gestures live on the POINTER — the row press and the scroll drag that same arm becomes — so without the carve-out a finger crossing the slop on a row would resolve to the phone-model pan and a held one to the region former, and the list would answer a tap and nothing else.

UNLIKE THE KEYBOARD IT SCROLLS: `folder_overlay.scroll_px` is THE PRODUCT'S FIRST SCROLL OFFSET — a pixel offset clamped through the one owner `folder_overlay::clamp_scroll`, reset to 0 at every rebuild, stepped ONE ROW PER WHEEL DETENT (wheel context 4, live over the band alone and swallowed everywhere else), carried by the band's own drag on glass, and moved by `scroll_row_into_view` so a walked highlight stays visible. No header row, no columns, no scrollbar. PAINT AND HIT CANNOT DRIFT: both walk the one `folder_overlay::for_each_row`, and `row_at` answers −1 for any point outside the band whatever a scrolled row's rect would contain.

ROW GLYPHS: `icons::Icon::Folder` (Breeze places/22/folder, what pcmanfm-qt and kdenlive's Open dialog paint) on folder rows AND on the `..` row (it names a folder), `icons::Icon::AudioXWav` (Breeze mimetypes/22/audio-x-wav, R14 — `audio-x-generic` stays the program's logo alone) on wav rows, swapped for `MediaPlaybackStart` on the transport's item's row (the bottom row's one-button-two-faces precedent). Both icons are fresh verbatim transcriptions; the roster is 49.

THE ROWS ARE CHROME (conventions.md's third clause). A row press ARMS — the same press may still become the band's scroll drag, so its identity is not certain — and a MOTIONLESS LIFT highlights and seeds the double-click candidate (press coordinates, release stamp: the marker seed's own split). Past the vertical drag gate (`drag_moved_threshold_px`, the product's one 8-authored-px crossing) the arm IS the scroll drag and its act is gone; once a drag, always a drag. THE ONE PRESS WHOSE IDENTITY IS CERTAIN — a recognized double-click's second press — acts AT THE PRESS and arms nothing, because the act may rebuild the listing under the pointer. `DoubleClickSurface::FolderRow` is the FIFTH surface (2026-08-28, born on the motionless-release seed timing the other four reached in 2026-08-15). A shift or ctrl press on a row is a consumed no-op and nothing on a row reads `kHoldBeatMs`: the design's long-press load (R10) was RETIRED BEFORE IT WAS BUILT by R18.

NOTHING POPS MID-GESTURE HERE EITHER. The row press's arm and the scrub's marker drag are MEMBERS OF `any_pointer_gesture_active`, the product's one "some pointer gesture is in flight" predicate, so the player's two arms hold the keyboard, the wheel and the cursor exactly as a marker or trim drag does: `on_key`'s player block asks that predicate itself and SWALLOWS every key while an arm stands — Esc included, so the mode cannot close under a held press, and Enter and Space cannot open a row or start a sound under one — with THE ONE HATCH the drag-modal gate's own modifier-exact Ctrl+Q, which falls through to the close road that takes the player down and drops both arms; `wheel_context` answers −1 for the same reason, so the band cannot scroll under a live arm; and the cursor map answers Arrow. A LISTING REBUILD CLEARS THE DOUBLE-CLICK SEED (`rebuild_rows`, the mutator's own line): a `FolderRow` candidate names a row INDEX, and the listing the rebuild installs renames every index, so a second press must not pair with a first taken on the listing before it. Every other road into a rebuild happens to pass a chokepoint that clears the candidate anyway, which is the callers' accident and not a contract the listing rests on.

### The modal row: the bottom strip's THIRD owner

`ModalDialogOwner` is `{None, Prompt, Editor, Player}`. The player's transport row takes the lane whole, with its own session id from the one modal counter, and `modal_dialog_live_session()` ranks prompt > player > editor. A PROMPT STILL OUTRANKS IT (its own load confirmation paints over its row exactly as the quit prompt paints over an editor); the player and an editor are NEVER LIVE TOGETHER — the opener refuses under any editor and the key router consumes every editor opener — so the order between those two is free.

THE ROW, left to right: **Previous · Play/Pause · Next · Load in place · Close**, then the CLOCK, then the PLAY-SCRUB across the free width (R5, revised by R18, which put Load in place on the row). The three transport buttons are GLYPH buttons — the roster's 22 px icon centred in the modal's 32 px box, `MediaSkipBackward` / `MediaPlaybackStart` swapped to `MediaPlaybackStop` while the transport is live / `MediaSkipForward` — the first glyph buttons the modal row has had; Load in place and Close keep the word box every prompt and editor button has always had. CLOSE IS LAST, the escape sentinel by construction as on every prompt. THE BUTTONS STAY WHOLE AND THE SCRUB GIVES, the row's own primacy rule. Faces, the two-strength focus ring, the feint, the arm-at-press / act-at-lift rule and the 700 ms tooltip dwell are the modal's, unforked; the tooltips are `render_player_button_hint` in the roster's own `"<word> (<key>)"` form — "Previous (Page Up)", "Play (Space)" / "Pause (Space)" (the two-faced button names the face it wears), "Next (Page Down)", "Load in place (')", "Close (Escape)" — derived from the act the button dispatches, never hand-listed. `PlayerButtonAct` is the third dispatch vocabulary beside a prompt's response key and an editor's OK bit.

THE CLOCK is the bottom row's own monospace cell said twice — `<position> / <length>`, the same face, size, cell metrics and authored offsets as the transport row's clock, so the digits never walk. THE PLAY-SCRUB is a track from the clock's right pad to the lane's right pad: the played portion in the accent, the marker a 1 px column the height of the track. NEITHER IS A BUTTON — the scrub takes no face, no focus and no tooltip. Both cells are PUBLISHED (`ModalDialogGeometry::clock` / `::scrub`, zero under every other owner) for the tick's per-position damage, under the owner-tag doctrine: published geometry may only SELECT, and the seek's frame is decided against the live item length through the one mapping `render_player_scrub_x_of` / `_frame_at` (banker's rounding onto the cells) that the painter's marker and the press router share.

THE SCRUB'S POINTER RULE (U4): a press ON THE MARKER'S grab band (`trim_endcap_grab_px()`, the trim endcaps' own 10 authored px) ARMS the marker drag — the marker's painted x follows the pointer while the sound continues where it was, and THE RELEASE COMMITS THE SEEK (the deferred-click shape); a press on the track ELSEWHERE SEEKS AT THE PRESS, its identity being certain, and arms nothing. The hard end drops the arm and commits nothing — three edges, the same for both arms: the pointer-leave hook, the button-lost edge and the FORCE-END FINALIZER (`finalize_active_drags`, which a resize, the WM close and the Ctrl+Q hatch run; membership there is `any_pointer_gesture_active`'s exactly, so an arm that is gesture-modal is also force-ended).

THE VEIL AND THE GREY. While the player stands the pointer has exactly THREE targets — the overlay's rows, the play-scrub, the modal row's buttons — and EVERY OTHER PRESS IS CONSUMED: the tabs, the flags, the waveform, the menu anchors. EVERY ROSTER BUTTON IS DEAD, `redesign_button_enabled`'s FIRST arm, ranked above the `h` partition: architect R3, *"everything else greys as in the `h` view"* — the player is a MODE like the `h` view rather than a question like a prompt, and the whole chrome is what it takes away. This is the one ruled exception to "modal gates are deliberately absent" in that predicate. The cursor is the Arrow everywhere; a two-finger pinch over the band reaches nothing.

THE ARM IS THE OVERLAY'S, NOT THE PLAYER'S (2026-08-28, with the picker): it reads `folder_overlay_stands`, so the picker greys the chrome exactly as the player does. Under the picker the dialog editor's veil has already made every roster button inert, and the grey is that veil's honest face rather than a second rule — the same argument the ruling makes for the player, arriving from the other side.

### Enter and leave

`GuiRenderPlayer::open()` IS THE ONE OPENER — bare `l`, bare `'` outside the `h` view, and the icon row's two buttons, which synthesize exactly those bare chords. Its callers refuse the modal states before it is asked (a prompt, an editor, the `h` view — where `l` is off the allowlist and `'` is the typed prompt — loading, no source); it refuses "No renders to play" when neither `render/` holds a wav nor `tmp/` a cell, takes the shared modal-open stop PAST every refusal, mints the session, clears the state whole, builds the root listing and damages the window.

`close()`'s ORDER IS LOAD-BEARING: stop (the fence) → the mode down → THE VIEW'S BUFFER REBOUND → only then the item's buffer freed, the engine holding the pointer until the rebind. The rebind is the S/T flip's own tail fork VERBATIM (`ensure_ready` in target view, `rebind_to_source` in source), with a direct bind of the source ahead of it because the target arm may DISPATCH rather than rebind. ITS CAUSES ARE FIVE SITES, inventoried at the declaration and nowhere else: the Close button; bare `Esc` and bare `l` in the router; the `l` toggle the icon row's Play renders button shares; the load's success; and THE CLOSE ROAD `GuiPrompt::request_close`, which takes the mode down AT ITS HEAD (`069a2445`) so Ctrl+Q and the compositor's title-bar X are one road and neither raises the unsaved-work prompt over a standing mode. OPEN PROJECT REFUSES UNDER THE PLAYER (`open_project_editor`'s own line, in the shape of the two guards above it): both roads already refuse earlier — Ctrl+O is consumed by the router, the veil consumes a press on the File anchor — and a reopen would tear the item and its bound buffer down from under it.

### The engine road

THE ITEM IS A WAV PLAYED AS IT IS. THE DECODE VOCABULARY, in order, every refusal its own words on the status line with the item unchanged: `wav_probe` → the RATE AND CHANNEL EQUALITY against the DEVICE ("Not the source's rate and channel count" — the engine was `init`ed with the source's and nothing in the tree resamples, so this is a refusal and never a conversion; a render of this project matches by construction) → `checked_audio_sample_count` on the probed shape → `wav_read_full` → THE SAME EQUALITY AGAIN, on the DECODED buffer's own `WavInfo`, in the same words → "Empty wav". The frozen `audio_io` reader is CALLED, NEVER CHANGED — no frozen touch in this arc.

THE EQUALITY IS ASKED TWICE BECAUSE THE PROBE'S ANSWER IS STALE BY CONSTRUCTION: `wav_read_full` REOPENS the path, and every writer in this tree publishes by rename (the render's own staging, the Synchronize act, wts), so the file it decoded need not be the one the probe read. The probe stays as the CHEAP EARLY REFUSAL — it is what lets the allocation policy answer off a header before any payload is read — and THE POST-DECODE CHECK IS WHAT THE BIND RESTS ON, the decoded info being the buffer describing itself: a mono or other-rate buffer under the engine's stride would be read past its end or played at the wrong speed. It is a SHAPE check and not an IDENTITY one (unlike the render cache's post-decode re-stat, which holds a fingerprint and must prove the bytes are that artifact's): any wav at the path is a legitimate item, and all the player needs is that what it binds fits the device.

THEN THE FENCE, THEN THE BIND: `stop_playback_if_playing` (the one stop body, whose `GuiPlayback::stop()` is the quiescence proof `rebind_buffer` requires) before the old buffer is released and the new one bound at offset 0.

THE SECOND LAUNCH BODY, recorded here and at its own head. `GuiPlaybackLifecycle::launch_playback_window` is the ONE launch body for THE PROJECT'S AUDIO and the player deliberately does not use it: everything that body seeds — the A/B audition clear, the playable gate against the project's domain, the scanner, the follow-scroll, the waveform damage — belongs to the WAVEFORM, which the player does not display. So `play_wav` / `toggle_pause` / `seek_to` call `playback.play` directly against the item's own `[0, frames)`; the project's resting playhead never moves, its scanner never runs, and `render_player.transport_live` is the player's own mirror of `playhead_scanner_active`. THE STOP IS STILL THE ONE STOP BODY: every pause, natural end and close takes `stop_playback_if_playing`, which carries the player's fork inside it (the fence, then the transport bit cleared and the modal row damaged instead of the scanner teardown), so the keyboard stop rule and the fence-before-rebind ordering hold by construction.

A PAUSE reads the resume point from the engine BEFORE the stop; a RESUME at or past the item's end replays from the start (a user act, not a loop). A LIVE SEEK is the engine's keep-alive shape — `play()` over a live session, the window unchanged — stopping one frame short of the exclusive end so the natural end takes over rather than firing a one-frame impulse; a PAUSED seek moves the resume point alone. `render_player_position` is the ONE reader class for the clock, the scrub, the press router and the seeks: the engine's cursor while live, the resume point otherwise.

NO RENDER IS DISPATCHED BY THE PLAYER, EVER. A running render continues in the background, and a target preview that completes meanwhile does NOT rebind under the player (`GuiTargetRender::complete_successful_buffer`'s guard) — the close's re-express binds it. The tick is forked at `on_tick`'s head: while the mode stands the player's tick takes the natural end (through the one stop body) and the per-position damage of the two published cells, and nothing below that line applies; the pre-paint scanner sampling returns for the same reason (the engine's cursor is the item's, not the project's).

### The load road (R18, R15, R21)

LOAD IN PLACE IS A BUTTON on the modal row — *"the load-in-place button can go in the bottom strip"* (R18, retiring the long-press / shift-click road before it was built) — with bare `'` as its chord inside the player. It acts on THE HIGHLIGHTED ROW. `GuiRenderPlayer::highlighted_entry` is the one question it asks: non-null exactly when the highlight is a LOAD-CAPABLE wav, which means a `tmp/` batch cell carrying an `AppState::RenderEntry` — the deliverable's rows carry none, so `render/` refuses. `render_player_load_in_place`'s refusals: the lock's SILENT one on a read-only tab (the button wears no disabled face by ruling, so the refusal is the act's), a SECOND SILENT ONE over a running or parked render (the load wipes `tmp/`, which must never race a batch publishing into it), and "Only batch renders load in place" for any other highlight. THE RUNNING-RENDER REFUSAL WENT SILENT 2026-08-28: it used to say "Render running; Esc cancels it", and the architect saw that line STUCK on screen after the render had finished — the paint chain ranks `queue_progress_text` ABOVE `transient_status_message`, so a transient written while a render's progress line stands is invisible until the line clears and then stale (a transient is cleared only by the next KEY press, and the button is a pointer act). The line is retired product-wide; the running render's own progress line ("Rendering...", the batch's "Rendering N of M (...)...") is the refusal's explanation, and the class is recorded at `AppState::transient_status_message`.

THE CONFIRMATION STAYS (R21, reversing R20 within minutes: dropping it would be *"a slight asymmetry"* between the two `'` roads, the `h` walk's Load prompt working well). The act PAUSES a live transport through the player's own pause — a stop that must keep the resume point, which the ordinary modal stop would not, and the second recorded exception at `stop_playback_for_modal_open` — and raises `DialogTrigger::LOAD_IN_PLACE_CONFIRM`: **"Load `<id>` in place?"**, OK / Cancel, the id in its one spelling (`render_entry_id`, `<batch folder>/<basename>.wav` — moved to `renders_dir.h` in this arc, and the player's confirmation is its only reader once the typed road goes, below).

ITS ENTER ANSWERS OK, and it is the ONE PROMPT RAISED ON ITS FIRST BUTTON (`8308def1`). `PromptInitialFocus` is a REQUIRED parameter of `PromptState::present` — a new raise states which button its Enter answers rather than inheriting one silently — and this prompt alone passes `FirstButton`: the load is not a destructive answer (it lands ONE undo entry, which the ordinary Ctrl+Z takes back) and its keyboard contract mirrors the `h` view's Load editor, the product's other `'` load prompt, whose Enter is its commit. THE TWO LOAD PROMPTS ANSWER ALIKE INSTEAD OF OPPOSITE WAYS. THE BUTTON ORDER IS UNTOUCHED by either value, so Cancel stays LAST on every prompt and the ESCAPE SENTINEL's derivation holds (five raisers now put `'\x1b'` last: unsaved work, the save-failed restatement, the paste confirmation, the error notice and this one).

OK runs `confirm_render_player_load` → the shared `load_render_entry_in_place`; success CLOSES the player (the close's re-express rebinds the view's buffer, then frees the item), a refusal says "Load refused" on the status line with its own cause on stderr. Cancel drops `pending_load`. THE ACT HAS ONE CALLER AGAIN, and the record at `load_render_entry_in_place` says why: the design (§4) sanctioned TWO roads onto it as the no-second-road doctrine's stated exception — glass needs a road that is not typing, and the history view's binding to the typed prompt is not worth untangling — but the second road was the typed prompt's renders-side Enter, which R19 left unreachable and which is now deleted, so the exception is spent and the doctrine holds here plainly. This caller copies the entry before calling, the act's tail wiping `tmp/`. The prompt reaches the acts through `GuiPrompt::input`, a back-pointer wired in main.cpp after both are constructed — the settings editor's own shape, and the same member the retired commit confirmation once used.

### `audio_player` is retired whole (R11)

THE IN-APP PLAYER REPLACED THE SPAWN, so there is nothing to name: the device-config key, the `.settings`-era semantics it carried, the settings editor's `audio_player=` arm and its Tab recall, `AppState::audio_player`, both backends' template values and `spawn_audio_player` itself are all DELETED. THE DEVICE CONFIG IS FOUR KEYS — `gui_scale`, `projects_path`, `projects_repo`, `last_project` — with THREE writers (`apply_gui_scale`, the editor's `projects_repo=` arm, `gui_main`'s `last_project` write); settings.md owns the file. ### The car reaches the player as keys

THE MediaSession IS BUILT (design §3, R7; `3ceb01c2`), and THE MECHANISM IS platform-seam.md's CAR SECTION — the session in the Java sliver, the JNI road down, the eventfd, the state push up, the audio focus. What belongs here is the OTHER END: `GuiRenderPlayer::on_media_command` turns each `GuiMediaCommand` into THE PLAYER'S OWN KEYS through `GuiPlatform::synthesize_key`, press and release, under `kCarStableCodeBase` = 1000 — so every car button is a chord the router above already binds and there is NO SECOND DISPATCH ROAD. `PlayPause` (the undivided toggle key, which the sliver's own `onMediaButtonEvent` maps rather than letting the framework split it) → Space UNCONDITIONALLY, Space being `play_button_act`'s own toggle and no gate belonging between a key that says "the other one" and an act that does it; Play → Space with the transport DOWN, Pause / Stop / a focus loss → Space with it LIVE (that state gate is a semantic pre-filter on the DIRECTIONAL kinds, not a second dispatch: Space is the toggle, and a head unit saying "play" to a live transport must not pause it), Next / Previous → Page Down / Page Up, FF / REW → Right / Left, `FocusGained` → nothing (NOTHING RECOVERS BY ITSELF). `SeekTo` is the road's one direct act, no keysym carrying an absolute position; its milliseconds are clamped to the item's own length BEFORE the conversion to frames, an arriving position being any int64 a head unit cares to send. `publish_media_state` is the one owner of what the head unit shows, called at six edges and never per tick.

A CAR BUTTON IS NOT A KEYBOARD WALKING A RING. The modal row's focus ring claims bare Space and Enter for the focused button, and the ring can be standing on one with nobody having walked to it — the FEINT assigns it passively when a finger presses a button and slides off — so a Play from the wheel would have pressed Close and taken the player down. The synthesis road CLEARS THE RING FIRST (the modal keyboard arm through its one owner `clear_modal_dialog_key_press`, then both focus fields, damaging the modal box), which gives it exactly the membership the rule wants: every kind that synthesizes a key, and no other. A PROMPT standing over the player drops the command whole, for the same reason one step louder.

NO DEVICE PAUSES TOO. `GuiPlayback::device_unavailable()` is true whenever the engine cannot sound — the AAudio disconnect latch and its refused reopen, and the device that NEVER CAME UP on either backend (an init that failed; the laptop without pipewire-jack) — and the tick forks on it BEFORE the natural-end test, pausing with "No audio device" and a "paused" push. Both shapes leave `playing` false exactly as a finished window does, so without the fork the player read silence as a natural end and walked the folder a wav per tick.

### The Open project picker (design §5, §2, R8, §8b's last line)

THE OVERLAY HAS TWO CONTENTS AND ONE OWNER TAG. `AppState::FolderOverlay::owner` is `{None, Player, ProjectPicker}` and IT IS THE STANDING PREDICATE: the band exists exactly while it has an owner, and `folder_overlay_stands` (app_state.h; `folder_overlay::stands` forwards to it, and the keyboard's header reads it directly because folder_overlay.h includes that header rather than the other way round) is what the slot's paint dispatch, the panel's painter, the row press claim, the hover walk, the wheel context, the keyboard's third term, the waveform's paint gate, the touch pan zone, the two-finger navigation body's refusal and the roster's greying arm all ask. The player writes `Player` at its open and `None` at its close; the Open prompt writes `ProjectPicker` at its open and `None` through `open_project_editor_exit_no_commit`, which is that prompt's ONE close body — Esc, Cancel, Ctrl+Q, the same-project no-op and the reopen's own tail all pass through it, so *the overlay leaves with the prompt* is a fact rather than a list.

THE ROWS ARE EXACTLY THE SET THE COMMIT WOULD ACCEPT: the enumerated folder names filtered by BOTH of `open_project_editor_commit`'s tests, the name grammar (`is_last_project_name` — a `.`/`..` name or one with edge whitespace refuses at Enter, so it is not a row either) and then the model (`resolve_project`), and an invalid folder *"just wouldn't show up"* (R8). IT IS LITERALLY ONE LIST: `build_project_picker_rows` writes the survivors as `folder_overlay.rows` AND as `app.open_project_candidates` in the same walk, so the band, the Tab completion and Enter agree by construction — the completion cannot offer a name the picker does not list and Enter then refuses. Folder glyph and name, the enumerator's byte order, NO `..` row and nothing below a project: a project is a leaf here (*"talking between folders is not expected"*). Built when the prompt opens and never kept fresh, R4's rule for the player's listings applied to this one: a project that appears or vanishes while the prompt stands shows at the next open. THE BAND OPENS ON THE CURRENT PROJECT'S ROW.

THE HIGHLIGHT AND THE FIELD ARE ONE THING (§8b: *"single click highlights and prepopulates the field, Enter/OK opens"*). A single click's motionless lift, and bare Up / Down, both run `project_picker_select`: the band seats on the row AND the row's name is written into the prompt's buffer through `text_editor::replace_selection` over a select-all — the editor's ONE incoming filter, a folder name being whatever the filesystem spells — with the caret at the end and the red state cleared. The other direction runs on every text change (`project_picker_track_field`, hung on the shared key route's repaint hook and on the completion's two arms): the buffer is compared EXACTLY against the row names, the band moving onto a match and leaving the list when there is none — a prefix names no one project, so it marks none. The one moment the two are not the same thing is the OPEN, where the band names the current project and the field is still empty; the prompt's empty-field Tab seed stays `last_project` — the picker changes what the seed is checked AGAINST, not what it is: a remembered name that is not in the list (gone, or no longer a project) falls to the first candidate, so a Tab always fills something the band can mark.

OPENING A ROW IS THE COMMIT. The double-click's second press and Enter both reach `folder_overlay_open_row`, whose picker arm seats the row and then calls `open_project_editor_commit` — the same body Enter in the field and the OK button reach, with `is_last_project_name`, `resolve_project`, the same-project no-op, `source_load_dry_run` and the reopen unchanged. THE PICKER IS A SECOND ROAD ONTO THE FIELD AND NEVER A SECOND ACT, and it is the no-second-road doctrine's stated exception for the design's own reason: GLASS NEEDS A ROAD THAT IS NOT TYPING. The sanction is recorded at `open_project_editor`'s declaration.

THE WIDGET IS UNFORKED. The band, the row pitch, the scroll clamp, the one row walk, the press arm and its vertical drag gate, the scroll drag, the three hard ends, the force-end finalizer, the `FolderRow` double-click surface and `any_pointer_gesture_active`'s membership are the panel's and change not at all; `folder_overlay::set_highlight` / `move_highlight` / `scroll_rows` are the widget's mechanics with the DAMAGE left to the caller, which is what lets both contents drive them without the panel holding a Viewport. What forks on the owner is exactly two acts — the motionless lift's highlight and the open — at `folder_overlay_highlight_row` and `folder_overlay_open_row`, and nothing else in the pointer file asks whose rows these are.

THE BAND IS THE VEIL'S THIRD ADMITTED SURFACE. The press claim is RANKED under the prompt gate and above BOTH veils, so while the Open prompt stands the dialog editor's veil admits the field, the buttons and the band — the band belonging to that prompt and feeding its field, which makes it a third piece of the dialog rather than an escape from the veil. The release and the motion are hoisted to the same rank, and the motion deliberately falls THROUGH after the row hover so the field's own click-to-caret and text drag keep working under a standing band. The claim owns its own left-button gate, so the rank costs the veils nothing. The WHEEL takes the same carve-out: `wheel_context` answers 4 over the band and −1 everywhere else while the overlay stands, ranked above the dialog editors' swallow for the same reason (and below the live-gesture refusal, which was already above it).

THE KEYS. Bare Up / Down are the list walk and are admitted at `modal_editor_key_blocked` — the admitted set's third growth ever, and the only one scoped to ONE editor — then acted on in `handle_open_project_editor_key` ahead of the shared modal route, which knows a field and a ring but no list. They walk from the field or from a focused button alike, they REPEAT (`repeat_eligible`'s picker arm, ranked above the focused-button refusal for the ring walk's own reason: a walk decides nothing but where the band is), and every modified spelling stays the strict rule's no-op, dropped at that gate. Everything else in the prompt is the editor's: Tab completes then walks the ring, Enter commits, Esc and Cancel close, Ctrl+S saves, Ctrl+Q hands on the close routing.

### What is deliberately not built

DELIBERATELY NOT, and the ruling is recorded at the Java sliver's class comment and in platform-seam.md: no notification, no foreground service, no background playback, no lock-screen transport — the tablet is a kiosk on a stand with the app in the foreground and the head unit reading the session over AVRCP, so the manifest gains no `<service>`, no `FOREGROUND_SERVICE*` / `POST_NOTIFICATIONS`, no `res/`.

THE FRAMEWORK'S DEFAULT `onMediaButtonEvent` IS NOT OURS, and that is why the toggle has a kind. The default holds a `KEYCODE_MEDIA_PLAY_PAUSE` press for `ViewConfiguration.getDoubleTapTimeout()` to see whether a second press follows and turns two quick presses into `onSkipToNext` while `ACTION_SKIP_TO_NEXT` is declared — a delay between the wheel button and the sound with nothing on screen to explain it, and a double-tap-to-Next gesture nobody ruled, Next being its own button on the wheel. So the sliver OVERRIDES it and maps the keycodes at once (the mechanics are platform-seam.md's), the undivided key going down as `PlayPause` and `KEYCODE_HEADSETHOOK` with it, which is what gave the enumerator a producer and put it back in the numbered table (2026-08-28, after one day deleted for having none). `ACTION_PLAY_PAUSE` stays DECLARED — the routing dispatches a key only for a declared action, the override's keys included.

AND THE STICK IS STILL NOT THE CAR ROUTE — Bluetooth is (the deliberately-unserved list above). The SAF road for the OTG stick is Synchronize's contingency, not the player's; it needs an architect ruling (settings.md's Projects section, platform-seam.md).

The player, the overlay, the picker, the modal row and the play-scrub are portable GUI (`src/gui/`) and run identically on both backends — on the laptop the car keys are simply the keyboard, and the picker's rows answer a mouse there exactly as they answer a finger on the tablet.
