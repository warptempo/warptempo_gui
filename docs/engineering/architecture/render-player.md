### Why it exists (design §0, his use cases)

THE CAR IS THE REASON. Parked, at night: open the player, see the project's renders, start one — without leaving the app and without Audacious (U1). Queue a variation series parked and audition it one after another while driving, WITHIN ONE FOLDER — *"in realistic use I would contain my work to one or the other folder anyway"* (U3). Play-scrub: tap a later point and the audio jumps there; grab the marker and drag, and the seek commits at the release (U4). Load a recipe deliberately from the player, with a confirmation (U6). The same auditioning on the LAPTOP, in-app rather than spawned (U5). And the bottom-strip modal STAYS the dialog machinery (U8): the history view's typed load-in-place rode it and *"untangling that will be complicated"*, so the overlay FED the existing modal instead of replacing it — until R22/R23 the same afternoon retired both typed prompts for the field-less picker, which is a modal owner of its own on that same machinery.

DELIBERATELY UNSERVED, and the list is the design's own: a waveform for the played render; loops (*"advancing is not looping"*); auto-advance ACROSS folders; a nested tree with connector lines; folder navigation BETWEEN projects; arbitrary paths; a recent-files list; the stick as the car route (Bluetooth is); the sunset score video; a real second window (tried, dropped — Wayland trouble); a centred floating modal box (tried, dropped — *"flat, no drop shadow, small, blocked the whole screen"*).

### The model (R1, R2, R4, revised by R16 / R17)

THE PLAYER NAVIGATES THE PROJECT'S OUTPUT FOLDERS THE REGULAR WAY — *"if we do it by folders in Open project, we may as well do it the regular way in the player too"*. The root lists `render` iff the deliverable folder holds a wav and `tmp` iff a batch cell exists; `tmp` lists its batch folders in the enumeration's own order; a batch lists its cells in that same order — the order `'` walks, reused as the play order; the deliverable folder lists its `*.wav` files in byte order of their names. The `..` row stands at the top of EVERY NON-ROOT listing and nowhere else (R13). A listing is built WHEN ITS FOLDER IS ENTERED and never kept fresh (R4): no cache, no watcher — a render that completes meanwhile shows on the next entry of that folder. `GuiRenderPlayer::rebuild_rows` is the one builder and `AppState::RenderPlayer::folder` (Root / Deliverable / Batches / Batch, the batch named by `batch_dir`) is where the listing is — never a free path.

A SINGLE CLICK ON A ROW HIGHLIGHTS IT AND DOES NOTHING ELSE (R16, the revision after the design's first pass: *"I'm expecting to use click on the item and then press a button to play or to load or whatever"* — the flags are already double-click, and single-click-to-open was pcmanfm-qt's habit, not the product's). THE HIGHLIGHT IS ONE FIELD (`folder_overlay.highlight_row`): the band AND the list's keyboard focus, one thing. OPENING a row is the flags' own DOUBLE-CLICK (double-tap on glass) or ENTER: a folder ENTERS, `..` goes UP, a wav PLAYS FROM ITS START.

THE PLAY BUTTON (and Space) PLAYS WHATEVER IS HIGHLIGHTED (R17): a highlighted wav that is not the transport's item plays from its start; a highlighted folder or `..` takes THE OPEN ACT — the car-stereo OK/Play convention, so glass never needs a double-tap to navigate; on the transport's own item it pauses / resumes. `GuiRenderPlayer::play_button_act` is that fork whole.

THE TRANSPORT'S ITEM IS NOT THE HIGHLIGHT. It keeps playing while the listing is navigated elsewhere, its row wears the transport glyph, and Previous / Next / AUTO-ADVANCE walk `item_folder` — THE WAV ROWS OF ITS FOLDER AS THEY WERE LISTED WHEN IT WAS PLAYED — never another folder and never a wrap (R2). Previous and Next never move the highlight.

NOTHING LOOPS, WITH ONE SANCTIONED EXCEPTION — **REPEAT ONE** (architect 2026-08-28, R26; the row's lamp and bare `r`). It is a TWO-STATE toggle, off or repeat the ONE item: *"the user can just press play once the playlist finishes... repeat one is much more useful"*, which is why there is no repeat-all. While it stands the natural end REPLAYS THE ITEM FROM ITS START through the player's own play road — the same road a user's Play takes, so the item stays the transport's and the head unit is published at that edge as at any play. The state is SESSION-ONLY (`AppState::RenderPlayer::repeat_one`, false at every `open()`, serialized nowhere), no media command maps to it and `publish_media_state` publishes no repeat mode. The exception is recorded at the rule's other statements — `render_player.h`'s model, `playback_lifecycle.h`'s bounded audition, CLAUDE.md's two digests and HELP.

AT THE FOLDER'S LAST WAV, with the lamp off, the transport stops with the item resting at its start — and **THE NEXT PLAY STARTS THE FOLDER'S FIRST WAV** (R27, the car's Play at the end of a playlist) rather than replaying that last one. `AppState::RenderPlayer::ended_at_folder_end` is the one bit that says the transport is resting THERE: the natural end's own last-wav arm is its ONE writer (an advance that REFUSED to decode leaves it clear — the bit names the end, not a failure), every play, resume, seek, row open, open and close clears it, and `play_button_act`'s TRANSPORT-ITEM arm is its one reader, so the act has one owner and the car's Play reaches it through the same Space as every other Play. The highlight does not move.

### The surface (R3, R5, R9, R24, R31–R33)

THE OVERLAY SITS WHERE THE ON-SCREEN KEYBOARD SITS — *"the overlay sits in the on-screen keyboard's place above the bottom strip, replacing the keyboard there"* (R3), *"the way the keyboard helps the bottom strip"*. THE BAND IS THE SLOT'S AND THE ROW IS THE BUTTON'S (R31/R33, the second pass the same day; the panel's first hours took both from the keyboard). The band takes the slot's x, its width and its BOTTOM EDGE — the bottom row's own lane, lifted, through the one owner `keyboard_slot_band` (app_state.h) that the keyboard's own `surface_rect` reads too — and its HEIGHT IS ITS CONTENT'S UP TO A CEILING: it grows a row at a time until it reaches THE MIDDLE OF THE WAVEFORM (`keyboard_slot_max_height_px`, the waveform's own midpoint through the vertical stack's one geometry owner, which is the window's midpoint wherever the centering is feasible), and past that it stops growing and scrolls — *"from the bottom strip up to the middle of the waveform... it grows with its content up to that cap, then scrolls"*, after the fixed four-row band read too short against his dummy folders. THE ROW IS EXACTLY THE ICON ROW'S BUTTON — the same 32 px box, the same 2 px gap between boxes, the same corner radius and the same 22 px glyph seated at the box's own `(32-22)/2` inset, every number read from render.h's icon-button block (*"we've gone for the button analogy"*; *"the buttons are good enough size for my finger"*) — and A NAME TOO LONG FOR THE LINE RUNS OFF THE EDGE, no wrap and no ellipsis (*"project and file names will be short"*), the painter clipping it to the row. The panel's one authored number of its own is a 2 px outer inset, pcmanfm-qt's 1 px item margin grown with the taller rows. THE COLOURS ARE THE FILE MANAGER'S (R32, kdenlive's project bin and pcmanfm-qt's compact view, which agree): the band's ground is `kModalFieldGround` #141618 and NOTHING ALTERNATES; a resting row paints no fill at all; HOVER is `kFolderRowHover` (the accent at the 0.3 click mix over that ground — the derivation ships, and it reproduces the architect's #204357 within one 8-bit step on the green) under a 1 px `kFolderRowHoverOutline` #3694c5 frame; the HIGHLIGHT is `kRedesignAccent` #3daee9; HOVERED + HIGHLIGHTED is `kFolderRowHoverSelected` #44bfff; the ink is `kRedesignLabel` white in every face, and a live press arm wears the highlight's own fill. THE TWO NEVER BOTH STAND, STRUCTURALLY: the overlay's owners — the player and the two pickers — are modes and not editors, each opener refuses under every editor, each router consumes every editor opener and each veil consumes every pointer press that could raise one, so the keyboard's editor term is false whenever the band stands. (`onscreen_keyboard::stands` carried `!folder_overlay_stands(a)` as a THIRD term for the afternoon the Open prompt kept a text editor under the picker's band; the term lost its producer with that field and was DELETED — a gate term exists iff a producer exists — with the record at the predicate.) R3's *"neither use needs typing"* is literal now on every content. `waveform_paint_area` gates on EITHER TENANT and clips on THE STANDING ONE'S OWN RECT: one gate, one clip, and the waveform is not painted where the slot paints — a real fork since the two tenants' heights parted, the overlay's being its listing's and the keyboard's its four key rows. THE BAND'S DAMAGE HAS TWO RECTS FOR THE SAME REASON: a damage INSIDE a standing band (the scroll, the highlight, one row's face) takes the band as it stands, while a LISTING REBUILD takes `folder_overlay::band_damage_rect` — the band at its ceiling — because the panel grows upward from a fixed bottom edge and a shorter listing would otherwise leave the departed rows painted above the new band; the slot's SHOW/HIDE comparator (main.cpp) takes `onscreen_keyboard::slot_damage_rect`, the taller of the two tenants' bands, neither of which it can ask for a height at the moment it fires. The as-painted bit is the SLOT'S (`AppState::keyboard_slot_painted_standing`, written only by `GuiPaintHandler::paint_keyboard_slot` on a rect that fully covers the STANDING TENANT'S band — the slot's tallest where neither stands, that being the frame the hide's own damage draws), not either tenant's. AND THE BAND YIELDS THE TOUCH PAN ZONE the way the keyboard's does (`touch_point_in_pan_zone`): the panel paints over the waveform, whose whole area is that zone, and both of the band's gestures live on the POINTER — the row press and the scroll drag that same arm becomes — so without the carve-out a finger crossing the slop on a row would resolve to the phone-model pan and a held one to the region former, and the list would answer a tap and nothing else.

UNLIKE THE KEYBOARD IT SCROLLS: `folder_overlay.scroll_px` is THE PRODUCT'S FIRST SCROLL OFFSET — a pixel offset clamped through the one owner `folder_overlay::clamp_scroll`, reset to 0 at every rebuild, stepped ONE ROW PER WHEEL DETENT (wheel context 4, live over the band alone and swallowed everywhere else), carried by the band's own drag on glass, and moved by `scroll_row_into_view` so a walked highlight stays visible. No header row, no columns, no scrollbar. PAINT AND HIT CANNOT DRIFT: both walk the one `folder_overlay::for_each_row`, and `row_at` answers −1 for any point outside the band whatever a scrolled row's rect would contain.

ROW GLYPHS: `icons::Icon::Folder` (Breeze places/22/folder, what pcmanfm-qt and kdenlive's Open dialog paint) on folder rows AND on the `..` row (it names a folder), `icons::Icon::AudioXWav` (Breeze mimetypes/22/audio-x-wav, R14 — `audio-x-generic` stays the program's logo alone) on wav rows, swapped for `MediaPlaybackStart` on the transport's item's row (the bottom row's one-button-two-faces precedent). Both icons are fresh verbatim transcriptions; the roster is 49.

THE ROWS ARE CHROME (conventions.md's third clause). A row press ARMS — the same press may still become the band's scroll drag, so its identity is not certain — and a MOTIONLESS LIFT highlights and seeds the double-click candidate (press coordinates, release stamp: the marker seed's own split). Past the vertical drag gate (`drag_moved_threshold_px`, the product's one 8-authored-px crossing) the arm IS the scroll drag and its act is gone; once a drag, always a drag. THE ONE PRESS WHOSE IDENTITY IS CERTAIN — a recognized double-click's second press — acts AT THE PRESS and arms nothing, because the act may rebuild the listing under the pointer. `DoubleClickSurface::FolderRow` is the FIFTH surface (2026-08-28, born on the motionless-release seed timing the other four reached in 2026-08-15). A shift or ctrl press on a row is a consumed no-op and nothing on a row reads `kHoldBeatMs`: the design's long-press load (R10) was RETIRED BEFORE IT WAS BUILT by R18.

NOTHING POPS MID-GESTURE HERE EITHER. The row press's arm and the scrub's marker drag are MEMBERS OF `any_pointer_gesture_active`, the product's one "some pointer gesture is in flight" predicate, so the player's two arms hold the keyboard, the wheel and the cursor exactly as a marker or trim drag does: `on_key`'s player block asks that predicate itself and SWALLOWS every key while an arm stands — Esc included, so the mode cannot close under a held press, and Enter and Space cannot open a row or start a sound under one — with THE ONE HATCH the drag-modal gate's own modifier-exact Ctrl+Q, which falls through to the close road that takes the player down and drops both arms; `wheel_context` answers −1 for the same reason, so the band cannot scroll under a live arm; and the cursor map answers Arrow. A LISTING REBUILD CLEARS THE DOUBLE-CLICK SEED (`rebuild_rows`, the mutator's own line): a `FolderRow` candidate names a row INDEX, and the listing the rebuild installs renames every index, so a second press must not pair with a first taken on the listing before it. Every other road into a rebuild happens to pass a chokepoint that clears the candidate anyway, which is the callers' accident and not a contract the listing rests on.

### Enter and leave

`GuiRenderPlayer::open()` IS THE ONE OPENER — bare `l`, bare `'` outside the `h` view, and the icon row's two buttons, which synthesize exactly those bare chords. Its callers refuse the modal states before it is asked (a prompt, an editor, a picker, the `h` view — where `l` is off the allowlist and `'` is the history picker — loading, no source); it refuses "No renders to play" when neither `render/` holds a wav nor `tmp/` a cell, takes the shared modal-open stop PAST every refusal, mints the session, clears the state whole, builds the root listing and damages the window.

`close()`'s ORDER IS LOAD-BEARING: stop (the fence) → the mode down → THE VIEW'S BUFFER REBOUND → only then the item's buffer freed, the engine holding the pointer until the rebind. The rebind is the S/T flip's own tail fork VERBATIM (`ensure_ready` in target view, `rebind_to_source` in source), with a direct bind of the source ahead of it because the target arm may DISPATCH rather than rebind. ITS CAUSES ARE FIVE SITES, inventoried at the declaration and nowhere else: the Close button; bare `Esc` and bare `l` in the router; the `l` toggle the icon row's Play renders button shares; the load's success; and THE CLOSE ROAD `GuiPrompt::request_close`, which takes the mode down AT ITS HEAD (`069a2445`) so Ctrl+Q and the compositor's title-bar X are one road and neither raises the unsaved-work prompt over a standing mode. OPEN PROJECT REFUSES UNDER THE PLAYER (`open_project_picker`'s own line, in the shape of the two guards above it): both roads already refuse earlier — Ctrl+O is consumed by the router, the veil consumes a press on the File anchor — and a reopen would tear the item and its bound buffer down from under it. THE CLOSE ROAD TAKES A PICKER DOWN THE SAME WAY (`close_picker`, the line after the player's in `request_close`), one close body each.

### The engine road

THE ITEM IS A WAV PLAYED AS IT IS. THE DECODE VOCABULARY, in order, every refusal its own words on the status line with the item unchanged: `wav_probe` → the RATE AND CHANNEL EQUALITY against the DEVICE ("Not the source's rate and channel count" — the engine was `init`ed with the source's and nothing in the tree resamples, so this is a refusal and never a conversion; a render of this project matches by construction) → `checked_audio_sample_count` on the probed shape → `wav_read_full` → THE SAME EQUALITY AGAIN, on the DECODED buffer's own `WavInfo`, in the same words → "Empty wav". The frozen `audio_io` reader is CALLED, NEVER CHANGED — no frozen touch in this arc.

THE EQUALITY IS ASKED TWICE BECAUSE THE PROBE'S ANSWER IS STALE BY CONSTRUCTION: `wav_read_full` REOPENS the path, and every writer in this tree publishes by rename (the render's own staging, the Synchronize act, wts), so the file it decoded need not be the one the probe read. The probe stays as the CHEAP EARLY REFUSAL — it is what lets the allocation policy answer off a header before any payload is read — and THE POST-DECODE CHECK IS WHAT THE BIND RESTS ON, the decoded info being the buffer describing itself: a mono or other-rate buffer under the engine's stride would be read past its end or played at the wrong speed. It is a SHAPE check and not an IDENTITY one (unlike the render cache's post-decode re-stat, which holds a fingerprint and must prove the bytes are that artifact's): any wav at the path is a legitimate item, and all the player needs is that what it binds fits the device.

THEN THE FENCE, THEN THE BIND: `stop_playback_if_playing` (the one stop body, whose `GuiPlayback::stop()` is the quiescence proof `rebind_buffer` requires) before the old buffer is released and the new one bound at offset 0.

THE SECOND LAUNCH BODY, recorded here and at its own head. `GuiPlaybackLifecycle::launch_playback_window` is the ONE launch body for THE PROJECT'S AUDIO and the player deliberately does not use it: everything that body seeds — the A/B audition clear, the playable gate against the project's domain, the scanner, the follow-scroll, the waveform damage — belongs to the WAVEFORM, which the player does not display. So `play_wav` / `toggle_pause` / `seek_to` call `playback.play` directly against the item's own `[0, frames)`; the project's resting playhead never moves, its scanner never runs, and `render_player.transport_live` is the player's own mirror of `playhead_scanner_active`. THE STOP IS STILL THE ONE STOP BODY: every pause, natural end and close takes `stop_playback_if_playing`, which carries the player's fork inside it (the fence, then the transport bit cleared and the modal row damaged instead of the scanner teardown), so the keyboard stop rule and the fence-before-rebind ordering hold by construction.

A PAUSE reads the resume point from the engine BEFORE the stop; a RESUME at or past the item's end replays from the start (a user act, not a loop). A LIVE SEEK is the engine's keep-alive shape — `play()` over a live session, the window unchanged — stopping one frame short of the exclusive end so the natural end takes over rather than firing a one-frame impulse; a PAUSED seek moves the resume point alone. `render_player_position` is the ONE reader class for the clock, the scrub, the press router and the seeks: the engine's cursor while live, the resume point otherwise.

NO RENDER IS DISPATCHED BY THE PLAYER, EVER. A running render continues in the background, and a target preview that completes meanwhile does NOT rebind under the player (`GuiTargetRender::complete_successful_buffer`'s guard) — the close's re-express binds it. The tick is forked at `on_tick`'s head: while the mode stands the player's tick takes the natural end (through the one stop body) and the per-position damage of the two published cells, and nothing below that line applies; the pre-paint scanner sampling returns for the same reason (the engine's cursor is the item's, not the project's).
