# Touch: One Unified Mode (wl_touch as the Pointer)

> Touch phase 1, architect-ratified 2026-08-11 — the touch arc's first CODE surface (row 8, the bottom toolbar, was its first GUI surface). This file is the arc's record the way kdenlive-redesign.md is the redesign's. The phase-1 code shipped UNVERIFIED ON SILICON: the authoring laptop has no touchscreen and the target is the Pi rig's ROADOM panel, so everything was written to be inert without the capability and correct by construction, with every judgment call recorded. THE PANEL HAS SINCE ARRIVED: the glass sessions of 2026-08-11 produced the first field-driven revisions (the phone model, the trim surface arc, the trim move — their records below, all SUPERSEDED IN THEIR TOUCH HALVES a day later), and the FIFTH GLASS RULING of 2026-08-12 — THE TIMER-FREE MODEL, the authoritative statement below — reshaped the whole core: the first finger is the pointer ON CONTACT, and the disambiguation window, both timing constants, the single-finger pan and the touch trim move are DELETED. Retune the constant on glass, not on argument.

### The Unified-Mode Ruling

THE PREMISE IS ONE UNIFIED MODE, the architect's one settled ruling for the arc: same look, same behavior, same interaction language for mouse and touch. Phase 1 makes it literal — TOUCH IS THE POINTER, translated whole at the platform boundary (`platform_wayland.cpp`), and the GUI cannot tell which device produced an event. No touch mode, no flag, no detection, no GUI-side branch. Everything inherits by construction: every gate, refusal, modal claim, read-only rule, hit test and gesture the pointer has applies to a finger with zero new GUI semantics — stated here once rather than re-proven per gesture. Since the timer-free model (2026-08-12) the premise is EXCEPTIONLESS again: the phone model's one ruled divergence — the single-finger waveform pan — is SUPERSEDED (its record below), and the only touch-specific gesture is the two-finger navigation.

### The Timer-Free Model (Fifth Glass Ruling, 2026-08-12, Architect-Dictated)

THE RULING, condensed from his dictation: FIRST FINGER = THE POINTER ON CONTACT, EVERYWHERE. No disambiguation window, no on-lift deferral, no long-press, no zone queries. A finger landing delivers the pointer immediately — the touch hold raised FIRST (the codex-round-2 rule kept: every delivery in the burst reads the primary button held), then the synthesized enter-motion at the contact point, then the left press there (`deliver_touch_contact_press`). From then on touch motion is pointer motion (coalesced to the wl_touch.frame boundary as before) and touch up is the release + the focus-forked translation end (`deliver_touch_translation_end`, UNCHANGED — the ordinary-leave / restore-motion fork, the sibling-hold suppression, all of it stays). A tap is press-then-release across its own two events; a drag crosses the GUI's drag gate by its own delivered motion, exactly as a mouse drag does.

SECOND FINGER = THE UPGRADE. A second finger landing while the pointer translation is live ENDS the translation by ORDINARY RELEASE — the finger-up path's own shape (flush the staged motion, release on the logical left's 1→0 edge, the focus-forked translation end), nothing new — and then SEEDS the two-finger navigation gesture at the join: both fingers' current positions are the gesture start, the latch measured from there (the existing two-finger latch rule, `kTouchSlopPx` Chebyshev on centroid travel OR distance change). THE ACCEPTED COST, the architect's own words, recorded at the site (`on_touch_down`'s upgrade arm): a staggered two-finger landing first nudges the playhead through the press-release burst — "I'd rather have a more responsive interface... building another lag is a no go."

IMPLEMENTATION LATITUDE, granted and NOT taken (the reasoning recorded at `on_touch_down`'s Idle arm): a SAME-FRAME double-down MAY stage the first down's delivery to the wl_touch.frame boundary and open nav directly with nothing delivered. Not taken because the staging is a mini pending state with its own edges, and a deliberate pinch's fingers land tens of milliseconds apart — several frames — so the staggered pair takes the accepted burst regardless and only the near-perfectly-simultaneous pair would ever be saved. Delivery at arrival keeps the machine at four honest phases: {Idle, Pointer, Nav, Drain}.

THE CONSEQUENCES, all falling out of the contact press with no new code: the upper waveform's playhead jumps at touch and a drag is the region/marker drag; the lower half's scrub fires at touch and a second tap stops; buttons act instantly, and a HELD transport arrow repeats from contact on the keyboard's own clock (the row-8 held-arrow repeat arms on the pointer press, which now IS the contact).

DELETED WHOLE with the window: `TouchPhase::Pending` and `TouchPhase::TrimMove`, `kTouchDisambiguateMs` (60 ms) and `kTouchTrimHoldMs` (500 ms), the deadline sampling (the timerfd tick call and the lazy per-event calls), the three window resolvers, the pan-zone and trim-band zone queries, the single-finger nav sub-state and its upgrade rebase, and the five extra `set_touch_nav_hooks` members (the hook set is the update/end pair again). `kTouchSlopPx` SURVIVES with a narrowed role: it is ONLY the navigation latch now — its old "resolves the window early" role died with the window, and its deliberate equality with `kDragMovedThresholdPx` is a feel statement rather than a burst mechanism (the press lands at contact, so a touch drag crosses the GUI's drag gate by its own motion).

### The Binding

`wl_touch` is CORE protocol — wl_seat's third capability, the `wl_data_device_manager` precedent: the same wayland.xml, no new library, no pkg-config entry, no generated stub. It is bound from the existing seat listener when `WL_SEAT_CAPABILITY_TOUCH` is advertised and released on capability loss (through the hard-end contract below). It is NOT a required global and NOT a required capability: absence is SILENCE — no stderr, nothing degraded. The laptop simply has no glass; a seat without touch is the ordinary case, not a poor environment. Touch events arrive batched under `wl_touch.frame` and are processed per frame like pointer frames: down/up edges dispatch at arrival (the button model), motion coalesces to the frame boundary (the captured-relative-motion model), so a sensor-rate panel costs one delivery per frame.

### The Translation Contract (The First Finger IS the Pointer, on Contact)

The bare-`e` precedent — a key translated at the boundary into the left mouse button — applied to glass. The translation state is the platform's own, keyed on the touch id; the GUI sees ordinary pointer deliveries. The phase machine and the AUTHORITATIVE edge inventory live at the touch state block in `platform_wayland.h` (the `transport_repeat` precedent: one list at the state, each fire site keeping only its own clause); the shape:

- FIRST touch-down (no touch state live) delivers the CONTACT PRESS BURST immediately: the synthesized enter-motion at the contact point, then the left press there. THE TOUCH HOLD BIT IS RAISED BEFORE THE ENTRY MOTION (codex round 2's rule, kept), so every delivery in the burst — the pre-press motion included — reads the primary button held, which is what the GUI's armed hover-open guard keys on (below). From then on touch motion is pointer motion and touch up is the release. Nothing is withheld: there is no window, no deferral, no queued motion.
- A touch-up that ends the translation delivers the release on the logical left's 1→0 edge and then THE TRANSLATION END ON THAT SAME EDGE, which FORKS ON PHYSICAL POINTER FOCUS since codex round 3 (one owner, `deliver_touch_translation_end`, shared by the touch-up site, the upgrade and the hard-end contract): with NO mouse focused on the surface, an ORDINARY pointer leave (`GuiPointerLeaveReason::OrdinaryLeave`) — the finger left the glass, which IS a leave, and the remembered-position machinery and the menu-row band rules already handle ordinary leaves with nothing special-cased; with the physical pointer FOCUSED, an ordinary restore MOTION at the mouse's last platform-tracked position (`pointer_x_`/`pointer_y_`, which touch never writes) INSTEAD of the leave — the finger lifted, so the unified pointer is now where the mouse is, and the motion re-derives hover, the menu-row exit test and the loop-settled cursor from truth (the round-3 cursor-residue fix). The restore motion runs the ordinary motion path WHOLE, deliberately unmarked — so with the menu row ARMED and the mouse resting on an anchor it hover-opens that menu, and with a menu OPEN and the mouse resting on the other anchor it hover-switches: judged the armed mode's own defining semantics for a pointer standing at that position, not surprises to suppress (suppression would need a "restore" mark on the motion, a device branch in spirit; recorded at the definition too). WHEN A SIBLING SOURCE STILL HOLDS the logical left (physical BTN_LEFT or bare-`e`), the release is suppressed and THE WHOLE END IS TOO — leave and restore alike (codex round 2): the unified pointer has not meaningfully left — the mouse is still there, mid-press — and the leave hook's clears are not mere hover cleanup (they disarm the row-8 held-arrow repeat and destroy an open popup's live press claim, state that belongs to the still-held press, not to the finger that lifted). A suppressed end can strand a hover face where the finger last was — the accepted-glitch class, self-healing on the next pointer event. The hard-end contract takes the same edge rule and the same fork.
- THE LOGICAL LEFT BUTTON IS ONE BIT WITH THREE SOURCES: `pointer_left_held_ || synth_left_held_ || touch_left_held_` — the evdev model for devices sharing BTN_LEFT, extended from the bare-`e` pair. A press is delivered only on the OR's 0→1 edge and a release only on its 1→0 edge, so the sources never double-deliver, and `current_mods()` ORs all three into `primary_button_held` (without which a touch drag would tear on its first motion). Each source's hold ends on ITS OWN stream's edges only: the keyboard edges end the synth hold, pointer-capability loss ends the physical and synth holds, the touch edges end the touch hold.
- The contact press is a pointer-button press in full: it kills an armed key repeat (layer 1 of the repeat contract — the edge list at `on_keyboard_key` names three press producers) and flushes both staged motions ahead of itself (the `flush_deferred_motion` invariant, which the touch delivery sites joined).
- Physical keyboard modifiers ride along to touch deliveries exactly as to a mouse: `current_mods()` is the one modifier truth, so Shift+tap is a Shift+click with no new code.

### The Feel Constant (One Ruled Retunable)

- `kTouchSlopPx` = 8.0 — physical px, the NAVIGATION LATCH and nothing else: a two-finger gesture navigates nothing until the centroid travels this far (Chebyshev) from the join or the finger distance changes by it, and the crossing update folds the whole accumulated delta. It DELIBERATELY EQUALS the GUI's one generic press-becomes-drag gate (`kDragMovedThresholdPx`; stated rather than shared — the platform sits below the GUI model): the press lands at contact, so a touch drag becomes a drag at the same travel a mouse drag does, by the GUI's own gate.

(SUPERSEDED, 2026-08-12 — the deleted constants' record, kept: `kTouchDisambiguateMs` = 60 was the one-finger-or-two window, sampled on the timerfd tick plus lazily per event; `kTouchTrimHoldMs` = 500 was the merged trim band's own Pending deadline — the fourth glass session's "beat", given its own constant after riding the 60 ms window broke the band's quick drag in the field the same day it shipped: 60 ms is shorter than an aimed drag's natural dwell, so nearly every deliberate band drag expired into the trim move before its motion reached the slop fork. Both died with the window itself; `kTouchSlopPx`'s old early-resolution role died with them.)

### Two Fingers = Pan + Zoom (Navigation-Class)

A second finger joining the first is the NAVIGATION gesture (the upgrade — the timer-free section above; the translation ends by ordinary release and the pair seeds at the join): per-frame centroid horizontal delta pans, per-frame finger-distance ratio zooms ABOUT THE CENTROID's column — the strip drag's dual-axis semantics. The gesture never delivers pointer events.

THE DELIVERY SHAPE TAKEN, and why (the phase-1 brief offered the strip-drag pointer entries as the preferred shape with a recorded fallback; this is the fallback, taken at the family's deepest shared point): a narrow platform-to-GUI hook set wired in main.cpp (`GuiPlatform::set_touch_nav_hooks` → `GuiInputHandler::apply_touch_nav_update` / `end_touch_nav`, the `set_keyboard_intent_cancel_hook` wiring precedent; the pair is the WHOLE set again since the timer-free model — the pan-zone query and the trim-move members are deleted) whose per-frame update drives `Viewport::apply_strip_drag_zoom` — the strip-drag family's own application chokepoint — so the level clamp, the viewport clamp (`clamp_viewport_start`), the synchronous per-frame rebuild and the either-axis follow suppression all come from the one owner. The family's POINTER-PRESS ARM (`arm_strip_drag_at` / `StripDragState`) is deliberately not driven, for two structural reasons recorded at the declaration: (1) the arm unconditionally CAPTURES THE REAL POINTER — it would hide and lock the mouse cursor during a touch gesture, and the capture's release warps the mouse and rewrites the platform's tracked pointer position to the gesture's restore x, corrupting real mouse state from glass; (2) a `strip_drag.active` gesture is TERMINATED by `on_motion`'s button-lost arm the moment any real mouse motion arrives without a held button — which is every mouse motion during a two-finger touch — so a nudged mouse would kill a live pinch. No ctrl-modified pointer press is faked anywhere (modifier state belongs to xkb).

The mechanics, all at their sites:

- The ZOOM mapping is LOGARITHMIC and constant-free: `new_level = level − log2(dist_ratio)`, so doubling the finger gap is exactly one zoom level in and the content between the fingers scales with the fingers. Pre-clamped into `[kMinZoom, effective_max_zoom_level]` exactly as `apply_strip_drag_at` pre-clamps (the chokepoint's `level_changed` contract names both callers).
- The PAN is folded into the same placement: the content under the PREVIOUS centroid column is placed at the CURRENT centroid column under the new level — one `apply_strip_drag_zoom` call does both axes. PER-FRAME ANCHORED (the ratified semantics), so there is no persistent song anchor and no edge-trick rebind to need.
- THE LATCH is the platform's, the strip drag's own press-becomes-drag model in the touch's own unit: nothing is delivered until the centroid travels `kTouchSlopPx` (Chebyshev) from the join OR the finger distance changes by that same slop, and the crossing update folds the whole accumulated delta — so a two-finger tap navigates nothing and costs the GUI nothing (the end hook fires only if an update was delivered).
- THE REFUSAL ANSWER IS THE WHEEL'S, per frame: the gesture is the wheel's navigation-class sibling — it delivers no pointer events, punches through the keyboard-modal flag editor exactly as the wheel does, and navigates the wheel's two surfaces — so `wheel_context` (the SINGLE wheel routing predicate) is asked at each frame's centroid. That refuses under the prompt, an open dropdown, the bottom-strip modal editors, loading/empty audio and ANY live pointer gesture, and applies only over the waveform or the top strip. A modal opening mid-gesture freezes it (the wheel's own behavior), and a live mouse gesture freezes it too, so two devices cannot write the viewport at once in that direction; the converse — a mouse gesture ARMING while a touch gesture is mid-flight — is accepted as the user's own two-handed act (each writer is incremental over live state and every write clamps through the one chokepoint). The wheel's dismissal pair (dropdown close, tooltip hide) is deliberately NOT copied — this is a refusal, not a press act — but every applied frame CLEARS THE DOUBLE-CLICK CANDIDATE (the C8 rule: navigation that moves content between two taps must not let the second tap consume as a double-click).
- One finger lifting ENDS the gesture (any end commits) — and the end DELIVERS THE STAGED FINAL FRAME FIRST: Wayland orders the `up` before the terminating `wl_touch.frame`, so motion batched with the lift is still staged when the end runs, and that motion is the user's own final leg (the any-end-commits family). This is what lets a short pinch whose only latch-crossing motion batches with the up act at all. The survivor is IGNORED until all fingers lift (the drain phase) — no downgrade to a fresh translation. A third finger is ignored. No pinch-vs-pan mode split: both axes live simultaneously, the strip drag's own model. The end is the grab-pan release's own tail — one predictor resync; each applied frame already rebuilt synchronously.
- The gesture joins neither the platform's key-repeat disarm list nor the transport hold's edge list: those are ruled event-edge enumerations (key press / pointer press / completed wheel emission) and a two-finger frame is none of them. Recorded as a judgment rather than an omission — a codex round can contest it.

### The Phone Model — SUPERSEDED (Second Glass Session, 2026-08-11; deleted at the Fifth, 2026-08-12)

THE RULING WAS (architect, at the rig's second glass session, his words condensed): "one finger drags the pan — that's how my phone works. One finger drags, two fingers zoom... try that as an intermediate." Phase 2 therefore accepted a DELIBERATE touch-scoped divergence from the unified premise on ONE surface, the WAVEFORM: a one-finger DRAG whose down point lay there was NAVIGATION — the disambiguation window's slop-crossing resolution FORKED on a pan-zone answer captured at the down (a third hook, `pan_zone(x, y)`, answering the waveform area, geometry only), delivered through the same nav update hook with the finger as the centroid and `dist_ratio` pinned at 1.0 (`touch_nav_single_`, a Nav sub-state), upgradeable IN PLACE by a second finger with the delta bases REBASED to the join. HOLD UNLOCKED THE POINTER: window expiry with the finger stationary was the deliberate escape to the ordinary pointer translation, which is what kept the lower-half scrub hold and every press-and-hold gesture alive on glass.

THE SUCCESSION THAT PRODUCED IT, kept for the record: the FIRST glass session's field report was zoom excellent, pan unusable — finger-distance jitter leaking into the per-frame ratio — and the remedy tried first was a zoom ENGAGEMENT HYSTERESIS (`kTouchZoomEngagePx` = 20, commit 26963af3), field-judged worse ("the previous way was much better") and REVERTED WHOLE the same day (5e1d7d11; do not re-propose the hysteresis on this gesture). The phone model was the ratified successor — the two axes split by FINGER COUNT rather than by per-axis engagement thresholds.

SUPERSEDED WHOLE at the fifth glass ruling (2026-08-12): the timer-free model makes the first finger the pointer ON CONTACT everywhere, so the waveform's one-finger drag is a pointer drag again (region/marker vocabulary), the pan-zone query, the resolution fork, `touch_nav_single_` and the upgrade's rebase are DELETED, and one-finger touch pan does not exist — pan on glass is the two-finger gesture (or alt+drag with a keyboard). The stems-on-waveform open question the phone model carried is MOOT in its touch form; whether stems leave the waveform for every device remains the architect's open question.

### The Ignore Rules

- A SECOND finger landing while the pointer translation is LIVE is THE UPGRADE (the timer-free section above) — the translation ends by ordinary release and the two-finger gesture seeds at the join. (SUPERSEDED, 2026-08-12: phase 1's rule was that this finger is IGNORED whole — "mid-gesture finger-count changes do not mutate a committed gesture". The upgrade replaces it; the accepted cost is the staggered-landing nudge, recorded above.)
- A THIRD finger during the navigation gesture is ignored; a finger landing during the drain is ignored — recorded (the touch-point count), not routed.
- When a gesture's end leaves other fingers on the glass, the remaining fingers are DRAINED — ignored until the glass is clear — rather than opening a fresh translation: the survivor of a committed gesture does not become a new gesture (a judgment recorded at the phase machine; the alternative would let a sloppy two-finger lift re-arm as a fresh press).

### The Hard Ends

`wl_touch.cancel` (the compositor claims the touches) and TOUCH capability loss share ONE contract (`hard_end_touch_stream`, the pointer-capability-loss precedent):

- a live pointer translation gets its release DELIVERED at the last position — one delivered release on the logical edge, a commit, not a vanish (a vanished hold would latch the drag-modal gate with no event left to lift it) — then the focus-forked translation end on the release's own edge, exactly as the finger's own lift delivers it (`deliver_touch_translation_end`, the one owner: the ordinary leave, or the restore motion at a focused mouse — The Translation Contract above; a sibling-held logical left suppresses release, leave and restore alike here too: whatever happened to the glass, the mouse is still mid-press and its live press claim is not this stream's to destroy);
- a live NAV gesture ENDS through its end path (commits, iff anything was applied) — but its STAGED FINAL FRAME IS DROPPED, unlike the finger's own lift, which delivers it first: the compositor's claim means that motion retroactively was not ours. The asymmetry with the translation's release is deliberate and recorded at `end_touch_nav_gesture` (the end split): the release MUST deliver because a vanished hold would latch the drag-modal gate with no event left to lift it, while dropped nav motion wedges nothing — the gesture is stateless per frame and holds nothing in the GUI open;
- all touch state is forgotten either way, through the one forget (`forget_touch_state`).

(The contract's Pending arm — "an unresolved disambiguation window is dropped silently" — is DELETED with the window, 2026-08-12: nothing undelivered exists any more; a first finger has always already pressed.)

When a touch end delivers a leave at all — the translation end's no-focus arm (a focused mouse gets the restore motion instead) — it is ORDINARY, never CapabilityLoss: that reason names the wl_pointer stream's own death, and a touch edge says nothing about the mouse. Shutdown (`destroy_wayland_state`) is NOT an input edge: the proxy is released with no deliveries, exactly as the keyboard and pointer teardowns there deliver nothing. The pointer- and keyboard-capability edges never reach into touch state, and touch edges never reach into theirs.

### Touch Never Writes the Pointer's Fields

A deliberate split, recorded at the state block: `pointer_x_`/`pointer_y_` are wl_pointer's own — the wheel emits there, the bare-`e` press lands there, the capture machinery rewrites them — and every touch delivery carries its own explicit coordinates, so the unified "one pointer" view lives in the DELIVERIES while each device's platform state stays self-consistent. Touch likewise does not clear `pointer_position_unknown_` (a touch says nothing about where the mouse cursor is drawn) and gates NO DELIVERY on `pointer_focused_` (wl_touch delivers only to the touched surface; a mouse-focus bit is not its business). The split has ONE deliberate reader since codex round 3: the translation end (`deliver_touch_translation_end`) reads `pointer_focused_` — and `pointer_x_`/`pointer_y_` on the focused arm — because the end is a MOUSE question ("is the mouse resting in the window, and where"), not a touch delivery to gate. Consequence, accepted: a stray mouse click during a touch session routes at the MOUSE's last known position, which is that click's honest answer.

### No Hover Under Touch — Cues, Not Functions

A finger has no resting position: contact IS a press (the timer-free model — the first finger presses on contact, always) and lift is the translation end — a leave, or the restore motion at a focused mouse (the round-3 fork, The Translation Contract above) — so the hover-derived surfaces simply never fire FROM GLASS: button hover faces, the 700 ms shift-tooltip dwell, and the menu-row armed-hover open all want a pointer that rests where nothing is pressed, and a finger never supplies one (the restore motion re-derives them at the MOUSE's resting position, never the finger's). These are CUES, not functions: every act they advertise is reachable by its chord or its press, so nothing is lost. For the faces and the dwell nothing was special-cased to make this true — it falls out of the end-on-lift contract — but THE ARMED HOVER-OPEN NEEDED ONE REAL GUARD (codex round 2 caught the original claim overclaiming): the armed mode deliberately survives an ordinary leave through row 1's band, so a finger lift there keeps it, and the next tap's CONTACT BURST delivers a pre-press entry motion that reached the hover-open and sprang an anchor's menu one event before the same burst's press toggle-closed it — a tap on Settings/Navigation visibly did nothing. The fix is device-blind, two halves: the platform raises the touch hold before delivering the entry motion (the finger is factually down, so the whole burst honestly reads the primary button held), and the GUI's hover-open refuses under a held primary button (on_motion's no-gesture tail) — a guard that also closes the mouse's own latent case, a held press that armed no gesture sliding along the armed row. So the claim stands BY THE GUARD, not by nature, and the tap opens the menu through the press path exactly as a click does. THE GUARD IS A THREE-MEMBER FAMILY RULE, built one member at a time across rounds 2-3 and stated whole at the close walk in `input_pointer.cpp`: NO ROW-1 HOVER ACT FIRES WHILE THE PRIMARY BUTTON IS HELD — the armed hover-OPEN (round 2, above), the open-menu anchor-SWITCH, and the open-menu hover CLOSE (both round 3; the close was the last unguarded member, added at the planner's judgment on the coder's flag). The SWITCH case: with a menu already OPEN, the burst's held entry motion reached the open-dropdown branch's hover anchor-switch — Settings open, tap Navigation: the motion switched to Navigation and the burst's press then found its own menu open and toggle-closed it, so the tap closed the popup instead of switching to it — and the identical held-primary guard now refuses the switch (the tap switches through the PRESS's own toggle, as a click does). That guard also fixes the mouse's own latent bug at that branch, confirmed at the site: press-hold an item (or the anchor press's drag-into-the-box claim) and slide across the other anchor, and the switch fired mid-press — toggle_dropdown's close wipes the whole Dropdown struct, destroying the popup's live press claim out from under the held button. The CLOSE case changes no tap outcome — press-anywhere-closes at on_button_press already closes the menu on the burst's own press, so the tap nets the same result by the cleaner path — while for the mouse a mid-press slide across a non-anchor (Quit, the view bar's three) now keeps the popup's live press claim exactly as the switch guard keeps it. Every unheld hover act is untouched. Two recorded edges of the same coin: whatever a resting HELD pointer does today (a pressed button's face, a dwell that elapses under a held press) a held finger does identically — the unified premise means there is no touch-specific answer either way — and a finger lifting with a mouse resting in the window delivers the translation end's RESTORE MOTION at the mouse's own position (the round-3 focus fork, in The Translation Contract above), so the resting mouse's hover face and cursor are re-derived from truth on the lift itself rather than stranded at the finger's zone; the leave — and the face loss it briefly carried for a coincidentally resting mouse, recorded here as an accepted glitch between rounds 2 and 3 — now fires only when no mouse is focused, where there is no face to lose. The one strand left in the accepted-glitch class is the sibling-suppressed end (a mouse held mid-press when the finger lifts), which delivers nothing and self-heals on the next pointer event.

(An inherited oddity stood here until the trim surface arc: a one-finger ruler drag WAS the strip drag, whose machinery captures the real pointer — so a touch ruler-drag hid the mouse cursor and the release warped the mouse to the anchor stem's column. The ruler's strip-drag entry is DELETED with the merge below, so the only pointer-capturing gestures reachable from glass are the waveform's ctrl and alt drags, which need a keyboard anyway; the record stays because the class — a touch-driven pointer gesture that captures the mouse — is real, and under the timer-free model it returns whenever such a gesture gains a bare plain-drag surface.)

### The Trim Surface Arc (Third Glass Session, 2026-08-11)

THE RULING (architect, the third glass session — a REDESIGN OF THE GESTURE ECONOMY from glass experience, his ranking: pan #1, zoom #2, region-draw #3, trim-move #4, the rare one; his words: "make the timestamp lane the same as the trim — a click on the timestamp translates into trim... drop zoom support there because zoom is already well supported in the waveform, touch and non-touch... let's experiment with making the region draw a drag on the trim bar"). BOTH DEVICES, UNIFIED — this arc is not touch-scoped code, it is the pointer surface reshaped with the finger in mind, and touch inherits all of it with ZERO new touch code:

- THE RULER MERGED INTO THE TRIM SURFACE: the trim bar, the ruler and their gap are ONE input band (`top_trim_surface_area`), every trim gesture and the span-framing double-click answering over its whole height — the endcap grab included, a fatter finger target being half the arc's point. The ruler's strip-drag ZOOM entry is DELETED (`arm_strip_drag_at` is one-entry again — the ctrl-waveform press); the ruler's PAINT is untouched.
- PLAIN DRAG ON THE BAND = REGION DRAW: anchor at the press column, playhead riding the moving end, the waveform placement press's region arm re-expressed on the band (its press-time acts deferred to the drag threshold so the band's motionless click stays a consumed nothing — `RegionDragState::band`). The BRIDGE (move-both-bounds) drag is DISPLACED off the plain press — to CTRL+DRAG at this session, and onto ALT+DRAG at the fourth (the section below); the ctrl+CLICK bound-set stays, split from the drag by the band's own 8px threshold.
- ON GLASS under the timer-free model that means: a one-finger drag on the band is a plain pointer drag FROM CONTACT — region draw off the caps, that bound's extend from an endcap — and a tap is the double-click's first click. (A POINTER-SIDE REVERT of this arc's merged band is a separate ruling landing in the NEXT commit; this file does not describe it.)

### The Trim Move (Fourth Glass Session, 2026-08-11; TOUCH HALF SUPERSEDED 2026-08-12)

THE RULING (architect, the fourth glass session, closing the third's recorded gap): his VOCABULARY split — ctrl = zoom (the waveform's ctrl-drag), ALT = move/pan (alt-drag pans the waveform) — so MOVING the trim window is ALT+DRAG on the merged band, and on glass "hold for a beat and then drag on the trim bar moves the trim bar — including the timestamp region."

THE MOUSE HALF, unified-pointer code with no touch in it (standing as of this commit; the pointer-side revert lands next commit): the bridge (pair) drag's modifier SWAPPED ctrl → alt (the arm moved to on_button_press's alt branch, `point_in_trim_bridge_span` unchanged; the cursor map's TrimResize rides the alt arm now).
