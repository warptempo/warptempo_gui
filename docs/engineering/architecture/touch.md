Retune the constants on glass, not on argument.

### The Unified-Mode Ruling

THE PREMISE IS ONE UNIFIED MODE, the architect's one settled ruling for the arc: same look, same behavior, same interaction language for mouse and touch. Phase 1 makes it literal — TOUCH IS THE POINTER, translated whole at the platform boundary (`platform_wayland.cpp`), and the GUI cannot tell which device produced an event. No touch mode, no flag, no detection, no GUI-side branch. Everything inherits by construction: every gate, refusal, modal claim, read-only rule, hit test and gesture the pointer has applies to a finger with zero new GUI semantics — stated here once rather than re-proven per gesture. The premise carries TWO ruled divergences since the eighth glass ruling, both scoped to the pan zone: the phone model's single-finger pan (its section below) and the region hold (the Pan-Primary section below), which drives the same region former the mouse's shift press drives; the only other touch-specific gesture is the two-finger navigation. THE MODAL DIALOG IS IN-WINDOW AGAIN, which keeps that inheritance intact for the modal surfaces too: a finger meets the veil, the field and the buttons exactly as the mouse does, and the glass modal trap stays closed by the dialog's own Cancel button, which every editor dialog publishes and the veil admits (the Quit/Save roster reach-through shrank to SAVE ALONE on 2026-08-13 when the Quit button became the File menu's item, and the exit it used to provide is the modal's own button now — conventions.md's modality section carries the ruling) (the PAINTED GATE of 2026-08-13 rides the same inheritance — a tap on a prompt button answers only once the box has painted, because the gate lives on the pointer's response claim, which is where a finger arrives) — the real-labwc-window dialog of 2026-08-12..13, whose per-surface input routing would have been the arc's one platform-level device branch, was reverted whole on 2026-08-13 (conventions.md's modality section carries the succession and the do-not-re-propose).

### The Windowed Model Returns (Sixth Glass Ruling, 2026-08-12 — Authoritative)

THE FIELD VERDICT THAT DROVE IT: the timer-free model (781ef948, the fifth ruling, that morning) went to glass the same day, and everything worked EXCEPT ZOOM — with the press landing at contact, every pinch whose fingers land even slightly apart first jumps the playhead: "it bounces around as you're zooming... every time I pinch, it jumps. That's the part that's distracting." The architect ruled the window back — "let's go back to having delay... with my phone I'm used to a delay" — and, told the ramifications, ratified the trade: THE WINDOW BUYS GESTURE VOCABULARY FOR ~60 MS OF TAP LATENCY. What the delay buys back: the jump-free pinch (zoom "extremely important especially given that the screen is smaller"), the tap/quick-drag/hold triple per surface, and the phone-model pan — which is also what killed the interim scrollbar plan. Tap-at-lift is his explicit ask: "moving the playhead was set to finger lift... let's go back to that."

THE RESTORED MACHINE is the last windowed one (0e633bcf/3035078a) minus the trim hold: phases {Idle, Pending, Pointer, Nav, Drain}, the 60 ms window resolving to a TAP at the lift (the whole burst at the lift — enter-motion + press at the down point, any queued motion, release, translation end), to TWO-FINGER NAV on a second down inside it (nothing ever delivered — the ruling's whole point), on slop-crossing motion to the PAN-ZONE FORK (waveform → single-finger nav, the phone model; elsewhere → the pointer with the crossing as queued motion), and on EXPIRY to the POINTER everywhere (hold unlocks the pointer — no band exception any more). (The expiry clause and the waveform-only zone were SUPERSEDED the same day by the eighth ruling's touch half, the Pan-Primary section below: the zone is the navigation surface now, and its expiry is the region hold — the pointer unlock survives off the zone.)

THE ONE KEEPER from the timer-free model, the architect's explicit ruling this session: the Pointer phase tracks whether the live translation has MOVED (Chebyshev ≥ `kTouchSlopPx` from the down point, latched once — `touch_translation_moved_`), and a SECOND finger landing during a live translation FORKS on it:

- MOVED (a live drag — marker, region, trim, the overview lane's box and bound drags): IGNORED whole, phase 1's own rule — "mid-gesture finger-count changes do not mutate a committed gesture." THE RULE IS EXCEPTIONLESS, and that is a restoration: the OVERVIEW LANE was excepted for one commit on 2026-08-15 (a moved translation there admitted the second finger, which was the only way its two-finger bounds gesture could be entered in practice — two fingers essentially never land inside the 60 ms window, so the first crossed the 8 px slop first and this guard discarded the second), and the exception went the same day with the gesture it existed for. A second finger arriving during ANY moved translation is ignored and the drag it interrupts simply continues, which is the architect's original ruling for this lane and the same thing a third finger does on the waveform.
- MOTIONLESS (a hold): THE UPGRADE — the translation ends through the one owner (`deliver_touch_translation_end`), then two-finger nav seeds at the join. The hold's press already placed the playhead at the window's expiry, so the upgrade adds NO additional jump — it only keeps a slow pinch (fingers landing more than the window apart) alive instead of dead; a sub-latch release of that pair does nothing further. THAT END IS THE **ABNORMAL** ONE since codex round 19 (2026-08-15), and the correction is one sentence: AN UPGRADE IS A CHANGE OF GESTURE, NOT A LIFT — the user did not raise a finger, they added one, and the ordinary release the upgrade used to deliver was SPEAKING FOR THE FINGER THEY DID NOT LIFT. Every act-at-lift surface read that release as a completed CLICK, and a Pointer-phase hold sits OFF the pan zone by construction, which is exactly where those surfaces are: **the class is every CHROME BUTTON** (all of them act at the lift since 2026-08-13 — a finger held on Render, Save, a transport button, a menu item or a modal's OK fired it the instant a second finger touched down, so a pinch ran a command), and the case codex found is **a finger held inside a STANDING REGION** (the region editor's carve-out puts it off the zone), where the motionless release's click act placed the playhead and hid the overlay on the upper half, or fired the scrub and its playback edge on the lower. **THE CLASS SHRANK ON 2026-08-17 and the loss is recorded rather than repaired:** the marker flag's click went back to the PRESS with the third clause (`conventions.md`), so a hold on a flag SELECTS AND LANDS at the 60ms expiry — where the resolution delivers the press — and the abnormal end has nothing left to withhold there. Un-committing a flag press was a property of the lift model and it is gone; undo is the mitigation, the standing answer for a pointer act that has no cancel. The nav surface's deferred click, which is the case above, is unaffected: its press still commits nothing. THE FIX REUSES THE PRODUCT'S EXISTING RULED SPELLING rather than adding a suppression: the abnormal end is what a LOST BUTTON already means — the hold bit drops so nothing sticks, the GUI is told through a MOTION carrying `primary_button_held` false, a MOVED drag finalizes exactly as a lost button does and an UNMOVED press commits NOTHING FURTHER (no click act still owed, no double-click seed, and no chrome dispatch, dispatching an armed press being the release's job). **NOTHING FURTHER RATHER THAN NOTHING**, and the qualifier is the shrink recorded two sentences up rather than a second ruling: a resolved MARKER touch has already run its click act at the synthesized press, and the abnormal end drops the `PendingMarkerPress` ALONE — the selection, the land and the playback stop it committed stand (the pending arm in `on_motion`, `input_pointer.cpp`), with undo the mitigation. What an unmoved press still commits nothing of is what was never owed until the lift: the nav surface's deferred click, the double-click seed, the armed chrome/modal/menu dispatch. **THAT FIX WAS INCOMPLETE FOR ONE ROUND, AND THE REASONING THAT LET IT PASS IS THE PART WORTH KEEPING** (codex round 20): the armed chrome/modal/menu presses are NOT button-lost consumers — unlike the motion-driven drag states, which have a button-lost end apiece, those three are claims on a FUTURE RELEASE, and the only thing that dropped them was the pointer-LEAVE hook. So "the standing lost-button shape" was true of one family and false of the other, and on the focused-mouse arm of the end's own focus fork — where the restore motion runs INSTEAD of the leave — a stale `ChromePress` survived the entire pinch, swallowing the next tap or dispatching its old command on a later release over the original target. Reusing a concept across two families that do not share it is what let it through. THE ANSWER IS TO GIVE THEM THE END THEY LACKED, at the one place the edge is observable: an unheld MOTION while one of them stands drops all three (`clear_release_time_press_arms`, at `on_motion`'s top), so the abnormal end reaches them on BOTH arms of the fork and a lost PHYSICAL button reaches them by the same sentence. THE PROMISE IS NOW FLAT: an upgrade ends every arm the vanished press could have committed, motion-driven and release-time alike, because the finger that armed them is not going to lift. What stays with the leave hook is what the pointer's POSITION owns — the roster's hover faces, the menu row's armed mode, the tooltip. `end_touch_left_hold(bool clean_release)` carries the fork; the two LIFT-shaped callers (the finger's own up, the hard end) pass true, the upgrade is the one caller that passes false.

THE REVERSED DESIGN, recorded so it is not re-proposed: a two-finger REGION gesture (hold one finger, tap/drag the second to span a region) was designed in the same session and REVERSED before landing — "I don't think hold-the-finger-down-and-tap-the-other is the right solution." The architect ratified the pinch half of the motionless-hold fork and dropped the region half.

### Pan-Primary, the Touch Half (Eighth Glass Ruling, 2026-08-12 — Authoritative)

THE RULING (architect, dictated with the mouse half and ratified whole; the touch half landed one commit after it): pan is the most common gesture and takes the PRIMARY drag on both devices — "drag to be single finger... region select to be hold and then drag because the pan is way more common" — so on glass the one-finger drag PANS and the deliberate region takes the HOLD, at his own duration: "maybe five hundred milliseconds". The lower half stayed reserved that day — "the lower half I'd like to reserve for scrubbing" — and SPENT ONE DAY OFF THE ZONE (superseded 2026-08-13, The Two Halves Become One Zone below).

THE GROWN ZONE: `touch_point_in_pan_zone` answers THE NAVIGATION SURFACE — the mouse vocabulary's own plain-drag surface, spelled exactly as the press router derives it: the waveform (its UPPER half on the day of this ruling, the WHOLE waveform since 2026-08-13) + the RULER + the MARKER lane MINUS its flag boxes (the painter's published rects — a finger landing on a flag resolves to the POINTER, so a quick flag drag is the immediate marker drag, the mouse's own carve-out). For that one day the LOWER half was OFF the zone — scrub was not pan, so a quick drag there resolved to the pointer and fired the scrub at resolution, the press's own act, accepted. Still asked ONCE at the first finger's down, surface geometry only, every refusal downstream.

THE TWO-DEADLINE FORK, the dead trim-band beat's exact pattern reborn: the zone answer picks the window's deadline at the down — ON the zone `kTouchRegionHoldMs` (the REGION-HOLD BEAT, which since 2026-08-16 IS `kHoldBeatMs` = 575, the product's one hold beat matched by convention to the compositor's key-repeat delay: the unification paragraph in Glass Gains the Shift Acts below carries the ruling, and the constant's whole argument is at `kHoldBeatMs`, gui_input.h; it rested at its own 500 from the eighth ruling until then), OFF it `kTouchDisambiguateMs` = 60 as before. The window's resolutions under the fork:

- UP inside = the TAP, everywhere: the whole pointer burst at the lift. On the navigation surface that burst's motionless press-release IS the mouse half's deferred click act — the playhead placement at the tap point — structurally the mouse click, nothing touch-side built for it. ON CHROME the same burst is the act-at-release pair for free (2026-08-13, kdenlive-redesign.md's act-at-release section): the burst's press arms and its release lands on the same button and acts, and a finger that slides off before lifting delivers its crossing as queued motion, so the lift lands off the button and cancels exactly as a mouse does. On the marker lane's empty stretch the same release seeds the EmptyLane double-click candidate, so DOUBLE-TAP reaches the marker CREATE (the verification trace below) — the create firing at the SECOND burst's PRESS since 2026-08-17, which inside a tap is the same instant its release arrives. ON A MARKER FLAG the burst's PRESS is the whole click (the third clause, `conventions.md`), so a tap selects at the moment it lifts and a HOLD selects at the 60ms expiry, where the resolution delivers that press.
- SLOP-CROSSING = the fork, unchanged: on the zone → SINGLE-FINGER NAV (the phone model's pan, the existing machinery whole); off it → the pointer resolution (trim-bar drags, flag drags, button presses; the lower-half quick drag was in this arm for one day and is on the zone now — the section below).
- SECOND DOWN inside = two-finger nav (the jump-free pinch), unchanged, whatever the zone answer.
- EXPIRY forks on the zone: OFF the zone → the pointer unlock at 60 ms, unchanged — hold-then-drag is still how the endcap/bridge grabs and every off-zone press-and-hold gesture work on glass. ON the zone, at the beat → THE REGION HOLD.

The refusal list lives in the begin (prompt, the seven editors, open dropdown, loading/empty audio, live pointer gesture — the dead trim-move begin's list MINUS the `h` view); a refused begin makes the stream dead through the drag's own !active guard. ### The Two Halves Become One Zone (2026-08-13 — Authoritative for the Zone)

THE MOUSE RULING'S TOUCH CONSEQUENCE, EMBRACED RATHER THAN ACCEPTED (architect 2026-08-13, at the same turn as the mouse half — `pointer-hit-testing.md` carries the full ruling): "the consequence to the touch is accepted, and in fact it's embraced, because currently finger down works in the upper half by waiting for the finger up, but on the lower half it immediately dispatches the scanner — so there's an asymmetry, and now that I understand it we should eliminate the asymmetry." The mouse's press-time scrub moved to the motionless release, so the lower half stopped being a surface of its own — and THE PAN ZONE IS THE WHOLE WAVEFORM NOW, plus the ruler and the marker lane's empty stretches, minus the flag boxes. The eighth ruling's "the lower half I'd like to reserve for scrubbing" is SUPERSEDED, one day old.

WHAT THAT BUYS ON GLASS, all of it inherited with ZERO touch code: a one-finger drag PANS anywhere on the waveform (the phone model over the whole surface); the REGION HOLD reaches anywhere on it, so hold-then-drag sweeps a region in the lower half too; a TAP on the lower half delivers its motionless press-release burst at the lift and that burst IS the deferred scrub act, at the tap point; and the two-finger pinch was never zone-scoped and is unchanged. The `h` view is unaffected — its whole waveform was already the zone.

IT DID NOT FOLLOW FOR FREE, and the record matters because the file claimed it would. `touch_point_in_pan_zone` was written as "spelled exactly as the press router derives it" but was in fact a HAND COPY of that derivation — its own `inside_waveform` arithmetic plus its own `!waveform_lower_half` term — so the mouse-side change would have left the zone behind. The fix is structural rather than a re-edit: the surface's geometry now has ONE owner, `point_on_nav_surface` (input_pointer.cpp), and this query is a single call to it, alongside the press router's shift claim, the ctrl strip-drag claim, the cursor map and the `h` view's router. FROM HERE IT DOES FOLLOW FOR FREE, by construction rather than by discipline.

THE CAPTURE-FROM-GLASS ODDITY STAYS CLOSED: the lower half's new gestures are the nav hooks' pan (no pointer events at all) and the region hook gesture (captures nothing), and its click act captures nothing either. Nothing on the grown zone reaches a capturing drag.

WHY THE BEAT CANNOT REPEAT THE TRIM-MOVE REGRESSION: the one-day `kTouchTrimHoldMs` died because the band's QUICK drag was a pointer drag, so an aimed drag's natural dwell expired into the move. On the pan zone the quick drag is the PAN — a different gesture class entirely — and only a deliberately motionless finger reaches the beat.

THE TRIM MOVE ITSELF STAYS DEAD: neither `kTouchTrimHoldMs` nor `TouchPhase::TrimMove` returned — the region hold revives the hook PATTERN, not that gesture (The Trim Move below carries the record). The trim bar is an ordinary pointer surface on glass — a hold past the 60 ms window then a drag, or a quick drag crossing the slop inside it, reaches the endcap/bridge machinery through the ordinary pointer path, and a double-tap on the bar is the span framing (two tap bursts, the same double-click machinery as the mouse).

### The Timer-Free Model — the One-Session Experiment (Fifth Glass Ruling, 2026-08-12; Superseded the Same Day)

THE RECORD, kept whole so its lesson is not relearned: the architect dictated pointer-on-contact that morning ("I'd rather have a more responsive interface... building another lag is a no go") — the first finger delivered the press burst AT CONTACT (`deliver_touch_contact_press`), a second finger upgraded by ordinary release + nav-seed-at-join, and the window, both timing constants, the deadline sampling, the three resolvers, the pan-zone/trim-band queries, the single-finger nav and the trim-move hook family were all DELETED (−400 net lines; the machine ran {Idle, Pointer, Nav, Drain}). It went to glass the same day and died there — the staggered-landing playhead nudge, accepted on paper, was intolerable in the hand at every pinch. THE LESSON: pointer-on-contact is unbeatable for responsiveness and unusable for pinch disambiguation — the two fingers of a real pinch land tens of milliseconds apart, so any model that presses on the first contact jumps before it can zoom. The window returned at the sixth ruling with the experiment's one durable piece, the motionless-hold upgrade (above).

### The Binding

`wl_touch` is CORE protocol — wl_seat's third capability, the `wl_data_device_manager` precedent: the same wayland.xml, no new library, no pkg-config entry, no generated stub. It is bound from the existing seat listener when `WL_SEAT_CAPABILITY_TOUCH` is advertised and released on capability loss (through the hard-end contract below). It is NOT a required global and NOT a required capability: absence is SILENCE — no stderr, nothing degraded. The laptop simply has no glass; a seat without touch is the ordinary case, not a poor environment. Touch events arrive batched under `wl_touch.frame` and are processed per frame like pointer frames: down/up edges dispatch at arrival (the button model), motion coalesces to the frame boundary (the captured-relative-motion model), so a sensor-rate panel costs one delivery per frame.

### The Translation Contract (One Finger IS the Pointer)

The bare-`e` precedent — a key translated at the boundary into the left mouse button — applied to glass. The translation state is the platform's own, keyed on the touch id; the GUI sees ordinary pointer deliveries. The phase machine and the AUTHORITATIVE edge inventory live at the touch state block in `platform_wayland.h` (the `transport_repeat` precedent: one list at the state, each fire site keeping only its own clause); the shape:

- FIRST touch-down (no touch state live) opens the DISAMBIGUATION WINDOW: {id, position, deadline} remembered, NOTHING delivered — plus the down point's PAN-ZONE answer captured once (the zone query, in The Phone Model below). The window exists to tell tap from drag from two fingers from the region hold; its deadline is the zone answer's own — the region-hold beat (`kTouchRegionHoldMs`) on the zone, `kTouchDisambiguateMs` off it (the eighth ruling's two-deadline fork, the Pan-Primary section above).
- The window RESOLVES TO POINTER on the finger lifting inside it (a tap, whichever deadline) and on the OFF-ZONE EXPIRY; the ON-ZONE expiry is the REGION HOLD (the Pan-Primary section above). Motion beyond the slop (`kTouchSlopPx`) FORKS on the captured zone answer: on the pan surface it resolves to SINGLE-FINGER NAV (no pointer events, ever — The Phone Model below), elsewhere to POINTER. Pointer resolution delivers the synthesized enter-motion at the ORIGINAL down point, then the left press there, then any queued motion — and for the tap, the release immediately after. THE TOUCH HOLD BIT IS RAISED BEFORE THE ENTRY MOTION (codex round 2), so every delivery in the resolution burst — the pre-press motion included — reads the primary button held: the finger has factually been down since the window opened, and that held reading is what the GUI's armed hover-open guard keys on (below). From then on touch motion is pointer motion and touch up is the release.
- The live translation carries the MOVED LATCH (the sixth glass ruling): seeded by the resolver from the window's own travel (a slop-crossing resolution enters already moved; expiry and tap enter motionless) and latched once by the Pointer motion arm at the same Chebyshev slop from the down point. A SECOND finger landing during the translation forks on it — moved = ignored whole, motionless = the upgrade, whose end is THE ABNORMAL one (codex round 19, The Windowed Model Returns above) — WITH THE THIN LANES' ONE EXCEPTION since 2026-08-15, where the overview strip and the trim bar ignore the second finger WHETHER THE FIRST HAS MOVED OR NOT, so a lane drag simply continues (the lane's own moved-arm exception of that morning, which admitted the second finger to reach a two-finger bounds gesture, went the same day with that gesture; The Overview Lane on Glass below carries both, and the edge inventory at the state block is authoritative).
- A touch-up that ends a pointer translation delivers the release on the logical left's 1→0 edge and then THE TRANSLATION END ON THAT SAME EDGE, which FORKS ON PHYSICAL POINTER FOCUS since codex round 3 (one owner, `deliver_touch_translation_end`, shared by the touch-up site, the motionless-hold upgrade and the hard-end contract — the upgrade being the one caller that asks for the ABNORMAL end, `clean_release = false`, so what rides the edge there is the lost-button MOTION and not a release; everything below is identical for all three): with NO mouse focused on the surface, an ORDINARY pointer leave (`GuiPointerLeaveReason::OrdinaryLeave`) — the finger left the glass, which IS a leave, and the remembered-position machinery and the menu-row band rules already handle ordinary leaves with nothing special-cased; with the physical pointer FOCUSED, an ordinary restore MOTION at the mouse's last platform-tracked position (`pointer_x_`/`pointer_y_`, which touch never writes) INSTEAD of the leave — the finger lifted, so the unified pointer is now where the mouse is, and the motion re-derives hover, the menu-row exit test and the loop-settled cursor from truth (the round-3 cursor-residue fix). The restore motion runs the ordinary motion path WHOLE, deliberately unmarked — so with the menu row ARMED and the mouse resting on an anchor it hover-opens that menu, and with a menu OPEN and the mouse resting on the other anchor it hover-switches: judged the armed mode's own defining semantics for a pointer standing at that position, not surprises to suppress (suppression would need a "restore" mark on the motion, a device branch in spirit; recorded at the definition too). WHEN A SIBLING SOURCE STILL HOLDS the logical left (physical BTN_LEFT or bare-`e`), the release is suppressed and THE WHOLE END IS TOO — leave and restore alike (codex round 2): the unified pointer has not meaningfully left — the mouse is still there, mid-press — and the leave hook's clears are not mere hover cleanup (they drop the armed chrome press, the modal's armed button and an open popup's live press claim, state that belongs to the still-held press, not to the finger that lifted). A suppressed end can strand a hover face where the finger last was — the accepted-glitch class, self-healing on the next pointer event. The hard-end contract takes the same edge rule and the same fork.
- THE LOGICAL LEFT BUTTON IS ONE BIT WITH THREE SOURCES: `pointer_left_held_ || synth_left_held_ || touch_left_held_` — the evdev model for devices sharing BTN_LEFT, extended from the bare-`e` pair. A press is delivered only on the OR's 0→1 edge and a release only on its 1→0 edge, so the sources never double-deliver, and `current_mods()` ORs all three into `primary_button_held` (without which a touch drag would tear on its first motion). Each source's hold ends on ITS OWN stream's edges only: the keyboard edges end the synth hold, pointer-capability loss ends the physical and synth holds, the touch edges end the touch hold.
- The resolution press is a pointer-button press in full: it kills an armed key repeat (layer 1 of the repeat contract — the edge list at `on_keyboard_key` names three press producers) and flushes both staged motions ahead of itself (the `flush_deferred_motion` invariant, which the touch delivery sites joined).
- Physical keyboard modifiers ride along to touch deliveries exactly as to a mouse: `current_mods()` is the one modifier truth, so Shift+tap is a Shift+click with no new code.

### The Disambiguation Constants (Ruled Retunables)

All three live in `platform_wayland.cpp`'s anonymous namespace, with the full tuning rationale at the site:

- `kTouchDisambiguateMs` = 60 — the one-finger-or-two window, the OFF-ZONE deadline since the eighth ruling. Sampled on the timerfd tick beside the key-repeat deadline (the run loop's one deadline-sampling spot) plus lazily at every touch event's arrival, so expiry lands within one tick (≤ ~16 ms) of the mark; the window is a feel bound, not an exact timer. Off-zone expiry resolves to the pointer — hold unlocks the pointer there.

### Two Fingers = Zoom Only (Navigation-Class)

Both fingers down inside the window — or a second finger joining a MOTIONLESS held translation (the upgrade, The Windowed Model Returns above) or a live single-finger pan (the rebase, The Phone Model below) — is the NAVIGATION gesture, and since 2026-08-14 it is ZOOM AND NOTHING ELSE: the per-frame finger-distance ratio zooms ABOUT A PIVOT THE GESTURE SEATS ON THE WAVEFORM AND HOLDS (the paragraph below; it was the LIVE CENTROID's column until later that same day), and the centroid's own horizontal travel is DISCARDED. THE GESTURE NEVER PANS. It never delivers pointer events. THIS SECTION DESCRIBES THE WAVEFORM'S GESTURE: a two-finger gesture whose FIRST FINGER CAME DOWN ON THE OVERVIEW LANE is REFUSED before any of it (The Overview Lane on Glass below, 2026-08-15) and takes none of the machinery here — no seat, no stem, no ratio. THE REFUSAL READS THE DOWN POINT AND NOT THE LIVE CENTROID, so this gesture KEEPS THE PINCH for its whole life even if the fingers carry the centroid onto the strip — the mirror of a lane pair being refused wherever the fingers wander, and both halves are wanted.

THE PINCH SEATS ITS PIVOT AND HOLDS IT (architect 2026-08-14, from the rig, later the same day than the zoom-only ruling below — carrying the mouse's own song-anchored pivot onto glass): "when the two-finger touch is first registered, it picks the point on the waveform, and the zoom pivot stays there no matter where the two fingers move on the screen." THE PIVOT IS A SONG FRAME, not a place on the glass: seated the moment the pinch REGISTERS — at the first two-finger frame that survives the `wheel_context` refusal, at the frame under that frame's centroid column — and held for the phase's life with its COLUMN re-derived against the live viewport every frame after. `apply_touch_nav_update` (input_pointer.cpp) forks its anchor on the finger count now — the one-finger pan stays per-frame anchored and stateless, the two-finger phase reads the seat — and `AppState::touch_nav_zoom` (`TouchNavZoomState`, app_state.h) is the gesture's FIRST GUI-side record; the old "every frame is applied whole and forgotten" is retired with it, at every site that said so.

THE POINT IS THE ONE THE MOUSE'S OWN FIX MADE (zoom-viewport-strip.md's `ScrollDragState` record, and the same wall case): away from the walls a live centroid and a held frame name the same point — a zoom has no pan term, so the content under a fixed column cannot move on its own — and they diverge only where the viewport SATURATES at frame 0 or the right edge. There the view is determined by the level alone, so a pivot pinned to the glass lets the audio drift out from under the fingers and pinching back out never returns what it came from; a HELD FRAME keeps its grip and slides its column across the glass instead, which is what makes the pinch reversible. THE EDGE CLAMP-AND-REBIND COMES WITH IT, `apply_nav_zoom_at`'s pivot block exactly: a pivot column pushed outside the waveform pins at the edge pixel and REBINDS the held frame to that pixel's content, so the zoom's focus never leaves the screen — the stateless model deliberately did without it because it had no persistent anchor for an off-screen column to corrupt, and now it does.

THE PARITY ARGUMENT IS THE ARCHITECT'S OWN, and it is the whole reason this landed on glass rather than staying a desk fix: "I imagine my right hand as being the same right hand on the touchpad, and then the left hand reaches for the control key and implicitly does the opposite... I'm keeping the same mental model on the laptop as I would like to have on the touchpad." The right hand plays the same part on both surfaces and CTRL is the left hand doing implicitly what the second finger does, so the two surfaces now agree on the pivot as well as on the sentence (PAN BY DEFAULT, ADD THE ZOOM MODIFIER AT ANY TIME, DROP IT AT ANY TIME). WHAT IT DOES NOT CHANGE, stated because the seat is easy to over-read: the fingers still zoom BY THEIR DISTANCE RATIO ALONE, so moving both fingers together still applies approximately nothing (ratio 1, centroid travel discarded — the zoom-only ruling untouched); moving ONE finger now zooms about the GRABBED point rather than about the moving midpoint.

THE SEAT'S LIFECYCLE, three rules: it is TAKEN at the first two-finger frame that SURVIVES THE `wheel_context` REFUSAL — so a frame the gesture refuses seats nothing and the gesture still navigates exactly the wheel's two surfaces — and DELIBERATELY NOT at the first frame that APPLIES anything (2026-08-14, correcting the shape this shipped with hours earlier and restoring the architect's own words, "when the two-finger touch is first registered, it picks the point on the waveform"): the zoom-only ruling forces `eff_dx` to a literal zero on every two-finger frame, so a pair landing and sliding TOGETHER carries only centroid travel, meets the exact-no-op return and dies there — under the old ordering that pinch seated nothing and painted no stem, and the pivot was in truth taken at the first frame whose finger DISTANCE changed, at whatever column the centroid had drifted to by then rather than at the point the fingers grabbed. THE SEAT THEREFORE SITS ABOVE THE EXACT-NO-OP RETURN and below the refusal, which is the ordering the two acts always deserved: seating is a NAVIGATION act and takes the refusal, while "this frame's deltas apply nothing" is a statement about the frame and has no business deciding where the gesture is anchored. Nothing about the seat's VALUE changed, only when it is taken; the double-click clear stays BELOW the no-op return (an applied frame invalidates a pending candidate, a frame that applies nothing must not), which is why the seat was hoisted around it rather than the return moved. It is CLEARED by any frame that ARRIVES not-two-finger, refused or not, which is the DOWNGRADE (the survivor's pan takes a fresh pivot from nothing, and a later upgrade re-seats) — the seat's two halves sit on OPPOSITE SIDES OF THE REFUSAL on purpose, seating being a navigation act and clearing being bookkeeping: a one-finger frame means the pinch is over whether or not that frame gets to navigate, and holding the anchor through a refused stretch of the survivor's pan would let a later upgrade zoom about a song frame the fingers had long since left; and it is CLEARED at `end_touch_nav`, every end included — a finger lift, `wl_touch.cancel`, touch-capability loss — so nothing can inherit a dead pinch's anchor. It cannot survive a hard end by construction: a seat exists only where a frame was DELIVERED, which is exactly the condition the end hook is owed on (the end split's own rule, at `end_touch_nav_gesture`). AND A FOURTH RULE JOINED THEM AT CODEX ROUND 20 — **A VIEW SWITCH CLEARS THE SEAT**, at all three switches (`t`, `Ctrl+Tab`, `p`) — all three of which stopped hiding the trim region overlay on 2026-08-19, a view change moving no playhead and touching no marker (the seat clear is unaffected: it is a CORRECTNESS rule about a stale song frame, not a view preference) — with the 1/2/3 selectors, the view bar and the S/T + W/P radios inheriting it by composition. `anchor_sample` is a song frame IN THE ACTIVE DOMAIN, and nothing stops a keyboard command or a mouse click on a radio from arriving with two fingers still down: an S/T flip would leave a SOURCE frame to be read as a TARGET one and the pinch would resume about an unrelated song point. The pre-arc stateless pinch could not have this — it kept nothing between frames — so the seat is what brought it, and the clear costs a live pinch nothing (its next frame seats afresh at the centroid, exactly as an upgrade does). THE TEMPTING FIX IS THE WRONG ONE and is recorded at the site: do NOT add touch navigation to `any_pointer_gesture_active` to make the switch unreachable — `apply_touch_nav_update` asks `wheel_context` per frame and `wheel_context` READS that predicate, so a live pinch would refuse itself. A VIEWPORT-ONLY move (`c`, `0`, `Tab`, paging, the wheel) needs no clear and gets none: it changes no domain, so the held frame goes on naming the same audio, and re-deriving its COLUMN against the live viewport every frame is exactly what the seat is for.

The mechanics, all at their sites:

- The ZOOM mapping is LOGARITHMIC and constant-free: `new_level = level − log2(dist_ratio)`, so doubling the finger gap is exactly one zoom level in and the content between the fingers scales with the fingers. Pre-clamped into `[kMinZoom, effective_max_zoom_level]` exactly as every other caller of the chokepoint pre-clamps (its `level_changed` contract names them all).
- THE PLACEMENT IS ONE `apply_strip_drag_zoom` CALL PER FRAME, SHARED WITH THE PHONE MODEL'S PAN, and its ANCHOR FORKS ON THE FINGER COUNT since the seated pivot (2026-08-14 — until then both counts were per-frame anchored off the live centroid, "no persistent song anchor and no edge-trick rebind to need", which is exactly the sentence the seat retires). ONE FINGER: per-frame anchored and stateless — the content under the PREVIOUS centroid column placed at the CURRENT centroid column under the new level, the ratio 1.0, a pure pan. TWO FINGERS: the HELD frame placed back at its own re-derived column, the centroid delta zero by the fork, a pure zoom about the grabbed point — with the edge clamp-and-rebind on that column, the paragraph above.
- THE LATCH is the platform's, the strip drag's own press-becomes-drag model in the touch's own unit: nothing is delivered until the centroid travels `kTouchSlopPx` (Chebyshev) from the gesture's start OR the finger distance changes by that same slop, and the crossing update folds the whole accumulated delta — so a two-finger tap navigates nothing and costs the GUI nothing (the end hook fires only if an update was delivered).
- THE REFUSAL ANSWER IS THE WHEEL'S, per frame: the gesture is the wheel's navigation-class sibling — it delivers no pointer events, punches through the keyboard-modal flag editor exactly as the wheel does, and navigates the wheel's two surfaces — so `wheel_context` (the SINGLE wheel routing predicate) is asked at each frame's centroid. - ONE FINGER LIFTING FROM THE PAIR IS THE DOWNGRADE since the one-model ruling (2026-08-14, superseding the drain-the-survivor rule and the "upgrade yes, downgrade no" asymmetry of 2026-08-11..14): the second finger is the ZOOM MODIFIER, droppable at any time, so its lift CONTINUES the gesture as the single-finger pan on the survivor. ### The Ignore Rules

- A SECOND finger landing while a pointer translation is LIVE forks on the MOVED latch (the sixth glass ruling): a MOVED translation — a live drag — IGNORES it whole, recorded (the touch-point count), not routed: mid-gesture finger-count changes do not mutate a committed gesture (the any-end-commits family; the architect's explicit mid-drag ruling). A MOTIONLESS translation — a hold — takes the UPGRADE instead, which ends the translation by THE ABNORMAL END (codex round 19 — an upgrade is a change of gesture, not a lift; the record is in The Windowed Model Returns above). THE THIN LANES ARE THE FORK'S ONE EXCEPTION since 2026-08-15 — a translation whose DOWN POINT lay on the overview strip or the trim bar ignores the second finger whether it has moved or not, so the lane's own drag continues (The Overview Lane on Glass below).
- A second finger landing INSIDE the window resolves to the two-finger gesture, and no press was ever delivered — nothing to unwind, the window's whole purpose.
- A SECOND finger landing during the REGION gesture (the eighth ruling's hold) is ignored whole — a committed gesture, the same family; there is no motionless sub-state to upgrade from, the hold having already become the former.
- A THIRD finger during the navigation gesture is ignored; a finger landing during the drain is ignored.
- When a gesture's end leaves IGNORED fingers on the glass, they are DRAINED — ignored until the glass is clear — rather than opening a fresh window: the survivor of a committed gesture does not become a new gesture (a judgment recorded at the phase machine; the alternative would let a sloppy lift re-arm as a fresh press). Since the one-model ruling the nav PAIR's own survivor is the exception BY RULING, not a drain case at all: one of two nav fingers lifting is the DOWNGRADE, the gesture continuing as its pan — only ignored fingers (a third, or a moved translation's ignored second) drain.

### The Hard Ends

`wl_touch.cancel` (the compositor claims the touches) and TOUCH capability loss share ONE contract (`hard_end_touch_stream`, the pointer-capability-loss precedent):

- a live pointer translation gets its release DELIVERED at the last position — one delivered release on the logical edge, a commit, not a vanish (a vanished hold would latch the drag-modal gate with no event left to lift it) — then the focus-forked translation end on the release's own edge, exactly as the finger's own lift delivers it (`deliver_touch_translation_end`, the one owner: the ordinary leave, or the restore motion at a focused mouse — The Translation Contract above; a sibling-held logical left suppresses release, leave and restore alike here too: whatever happened to the glass, the mouse is still mid-press and its live press claim is not this stream's to destroy);
- a live NAV gesture — single- or two-finger, one arm — ENDS through its end path (commits, iff anything was applied) — but its STAGED FINAL FRAME IS DROPPED, unlike the finger's own lift, which delivers it first: the compositor's claim means that motion retroactively was not ours. The asymmetry with the translation's release is deliberate and recorded at `end_touch_nav_gesture` (the end split): the release MUST deliver because a vanished hold would latch the drag-modal gate with no event left to lift it, while dropped nav MOTION wedges nothing — a frame that never applies simply leaves the viewport where the last delivered frame put it. WHAT THE GESTURE DOES HOLD IS THE SEATED PIVOT, and that is what makes the END HOOK MANDATORY rather than optional (the "stateless per frame, holds nothing open" reading this line carried was true only until the seat landed on 2026-08-14 and inverted the reason): `TouchNavZoomState::seated` is persistent GUI state with a PAINTED consequence — the anchor stem — so a hard end that merely dropped the frame and returned would leave a dead pinch's anchor seated and its stem painted over a settled view, and a later upgrade would zoom about it. The end path is what clears the seat and damages the erase (through `clear_touch_zoom_seat`, one of its seven sites). The DELIVERED-ONLY gate on that hook is safe for exactly one reason, recorded at `end_touch_nav_gesture`: a seat can exist only where a frame was DELIVERED — it is taken by the first two-finger frame surviving the GUI's refusal, applied or not — which is precisely the condition the hook is owed on, so the two can never disagree;
- a live REGION gesture (the eighth ruling) takes the Nav shape — staged final frame DROPPED — but its `region_end` ALWAYS fires once the gesture began, unlike the nav end's delivered-gate: the GUI-side region drag holds the drag-modal gate open and its release path is owed (the trim-move end's old terms; the split is at `end_touch_region_gesture`);
- an unresolved disambiguation window is dropped silently (nothing was delivered, so there is nothing to end);
- all touch state is forgotten either way, through the one forget (`forget_touch_state`).

When a touch end delivers a leave at all — the translation end's no-focus arm (a focused mouse gets the restore motion instead) — it is ORDINARY, never CapabilityLoss: that reason names the wl_pointer stream's own death, and a touch edge says nothing about the mouse. Shutdown (`destroy_wayland_state`) is NOT an input edge: the proxy is released with no deliveries, exactly as the keyboard and pointer teardowns there deliver nothing. The pointer- and keyboard-capability edges never reach into touch state, and touch edges never reach into theirs.

### Touch Never Writes the Pointer's Fields

A deliberate split, recorded at the state block: `pointer_x_`/`pointer_y_` are wl_pointer's own — the wheel emits there, the bare-`e` press lands there, the capture machinery rewrites them — and every touch delivery carries its own explicit coordinates, so the unified "one pointer" view lives in the DELIVERIES while each device's platform state stays self-consistent. Touch likewise does not clear `pointer_position_unknown_` (a touch says nothing about where the mouse cursor is drawn) and gates NO DELIVERY on `pointer_focused_` (wl_touch delivers only to the touched surface; a mouse-focus bit is not its business). The split has ONE deliberate reader since codex round 3: the translation end (`deliver_touch_translation_end`) reads `pointer_focused_` — and `pointer_x_`/`pointer_y_` on the focused arm — because the end is a MOUSE question ("is the mouse resting in the window, and where"), not a touch delivery to gate. Consequence, accepted: a stray mouse click during a touch session routes at the MOUSE's last known position, which is that click's honest answer.

### No Hover Under Touch — Cues, Not Functions

A finger has no resting position: contact is a press (after the window, when the stream resolves to the pointer at all — a pan-zone drag is navigation and never presses) and lift is the translation end — a leave, or the restore motion at a focused mouse (the round-3 fork, The Translation Contract above) — so the hover-derived surfaces simply never fire FROM GLASS: button hover faces, the 700 ms shift-tooltip dwell, and the menu-row armed-hover open all want a pointer that rests where nothing is pressed, and a finger never supplies one (the restore motion re-derives them at the MOUSE's resting position, never the finger's). These are CUES, not functions: every act they advertise is reachable by its chord or its press, so nothing is lost. For the faces and the dwell nothing was special-cased to make this true — it falls out of the end-on-lift contract — but THE ARMED HOVER-OPEN NEEDED ONE REAL GUARD (codex round 2 caught the original claim overclaiming): the armed mode deliberately survives an ordinary leave through row 1's band, so a finger lift there keeps it, and the next tap's resolution burst delivers a pre-press entry motion that reached the hover-open and sprang an anchor's menu one event before the same burst's press toggle-closed it — a tap on Settings/Navigation visibly did nothing. The fix is device-blind, two halves: the platform raises the touch hold before delivering the entry motion (the finger has been down since the window opened, so the whole burst honestly reads the primary button held), and the GUI's hover-open refuses under a held primary button (on_motion's no-gesture tail) — a guard that also closes the mouse's own latent case, a held press that armed no gesture sliding along the armed row. So the claim stands BY THE GUARD, not by nature, and the tap opens the menu through the press path exactly as a click does. THE GUARD IS A THREE-MEMBER FAMILY RULE, built one member at a time across rounds 2-3 and stated whole at the close walk in `input_pointer.cpp`: NO ROW-1 HOVER ACT FIRES WHILE THE PRIMARY BUTTON IS HELD — the armed hover-OPEN (round 2, above), the open-menu anchor-SWITCH, and the open-menu hover CLOSE (both round 3; the close was the last unguarded member, added at the planner's judgment on the coder's flag). ### Glass Gains the Shift Acts — the Chrome Shift Long Press (2026-08-13)

THE RULING (architect, stream B): "SHIFT LONG-PRESS on the shift-admitting buttons — Render, paste, the walk's Older/Newer: press and HOLD ~500ms, then release-on-button = the SHIFT-modified dispatch, like the waveform hold = the shift former on glass; a short press stays the plain act; slide-away cancels both. The hold reaches the shifted DISPATCH directly, at the one place the lift already builds the chord.

IT IS GUI CODE OVER ORDINARY POINTER DELIVERIES, which is why this file records it rather than owning it: `kdenlive-redesign.md`'s act-at-release section is authoritative (the arm's `press_ms` stamp, the `kChromeShiftHoldMs` beat, the membership predicate, the Render-cancel answer). What is TOUCH's to state is the verification, done against the translation rather than assumed: a chrome button is OFF the pan zone (`touch_point_in_pan_zone` is the navigation surface), so a finger landing on one takes the 60 ms deadline and `resolve_touch_window_to_pointer` delivers the press at the expiry — driven by the timerfd tick as well as by event arrival, so a motionless finger's press lands on time with no further events — and the release comes at the LIFT. The GUI therefore measures lift-minus-expiry: one disambiguation window (~60 ms) SHORTER than the finger was physically down, a constant offset well inside the beat's own tolerance, deliberately not compensated. A finger sliding off the button delivers ordinary `Pointer`-phase motion and releases off the rect, cancelling the plain act and the shifted one alike; `wl_touch.cancel` and capability loss end it through the hard-end contract as they end any translation. **THE BEAT NEEDS NO FEEDBACK AND IS RULED TO NEED NONE** (architect 2026-08-13): the gesture is THIS surface's — the glass rig has no keyboard, which is the whole reason it exists — and TOOLTIPS DO NOT SHOW ON THE TOUCH PANEL, so a hint at the beat would ride a surface the gesture's only user never sees. Nothing is to be built for it; the beat passes silently and the act shows at the lift.

THE THREE HOLD THRESHOLDS ARE ONE NUMBER NOW (architect 2026-08-16), **DIRECTLY SUPERSEDING** this section's own standing ruling, recorded here in full because it was as deliberate as its replacement: "THE BEAT IS NOT `kTouchRegionHoldMs`, though both rest at 500 and both came from the architect's '~500ms'. That one is a platform disambiguation deadline tuned for a gesture that might have been a pan; this one is a GUI press metric tuned for a stationary press on a small button. They are equal by coincidence of two rulings and may be retuned independently — do not fold them together." THEY ARE FOLDED TOGETHER. `kHoldBeatMs` = 575 is born in gui_input.h as THE PRODUCT'S ONE HOLD-BEAT DURATION — how long any deliberate hold must rest before it crosses into its held meaning — and BOTH constants now read it: `kChromeShiftHoldMs` (this section's beat, app_state.h) and `kTouchRegionHoldMs` (the pan zone's deadline, platform_wayland.cpp). The two constants SURVIVE as named readers of one number, so each site still says which hold it is talking about and nothing had to be renamed; what died is the independence, and a retune now moves all of them. 575 IS NOT A NEW TUNING BUT A CONVENTION WITH THE COMPOSITOR: it is the architect's own labwc `<repeatDelay>575</repeatDelay>` (with `<repeatRate>25</repeatRate>` beside it), so the beat the hand already knows from every held key on the desktop is the beat this program's own holds use, and the chrome shift hold, the touch region hold and the keyboard's first repeat all cross their mark together. THE KEY DELAY ITSELF IS DELIBERATELY NOT THIS CONSTANT and never should be: key repeat arrives from the compositor through `wl_keyboard.repeat_info`, so it tracks the desktop and moves with every other application if the architect ever retunes it — hard-coding it to match would be the one place the product fights the desktop and would buy nothing, the two numbers already agreeing. The old ruling's distinction was true and is simply no longer the deciding one: one hold beat across the product is worth more than two separately tunable ones, and neither rides `gui_scale` (a duration is not a length).

## The Pan Zone Yields Inside the Trim Overlay (2026-08-15; trim's own drags since 2026-08-18)

THE REGION BECAME THE TRIM EDITOR THAT DAY AND BECAME THE TRIM OUTRIGHT ON 2026-08-18 (region-scrub-esc.md is the ruling's home): the WAVEFORM TRIM OVERLAY is the trim's second painted surface, DERIVED from the resting bounds every frame, and it carries three motions — drag inside it to move the window, drag within a bound's grab band to move that bound, drag anywhere else to pan — which since 2026-08-18 ARE trim's own bridge and endcap drags rather than a region editor of their own (`arm_pending_trim_drag`; `RegionEditDragState` and `apply_region_edit_drag_at` are deleted). BARE `x` — the icon row's **Show trim region** button and its keyboard twin — shows and hides it, bringing the span into view on the show half (`Ctrl+Shift+X`, its chord from 2026-08-16, is unbound again), and THE REGION HOLD'S SWEEP RAISES IT FOR THE STROKE — up at the press so the finger can see the span it is drawing, that button lit with it (`show_trim_region_overlay`, the one raise owner), and DOWN AGAIN AT THE LIFT (`commit_region_sweep`, architect 2026-08-20: a stroke means "this exact region", and once it is set the overlay is noise). A press on the 9 px band itself raises nothing at all any more — the one-day 2026-08-19 raise departed on 2026-08-20 with the architect's laptop/glass reversal; region-scrub-esc.md carries both rulings. THE TOUCH ARGUMENT SURVIVES THAT INTACT, and it is the whole reason the surface exists: trim has to be editable on something hundreds of pixels tall rather than on nine, so the finger shows the overlay with the **Show trim region** button (bare `x`'s twin), edits the bounds and the bridge on the big surface for as long as it likes, and puts it away when done. The feature exists for the panel, where a 9 px lane is under a fingertip.

AND IT DOES NOT INHERIT FOR FREE — THIS IS THE ONE PLACE THE ARC'S "touch is the pointer" PREMISE NEEDED A LINE OF CODE. The whole waveform is the PAN ZONE, so a finger landing inside a region would resolve to the phone model's single-finger pan and NEVER DELIVER A PRESS, and the region's move / bound drags live on the pointer. `touch_point_in_pan_zone` therefore answers FALSE wherever `region_manipulation_hit` answers non-None, which lets the finger resolve to the pointer translation and reach the drag — THE FLAG BOX CARVE-OUT'S EXACT SHAPE, one level up: a finger on a flag already leaves the zone so the marker drag is reachable, and a finger inside a region now leaves it so the region's editor is. IT IS THE SAME ONE PREDICATE the mouse press claim and the cursor map read, so the three consumers cannot disagree about where a region begins — and it is that owner, not a second spelling, that keeps the RULER and the MARKER LANE out of the exclusion: they answer None there by the hit test's own y-gate, so a region covering their columns still pans from them, which is the architect's reason a pan and a zoom stay reachable under a full-width region.

WHAT A FINGER DOES, per surface, with a region standing. INSIDE the span (outside both grab bands): off the zone, so the ordinary 60ms/8px window governs — a quick drag delivers its press at the slop crossing and MOVES THE SPAN, a hold past 60 ms unlocks the pointer and then moves it, and a motionless TAP is the press+release burst at the lift, which is a motionless release on the region and therefore THE WAVEFORM'S ORDINARY CLICK ACT, each half's own: on the UPPER half the playhead placement, which HIDES the overlay — the architect's deliberate escape hatch and the degenerate full-window case's exit, and a hide that discards nothing, the trim surviving under it — and on the LOWER half the scrub, which leaves the overlay up exactly as it always has (a full-window overlay covers both halves, so the hatch is always in reach). ON A BOUND (within `trim_endcap_grab_px()`): the same window, the drag moving THAT bound while its partner holds. OUTSIDE the span on the waveform, and ON THE RULER or the MARKER LANE anywhere: unchanged — the pan zone, the phone-model pan, the region-hold beat, all exactly as before.

THE REGION HOLD'S ANSWER, recorded rather than discovered. The hold is a PAN-ZONE gesture by construction (the zone's own stretched deadline drives it), so a hold INSIDE an existing region no longer reaches it: that finger is off the zone, its 60 ms expiry unlocks the pointer, and what it gets is the region's editor. SO A NEW REGION CANNOT BE DRAWN FROM INSIDE A STANDING ONE ON GLASS. That is ACCEPTED and it is coherent: a hold started OUTSIDE the region still draws (and the sweep may then run straight through the old span — only the DOWN point is asked), and a single tap inside HIDES the overlay (the trim under it untouched), after which the hold works anywhere. The desk keeps its unconditional route regardless — SHIFT ALWAYS SWEEPS, even inside the overlay, because shift bypasses the overlay's editor by ruling; since 2026-08-19 that press SHOWS the overlay rather than hiding it, so the new sweep is drawn on a surface that stays visible for the whole stroke — and collapses at the lift since 2026-08-20, the raise being bracketed by the stroke it serves.

NOTHING ELSE ON GLASS MOVES. The two-finger pinch is untouched: the exclusion is asked at the FIRST finger's down alone, and a pair whose first finger lands inside a region simply takes the off-zone path it already took for flags and lanes, where the second contact arriving inside the window is the ordinary two-finger nav. The thin-lane query, the hard-end contract, the moved latch and the downgrade are all unchanged. AND THE SCISSORS GAINED THE GLASS PATH TO `Shift+X` the same day: the trim button is SHIFT-ADMITTING, so the SHIFT LONG PRESS reaches the maximizer — which the panel previously could not reach at all, meaning a keyboardless rig could set a trim window and never get back out of it. THE ADMISSION OUTLIVED THE BUTTON (2026-08-18): the scissors were retired with the roster relayout and the admission moved to the TRIM REGION button, which dispatches bare `x` and admits shift for `Shift+X` exactly as the scissors did.

**THE GLASS TRIM STORY IS CLOSED (2026-08-18).** A keyboardless rig now has a complete trim vocabulary by finger alone: SET a window by the region HOLD, whose sweep writes the trim per motion event under no width rule at all (the `kMinTrimSpanFrames` floor stood one day and is retired, architect 2026-08-19), so a drag past the slop commits exactly the window it drew; ADJUST it by showing the overlay with the **Show trim region** button (the sweep's own raise collapses at its lift since 2026-08-20, so adjusting is a second, deliberate act) and dragging either bound or the interior of it, on a surface hundreds of pixels tall; and GET BACK OUT to the whole song three ways — the shift long press on the Show trim region button, its shift-click, or collapsing a span onto itself — a bound dragged onto its partner, or since 2026-08-19 a SWEEP that ends where it began — which resets to the full window through `auto_clear_crossed_trim`, the one escape every former shares. Nothing here needs the 9 px lane and nothing here needs a keyboard.
