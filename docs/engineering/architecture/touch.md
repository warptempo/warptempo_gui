# Touch: One Unified Mode (wl_touch as the Pointer)

> Touch phase 1, architect-ratified 2026-08-11 — the touch arc's first CODE surface (row 8, the bottom toolbar, was its first GUI surface). This file is the arc's record the way kdenlive-redesign.md is the redesign's. The phase-1 code shipped UNVERIFIED ON SILICON: the authoring laptop has no touchscreen and the target is the Pi rig's ROADOM panel, so everything was written to be inert without the capability and correct by construction, with every judgment call recorded. THE PANEL HAS SINCE ARRIVED: the glass sessions of 2026-08-11 produced the first field-driven revisions (the phone model, the trim surface arc, the trim move — their records below; the trim surface arc's pointer half was REVERTED WHOLE a day later — The Pointer-Side Revert below closes it), the FIFTH GLASS RULING of 2026-08-12 tried THE TIMER-FREE MODEL — the first finger as the pointer ON CONTACT, the window deleted — the SIXTH GLASS RULING, the same day, REVERSED IT after one glass session: THE WINDOWED MODEL RETURNS — the 60 ms disambiguation window, tap-at-lift, the phone model's one-finger pan, with ONE keeper from the experiment (the motionless-hold upgrade) and the trim hold staying dead — and the EIGHTH GLASS RULING, still the same day, layered PAN-PRIMARY onto that machine (its own section below, the current authoritative statement): the pan zone grows to the whole navigation surface, its window stretches to the ~500 ms region-hold beat whose expiry drives the region former through a reborn hook family, and hold-unlocks-the-pointer survives OFF the zone only. Retune the constants on glass, not on argument.

### The Unified-Mode Ruling

THE PREMISE IS ONE UNIFIED MODE, the architect's one settled ruling for the arc: same look, same behavior, same interaction language for mouse and touch. Phase 1 makes it literal — TOUCH IS THE POINTER, translated whole at the platform boundary (`platform_wayland.cpp`), and the GUI cannot tell which device produced an event. No touch mode, no flag, no detection, no GUI-side branch. Everything inherits by construction: every gate, refusal, modal claim, read-only rule, hit test and gesture the pointer has applies to a finger with zero new GUI semantics — stated here once rather than re-proven per gesture. The premise carries TWO ruled divergences since the eighth glass ruling, both scoped to the pan zone: the phone model's single-finger pan (its section below) and the region hold (the Pan-Primary section below), which drives the same region former the mouse's shift press drives; the only other touch-specific gesture is the two-finger navigation.

### The Windowed Model Returns (Sixth Glass Ruling, 2026-08-12 — Authoritative)

THE FIELD VERDICT THAT DROVE IT: the timer-free model (781ef948, the fifth ruling, that morning) went to glass the same day, and everything worked EXCEPT ZOOM — with the press landing at contact, every pinch whose fingers land even slightly apart first jumps the playhead: "it bounces around as you're zooming... every time I pinch, it jumps. That's the part that's distracting." The architect ruled the window back — "let's go back to having delay... with my phone I'm used to a delay" — and, told the ramifications, ratified the trade: THE WINDOW BUYS GESTURE VOCABULARY FOR ~60 MS OF TAP LATENCY. What the delay buys back: the jump-free pinch (zoom "extremely important especially given that the screen is smaller"), the tap/quick-drag/hold triple per surface, and the phone-model pan — which is also what killed the interim scrollbar plan. Tap-at-lift is his explicit ask: "moving the playhead was set to finger lift... let's go back to that."

THE RESTORED MACHINE is the last windowed one (0e633bcf/3035078a) minus the trim hold: phases {Idle, Pending, Pointer, Nav, Drain}, the 60 ms window resolving to a TAP at the lift (the whole burst at the lift — enter-motion + press at the down point, any queued motion, release, translation end), to TWO-FINGER NAV on a second down inside it (nothing ever delivered — the ruling's whole point), on slop-crossing motion to the PAN-ZONE FORK (waveform → single-finger nav, the phone model; elsewhere → the pointer with the crossing as queued motion), and on EXPIRY to the POINTER everywhere (hold unlocks the pointer — no band exception any more). (The expiry clause and the waveform-only zone were SUPERSEDED the same day by the eighth ruling's touch half, the Pan-Primary section below: the zone is the navigation surface now, and its expiry is the region hold — the pointer unlock survives off the zone.)

THE ONE KEEPER from the timer-free model, the architect's explicit ruling this session: the Pointer phase tracks whether the live translation has MOVED (Chebyshev ≥ `kTouchSlopPx` from the down point, latched once — `touch_translation_moved_`), and a SECOND finger landing during a live translation FORKS on it:

- MOVED (a live drag — marker, region, trim, strip): IGNORED whole, phase 1's own rule — "mid-gesture finger-count changes do not mutate a committed gesture."
- MOTIONLESS (a hold): THE UPGRADE — the translation ends by ordinary release through the one owner (`deliver_touch_translation_end`), then two-finger nav seeds at the join. The hold's press already placed the playhead at the window's expiry, so the upgrade adds NO additional jump — it only keeps a slow pinch (fingers landing more than the window apart) alive instead of dead; a sub-latch release of that pair does nothing further.

THE REVERSED DESIGN, recorded so it is not re-proposed: a two-finger REGION gesture (hold one finger, tap/drag the second to span a region) was designed in the same session and REVERSED before landing — "I don't think hold-the-finger-down-and-tap-the-other is the right solution." The architect ratified the pinch half of the motionless-hold fork and dropped the region half.

### Pan-Primary, the Touch Half (Eighth Glass Ruling, 2026-08-12 — Authoritative)

THE RULING (architect, dictated with the mouse half and ratified whole; the touch half landed one commit after it): pan is the most common gesture and takes the PRIMARY drag on both devices — "drag to be single finger... region select to be hold and then drag because the pan is way more common" — so on glass the one-finger drag PANS and the deliberate region takes the HOLD, at his own duration: "maybe five hundred milliseconds". The lower half stays reserved — "the lower half I'd like to reserve for scrubbing."

THE GROWN ZONE: `touch_point_in_pan_zone` answers THE NAVIGATION SURFACE — the mouse vocabulary's own plain-drag surface, spelled exactly as the press router derives it: the waveform's UPPER half (the WHOLE waveform inside the `h` history view, which has no scrub) + the RULER + the MARKER lane MINUS its flag boxes (the painter's published rects — a finger landing on a flag resolves to the POINTER, so a quick flag drag is the immediate marker drag, the mouse's own carve-out). The LOWER half LEAVES the zone: scrub is not pan, so a quick drag there resolves to the pointer and fires the scrub at resolution — the press's own act, accepted. Still asked ONCE at the first finger's down, surface geometry only, every refusal downstream.

THE TWO-DEADLINE FORK, the dead trim-band beat's exact pattern reborn: the zone answer picks the window's deadline at the down — ON the zone `kTouchRegionHoldMs` = 500 (the REGION-HOLD BEAT; labwc's own 575 ms repeat delay is the same order), OFF it `kTouchDisambiguateMs` = 60 as before. The window's resolutions under the fork:

- UP inside = the TAP, everywhere: the whole pointer burst at the lift. On the navigation surface that burst's motionless press-release IS the mouse half's deferred click act — the playhead placement at the tap point — structurally the mouse click, nothing touch-side built for it. On the marker lane's empty stretch the same release seeds the EmptyLane double-click candidate, so DOUBLE-TAP reaches the marker CREATE (the verification trace below).
- SLOP-CROSSING = the fork, unchanged: on the zone → SINGLE-FINGER NAV (the phone model's pan, the existing machinery whole); off it → the pointer resolution (trim-bar drags, flag drags, button presses — and a lower-half quick drag fires the scrub at resolution).
- SECOND DOWN inside = two-finger nav (the jump-free pinch), unchanged, whatever the zone answer.
- EXPIRY forks on the zone: OFF the zone → the pointer unlock at 60 ms, unchanged — hold-then-drag is still how the endcap/bridge grabs and every off-zone press-and-hold gesture work on glass. ON the zone, at the beat → THE REGION HOLD.

THE REGION HOLD is a hook-family gesture, the deleted trim-move members' exact shape reborn (`TouchPhase::Region`; `set_touch_nav_hooks` grew to SIX members — update, end, pan_zone, region_begin, region_update, region_end): `region_begin(x, y)` fires at the DOWN point, `region_update(x, y)` at most once per `wl_touch.frame`, `region_end()` on lift or hard end. NOTHING pointer-shaped starts — no press, no hold bit, no release, no translation end (the single-finger nav's model) — and a second finger landing during the gesture is IGNORED whole (a committed gesture, the moved-drag rule's family). The GUI bodies (`begin_touch_region` / `update_touch_region` / `end_touch_region`, input_pointer.cpp) ARE the one region former's machinery: the begin runs THE SHIFT FORMER'S OWN PRESS HALF at the down point — forking on the `h` mode exactly as the shift press does (the view ADMITS the former as its view-local vocabulary: the mode-focus clear instead of the store deselect) — the update rides the ONE motion path (playhead on the moving end), and the end is the release path (a motionless hold-lift rests NO span and leaves the playhead where the begin seated it — the former's motionless-release rule, which is what makes a long-press-then-lift a PLACEMENT). The refusal list lives in the begin (prompt, the five editors, open dropdown, loading/empty audio, live pointer gesture — the dead trim-move begin's list MINUS the `h` view); a refused begin makes the stream dead through the drag's own !active guard. The gesture joins the hard-end contract on the trim-move's old terms: its end ALWAYS fires once begun (the drag holds the drag-modal gate open), the staged final frame delivering on the finger's own lift and dropping on a hard end.

WHY THE BEAT CANNOT REPEAT THE TRIM-MOVE REGRESSION: the one-day `kTouchTrimHoldMs` died because the band's QUICK drag was a pointer drag, so an aimed drag's natural dwell expired into the move. On the pan zone the quick drag is the PAN — a different gesture class entirely — and only a deliberately motionless finger reaches the beat.

THE TRIM MOVE ITSELF STAYS DEAD: neither `kTouchTrimHoldMs` nor `TouchPhase::TrimMove` returned — the region hold revives the hook PATTERN, not that gesture (The Trim Move below carries the record). The trim bar is an ordinary pointer surface on glass — a hold past the 60 ms window then a drag, or a quick drag crossing the slop inside it, reaches the endcap/bridge machinery through the ordinary pointer path, and a double-tap on the bar is the span framing (two tap bursts, the same double-click machinery as the mouse).

### The Timer-Free Model — the One-Session Experiment (Fifth Glass Ruling, 2026-08-12; Superseded the Same Day)

THE RECORD, kept whole so its lesson is not relearned: the architect dictated pointer-on-contact that morning ("I'd rather have a more responsive interface... building another lag is a no go") — the first finger delivered the press burst AT CONTACT (`deliver_touch_contact_press`), a second finger upgraded by ordinary release + nav-seed-at-join, and the window, both timing constants, the deadline sampling, the three resolvers, the pan-zone/trim-band queries, the single-finger nav and the trim-move hook family were all DELETED (−400 net lines; the machine ran {Idle, Pointer, Nav, Drain}). It went to glass the same day and died there — the staggered-landing playhead nudge, accepted on paper, was intolerable in the hand at every pinch. THE LESSON: pointer-on-contact is unbeatable for responsiveness and unusable for pinch disambiguation — the two fingers of a real pinch land tens of milliseconds apart, so any model that presses on the first contact jumps before it can zoom. The window returned at the sixth ruling with the experiment's one durable piece, the motionless-hold upgrade (above).

### The Binding

`wl_touch` is CORE protocol — wl_seat's third capability, the `wl_data_device_manager` precedent: the same wayland.xml, no new library, no pkg-config entry, no generated stub. It is bound from the existing seat listener when `WL_SEAT_CAPABILITY_TOUCH` is advertised and released on capability loss (through the hard-end contract below). It is NOT a required global and NOT a required capability: absence is SILENCE — no stderr, nothing degraded. The laptop simply has no glass; a seat without touch is the ordinary case, not a poor environment. Touch events arrive batched under `wl_touch.frame` and are processed per frame like pointer frames: down/up edges dispatch at arrival (the button model), motion coalesces to the frame boundary (the captured-relative-motion model), so a sensor-rate panel costs one delivery per frame.

### The Translation Contract (One Finger IS the Pointer)

The bare-`e` precedent — a key translated at the boundary into the left mouse button — applied to glass. The translation state is the platform's own, keyed on the touch id; the GUI sees ordinary pointer deliveries. The phase machine and the AUTHORITATIVE edge inventory live at the touch state block in `platform_wayland.h` (the `transport_repeat` precedent: one list at the state, each fire site keeping only its own clause); the shape:

- FIRST touch-down (no touch state live) opens the DISAMBIGUATION WINDOW: {id, position, deadline} remembered, NOTHING delivered — plus the down point's PAN-ZONE answer captured once (the zone query, in The Phone Model below). The window exists to tell tap from drag from two fingers from the region hold; its deadline is the zone answer's own — the region-hold beat (`kTouchRegionHoldMs`) on the zone, `kTouchDisambiguateMs` off it (the eighth ruling's two-deadline fork, the Pan-Primary section above).
- The window RESOLVES TO POINTER on the finger lifting inside it (a tap, whichever deadline) and on the OFF-ZONE EXPIRY; the ON-ZONE expiry is the REGION HOLD (the Pan-Primary section above). Motion beyond the slop (`kTouchSlopPx`) FORKS on the captured zone answer: on the pan surface it resolves to SINGLE-FINGER NAV (no pointer events, ever — The Phone Model below), elsewhere to POINTER. Pointer resolution delivers the synthesized enter-motion at the ORIGINAL down point, then the left press there, then any queued motion — and for the tap, the release immediately after. THE TOUCH HOLD BIT IS RAISED BEFORE THE ENTRY MOTION (codex round 2), so every delivery in the resolution burst — the pre-press motion included — reads the primary button held: the finger has factually been down since the window opened, and that held reading is what the GUI's armed hover-open guard keys on (below). From then on touch motion is pointer motion and touch up is the release.
- The live translation carries the MOVED LATCH (the sixth glass ruling): seeded by the resolver from the window's own travel (a slop-crossing resolution enters already moved; expiry and tap enter motionless) and latched once by the Pointer motion arm at the same Chebyshev slop from the down point. A SECOND finger landing during the translation forks on it — moved = ignored whole, motionless = the upgrade (The Windowed Model Returns above; the edge inventory at the state block is authoritative).
- A touch-up that ends a pointer translation delivers the release on the logical left's 1→0 edge and then THE TRANSLATION END ON THAT SAME EDGE, which FORKS ON PHYSICAL POINTER FOCUS since codex round 3 (one owner, `deliver_touch_translation_end`, shared by the touch-up site, the motionless-hold upgrade and the hard-end contract): with NO mouse focused on the surface, an ORDINARY pointer leave (`GuiPointerLeaveReason::OrdinaryLeave`) — the finger left the glass, which IS a leave, and the remembered-position machinery and the menu-row band rules already handle ordinary leaves with nothing special-cased; with the physical pointer FOCUSED, an ordinary restore MOTION at the mouse's last platform-tracked position (`pointer_x_`/`pointer_y_`, which touch never writes) INSTEAD of the leave — the finger lifted, so the unified pointer is now where the mouse is, and the motion re-derives hover, the menu-row exit test and the loop-settled cursor from truth (the round-3 cursor-residue fix). The restore motion runs the ordinary motion path WHOLE, deliberately unmarked — so with the menu row ARMED and the mouse resting on an anchor it hover-opens that menu, and with a menu OPEN and the mouse resting on the other anchor it hover-switches: judged the armed mode's own defining semantics for a pointer standing at that position, not surprises to suppress (suppression would need a "restore" mark on the motion, a device branch in spirit; recorded at the definition too). WHEN A SIBLING SOURCE STILL HOLDS the logical left (physical BTN_LEFT or bare-`e`), the release is suppressed and THE WHOLE END IS TOO — leave and restore alike (codex round 2): the unified pointer has not meaningfully left — the mouse is still there, mid-press — and the leave hook's clears are not mere hover cleanup (they disarm the row-8 held-arrow repeat and destroy an open popup's live press claim, state that belongs to the still-held press, not to the finger that lifted). A suppressed end can strand a hover face where the finger last was — the accepted-glitch class, self-healing on the next pointer event. The hard-end contract takes the same edge rule and the same fork.
- THE LOGICAL LEFT BUTTON IS ONE BIT WITH THREE SOURCES: `pointer_left_held_ || synth_left_held_ || touch_left_held_` — the evdev model for devices sharing BTN_LEFT, extended from the bare-`e` pair. A press is delivered only on the OR's 0→1 edge and a release only on its 1→0 edge, so the sources never double-deliver, and `current_mods()` ORs all three into `primary_button_held` (without which a touch drag would tear on its first motion). Each source's hold ends on ITS OWN stream's edges only: the keyboard edges end the synth hold, pointer-capability loss ends the physical and synth holds, the touch edges end the touch hold.
- The resolution press is a pointer-button press in full: it kills an armed key repeat (layer 1 of the repeat contract — the edge list at `on_keyboard_key` names three press producers) and flushes both staged motions ahead of itself (the `flush_deferred_motion` invariant, which the touch delivery sites joined).
- Physical keyboard modifiers ride along to touch deliveries exactly as to a mouse: `current_mods()` is the one modifier truth, so Shift+tap is a Shift+click with no new code.

### The Disambiguation Constants (Ruled Retunables)

All three live in `platform_wayland.cpp`'s anonymous namespace, with the full tuning rationale at the site:

- `kTouchDisambiguateMs` = 60 — the one-finger-or-two window, the OFF-ZONE deadline since the eighth ruling. Sampled on the timerfd tick beside the key-repeat deadline (the run loop's one deadline-sampling spot) plus lazily at every touch event's arrival, so expiry lands within one tick (≤ ~16 ms) of the mark; the window is a feel bound, not an exact timer. Off-zone expiry resolves to the pointer — hold unlocks the pointer there.
- `kTouchRegionHoldMs` = 500 — the pan zone's own stretched window, the REGION-HOLD BEAT (the eighth ruling; the architect's "maybe five hundred milliseconds", the same order as labwc's 575 ms repeat delay). On-zone expiry at this mark is the region hold (the Pan-Primary section above).
- `kTouchSlopPx` = 8.0 — physical px; resolves the window early on motion (a finger already dragging should not wait the window out, the resolution forking on the down point's pan-zone answer — The Phone Model below), is the navigation gestures' latch, and is the live translation's MOVED latch (the second-down fork above). It DELIBERATELY EQUALS the GUI's one generic press-becomes-drag gate (`kDragMovedThresholdPx`; stated rather than shared — the platform sits below the GUI model), so a slop-crossing resolution's queued motion crosses the GUI's own drag gate in the same burst as the press: a touch drag becomes a drag the moment it resolves.

(`kTouchTrimHoldMs` = 500 — the merged trim band's stretched Pending deadline, the fourth glass session's "beat" — is DEAD and did not return: it was born of a same-day field regression (riding the 60 ms window made the "hold" shorter than an aimed drag's natural dwell, so every deliberate band drag expired into the trim move) and died with the trim move itself at the fifth ruling. `kTouchRegionHoldMs` revives the two-deadline PATTERN on a surface whose quick drag is the pan rather than a pointer drag, so the dwell collision cannot recur — the constants' comment carries the argument. The Trim Move below carries the gesture's record.)

THE FOLLOW CHASE IS PAUSED WHILE ANY CONTACT IS DOWN (2026-08-12), which is what makes the window's DEFERRED conversions honest: the pre-paint autopager refuses under `touch_contact_active()` — the phase machine simply not Idle — so the tap's lift-time click burst and the region hold's expiry-time begin both convert the DOWN point through the DOWN-TIME viewport rather than one the chase paged underneath it in the meantime (up to `kTouchDisambiguateMs` off the zone, up to `kTouchRegionHoldMs` on it). Nothing GUI-side is armed during Pending, so the GUI's own `any_pointer_gesture_active` cannot see that window; the platform query is what covers it, and Drain rides along by construction (nothing converts during a drain — the rule is "any contact down", not a phase list). A PAUSE, not a follow-suppression producer: nothing is written, and the chase resumes and catches up on the next tick once the glass clears.

### Two Fingers = Pan + Zoom (Navigation-Class)

Both fingers down inside the window — or a second finger joining a MOTIONLESS held translation (the upgrade, The Windowed Model Returns above) or a live single-finger pan (the rebase, The Phone Model below) — is the NAVIGATION gesture: per-frame centroid horizontal delta pans, per-frame finger-distance ratio zooms ABOUT THE CENTROID's column — the strip drag's dual-axis semantics. The gesture never delivers pointer events.

THE DELIVERY SHAPE TAKEN, and why (the phase-1 brief offered the strip-drag pointer entries as the preferred shape with a recorded fallback; this is the fallback, taken at the family's deepest shared point): a narrow platform-to-GUI hook set wired in main.cpp (`GuiPlatform::set_touch_nav_hooks` → `GuiInputHandler::apply_touch_nav_update` / `end_touch_nav`, the `set_keyboard_intent_cancel_hook` wiring precedent; the set is SIX members since the eighth ruling — update, end, the phone model's pan-zone query, and the region trio begin/update/end, the dead trim-move members' pattern reborn for the region former) whose per-frame update drives `Viewport::apply_strip_drag_zoom` — the strip-drag family's own application chokepoint — so the level clamp, the viewport clamp (`clamp_viewport_start`), the synchronous per-frame rebuild and the either-axis follow suppression all come from the one owner. The family's POINTER-PRESS ARM (`arm_strip_drag_at` / `StripDragState`) is deliberately not driven, for two structural reasons recorded at the declaration: (1) the arm unconditionally CAPTURES THE REAL POINTER — it would hide and lock the mouse cursor during a touch gesture, and the capture's release warps the mouse and rewrites the platform's tracked pointer position to the gesture's restore x, corrupting real mouse state from glass; (2) a `strip_drag.active` gesture is TERMINATED by `on_motion`'s button-lost arm the moment any real mouse motion arrives without a held button — which is every mouse motion during a two-finger touch — so a nudged mouse would kill a live pinch. No ctrl-modified pointer press is faked anywhere (modifier state belongs to xkb).

The mechanics, all at their sites:

- The ZOOM mapping is LOGARITHMIC and constant-free: `new_level = level − log2(dist_ratio)`, so doubling the finger gap is exactly one zoom level in and the content between the fingers scales with the fingers. Pre-clamped into `[kMinZoom, effective_max_zoom_level]` exactly as `apply_strip_drag_at` pre-clamps (the chokepoint's `level_changed` contract names both callers).
- The PAN is folded into the same placement: the content under the PREVIOUS centroid column is placed at the CURRENT centroid column under the new level — one `apply_strip_drag_zoom` call does both axes. PER-FRAME ANCHORED (the ratified semantics), so there is no persistent song anchor and no edge-trick rebind to need.
- THE LATCH is the platform's, the strip drag's own press-becomes-drag model in the touch's own unit: nothing is delivered until the centroid travels `kTouchSlopPx` (Chebyshev) from the gesture's start OR the finger distance changes by that same slop, and the crossing update folds the whole accumulated delta — so a two-finger tap navigates nothing and costs the GUI nothing (the end hook fires only if an update was delivered).
- THE REFUSAL ANSWER IS THE WHEEL'S, per frame: the gesture is the wheel's navigation-class sibling — it delivers no pointer events, punches through the keyboard-modal flag editor exactly as the wheel does, and navigates the wheel's two surfaces — so `wheel_context` (the SINGLE wheel routing predicate) is asked at each frame's centroid. That refuses under the prompt, an open dropdown, the dialog modal editors, loading/empty audio and ANY live pointer gesture, and applies only over the waveform or the top strip. A modal opening mid-gesture freezes it (the wheel's own behavior), and a live mouse gesture freezes it too, so two devices cannot write the viewport at once in that direction; the converse — a mouse gesture ARMING while a touch gesture is mid-flight — is accepted as the user's own two-handed act (each writer is incremental over live state and every write clamps through the one chokepoint). The wheel's dismissal pair (dropdown close, tooltip hide) is deliberately NOT copied — this is a refusal, not a press act — but every applied frame CLEARS THE DOUBLE-CLICK CANDIDATE (the C8 rule: navigation that moves content between two taps must not let the second tap consume as a double-click).
- One finger lifting ENDS the gesture (any end commits) — and the end DELIVERS THE STAGED FINAL FRAME FIRST: Wayland orders the `up` before the terminating `wl_touch.frame`, so motion batched with the lift is still staged when the end runs, and that motion is the user's own final leg (the any-end-commits family). This is what lets a short pinch whose only latch-crossing motion batches with the up act at all. The survivor is IGNORED until all fingers lift (the drain phase) — no downgrade to single-finger nav. A third finger is ignored. No pinch-vs-pan mode split: both axes live simultaneously, the strip drag's own model. The end is the grab-pan release's own tail — one predictor resync; each applied frame already rebuilt synchronously.
- The gesture joins neither the platform's key-repeat disarm list nor the transport hold's edge list: those are ruled event-edge enumerations (key press / pointer press / completed wheel emission) and a two-finger frame is none of them. Recorded as a judgment rather than an omission — a codex round can contest it.

### The Phone Model: One Finger Pans the Waveform (Second Glass Session, 2026-08-11; Restored with the Window, 2026-08-12)

THE RULING (architect, at the rig's second glass session, 2026-08-11, his words condensed): "one finger drags the pan — that's how my phone works. One finger drags, two fingers zoom... try that as an intermediate." A DELIBERATE touch-scoped divergence from the unified premise on ONE surface, the WAVEFORM: a one-finger DRAG whose down point lies there is NAVIGATION — the finger drags the pan — not a pointer drag. Everywhere else (the marker lane, the ruler, the trim bar, every strip and button) the translation is untouched. (The model was deleted for the timer-free model's one session and RESTORED with the window at the sixth ruling — the pan that the delay buys back is half of why the window returned.)

THE SECOND GLASS RECORD, the succession that produced the ruling: the FIRST glass session's field report (same day) was zoom excellent, pan unusable — finger-distance jitter leaking into the per-frame ratio — and the remedy tried first was a zoom ENGAGEMENT HYSTERESIS (`kTouchZoomEngagePx` = 20, commit 26963af3): the field verdict was pan still caught and zoom got STICKIER — "the previous way was much better" — so it was REVERTED WHOLE the same day (5e1d7d11; do not re-propose the hysteresis on this gesture) and the PHONE MODEL is the ratified successor: the two axes are split by FINGER COUNT rather than by per-axis engagement thresholds — one finger pans (one finger has no distance, so no zoom and no jitter to leak), two fingers zoom.

THE ZONE QUERY: the third hook on `set_touch_nav_hooks` (`pan_zone(x, y)` — the family's one owner grew a member rather than a sibling setter appearing), asked ONCE per touch stream at the FIRST finger's down; the answer is captured beside the down point, picks the window's deadline (the eighth ruling's two-deadline fork) and both one-finger resolutions fork on it. main.cpp wires it to `GuiInputHandler::touch_point_in_pan_zone` — the WAVEFORM AREA when the phone model shipped, GROWN by the eighth ruling to THE NAVIGATION SURFACE (upper waveform half, whole in the `h` view; the ruler; the marker lane minus its flag boxes — the Pan-Primary section above): every refusal (modal, prompt, dropdown, loading/empty audio, live pointer gesture) stays downstream — at the nav body's per-frame `wheel_context` answer, exactly where the two-finger gesture keeps it, so a refused pan FREEZES like a refused pinch rather than falling back to a pointer drag, and in the region begin's own gate list for the hold. The `h` history view pans by the same answer over its own surface shape, and that mode admits pan/zoom (the wheel class), so the view needs nothing new (verified at the query's declaration).

THE RESOLUTION FORK — the disambiguation window's ends under the phone model:

- SLOP-CROSSING MOTION with the down point IN the pan zone → SINGLE-FINGER NAV: delivered through the SAME nav update hook — the finger is the centroid, `dist_ratio` pinned at 1.0 (no distance, no zoom), so the GUI nav body needs NO fork and has none — latched and folded exactly as the two-finger latch is (the seed measures its latch from the DOWN point in the same Chebyshev metric, so the first delivered frame folds the whole accumulated delta). No press is ever delivered — the window's whole purpose. The platform shape: single-finger nav is a Nav SUB-STATE (`touch_nav_single_`), not a sixth phase, so the frame delivery, the latch, the end path, the drain and the hard-end arm are shared verbatim rather than duplicated per switch arm.
- TAP (up inside the window) → the ordinary click burst, UNCHANGED: a tap places the playhead, and — as this record was written — A TAP ON A STEM STILL SELECTED. The ratified intermediate deliberately scoped to DRAGS — pan replaces stem-DRAGGING from the waveform — and whether stems leave the waveform ENTIRELY (for every device, or only under touch) was the ARCHITECT'S OPEN QUESTION then. IT CLOSED 2026-08-12, the seventh glass ruling, THE OTHER WAY AND FOR EVERY DEVICE: marker stems are POINTER-INERT in all contexts (paint untouched), so a tap on a stem is the placement press like any waveform tap — the waveform-height clamp keeps the flag lane in easy reach everywhere, which is what the removal was waiting for (marker-ui.md carries the succession; pointer-hit-testing.md the deleted machinery).

THE UPGRADE, AND THE RECORDED ASYMMETRY: a SECOND finger landing during single-finger nav UPGRADES it to the two-finger gesture IN PLACE — the same hook stream, the delta bases REBASED to the join (the centroid jumps from the finger to the pair's midpoint at the join; folding that jump would pan by half the finger gap), so pan continuity is preserved and zoom is relative to the join; the latch state carries (a live pan does not re-latch), and an undelivered staged single-finger frame is dropped at the join — a sub-frame sliver the rebase supersedes. The REVERSE deliberately does NOT happen: a finger lifting from two-finger nav ENDS the gesture and the survivor drains — no downgrade to single-finger nav. Upgrade yes, downgrade no: a lift is an end, the any-end-commits family.

ENDS: finger up = the nav end (commits; the end hook fires iff an update was delivered — the existing rule), and hard ends run through the SAME `hard_end_touch_stream` Nav arm as two-finger nav, staged-frame drop included. No release, no leave, no translation end on any of them: single-finger nav never held the logical button — nothing pointer-shaped ever started.

### The Ignore Rules

- A SECOND finger landing while a pointer translation is LIVE forks on the MOVED latch (the sixth glass ruling): a MOVED translation — a live drag — IGNORES it whole, recorded (the touch-point count), not routed: mid-gesture finger-count changes do not mutate a committed gesture (the any-end-commits family; the architect's explicit mid-drag ruling). A MOTIONLESS translation — a hold — takes the UPGRADE instead (The Windowed Model Returns above).
- A second finger landing INSIDE the window resolves to the two-finger gesture, and no press was ever delivered — nothing to unwind, the window's whole purpose.
- A SECOND finger landing during the REGION gesture (the eighth ruling's hold) is ignored whole — a committed gesture, the same family; there is no motionless sub-state to upgrade from, the hold having already become the former.
- A THIRD finger during the navigation gesture is ignored; a finger landing during the drain is ignored.
- When a gesture's end leaves other fingers on the glass, the remaining fingers are DRAINED — ignored until the glass is clear — rather than opening a fresh window: the survivor of a committed gesture does not become a new gesture (a judgment recorded at the phase machine; the alternative would let a sloppy two-finger lift re-arm as a fresh press).

### The Hard Ends

`wl_touch.cancel` (the compositor claims the touches) and TOUCH capability loss share ONE contract (`hard_end_touch_stream`, the pointer-capability-loss precedent):

- a live pointer translation gets its release DELIVERED at the last position — one delivered release on the logical edge, a commit, not a vanish (a vanished hold would latch the drag-modal gate with no event left to lift it) — then the focus-forked translation end on the release's own edge, exactly as the finger's own lift delivers it (`deliver_touch_translation_end`, the one owner: the ordinary leave, or the restore motion at a focused mouse — The Translation Contract above; a sibling-held logical left suppresses release, leave and restore alike here too: whatever happened to the glass, the mouse is still mid-press and its live press claim is not this stream's to destroy);
- a live NAV gesture — single- or two-finger, one arm — ENDS through its end path (commits, iff anything was applied) — but its STAGED FINAL FRAME IS DROPPED, unlike the finger's own lift, which delivers it first: the compositor's claim means that motion retroactively was not ours. The asymmetry with the translation's release is deliberate and recorded at `end_touch_nav_gesture` (the end split): the release MUST deliver because a vanished hold would latch the drag-modal gate with no event left to lift it, while dropped nav motion wedges nothing — the gesture is stateless per frame and holds nothing in the GUI open;
- a live REGION gesture (the eighth ruling) takes the Nav shape — staged final frame DROPPED — but its `region_end` ALWAYS fires once the gesture began, unlike the nav end's delivered-gate: the GUI-side region drag holds the drag-modal gate open and its release path is owed (the trim-move end's old terms; the split is at `end_touch_region_gesture`);
- an unresolved disambiguation window is dropped silently (nothing was delivered, so there is nothing to end);
- all touch state is forgotten either way, through the one forget (`forget_touch_state`).

When a touch end delivers a leave at all — the translation end's no-focus arm (a focused mouse gets the restore motion instead) — it is ORDINARY, never CapabilityLoss: that reason names the wl_pointer stream's own death, and a touch edge says nothing about the mouse. Shutdown (`destroy_wayland_state`) is NOT an input edge: the proxy is released with no deliveries, exactly as the keyboard and pointer teardowns there deliver nothing. The pointer- and keyboard-capability edges never reach into touch state, and touch edges never reach into theirs.

### Touch Never Writes the Pointer's Fields

A deliberate split, recorded at the state block: `pointer_x_`/`pointer_y_` are wl_pointer's own — the wheel emits there, the bare-`e` press lands there, the capture machinery rewrites them — and every touch delivery carries its own explicit coordinates, so the unified "one pointer" view lives in the DELIVERIES while each device's platform state stays self-consistent. Touch likewise does not clear `pointer_position_unknown_` (a touch says nothing about where the mouse cursor is drawn) and gates NO DELIVERY on `pointer_focused_` (wl_touch delivers only to the touched surface; a mouse-focus bit is not its business). The split has ONE deliberate reader since codex round 3: the translation end (`deliver_touch_translation_end`) reads `pointer_focused_` — and `pointer_x_`/`pointer_y_` on the focused arm — because the end is a MOUSE question ("is the mouse resting in the window, and where"), not a touch delivery to gate. Consequence, accepted: a stray mouse click during a touch session routes at the MOUSE's last known position, which is that click's honest answer.

### No Hover Under Touch — Cues, Not Functions

A finger has no resting position: contact is a press (after the window, when the stream resolves to the pointer at all — a pan-zone drag is navigation and never presses) and lift is the translation end — a leave, or the restore motion at a focused mouse (the round-3 fork, The Translation Contract above) — so the hover-derived surfaces simply never fire FROM GLASS: button hover faces, the 700 ms shift-tooltip dwell, and the menu-row armed-hover open all want a pointer that rests where nothing is pressed, and a finger never supplies one (the restore motion re-derives them at the MOUSE's resting position, never the finger's). These are CUES, not functions: every act they advertise is reachable by its chord or its press, so nothing is lost. For the faces and the dwell nothing was special-cased to make this true — it falls out of the end-on-lift contract — but THE ARMED HOVER-OPEN NEEDED ONE REAL GUARD (codex round 2 caught the original claim overclaiming): the armed mode deliberately survives an ordinary leave through row 1's band, so a finger lift there keeps it, and the next tap's resolution burst delivers a pre-press entry motion that reached the hover-open and sprang an anchor's menu one event before the same burst's press toggle-closed it — a tap on Settings/Navigation visibly did nothing. The fix is device-blind, two halves: the platform raises the touch hold before delivering the entry motion (the finger has been down since the window opened, so the whole burst honestly reads the primary button held), and the GUI's hover-open refuses under a held primary button (on_motion's no-gesture tail) — a guard that also closes the mouse's own latent case, a held press that armed no gesture sliding along the armed row. So the claim stands BY THE GUARD, not by nature, and the tap opens the menu through the press path exactly as a click does. THE GUARD IS A THREE-MEMBER FAMILY RULE, built one member at a time across rounds 2-3 and stated whole at the close walk in `input_pointer.cpp`: NO ROW-1 HOVER ACT FIRES WHILE THE PRIMARY BUTTON IS HELD — the armed hover-OPEN (round 2, above), the open-menu anchor-SWITCH, and the open-menu hover CLOSE (both round 3; the close was the last unguarded member, added at the planner's judgment on the coder's flag). The SWITCH case: with a menu already OPEN, the burst's held entry motion reached the open-dropdown branch's hover anchor-switch — Settings open, tap Navigation: the motion switched to Navigation and the burst's press then found its own menu open and toggle-closed it, so the tap closed the popup instead of switching to it — and the identical held-primary guard now refuses the switch (the tap switches through the PRESS's own toggle, as a click does). That guard also fixes the mouse's own latent bug at that branch, confirmed at the site: press-hold an item (or the anchor press's drag-into-the-box claim) and slide across the other anchor, and the switch fired mid-press — toggle_dropdown's close wipes the whole Dropdown struct, destroying the popup's live press claim out from under the held button. The CLOSE case changes no tap outcome — press-anywhere-closes at on_button_press already closes the menu on the burst's own press, so the tap nets the same result by the cleaner path — while for the mouse a mid-press slide across a non-anchor (Quit, the view bar's three) now keeps the popup's live press claim exactly as the switch guard keeps it. Every unheld hover act is untouched. Two recorded edges of the same coin: whatever a resting HELD pointer does today (a pressed button's face, a dwell that elapses under a held press) a held finger does identically — the unified premise means there is no touch-specific answer either way — and a finger lifting with a mouse resting in the window delivers the translation end's RESTORE MOTION at the mouse's own position (the round-3 focus fork, in The Translation Contract above), so the resting mouse's hover face and cursor are re-derived from truth on the lift itself rather than stranded at the finger's zone; the leave — and the face loss it briefly carried for a coincidentally resting mouse, recorded here as an accepted glitch between rounds 2 and 3 — now fires only when no mouse is focused, where there is no face to lose. ### The Dialog Window's Per-Surface Routing (2026-08-12 evening)

THE MODAL DIALOG IS A SECOND SURFACE (its own labwc window — conventions.md
owns the modality contract), and the touch stream ROUTES BY ITS FIRST
FINGER'S SURFACE: wl_touch.down carries the touched surface, the platform
captures it once at the stream's open (the pan-zone answer's own pattern —
one capture, fixed for the stream's life), and the whole stream follows it.

- A DIALOG-owned stream is the plain one-finger pointer translation into the
  dialog's own hooks, in dialog-local coordinates: a tap is a click at the
  lift (a prompt button answers, OK/Cancel answer, the field takes the
  caret), hold unlocks the pointer at the 60 ms mark (hold-then-drag drags a
  field selection). The PAN-ZONE QUERY IS NEVER ASKED for a dialog down —
  it answers MAIN-window geometry — so the zone answer rests false, the
  window runs the short deadline, and nothing nav- or region-shaped can arm
  on a dialog: a SECOND finger on a dialog stream is ignored whole (there is
  no pinch and no upgrade there; nothing in a dialog pans or zooms). The
  translation's end delivers its release and nothing more — the leave hook
  and the restore motion are MAIN-window state repairs, and a finger lifting
  from the dialog moved none of them. THE ZOMBIE GATE COVERS GLASS BY
  CONSTRUCTION: a dialog stream translates into the dialog's own entry points,
  and those refuse while the window stands over an answered modal state (the
  span between the answering input and the run loop's settled tail — one
  gate, `modal_dialog_state_standing`, for mouse and finger alike), so a tap
  queued behind the answer cannot press a button whose answer no longer
  exists. A DELIVERY-TIME GATE IS SOUND *HERE* and nowhere else on glass:
  the dialog's entry points ask the modal-state question on behalf of the
  surface that owns the input, so "the state is gone" and "this input is
  dead" are the same fact — whereas a MAIN-surface stream's deferred act is
  measured against the main window's veil, which the answer itself removes,
  which is why that side is poisoned at its origin instead (next bullet).
- A MAIN-owned stream BORN WHILE A DIALOG STANDS IS POISONED AT ITS FIRST
  DOWN (codex round 9, 2026-08-12): the platform enters `Drain` right there
  instead of `Pending`, so the stream delivers NOTHING for its whole life —
  no tap, no slop-crossing pointer or single-finger pan, no region hold, no
  two-finger nav (the stream is one unit; a second finger landing in a drain
  is ignored like any other) — and the poison's whole lifecycle is that
  phase: set at the down, cleared by the ordinary drain exit when every
  finger has lifted. THE TEST IS "WAS THE GUI MODAL WHEN THIS FINGER LANDED",
  BY EITHER WITNESS — the dialog toplevel (live modal or zombie) OR the modal
  state alone, which is the span from an opener's own dispatch until the
  settled tail opens the window, the zombie span's mirror image at the other
  end and equally reachable inside one dispatch batch —
  because THE ORIGIN IS WHAT DECIDES: this window converts a down at
  its LIFT, its SLOP CROSSING or its EXPIRY, so a resolution that re-asks
  the modal state reads a state the answering input may already have cleared
  — finger down under the modal, Esc, finger up, and the tap OUTRUNS the
  settled tail into a main-window command behind a dialog still on screen
  (over Delete/Render/Quit, a fired command). Delivery-time gating cannot
  answer that; origin-time gating can, and does. The rule it instances —
  AN INPUT THAT ORIGINATED WHILE A DIALOG STOOD IS DEAD, whatever the state
  when it resolves — is stated once at `GuiPlatform`'s `dialog_open()` block,
  with the keyboard's two sites beside it.
  The GUI's standing veil gates still answer everything a LIVE-modal stream
  used to reach (a tap's press-release consumed, a two-finger nav frozen at
  the per-frame wheel-context refusal, a region hold refused in its begin);
  the poison makes main-window glass inert BY CONSTRUCTION rather than by
  the veil's reach, which is what closes the span the veil does not cover.
- The two-deadline window therefore applies PER SURFACE by construction: the
  deadline is picked from the captured surface's own answer at the down, and
  no mid-stream event can re-ask it.

This is what makes every dialog ANSWERABLE ENTIRELY ON GLASS with no special
case: the dialog's buttons are ordinary tap targets on its own surface, and
the WM close button (the session's Esc) is labwc's own tap target — the
modal-trap class of defect (an exit-less accidental editor, 2026-08-11) is
structurally gone, which is why the veil's Quit/Save reach-through was
deleted with the move (the succession record at input_pointer.cpp's top).
