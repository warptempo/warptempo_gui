# Where the chrome deviates from Windows 2000

The GUI's chrome is a period Windows measured pixel by pixel (the unit, the DrawEdge families, the roster case, the fonts' cells; `CLAUDE.md`'s rounding and colour rules, `src/gui/render.h`'s palette block), in ONE CHROME VOCABULARY (architect 2026-10-06, "pivot to Win2K as the official supported version and focus on that"; `src/gui/chrome_spec.h`): Windows 2000's "Windows Standard" chrome as ReactOS 0.4.16 draws it. Windows 95's chrome, the look of 2026-10-06's morning, was dropped the same evening and stands in git history; its colours remain a theme file, `windows-95-standard`. This list records the FEW MAJOR DESIGN DECISIONS where the chrome deliberately departs from Windows 2000, one line each, with the reason and the owner (architect 2026-10-03: "start a list of things where we deviate from Windows"): first where it follows ReactOS, then the product's own departures in drawing, then its own elements and behaviour. The obvious (antialiased proportional faces, a theme file's own colours, Display-P3 bytes) is not a deviation worth a line; the rule stands at its owner and is not restated here.

## Where it follows ReactOS rather than Windows 2000

The vocabulary is named for Windows 2000, whose chrome ReactOS 0.4.16 reproduces in all 29 colours and every metric (Windows XP's Classic and Server 2003's are the same), but its law is the architect's ReactOS captures (2026-10-06: "get it as accurate to ReactOS as we can"); where ReactOS itself departs from Windows 2000, the product follows ReactOS on purpose.

- **The caption's gradient spans the whole caption** (2026-10-06): Windows 2000 holds the icon cell at the start colour, ramps across the title alone and holds the buttons' stretch at the end colour; ReactOS ramps the whole caption rect, under the icon and the buttons (`GRADIENT_FILL_RECT_H` over the full caption), smooth 24-bit as Windows 2000 itself drew at 24 and 32 bits. Owner: `paint_caption_gradient` (render.cpp).
- **Minimise and Restore are ReactOS's Marlett's** (2026-10-06): Windows 2000 draws Minimise 6 x 2 and Restore's two windows 6 wide; ReactOS's own Marlett, drawn at 12 ppem, gives Minimise 7 x 2 and windows 7 wide. Maximise is Windows' own. Owner: paint_handler.cpp's caption glyph block (`kCaptionMinimizeGlyph`, `kCaptionRestoreGlyph`).
- **The face is Wine Tahoma** (2026-10-06): Windows 2000 sets its chrome in Microsoft's Tahoma; ReactOS ships Wine's metric-matched Tahoma (Vera-based, LGPL-2.1+), which the product bundles and draws as its antialiased outline at every size. Owner: `src/gui/gui_font.h` (`kGuiFaceSetWin2000`), `fonts/README.md`.
- **The icons are Tango's, greyed by saturation when disabled** (2026-10-06): Windows 2000 wears Microsoft's own icons; ReactOS wears Tango's, and so does the product — Tango 0.8.90's drawings — with ReactOS's disabled face, comctl32's ILS_SATURATE at 192 / 255 (each pixel its .30 / .59 / .11 luminance, alpha kept). Owner: `src/gui/icons.h`'s head, `assets/icons/tango/README.md`, `icons::draw_disabled`.

## The product's own departures in drawing

The chrome is scalable (the tablet runs at up to 300 %), so where Windows drew a pixel picture at 96 dpi the product draws what that picture would have been at the panel's own resolution.

- **The relief's corners are mitred** (2026-10-06, the architect overruling Windows' square join): DrawEdge joins a light edge to a dark one squarely, the dark side owning the corner pixel; here, the chrome being scalable, the top-right and bottom-left corner blocks of every raised and sunken edge are split along the 45-degree diagonal, antialiased, as a real bevel's corner is ("Anything the original designers would have drawn with a diagonal if they could, we should"); the one-colour frames and the cards' flat line stay square. Owner: `paint_relief_frame` (render.cpp).
- **A dither's cell is one device px** (2026-10-06, "dithering gets translated into whatever it perceptually becomes"): the checked face and the trim track's checker are Windows' pattern brush, Hilight over the ground, at the panel's own pixel, never at a scaled Windows px and never a blended colour; at 300 % the pattern is three times finer than a 96-dpi screen's. Owner: `paint_checker_rect` (render.cpp), render.h's palette head.
- **The glyphs are scalable outlines** (2026-10-06): the icons are Tango's scalable drawings, read through the product's own SVG-subset reader and rasterised at every scale, never a 16- or 24-px bitmap scaled up; the caption's Close is the smooth outline of Marlett's 8 x 7 staircase (the architect's pick of the mocks), not the staircase; the playhead's head is WordPad's ruler marker as a filled outline. Owner: `src/gui/svg_icon.h`, paint_handler.cpp's caption glyph block (`kCaptionCloseOutline`), render.h's playhead block.

## The program's own elements and behaviour

- **The trim lane is a scroll bar whose thumb is the kept region** (2026-10-02/03): Windows' thumb shows the viewport and its arrow buttons stand at the bar's ends; here the thumb spans the kept region between the trim bounds, and the two 16-px arrow buttons ride the thumb's own ends as the bounds' handles (the left arrow is the begin, the right the end). Owner: the trim lane block at `kTrimLaneHeightPx` and `render_trim_flags` (render.h / render.cpp).
- **The flags are not Windows at all** (2026-10-03; the kinds 2026-10-04): the marker flags are the Acid flag — the program's own flat box in its kind's colour (warp, phase reset, and the history view's added and removed, the invalid flag wearing the removed red) under the one flag label, a one-px outline in the theme's DkShadow, selection a brighter face of the kind's own colour under the one selected label (Windows' selected-icon reversal, a face that changes rather than a frame that appears; the outline stays DkShadow), and the in-place label edit is the selected flag opened for edit, its box the selected face under its label where Windows' F2 rename stands on the Window ground — standing on the well's border with one Windows px of air above for the ruler's minor ticks, its stem crossing the well's top lines between the outline carried down both its sides, so the stem never touches the well (2026-10-05). Owner: render.h's marker-lane paragraph, the marker-lane block at `kMarkerLaneAirPx` (render.h).
- **Cards, not message boxes** (the Windows look 2026-10-02): what Windows would say in a modal message box the app says on a notification card — the cards stacking down from the toolbar's band at the top right, each in Windows' tooltip frame, timed and closed by a press — so a message never stops the work. Owner: `src/gui/notifications.h`'s head, `paint_notifications` (paint_handler.cpp).
- **A refused entry is a selected text and a card** (2026-10-03): Windows answers a refused entry with a message box or a balloon; here a refused Enter selects the field's whole text, the first keystroke replacing it, and the reason is a notification card — no frame, colour or glyph changes on the field (the red frame retired the same day: "a red outline and the card is redundant"). Owner: `text_editor::refuse` (text_editor.h), `render_flag_editor_box` (render.cpp), the dialog field in `paint_modal_dialog` (paint_handler.cpp).
- **Row 8 is one time field and a line of words, not a status bar of parts** (2026-10-03; the field 2026-10-05): the clock alone sits in a one-line sunken time field, 17 Windows px tall in the body face, the period's (ACID Pro 3.0, Vegas Audio); the state / history line is painted on the bare chrome at the normal face, the Vegas / ACID pattern (words on the chrome, a time in a sunken cell). Owner: `paint_bottom_row_buttons_and_clock` (paint_handler.cpp).
- **Chrome buttons act at the lift, hotkeys at the press** (2026-08-13): Windows' buttons act at the release too, but its menus and toolbars differ in places; here the one timing doctrine covers every roster button, with the anchors' toggle, dismissals and the keyboard at the press. Owner: the doctrine block at `GuiInputHandler::on_key` (input_handler.h), `AppState::ChromePress`.
- **The tooltip waits for a still pointer, within a slop** (2026-09-29; recorded 2026-10-06): Windows 95's tooltip counts every mouse move, a mouse at rest not jittering; here the wait restarts, and a standing tooltip's life restarts, only on a motion of more than 3 Windows px from where the pointer was anchored on either axis — Android's own hover slop (4dp), because a hovering S Pen jitters and would otherwise starve the wait. Not a departure: a hint gone down by its 5 s life or by a click on its button does not come back while the pointer stays on that button, however it moves, until the pointer leaves the button and arrives again — Windows' own rule (2026-10-06). Owner: `kTooltipHoverSlopPx` (render.h), `note_tooltip_hover` (input_pointer.cpp).
- **A tooltip can carry a second line** (2026-07-31; the two-line form needs comctl32 4.70, after Windows 95): on a button whose press admits a modifier the tooltip adds a line naming the modified act, so the twin a Shift- or Ctrl-click reaches is discoverable without a menu. Owner: `redesign_button_tooltip` (app_state.h), `paint_shift_tooltip` (paint_handler.cpp).
- **No hover faces but the toolbars' hot case** (2026-10-02, the frozen design; 2026-10-06): the dialogs, the lists, the menus and the caption paint no hover state; the one hover face is the toolbars' hot case, Explorer's own flat toolbar (not a departure). Owner: render.h's palette head, `paint_toolbar_box` (paint_handler.cpp).
