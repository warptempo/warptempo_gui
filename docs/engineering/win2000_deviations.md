# Where the chrome deviates from Windows 2000

The GUI's chrome is a period Windows measured pixel by pixel (the unit, the DrawEdge families, the roster case, the fonts' cells; `CLAUDE.md`'s rounding and colour rules, `src/gui/render.h`'s palette block), in ONE CHROME VOCABULARY today (`src/gui/chrome_spec.h`): Windows 2000's "Windows Standard" chrome as ReactOS 0.4.16 draws it (architect 2026-10-06, "pivot to Win2K as the official supported version"), the default and the metric base whose layout a later vocabulary fits (Windows XP is the next, architect 2026-10-09); this list is Windows 2000's. Windows 95's chrome, the look of 2026-10-06's morning, was dropped the same evening, and GNOME 2's Clearlooks and Solaris 9's CDE, the second and third vocabularies of 2026-10-07 and 2026-10-08, were removed 2026-10-09 ("you can definitely drop Clearlooks and CDE. I'm definitely adding XP"); their departures stand in git history, and their colors remain in the theme catalog's record (`docs/themes/`), CDE's captions as built-in schemes (Clearlooks' dropped 2026-10-10). This list records the FEW MAJOR DESIGN DECISIONS where the chrome deliberately departs from Windows 2000, one line each, with the reason and the owner (architect 2026-10-03: "start a list of things where we deviate from Windows"): first where it follows ReactOS and where a capture of the real OS overrules ReactOS, then the product's own departures in drawing, then its own elements and behaviour. The obvious (antialiased proportional faces, a chrome's compiled colors, Display-P3 bytes) is not a deviation worth a line; the rule stands at its owner and is not restated here.

## Where it follows ReactOS rather than Windows 2000, and where a capture overrules ReactOS

The vocabulary is named for Windows 2000, whose chrome ReactOS 0.4.16 reproduces in all 29 colours and every metric (Windows XP's Classic and Server 2003's are the same). THE LAW IS A CAPTURE OF THE REAL OS WITH GOOD PROVENANCE (architect 2026-10-09 ~17:40: "ReactOS officially always gives way to screenshots — official screenshots with good provenance like guidebookgallery"): where such a capture and ReactOS differ, the capture wins; ReactOS fills in only where no capture rules (2026-10-06: "get it as accurate to ReactOS as we can"). The lines below where the product follows ReactOS are the places no capture of the real OS overrules it — they are drawing departures ReactOS made on purpose and the product keeps — and the last two lines are where a capture did.

- **The caption's gradient spans the whole caption** (2026-10-06): Windows 2000 holds the icon cell at the start colour, ramps across the title alone and holds the buttons' stretch at the end colour; ReactOS ramps the whole caption rect, under the icon and the buttons (`GRADIENT_FILL_RECT_H` over the full caption), smooth 24-bit as Windows 2000 itself drew at 24 and 32 bits. Owner: `paint_caption_gradient` (render.cpp).
- **Minimise and Restore are ReactOS's Marlett's** (2026-10-06): Windows 2000 draws Minimise 6 x 2 and Restore's two windows 6 wide; ReactOS's own Marlett, drawn at 12 ppem, gives Minimise 7 x 2 and windows 7 wide. Maximise is Windows' own. Owner: paint_handler.cpp's caption glyph block (`kCaptionMinimizeGlyph`, `kCaptionRestoreGlyph`).
- **The face is Wine Tahoma** (2026-10-06): Windows 2000 sets its chrome in Microsoft's Tahoma; ReactOS ships Wine's metric-matched Tahoma (Vera-based, LGPL-2.1+), which the product bundles and draws as its antialiased outline at every size. Owner: `src/gui/gui_font.h` (`kGuiFaceSetWin2000`), `fonts/README.md`.
- **The icons are Tango's, greyed by saturation when disabled** (2026-10-06): Windows 2000 wears Microsoft's own icons; ReactOS wears Tango's, and so does the product — Tango 0.8.90's drawings — with ReactOS's disabled face, comctl32's ILS_SATURATE at 192 / 255 (each pixel its .30 / .59 / .11 luminance, alpha kept). Owner: `src/gui/icons.h`'s head, `assets/icons/tango/README.md`, `icons::draw_disabled`.
- **The menu row's labels sit at the plain menu bar's seat, not ReactOS's Explorer band's** (architect 2026-10-09 ~17:40: "File Edit Settings looks off center … in winme.png and win2000pro.png the capitals are closer to the top"): ReactOS's Explorer menu band (browseui's CMenuBand, tmp/reactos-hover.png) draws its list-style toolbar button's text DT_VCENTER, standing the 13-row cell at (19 − 13) / 2 = 3 rows and the cap's top on row 6 of the 19; the architect's WordPad captures of the real OS (tmp/shots/winme.png: the band rows 22–41, the cap 27–35; tmp/shots/win2000pro.png: the cap 28–35, the i's dot on 27) put the cap's top on ROW 5 — 5 W of air above the cap, 6 below the baseline — the plain window menu bar's seat (menu.c), which is the cap band centred in the 19 rows by floor((19 − 8) / 2), the product's one cap-centring rule for a chrome box. The open title's sunken box and its one-W push are unchanged. Owner: `GuiPaintHandler::paint_menu_row` (paint_handler.cpp), `redesign_baseline`.
- **No etched pair stands above the menu row, nor under it** (2026-10-10): ReactOS's Explorer rebar draws its etched pair at the band's top, over the menu band (tmp/reactos.png rows 32–33); the plain window's menu bar draws none above it, and the program's is Cool Edit's plain menu bar, which draws none under it either — the line under the menu row is the program's frame (the Cool Edit section below). WordPad's etched pair under its menu bar, in the architect's captures of the real OS (tmp/win2000pro.png and tmp/winme.png: row 42 Shadow, row 43 Hilight), is its REBAR's band border, the wrong family for a plain menu bar; it stood under the menu row for the morning of 2026-10-10. Owner: `GuiPaintHandler::paint_menu_row`, render.h's menu-row block.

## The product's own departures in drawing

The chrome is scalable (the tablet runs at up to 300 %), so where Windows drew a pixel picture at 96 dpi the product draws what that picture would have been at the panel's own resolution.

- **The relief's corners are mitred** (2026-10-06, the architect overruling Windows' square join): DrawEdge joins a light edge to a dark one squarely, the dark side owning the corner pixel; here, the chrome being scalable, the top-right and bottom-left corner blocks of every raised and sunken edge are split along the 45-degree diagonal, antialiased, as a real bevel's corner is ("Anything the original designers would have drawn with a diagonal if they could, we should"); the one-colour frames and the cards' flat line stay square. THE RULE REACHES THE PROGRAM since 2026-10-09 ~12:40 ("any jagged staircase of pixel lines, even if it's only two lines, we take as an intention of a diagonal"): Cool Edit's square pixel corners are read as the period's limit, every two-tone corner of its rings and every two-line staircase drawn as the diagonal (the program's section below). Owner: `paint_relief_frame` (render.cpp), cool_edit_paint.h's head.
- **A dither's cell is one device px** (2026-10-06, "dithering gets translated into whatever it perceptually becomes"): the checked face and the popup lists' scroll-bar track (the trim track's too until 2026-10-09) are Windows' pattern brush, Hilight over the ground, at the panel's own pixel, never at a scaled Windows px and never a blended colour; at 300 % the pattern is three times finer than a 96-dpi screen's. Owner: `paint_checker_rect` (render.cpp), render.h's palette head.
- **The glyphs are scalable outlines** (2026-10-06): the icons are Tango's scalable drawings, rendered by resvg at every scale, never a 16- or 24-px bitmap scaled up; the caption's Close is the smooth outline of Marlett's 8 x 7 staircase (the architect's pick of the mocks), not the staircase. (The playhead's head was WordPad's ruler marker as a filled outline until 2026-10-09; it is Cool Edit's triangle since, the program's section below.) Owner: `src/gui/svg_icon.h`, paint_handler.cpp's caption glyph block (`kCaptionCloseOutline`).
- **The menus' access keys are always underlined** (architect 2026-10-10: "accelerators are not used on the tablet, but they are part of the aesthetic and therefore important"; "never hidden, since the tablet won't see them otherwise"): Windows 2000 hides the menu row's and its pull-downs' mnemonic underlines until Alt is held (the keyboard-cues setting's default); here they are always drawn, as ReactOS draws them, DrawText's prefix underline read off tmp/reactos-menu2.png — one W thick, one W of air under the baseline, the marked glyph's advance less one W — in every face the label takes (at rest, the open title's push, the lit row's selected text, the disabled emboss's two copies). Alt+F / Alt+E / Alt+S open the menus and a row's letter runs it. The dialog buttons' access keys (the same day, Windows strict) are drawn always the same way. Owner: `access_underline_rect` (paint_handler.cpp), `dropdown_title_access_key` and `PromptState` (app_state.h).
- **A disabled checked button keeps its sunken edge** (2026-10-08, the architect on the view group greyed under the color picker: the checker without the edge read as "a different color but not sunken"): ReactOS's flat toolbar draws a disabled checked button with the checker and no edge (toolbar.c's `TOOLBAR_DrawFrame` sinks the edge only for an interactive button); here the checked face is the checker inside the sunken line whatever the enabled bit, the disabled state only saturating the glyph — a checked button that loses its edge when disabled no longer reads as checked. (Superseded 2026-10-09: the toolbars are the program's, Cool Edit's case, whose dead checked button keeps its down face the same way — the section below.)

## The program's own elements and behaviour

- **The trim lane is a scroll bar whose thumb is the kept region** (2026-10-02/03): Windows' thumb shows the viewport and its arrow buttons stand at the bar's ends; here the thumb spans the kept region between the trim bounds, and the two 16-px arrow buttons ride the thumb's own ends as the bounds' handles (the left arrow is the begin, the right the end). (Superseded 2026-10-09: the trim lane is Cool Edit's view bar, the program's section below.)
- **The flags are not Windows at all** (2026-10-03; the kinds 2026-10-04): the marker flags are the Acid flag — the program's own flat box in its kind's colour (warp, phase reset, and the history view's added and removed; an invalid flag wears the removed pair, one red for both, the context telling them apart — 2026-10-04, again 2026-10-07) under the one flag label, a one-px outline in the palette's own `flag_outline` role (the fifteenth since 2026-10-08, its compiled default the canvas's black; from 2026-10-07 to 2026-10-08 the outline was the canvas by rule), never the scheme's DkShadow (2026-10-07: "otherwise it will poke out when going into the canvas"), selection a brighter face of its pair's own colour under the same one label (Windows' selected-icon reversal, a face that changes rather than a frame that appears; the outline stays) — and the in-place label edit departs from Windows' F2 rename too (2026-10-07, under every chrome): opening the editor recolours nothing, the flag keeping its selected face, its outline and its stem, its text and caret the one flag label, the selected substring the chrome's selected pair (render.h's EDITING paragraph) — standing on the well's border with one Windows px of air above for the ruler's minor ticks, its stem crossing the well's top lines between the outline carried down both its sides, so the stem never touches the well (2026-10-05). (Superseded 2026-10-09: the flags are Cool Edit's cues, the program's section below, in Cool Edit's two cue colors since ~11:50; the kinds' faces went with the box, and the invalid flag's red stays on its label.)
- **Cards, not message boxes** (the Windows look 2026-10-02): what Windows would say in a modal message box the app says on a notification card — the cards stacking down from the toolbar's band at the top right, each in Windows' tooltip frame, timed and closed by a press — so a message never stops the work. Owner: `src/gui/notifications.h`'s head, `paint_notifications` (paint_handler.cpp).
- **A refused entry is a selected text and a card** (2026-10-03): Windows answers a refused entry with a message box or a balloon; here a refused Enter selects the field's whole text, the first keystroke replacing it, and the reason is a notification card — no frame, colour or glyph changes on the field (the red frame retired the same day: "a red outline and the card is redundant"). Owner: `text_editor::refuse` (text_editor.h), `render_flag_editor_box` (render.cpp), the dialog field in `paint_modal_dialog` (paint_handler.cpp).
- **Row 8 is one time field and a line of words, not a status bar of parts** (2026-10-03; the field 2026-10-05): the clock alone sits in a time field in the body face and the state / history line is painted beside it at the normal face. Since 2026-10-09 both are Cool Edit's (the section below): the dark field in its own gripper / end-bar group, the line in the panel label's shadowed pair. Owner: `paint_bottom_row_buttons_and_clock` (paint_handler.cpp).
- **Chrome buttons act at the lift, hotkeys at the press** (2026-08-13): Windows' buttons act at the release too, but its menus and toolbars differ in places; here the one timing doctrine covers every roster button, with the anchors' toggle, dismissals and the keyboard at the press. Owner: the doctrine block at `GuiInputHandler::on_key` (input_handler.h), `AppState::ChromePress`.
- **The tooltip waits for a still pointer, within a slop** (2026-09-29; recorded 2026-10-06): Windows 95's tooltip counts every mouse move, a mouse at rest not jittering; here the wait restarts, and a standing tooltip's life restarts, only on a motion of more than 3 Windows px from where the pointer was anchored on either axis — Android's own hover slop (4dp), because a hovering S Pen jitters and would otherwise starve the wait. Not a departure: a hint gone down by its 5 s life or by a click on its button does not come back while the pointer stays on that button, however it moves, until the pointer leaves the button and arrives again — Windows' own rule (2026-10-06). Owner: `kTooltipHoverSlopPx` (render.h), `note_tooltip_hover` (input_pointer.cpp).
- **A tooltip can carry a second line** (2026-07-31; the two-line form needs comctl32 4.70, after Windows 95): on a button whose press admits a modifier the tooltip adds a line naming the modified act, so the twin a Shift- or Ctrl-click reaches is discoverable without a menu. Owner: `redesign_button_tooltip` (app_state.h), `paint_shift_tooltip` (paint_handler.cpp).
- **No hover faces on a button** (2026-10-02, the frozen design): the dialogs, the lists, the menus and the caption paint no hover state; the toolbars' hot case, Explorer's own flat toolbar (2026-10-06), went with the program's band (2026-10-09: Cool Edit's case has none). Owner: render.h's palette head, `ProgramSpec::case_hot_face` (program_spec.h).
- **A popup list's scroll arrow steps once per press** (2026-10-08): Windows repeats a held scroll arrow; here one press scrolls one row and the arrow stays pressed until the lift, the product's simplification. The wheel takes Windows' three lines a notch. Owner: render.h's popup scroll block.
- **The color picker is the program's own** (2026-10-07): Windows' ChooseColor dialog, with its swatch grid, its custom-color wells, its hue-and-saturation field and its luminance bar, was not the model; the program's colors are picked on one slim card without a caption, GNOME 2's own GtkHSV wheel (the hue ring with the saturation-and-value triangle inside it) under every chrome, beside the live chrome's own combo boxes, sliders, sunken hex field and push buttons, with OLD and NEW swatches, Copy and Paste between elements and a menu of named palettes (`src/gui/color_picker.h`'s head).
