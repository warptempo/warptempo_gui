# warptempo_gui

warptempo_gui time-warps a recording toward the tempos you choose. It was built for taking commercial orchestral recordings to historically informed tempos, phrase by phrase, by ear.

The engine is a PGHI phase vocoder (Prusa and Holighaus's phase-gradient heap integration, "Phase Vocoder Done Right") run at the authors' own parameters, N = 4096 with 75 % overlap, at the source's own sample rate, and extended in two ways: the analysis hop varies frame by frame with the warp map, and phase resets are placed by hand. Those two and its other departures from the paper (the output timing contract, the pinned randomness of quiet bins) are deliberate and recorded in the engine's own comments. A render is a finished 24-bit WAV through an unconditional limiter, and it is deterministic: the same project on the same machine and build renders the same bytes every time.

The program runs on one Android tablet; the laptop builds it and renders the same files with a headless CLI. Installing, building, rendering, the tablet and all upkeep are in [`INSTALL.md`](INSTALL.md).

The interface documents itself. With the tooltip lamp lit (TIPS, Toggle Tooltips, in the icon row), hovering the S Pen over any button names its act (while a box stands over the waveform, the key that opened it and TIPS still answer, the other top-row keys staying silent) and, where a long press does more, that gesture. TIPS stays as it was left, in every piece and at every launch. A button's tooltip answers the pen anywhere on the button, its border included (the keys of one group touch, so the pen sliding along a group never falls between two; the ground between two groups answers nothing); it shows after half a second (GTK's own wait) and stays as long as the pen stays on the button, going at once when the pen leaves it or touches the glass; once one tooltip has shown, the next button's shows after 60 milliseconds as long as the pen reaches it within half a second of leaving the last one; after a tap the half-second wait starts again from the lift. A tooltip stands just under a top-row key and just above every other button, its left edge on the button's (pulled in where it would run past the screen's right edge), in the same place however the pen came onto the button, and never moves while it stands. Hovering over a pass or a label reference shows, the same way and just above the marker, its first character over the first character of the marker's label, the value it resolves to — the very value a long press on JUMP copies; a struck warp marker, one within a millisecond of another, shows 1.00 whatever it carries, the tempo it plays at; any other marker with no such value (one that owns its tempo, a disabled one, a phase reset, or the history view's) shows none — except that a marker whose tempo adjustment its label cuts short ("...") shows its whole label on one line, struck where the label is struck. Every key of the program carries its drawing alone, no name printed under it (the on-screen keyboard's keys wear their characters on them instead); this guide calls each key by a short name (OPEN, SAVE, PLAY …), the act its tooltip names in words, and holds only what the tooltips cannot show: what the markers mean and how a movement is worked. One gesture recurs throughout: a long press (holding a key a moment before lifting) reaches a key's second act where it has one. A key that switches something on and off (a lamp, lit in the theme's accent) has no second act; a key that opens a box over the waveform may. The S Pen's button does nothing on a key — a press with it held is ignored — and works on the waveform alone (zoom, and the trim bar's begin and end).

## How the program says no

When a press refuses, the program says why in a card at the top right of the window. Silence means the press did nothing where it landed, or a command that acts in one place was already at its state there, or a question, a progress page or an editor (the flag editor, the bound editor, the Settings line, a name editor, the BPM form's fields) was standing and the command was not its own: under a question or a progress page its buttons are the way out, and under an editor the on-screen keyboard's Esc and Enter keys. So this guide lists no refusals: the program tells you at the moment it matters. A field that refuses what was entered also selects its whole text, so the next keystroke replaces it.

A card leaves on its own after a few seconds, waiting while the pen hovers over it or a press holds it; a few failures, such as a checkpoint that did not reach GitHub, stay until dismissed, on a red card instead of the grey. A tap anywhere on a card dismisses it, and a long press on any card dismisses them all. One red card says what is true rather than what happened: while any warp markers coincide (each within a millisecond of the next, the ones whose labels stand struck), "2 warp markers coincide at 00:12.345" stands at once, saying where (one time for each place the markers coincide, up to three, then "and K more", read as the view on screen reads time) and following the markers, and goes by itself when none do; tapped away, it comes back at the next edit that leaves markers coinciding, or when the piece is opened again.

## The concepts

### Warp markers

A warp marker sets the playback rate from its position until the next marker's. It carries a tempo, a stretch ratio in which 1.00 is the recording as it was made. The tempo may be written as a main speed plus a few signed adjustments that add up to it, so a section's headline speed stays visible beside the corrections made to it, and a marker may carry a fine scale on top, for matching a neighbouring section's effective tempo. A disabled marker contributes nothing, as if it were not there. Positions are authored in source view, the unprocessed recording, where a marker is pinned against an acoustic event: a little ahead of it, in the tail of what precedes, a far steadier cue than the silence before an attack. Tempos are judged in target view, the recording as it will render.

### Inheritance

A marker with no tempo of its own, a pass, takes the tempo of the nearest earlier marker that owns one; disabled markers and label references are passed over, and a pass with no owner before it plays as recorded. The resolution is live: disable or delete the owner and every pass after it re-resolves to the next owner back.

### Label definitions and references

This is the program's reason to exist. A marker can define a named label, and a later marker can reference it. The reference renders its own segment at whatever tempo gives that segment the definition's duration, so editing the definition moves every reference with it. DISABLE disables or enables each selected marker alone: a disabled definition's references are struck and play as recorded until the definition is enabled again, and DELETE likewise removes only the selected markers, a deleted definition's references struck from then on. A long press makes both carry the references: a long press on DISABLE disables or enables a definition and every reference to it together, and one on DELETE deletes the references with it; both need a label definition in the selection. While a definition is disabled its label may be defined again on another marker; if both definitions are then enabled, the two of them and every reference to the label are struck, and the references play as recorded until one definition is disabled, deleted or renamed. The marker's text editor still refuses a label that an enabled definition holds. A definition on a struck warp marker (one within a millisecond of another, below) defines nothing: its references are struck and play at 1.00, as the struck marker itself does. That is what ties a recapitulation to its exposition, or a repeat to its first time, through every later edit. A reference is confirmed against its definition by ear with the A/B audition: with one tab parked at the definition and the other at the reference, it plays the same short span from each tab's playhead, back to back, and any press on the window ends it, for it is meant to be heard hands-off.

### Phase resets

Phase resets are a separate collection of markers, authored in either audio view: in target view, where they are protected by ear, and in source view, where they are transcribed against the recording's own attacks. The vocoder's frame-to-frame phase coherence keeps sustained sound clean but smears an attack; at a phase reset it re-seats its phase instead, so the attack (an entry, a pizzicato, a timpani stroke) arrives clean. A reset belongs a lead-in ahead of its transient, and the drop does that arithmetic for you in either view, taking the lead-in once, and places a reset wherever it lands, a place where one already stands included (two resets within one step of the vocoder's analysis lattice act as one; two a little further apart may click, and are moved apart by ear), while a warp marker's drop refuses a place closer than one nudge (a millisecond) to another warp marker, disabled or not ("A marker already stands here"); DROP is dimmed where neither its tap nor its long press would place anything; a drag or a nudge moves a reset exactly where it is put, and the lead-in's outline, four solid near-black lines, is drawn in target view alone. Each one trades a little local coherence for the clean attack, so they are placed one by one, and nudged only when a dip, pop or crackle is heard.

### Two tabs, four views

The two tabs, A and B, are two viewpoints (viewport, zoom, playhead and trim) over one shared set of markers, so one can sit at a definition while the other sits at its reference. The active tab is the lit one of the A and B pair at the right end of the icon row, after the view buttons; a press on either switches the tab, the lit one popping out to the other as on the view pairs. A long press on NEXT in the bottom row walks both tabs, stepping each to its next marker and centering it, and comes back to the tab it started from; a long press on PREV walks both back the same way (neither long press acts while GRID is lit or in the history view, and a walk runs only where both tabs have a marker that way; otherwise a card names the tab that has none). The view is chosen by two pairs of buttons at the right end of the icon row, before the tab pair, each pair a radio with one member down: SOURCE and TARGET choose the audio, WARP and PHASE the markers, each key changing its own half and leaving the other alone; pressing the member already down pops it out and selects its partner. Their four combinations are the four views: source + warp, where warp markers are placed; source + phase reset, where resets are transcribed against the recording; target + warp, where tempos are judged; and target + phase reset, where the transients are protected.

The bottom row carries the clock alone at the left (in a bold larger than any other text, 14 W, and a quieter grey, its thousandths of a second kept), and five runs of keys at the right, the least used at the left and the most used at the right: COPY and PASTE (COPY copies the phase resets of the selected run of warp markers, on a read-only tab too; PASTE pastes them onto one selected warp marker, and a long press on PASTE pastes their enabled / disabled state alone, selecting the resets it changed and bringing them into view; a paste that would change nothing says so on a card ("The phase resets already match the copied ones", or for the long press "… the copied state"); both are dimmed, and say "The range contains a struck marker", while the stretch of warp markers they would read or write holds a struck one); START and END, which move the playhead to the trim's begin and end (a long press goes to the piece's own start or end instead, past the trim); the marker verbs DROP (which centers the playhead on the new marker as NEXT does, at the working zoom alone, and, like the other acts that spend a selection, puts ADD SEL out; a double tap on an empty spot of the marker lane drops a marker there too, by the same rules and with the same refusals), DELETE, DISABLE, INHERIT (which toggles every selected warp marker, an owner becoming a pass and a pass an owner at the tempo it sounded at before the press, the selection kept), JUMP, EDIT (opens the marker's editor, as a double tap on its flag or on a grid iteration's bound does, play stopping and the on-screen keyboard rising; a long press pastes the copied text onto every selected marker's line, as if typed into its editor), ADD SEL (while it is lit, the next drag of one finger or the pen across the waveform selects every marker it spans (the span from exactly where it touched down to exactly where the finger or pen is now stands lightened toward white over everything the waveform area draws while the drag lasts, by 3 %, Arc's own list-hover white), adding them to the selection, the last one reached becoming the focus with the playhead on it when the drag lifts, and the lamp goes out; TRIM and ADD SEL are a pair, lighting either puts the other out, and the history view's opening puts both out; a zoom puts out neither) and DRAG (a long press on DELETE or DISABLE carries a label definition's references with it); DOWN, UP, PREV and NEXT (DOWN and UP step one at a time, and the four arrows, DOWN, UP, LEFT and RIGHT, repeat while held, which PREV and NEXT do not; PREV and NEXT are dimmed where there is no marker left that way); and CENTER, LEFT, RIGHT and PLAY. In the car, the console's rewind and fast forward have two modes: while the piece plays they move the play three seconds back and forward, the loop wrapping at its end and stopping at its begin; stopped they undo and redo, and nothing plays until play is pressed. In the render player the same two move a playing render three seconds back and forward (a fast forward inside its last three seconds ends it, as playing to its end does); with the render paused or none playing they step the chosen line up and down instead, the first line's rewind going up to the folders, so changing track from the car is pause, step, play. The icon row opens with OPEN, SAVE, UNDO, REDO and RENDER. In the icon row, IN and OUT (Zoom In and Zoom Out), left of FIT, zoom the waveform in or out about its middle, each press twice as close or twice as far, stopping at the deepest zoom and at the whole piece; FIT (Zoom to Fit) shows the whole piece and, pressed again, goes back to the view it left — a zoom in between, by IN or by any other zoom, ends that return, and the next FIT shows the whole piece again; a long press frames the trim, as a double tap on the trim bar does (two taps on the bar within about six tenths of a second and a few pixels of each other; the first acts as a tap always does, and the second never drags; the bar has no long press, so a press held still on it and lifted is only a tap). The framing shows at most a twentieth of the window as empty space on either side of the trim, and none on a side that stands on the piece's start or end. A long press on PRESETS resets the trim to the whole piece (PRESETS, below). A double tap is slow and deliberate by design, so a marker is never dragged sideways by accident: on a marker's flag (a phase reset's flag has no editor, so two taps there are two taps), on an empty spot of the marker lane or on the trim bar, never on a key, a list's row or an editor's text. With the S Pen's button held, a tap on the trim bar moves whichever end of the trim is nearer the pen to the tap (a tap within half a pixel of an end does nothing). A key's drawing shows what its next press does, and its tooltip names it, so a few change with the state: RENDER shows Cancel's cross while a single render runs (a batch's progress page has its own Cancel, and RENDER keeps its drawing behind it), SAVE shows a commit in the history view (a pull there when GitHub has newer checkpoints, and two arrows, one up and one down, when the press would ask GitHub again, while offline), FIT shows going back when the press would go back to the view it left, PLAY shows stop while playing, INHERIT shows a link (the tie) under the lit GRID lamp, and the player's PLAY shows pause, or an open folder over a folder; a toggle keeps its one drawing, its key lit while it is on.

FOLLOW and RESTRICT (RESTR), after MAGNIFY in the icon row, are two lamps for what the window does while the piece plays, and lighting one puts the other out. With FOLLOW lit the moving playback line runs to the middle of the window and then stays there while the waveform moves under it, so the playback stays in sight; a play begun right of the middle is brought to the middle at once, one begun off the window's left edge is brought back into view, and at the end of the piece the waveform stops and the line runs on to the edge. A touch held on the window holds the picture still while the line runs on, and the picture catches up when it lifts. With RESTRICT lit the playback stays in the window instead: it stops when the moving line reaches the window's right edge, the playhead resting where it was, and while the playhead is outside the window PLAY plays nothing and says "Playhead is off screen". So a press in the waveform's lower channel, released without moving, plays from there and stops at the right edge. The A/B audition and the car's play are never stopped by it, and the render player and the score viewer are players of their own. Besides its own key, nothing but the other lamp puts either out: a zoom, a pan, a switch of tab, a box opened, PLAY and STOP leave them as they are, and so does the history view, where their keys are not shown and, nothing playing there, they do nothing; they stand as they were when it closes. Both lamps are kept on the device: each opens as it was left, in every piece and at every launch. MAGNIFY stays as it was left, in every piece and at every launch, lighting as soon as the piece's magnified picture is ready.

TRIM in the icon row, after RESTRICT, is the lamp for setting the trim on the waveform. While it is lit, the next drag of one finger or the pen across the waveform (without the pen's button) sets the current tab's trim from where it touched down to where it lifts, instead of moving the view, and leaves the playhead where it was; when that drag is done the lamp goes out. While the drag stands, from the first change it makes to the trim, the span the trim now covers is lightened toward white over everything the waveform area draws (the waveform, its lines, the markers' lines and the playhead's included), by 3 %, Arc's own list-hover white; it is gone when the finger lifts. While it stands, the trim bar shows the end the pen is moving held, its cap in the accent and following the pen, the other cap white. It also goes out when it is pressed again, when ADD SEL is lit, when the trim changes any other way (its bar dragged, a preset applied, the trim reset) and when the history view opens; a tap on the waveform still places the playhead (or, in the lower channel, plays from there), and the lamp stays lit through a pan, a zoom, a switch to the other tab and under any box or question. It is dark whenever a piece opens, works on a read-only tab and while grid iterations is on, and is not in the history view, which never changes the trim.

PRESETS, after TRIM, opens the piece's list of named trim presets in the box over the waveform, each line a preset's name, in the order of the music — by where each begins, the shorter first of two that begin together; the list opens on the preset that matches the current tab's trim, or on the first. A tap on a line applies that preset to the current tab's trim and frames it, as a double tap on the trim bar does, and the list closes. DOWN and UP at the right below the list move the choice without applying anything, which is the way to a preset you mean to save into or delete. SAVE gives the chosen preset the current trim, its name kept, and DELETE removes it, each after asking, with Cancel the answer ready; SAVE is dimmed on a preset that already holds the current trim, and with no presets yet it does what SAVE AS does. SAVE AS asks for a name and saves the current trim under it as a new preset, the new line chosen when the list comes back (a name must be new to the list, not empty, and must not begin or end with a space; Cancel comes back to the list with nothing saved). RENAME opens the same page holding the chosen preset's name, and Enter renames it by the same rules (the same name back changes nothing, and Esc leaves it); the list keeps the order of the music, so a rename moves a preset only among presets on the very same trim. A name that could not name a file (one holding a slash, or `.` or `..`) is refused too, because RENDER names its files after the presets. RENDER, the word just before Close, renders the chosen preset: the piece as it stands now under that preset's trim, the current trim left untouched, as a file named after the preset with its place in the list in front (`3_<name>`, or `03_<name>` in a list of ten or more, the way a spread's cells are numbered), into the render player's newest folder when that folder holds presets (replacing that preset's file there while the list is as it was) and into a new folder, `N_presets`, otherwise; the main window comes back, the render's line in the status bar. A long press on RENDER renders every preset (one whose name could not name a file is left out, a card saying to rename it), always into a new folder, a box counting them as it does a spread's cells, and the render player opens on the folder when the last is done, its files in the order of the music. Either way the list closes once a render starts, and the renders can be loaded in place like any other; RENDER is dimmed with no preset chosen and while grid iterations is on, and a preset saved earlier under a name that could not name a file still applies and saves, RENDER saying to rename it and the list staying. Close, or PRESETS again, closes the list (the key's tooltip reads Close while the list stands). A long press on PRESETS resets the current tab's trim to the whole piece, with the list closed or standing — the list then stays, its line chosen, and the new trim shows when it closes; TRIM, a lamp, has no long press. The presets live beside the piece, in its folder's `trim/presets.txt`, and are kept at once on this device, never saved with the piece, committed or carried to the other device, like the score's page range; on a read-only tab and while grid iterations is on the list works as ever, the trim being the tab's viewpoint rather than the piece.

Save is the unsaved-work mark: it lights when the markers or the piece's settings block (the scale, the BPM and the rest) change, and is dimmed again once saved or undone back to the saved state. The tabs' viewpoints, their trims and their read-only locks never light it; they are written with the next save of such a change, and a trim or lock changed on its own is kept only by Force Save and Commit, the history view's SAVE, which saves the piece before it commits (a long press on SAVE in the main window, Save and Commit, exists only while SAVE is lit).

### History

A piece's checkpoints are git commits in the projects repository. The tablet authors and commits, by two acts under the title `Update <piece>` with nothing asked, each committing any change git sees in the piece's files and saying "Nothing to commit" when there is none, and each pulling instead when GitHub has newer checkpoints: Save and Commit, a long press on SAVE in the main window, there only while SAVE is lit; and Force Save and Commit, SAVE in the history view, which commits a trim or view change alone too. GitHub is the hub, and the laptop takes the tablet's commits with plain git, to render them. A tap on SAVE in the main window is the plain save. The history view walks the checkpoints and shows each as a diff of flags, from which a difference can be reverted or a whole checkpoint loaded in place; a marker removed shows grey, its label struck through, a marker added or changed in place shows in the accent color, as a marker that is on does, a changed marker's label its old text struck through and then its new text (a `#` in front of a side that is off), a moved marker being one removed at its old place and one added at its new, and NEXT visits each; with TIPS lit, hovering the pen over one shows its label on one line exactly as the marker shows it, so a crowded neighbor stays readable; the mechanics are in [`INSTALL.md`](INSTALL.md)'s Daily use. The view always opens on the most recent checkpoint. Over the checkpoints the status bar reads the checkpoint's place in the walk, then the date and time it was committed, in this device's own time and written as the title bar's clock writes it (`03/17 | 2026-11-24 3:38 PM`), then how the device stands against GitHub; the session's own timeline shows no date. CUMUL (Toggle Cumulative) compares every step against the current state instead of against the next newer one, and keeps that reading until it is pressed again or the view is left; it is dark at every visit. SESSION (Toggle Session History) walks this session's own undo and redo timeline instead of the checkpoints, one step per state Undo and Redo can reach, and the view opens on it by itself where the device has no clone to read the checkpoints from.

### Settings

The icon row's SETUP key opens the settings list in the box over the waveform: each of the eighteen settings below as its line, `key=value` with the value it holds now, the device's first and then the piece's, no title over either (the lists carry no titles); among the piece's lines `scale` and `bpm`, which BPM iterations write, stand dimmed, there to read and taking no press. While the list stands the key stays lit, and pressing it again closes it (its tooltip reads Close then), as does the Close button under the list. A tap on a line opens that setting on a page of its own, in place of the list: one text field across the page's width, 12 W in from its left and top, holding the value alone, ready to edit, the cursor at its end; the setting's name is not shown there and cannot be changed, and nothing else is on the page. The on-screen keyboard's Enter applies the value and its Esc leaves everything as it was, and either way the settings list comes back on that line. Four lines list their setting's values instead, in the settings list's place, the value it holds now chosen and in the middle: `true_colors` `on` and `off`, `waveform_grow` 0 up to 2 in steps of a quarter, `noise` the folders under `noise/` and `noise_crossfade_s` 1 to 30, each value written as in its line (the grow being in device pixels and the crossfade in seconds). A tap on a value applies it at once, keeps it for the next launch and returns to the settings list, on that setting's line, now showing the new value; the value already held changes nothing and returns the same; Cancel returns to the settings list with nothing applied; and SETUP closes the whole list, as over the settings list itself. These four never open a typed line. In the open entry the on-screen keyboard's Tab (the key wearing the indent drawing) brings back the value the setting holds now when the entry is empty, so a value cleared by hand is edited rather than retyped, and in `projects_path` it completes what you have typed of a folder's name; a Tab that adds nothing does nothing. The settings: `title`, `notes`, `url` and `cover`, the piece's own, saved with it and undone with UNDO; `score_path`, the piece's score folder (Score, below), read at once and kept at once on this device, never with the save; `gui_scale`, the window's scale, applied at once; `projects_repo` and `projects_path`, where the projects live (a new path takes effect at the next Open Project or launch, as a card says); `true_colors`, `on` or `off` (Colors, below); `waveform_grow`, how far the waveform's drawn shape is grown on every side before its edge pixels take their shade, in device pixels, a multiple of 0.25 up to 2 (Ableton Live's at 125 % Windows scale sits between 0.5 and 0.75; a setting of this device and the one length on the waveform that is one, since the waveform's bars and drawn shape stand in device pixels whatever the scale and another tablet may not have this screen's density), applied at once; `noise`, `noise_attenuation_level` and `noise_crossfade_s`, the sound's folder, the fixed decibels it plays at under the NOISE slider's number, and its crossfade (Noise, below), applied at once; `osk_key_w` and `osk_key_h`, the on-screen keyboard's keys' width and height, and `osk_font_px`, the size of their labels, numbers under tuning (the panel, below), applied at once — everything after the piece's five belonging to the device, kept in its config for the next launch. The background sound's level has no line: it is seen and set on the icon row's NOISE slider alone (Noise, below), and kept in the device's config the same. A value its setting refuses is refused, its reason on a card and the whole entry selected. On a read-only tab, and while grid iterations is on, the list and its lines still open: the device's keys apply as ever, and so does `score_path`, the score folder being kept on this device and not with the piece; the piece's other four, `title`, `notes`, `url` and `cover`, are refused, a card saying why, until the lock is lifted.

### Score

A movement's sheet music can stand beside it: the SCORE key in the icon row opens the score viewer, which shows the score one page at a time in the box over the waveform, each page centered and scaled to fit the box, and closes it again (its tooltip reads Close while the viewer stands). The pages are a folder made by `tools/score_extract.py` ([`INSTALL.md`](INSTALL.md)) from a sheet-music video, or from a PDF of the printed score: then each page the viewer shows is a stretch of a printed page cut between its systems (the next system's top may show at the bottom, and on an occasional page, a recitative's, the cut may fall through a staff or repeat part of another), every one at the same size so the staves never grow or shrink as you turn, and the PDF's pages without music (the cover, a preface) are left out; a piece whose own folder holds them as `score/` shows them with nothing typed. When one video carries the whole symphony, the other movements name the first one's folder with the setting `score_path`, relative to the project's folder (`../550 - 1/score`); an empty `score_path=` turns the score off. With no score, or a score folder the program cannot read, the SCORE key is dimmed and the piece opens as ever. The viewer numbers the pages its own way, 1 to the last, never by the numbers printed on them. The viewer is a player of its own: it plays the score folder's `audio.wav`, the video's soundtrack, and the page on screen is always the page that sound belongs to, turning by itself as it plays. Below the page, PLAY starts it and PAUSE stops it, BACK and FWD either side of it skip three seconds back and forward, ten with a long press, the slider is a media player's, moving with the sound and seeking where you let go of it, the page under its handle shown as you drag it (a playing sound pauses while the slider is held and plays on from where it is let go), and the label beside it reads the time into the page range and the range's length in whole minutes and seconds (`02:45 / 07:12`, a second counted once it is complete; the render player's reads the same way). When it reaches the range's end it stops dead and rests there, the last page on screen (a slider let go at the end does the same); PLAY there plays the range again from its start. Nothing in the viewer follows the piece: the piece's own playback stops when the viewer opens, its playhead stays where it was, and keeping the two together is yours to do by hand. A score folder with no playable `audio.wav` (a PDF's never has one) is turned by hand instead: PREV and NEXT turn one page, the slider shows the page under its handle as you drag it, and the label beside it reads which page of how many, counted within the page range. The page range is how a movement is cut out of the whole: START makes the page on screen the range's first, END its last, and RESET, after asking (a playing soundtrack pauses for the question), opens the range to every page again; once set, the player and its slider stay inside the range and nothing outside it plays. Like the trim they have no undo, and they are kept at once; on a read-only tab or while grid iterations is on they are dimmed, while turning pages and playing stay free. Each tab remembers where it was — the moment in the soundtrack, or the page when there is none — written as you go, and the viewer opens there.

### Noise

A bed of sound — rain, or whatever the chosen folder holds — can play under the work, to mask the room, and its loudness is the NOISE slider in the icon row, a little past TIPS: an entry field as tall as a key whose number, centered in it, is that level in decibels, with one decimal under 10 and none from 10 on ("-9.5", "-22"), and along whose foot a thin line in the theme's accent runs from the left as far as the level, the whole width at 0.0, a sliver at -60 and none at `-inf`, which is off (the level itself is kept exactly as the drag left it, finer than the number shows). Drag it to set the level: the drag is relative, the level moving with the finger from where it stood, a long stroke for a fine change and a short one for a coarse one (the whole range is about three quarters of the waveform's width), the sound following every motion; a tap turns the slider off (`-inf`) and the next tap brings back the level it had, kept on the device across pieces and launches (0.0 until a tap first turns it off); the number stays in the light text grey at `-inf`, the slider off but still working (it greys, and the line goes, only when the slider is dimmed, below). Above `-inf` the sound plays continuously, under everything the program plays — the piece, the A/B audition, the render player, the score's soundtrack — and through the silence between them, at that one level, which nothing playing ever lowers or pauses; it plays from the moment the program starts, at the level kept from last time; a tablet set up fresh starts at the level the program ships with, the tablet's own as last kept (silent only if that is `-inf`). It is a set of recordings played one after another in random order, never the same one twice in a row, each fading into the next over a few seconds; a rise from `-inf` starts afresh with one of them from its beginning, fading in over a fifth of a second once its recordings are loaded (the first rise reads them), and a fall to `-inf` fades out almost at once; dragging back and forth across `-inf` only turns the sound down and up again. Their own differences in loudness are kept, as nature's are. A folder of one recording loops it onto itself through the same fade. Leaving the slider at `-inf` frees the recordings from memory; the next rise reads them again. If the sound device changes (headphones connected or taken off), the sound stops with the music, its level untouched, and comes back, from a fresh recording, at the next play — Play, the audition, or the car's play — or at a tap on the NOISE slider: while the sound is off this way, a tap on a slider that is not at `-inf` only opens the sound again (the next tap turns it off as usual), a tap at `-inf` brings back its level and opens the sound together, and a drag that ends above `-inf` opens it too; nothing else plays, and "Playback is unavailable on this device" says when it cannot. The recordings are a folder `noise/<name>/` beside the device config ([`INSTALL.md`](INSTALL.md)), one folder per sound, and the setting `noise` names the one that plays (its line on the settings list lists the folders there); changing `noise` drops the recordings from memory and, with the level above `-inf`, reads the new folder at once. The named folder's recordings are kept in memory from the first rise until the program closes, the folder changes or the slider is left at `-inf`; nothing limits their size, and about 35 minutes, about 400 MB at 48 kHz, is a sensible ceiling. The setting `noise_level` is the slider's value, in decibels from -60 to 0, or `-inf`, kept exactly and shown to one decimal under 10 and to the whole decibel from 10; the sound plays at that level plus the setting `noise_attenuation_level`, a fixed number of decibels from -60 to 0, applied to the recordings as they are — so with the slider at 0.0 and the attenuation at -9 a moment that reached full scale in its file plays at about a third of full scale. The slider shows, drags and resets its own number alone, whatever the attenuation; the attenuation never turns the sound on or off. `noise_crossfade_s` is the length of the fade from one recording to the next, in whole seconds from 1 to 30 (a sound of other recordings may want another). `noise` and `noise_crossfade_s` list their values on the settings list (Settings, above), a tap applying one; `noise_attenuation_level` opens its line to type; all three apply at once and are kept on the device, as is `noise_level`, which the slider alone sets and the settings list does not show. With no folder of that name, or one the program cannot read, the NOISE slider is dimmed and takes no drag. The sound and the music are added together — the music, everything the program plays itself (the piece, the A/B audition, the render player and the score's soundtrack), always at its files' own level — and a sum louder than full scale is clipped in the program rather than at the speaker; whenever that happens the right of the two small squares beside the battery flashes red (the title bar, below). The tablet's own volume sets how loud it all is.

## A working method

How a movement actually gets authored, in the order that works:

1. Find the introduction measures that define the tempo. Set warp markers there, set the corresponding phase resets, then run BPM iterations and pick the tempo that sits right: with the measures' markers selected, BPM in the icon row opens a form in the box over the waveform, three lines, `beats=` (the beats the selection spans), `low=` and `high=` (the slowest and the fastest tempo to try), blank at every open; a tap on a line opens it to edit as on the settings list, an empty value clearing it; RENDER, dimmed until all three are set and low is not above high, renders the spread, one tempo per whole bpm from low; Cancel or BPM again closes the form with nothing rendered.
2. Work down the piece in chronological order, moving between the views (SOURCE / TARGET for the audio, WARP / PHASE for the markers): source + warp to place markers, target + warp for tempo adjustments by ear, and target + phase reset to protect the transients (source + phase reset when a reset is better placed against the recording itself).
3. Once some headway is made, copy label definitions into references (the exposition into the repeat and the recapitulation), and paste the definition's phase resets across with the propagate commands, all from the warp view: the bottom row's COPY key copies the phase resets of a run of selected warp markers, PASTE beside it pastes them onto one selected warp marker, and a long press on PASTE pastes their enabled / disabled state alone onto the matching resets there.
4. Phase resets are set with the phase-reset drop and nudged only when the phase alignment causes an audible dip, pop or crackle.
5. The A/B audition is crucial for confirming that label references match their definitions: it is the crux of the tempo-locking mechanism, which lets precise timing be replicated in the sonata-form fashion of repeated rhythmic motifs and phrases, at both large and small scale.
6. Grid and BPM iterations, trimmed target previews and the history view carry the rest: render a spread (a box counts the cells as they render, its Cancel stops the spread, and the render player opens on the spread when the last cell is done), listen in the render player (its DELETE removes the chosen render, a long press its whole folder; over the list of folders, the chosen folder, a long press all of them; each asks first), load the winner in place, checkpoint.

On timing: the scanner's smooth movement lets a warp marker land essentially at the onset of an attack, so place against what you see and hear together.

## Where the rest lives

- [`INSTALL.md`](INSTALL.md): installing, building, rendering, the tablet, daily use, trouble, and migrating an older project folder.
- The tooltips: every button's act and, where a long press does more, that gesture, with the tooltip lamp lit.
- There is no menu bar: OPEN (Open Project) is the icon row's first key, and its long press is Revert. It opens the project list, and while the list stands the key stays lit and pressing it again closes the list, while its long press still reverts. A question about unsaved work — choosing another project, or reverting — stands over the list: Cancel returns to the list as it was, and the answer that goes ahead reopens the program on the project. Quit is Android's Back, and Settings is the icon row's SETUP key.
- Questions and editors: a question fills the page and is answered by its buttons at the bottom right; a value (a setting, a preset's name, the BPM form's fields) is edited on a page of its own with one text field, and the on-screen keyboard's Enter keeps it and its Esc leaves it as it was.
- A label being edited in the marker lane (a marker's, or a grid iteration's bound): the on-screen keyboard's Enter keeps it and its Esc leaves it as it was; opening it stops play, and everything else in the window waits until the label is closed, a press or tap outside it included, the on-screen keyboard's keys aside. Erasing a grid iteration's bound and pressing Enter sets that bound to zero and leaves the other as it is; where zero would put the lower above the upper (or the upper under the lower) a card refuses it, and UP and DOWN from the empty text the same; both bounds at zero is no bracket.
- While an editor's on-screen keyboard stands it covers the bottom of the window (the status bar, the bottom row of buttons and the foot of the waveform); nothing above it moves, and every button of the top row greys. Opening any editor stops play.
- The source comments and `docs/engineering/closed_questions.md`: the engineering record of the rulings behind the behaviour, not user reading.
